内容简介｜SYNOPSIS

胡光墉（1823—1885 ），字雪岩，安徽绩溪人。作者在书中极力铺陈了主人公的传奇经历，胡雪岩初在钱庄学徒，因囊助潦倒的冗吏王有龄旋升，以致自身失业，王有龄感其思，遂二人结生死之交。后胡雪岩利用王有龄在宫场上的发达，开设钱庄，在官府势力、漕帮首领和外商买办之间，层层投靠，左右逢源，以精细的连环计算，收买人民、网罗赌棍、拉拢富商，混迹于勾栏赌场，竭力经营丝茶生意，大做军火勾当。其问，收姬纳妾，邀友狎妓，不惜重金为妓女赎身，又转手奉送于高官……由于胡雪岩精于谋划，手段灵活，因此，很快得以暴发，终至在上海、杭州立足，由市井布衣，跻于江浙大贾之列。列于《胡雪岩》（上、中、下）之后的《红顶商人》和《灯火楼台》等书（亦为台湾著名作家高阳先生所著，本公司出版），将继续向广大读者展现胡雪岩，这个半封建、半殖民地让会中的特定历史产物如何由商而宫、暴起暴落的曲折激烈的场面。

Hu Guangyong (1823–1885), known by his courtesy name, Xueyan, was a native of Jixi, Anhui. In this novel, the author recounts his protagonist's extraordinary career in rich detail. Hu Xueyan begins as an apprentice in a private bank. After providing financial assistance that helps Wang Youling, an impoverished minor official, advance in his career, Hu loses his own job. Deeply grateful, Wang forms a bond with him that neither life nor death can break. Later, drawing on Wang's growing influence in official circles, Hu establishes his own bank. He cultivates patrons among government officials, leaders of the grain-transport associations, and Chinese intermediaries working for foreign merchants, building connections on every side. Through a succession of carefully calculated moves, he buys people's loyalty, recruits gamblers, and courts wealthy merchants. Frequenting brothels and gambling houses, he energetically develops his silk and tea businesses and engages extensively in the arms trade. Meanwhile, he acquires concubines, visits courtesans with his friends, and spends lavishly to buy women out of prostitution, only to present them in turn to high officials... An astute planner with flexible methods, Hu rapidly amasses a fortune. He establishes himself in Shanghai and Hangzhou, rising from the ranks of ordinary townspeople to become one of the great merchants of Jiangsu and Zhejiang. The Red-Topped Merchant and Towers of Light, which follow the three volumes of Hu Xueyan, are also by the celebrated Taiwan-based author Gao Yang and are published by our company. They continue the turbulent story of this figure shaped by a particular historical age: a semi-feudal, semi-colonial society. Readers will see how he moves from commerce into official life, and how his spectacular rise gives way to a dramatic fall.

楔子｜PROLOGUE

在清朝咸丰七年，英商麦加利银行设分行于上海以前，全国金融事业，为两个集团所掌握，商业上的术语称为“帮”，北方是山西帮，南方地宁绍帮，所业虽同，其名则异，大致前者称为“票号”，后者称是“钱庄”。

Before the British Chartered Bank opened a branch in Shanghai in the seventh year of the Xianfeng reign, China's financial business was controlled by two groups, known in commercial usage as bang, or merchant networks. In the north stood the Shanxi network; in the south, the Ningbo–Shaoxing network. Although they pursued the same line of business, their establishments bore different names. Broadly speaking, those in the north were called piaohao, or remittance banks, while those in the south were known as qianzhuang, or private banks.

山西帮又分为祁、太，平三帮，祁县、太谷、平遥，而始创票号者，为平遥人雷履泰。他最初受雇于同县李姓，在天津主持一定颜料铺，招牌叫做“日升昌”，其时大约在乾隆末年。日升昌在雷履泰的悉心照料之下，营业日盛，声誉日起，连四川都知道这块“金字招牌”，因为雷履泰经常入川采购铜绿等等颜料，信用极好。

The Shanxi network was itself divided into three groups, based in Qixian, Taigu, and Pingyao. The founder of the remittance-bank business was Lei Lütai, a native of Pingyao. He had originally been employed by a man surnamed Li from the same county to manage a pigment shop in Tianjin called Rishengchang. This was around the end of the Qianlong reign. Under Lei's careful management, the business prospered and its reputation grew. Its name became a guarantee of quality known even in Sichuan, where Lei frequently purchased verdigris and other pigments and enjoyed an excellent reputation for reliability.

四川与他省的交通最不便，出川入川携带大批现金，不但麻烦，而且有风险。于是雷履泰创行汇兑法，由日升昌收银出票，凭票到指走地点的联号兑取现银。当然，汇兑要收汇费，名为“汇水”。汇水并无定额，是根据三个因素计算出来的：第一，路途的远近，远则贵，近则廉。第二，银根的松紧，大致由小地方汇到大地方来得便宜，由大地方汇到小地方来得贵，因为地方大则银根松，地方小则银根紧，如某处缺乏现金，而有待兑的汇票，则此时有客户交汇，正好济急，反有倒过来贴补客户汇费的。

Travel between Sichuan and the other provinces was particularly difficult. Carrying large amounts of cash into or out of the province was not only inconvenient but also dangerous. Lei therefore introduced a system of remittance: Rishengchang accepted silver and issued a draft that could be exchanged for silver at an affiliated establishment in a designated location. A remittance fee, known as huishui, was naturally charged. The fee was not fixed but depended on three factors. The first was distance: the farther the destination, the higher the charge. The second was the availability of ready money. As a rule, it cost less to remit funds from a small town to a large city than in the opposite direction, since ready money was more plentiful in larger places and scarcer in smaller ones. If a branch was short of cash and had outstanding drafts to redeem, a customer bringing in silver for remittance would provide much-needed relief. In such cases, the bank might even pay the customer a premium instead of charging a fee.

最后是计算银锭的成色，银锭的大小，通常分为三种，最大的五十两，为了便于双手携捧，做成两头翘起的马蹄式，即所谓“元宝”，而出于各省藩库的，称为“官宝”，其次是中锭，重十两，有元宝形的，称为“小元宝”，但通常都做成秤锤式，最小的或三两，或五两，通称“银锞”。再就是碎银，轻重不等。此外各省有其特殊的形制，如江浙称为“元丝”，底凹上凸，以便叠置。但不管任何形状、大小，银子的成色，各地不同，需要在交汇时核算扣足。

The final consideration was the purity of the silver ingots. Ingots generally came in three sizes. The largest weighed fifty taels and were shaped like a horseshoe with raised ends, making them easier to lift with both hands. These were the ingots known as yuanbao; those issued by provincial treasuries were called official ingots. Medium-sized ingots weighed ten taels. Some resembled the larger yuanbao and were called small yuanbao, though most were shaped like scale weights. The smallest weighed three or five taels and were commonly called silver lumps. There was also broken silver, which varied in weight. Each province had its own distinctive forms. In Jiangsu and Zhejiang, for example, the ingots known as yuansi had concave bases and convex tops so that they could be stacked. Whatever their shape or size, however, the purity of silver differed from place to place. These differences had to be calculated and fully allowed for when a remittance was made.

由于汇兑凭票兑银，所以叫做“票号”。早先运送现银的方法，如果不是随身携带，就得交镖局保送，费用大，麻烦多，走得慢，而且还有风险，万一被动或者出了其他意外，镖局虽然照赔，但总是件不愉快的事，所以票号一出，请教走镖英雄好汉的人就少了。早期的票号，多为大商号兼营的副业，到咸丰初年，始有大量专营的票号出现。但票号的势力不得越长江而南，因为江南的钱庄，为保护本身的利益，一方面仿照票号的成例，开办汇兑业务，一方面力拒票号的侵入。至于票号除汇兑以外，以后亦经营存款及放款，所以票号与钱庄的业务，由于彼此仿效的结果，几乎完全相同，只是在规模上，钱庄逊于票号而已。

Since silver was paid out against a draft, these establishments came to be called piaohao, literally “draft houses.” Previously, anyone wishing to move silver had either to carry it personally or entrust it to an armed escort agency. The latter option was expensive, troublesome, and slow, and it still involved risk. If the silver was stolen or some other mishap occurred, the escort agency would reimburse the loss, but the experience was unpleasant nonetheless. Once remittance banks appeared, fewer people had occasion to seek the services of those valiant escorts. The earliest remittance banks were mostly side businesses run by large trading firms. Only in the early years of the Xianfeng reign did establishments devoted exclusively to remittance begin to appear in considerable numbers. Their influence, however, did not extend south of the Yangtze. To protect their own interests, the private banks of the lower Yangtze region copied the remittance system while firmly resisting the northern banks' expansion. The remittance banks, in turn, later began accepting deposits and making loans. Through this process of mutual imitation, the two types of bank came to conduct almost identical business, although private banks generally remained smaller.

钱庄业多为宁绍帮所经营，而镇江帮有后来居上之势。但在同治到光绪初年，全国最大的一家钱庄，规模凌驾票号而上之，同时他的主人亦不属于宁绍帮，是为当时金融业中的一个特例。这家钱庄的字号叫“阜康”，它的主人是杭州人。

Most private banks were operated by the Ningbo–Shaoxing network, though the Zhenjiang network was beginning to catch up and overtake it. Yet from the Tongzhi reign into the early years of Guangxu, the largest private bank in China surpassed even the remittance banks in scale. Its proprietor did not belong to the Ningbo–Shaoxing network either. It was an exception in the financial world of its time. The bank was called Fukang. Its proprietor was a man from Hangzhou.

正文｜THE NOVEL

胡雪岩全传——平步青云（上）

The Complete Story of Hu Xueyan — A Swift Rise to Prominence, Volume I

第一章｜CHAPTER ONE

有个福州人，名叫王有龄，他的父亲是候补道，分发浙江，在杭州一住数年，没有奉委过什么好差使。老病侵寻，心情抑郁，死在异乡。身后没有留下多少钱，运灵柩回福州，要好一笔盘缠，而且家乡也没有什么可以倚靠的亲友，王有龄就只好奉母寄居在异地了。

There was a man from Fuzhou named Wang Youling. His father, an expectant circuit intendant assigned to Zhejiang, had lived in Hangzhou for several years without receiving any worthwhile appointment. Worn down by age and illness, and burdened by disappointment, he died far from home. He left little money. Transporting his coffin back to Fuzhou would have cost a substantial sum, and there were no relatives or friends in their home town on whom the family could rely. Wang Youling therefore had little choice but to remain in Hangzhou with his mother.

境况不好，而且举目无亲，王有龄混得很不成样子，每天在“梅花碑”一家茶店里穷泡，一壶“龙井”泡成白开水还舍不得走，中午四个制钱买两个烧饼，算是一顿。三十岁的人，潦倒落拓，无精打采，叫人看了起反感。他的架子还大，经常两眼朝天，那就越发没有人爱理他了。唯一的例外是个二十岁左右的少年，王有龄只知道他叫“小胡”。小胡生得一双四面八方都照顾得到的眼睛，加上一张常开的笑口，而且为人“四海”，所以人缘极好。不过，王有龄跟他只是点头之交，也识不透他的身分，有时很阔气，有时似乎很窘，但不管如何，总是衣衫光鲜，象这初夏的天气，一件细白夏布长衫，浆洗得极其挺括，里面是纺绸小褂裤，脚上白竹布的袜子，玄色贡缎的双梁鞋，跟王有龄身上那件打过补钉的青布长衫一比，小胡真可以说是“公子哥儿”了。他倒是有意结交王有龄，王有龄却以自惭形秽，淡淡地不肯跟他接近。

Short of money and with no one to turn to, Wang lived in miserable circumstances. He spent his days lingering in a teahouse at Meihuabei, reluctant to leave even after his pot of Longjing tea had been brewed until it tasted like plain water. At noon he spent four copper coins on two baked flatbreads and called that a meal. At thirty, he was shabby, dispirited, and down on his luck—a sight that put people off. Yet he still gave himself airs, often looking down his nose at everyone, which made them even less inclined to have anything to do with him. The sole exception was a young man of about twenty, whom Wang knew only as Young Hu. He had eyes that seemed to take in everything around him, a ready smile, and an easy generosity that made him welcome wherever he went. Wang, however, knew him only well enough to exchange a nod and could not make out his circumstances. At times he seemed well supplied with money; at others, decidedly short of it. Whatever his fortunes, he was always neatly dressed. In this early-summer weather, for instance, he wore a fine white ramie gown, washed and starched until it was beautifully crisp, over a short silk jacket and silk trousers. His socks were of white cotton, and his black satin shoes had twin raised seams. Beside Wang's patched blue cotton gown, Young Hu's appearance was positively that of a wealthy young gentleman. Young Hu clearly wished to make friends with Wang, but Wang felt ashamed of his own poverty and kept him at a distance.

这一天下午的茶客特别多，小胡跟王有龄“拼桌”，他去下了两盘象棋，笑嘻嘻走回来说：“王有龄，走，走，我请你去‘摆一碗’。”摆一碗是杭州的乡谈，意思是到小酒店去对酌一番。“谢谢。不必破费。”“自有人请客。你看！”他打开手巾包，里面包有二两碎银子，得意地笑道：“第一盘‘双车错’，第二盘‘马后炮’，第三盘，小卒‘逼宫’，杀得路断人稀。不然，我还要赢。”为了盛情难却，王有龄跟着去了。一路走到“城隍山”——“立马吴山第一峰”的吴山，挑了个可以眺望万家灯火的空旷地方，一面喝酒一面闲谈。

One afternoon the teahouse was unusually crowded, and the two shared a table. Young Hu went off to play a couple of games of Chinese chess. When he returned, he was beaming. “Wang Youling, come along! Let me buy you a drink.” His expression, “set out a bowl,” was a Hangzhou phrase for going to a little tavern to share some wine. “Thank you, but you needn't go to the expense.” “Someone else is paying. Look!” He opened a bundle wrapped in a handkerchief to reveal two taels of broken silver. Grinning proudly, he said, “In the first game I caught him with a pair of chariots; in the second, with a cannon behind a horse. In the third, a little pawn cornered his general. I routed them so thoroughly that there was no one left to play. Otherwise, I'd have won still more.” Unable to resist such a warm invitation, Wang went along. They walked to City God Hill—the Wushan celebrated in the line, “Rein in your horse upon Wushan's foremost peak”—and chose an open spot overlooking the lights of countless homes. There they drank and talked.

酒到半酣，闲话也说得差不多了，小胡忽然提高了声音说：“王有龄，我有句话，老早想问你了。我看你不是没本事的人，而且我也懂点‘麻衣相法’，看你是大贵之相，何以一天到晚‘孵’茶店？”王有龄摇摇头，拈了块城隍山上有名的油饼，慢慢咬着，双眼望着远处，是那种说不出来的茫然落寞。“叫我说什么？”王有龄转过脸来盯着小胡，仿佛要跟他吵架似的，“做生意要本钱，做官也要本钱，没本钱说什么？”“做官？”小胡大为诧异，“怎么做法？你同我一样，连‘学’都没有‘进’过，是个白丁。哪里来的官做？”“不可以‘捐班’吗？”

When the wine had begun to take effect and their small talk was nearly exhausted, Young Hu suddenly raised his voice. “Wang Youling, there's something I've been wanting to ask you for a long time. You don't strike me as a man without ability. I also know a little of the Mayi art of face-reading, and yours looks like the face of a man destined for high office. Why do you spend all day nesting in a teahouse?” Wang shook his head. He picked up one of the fried cakes for which City God Hill was famous and ate it slowly, gazing into the distance with an expression of bewildered loneliness that was hard to put into words. “What am I supposed to say?” He turned and stared at Young Hu as though about to quarrel with him. “It takes capital to go into business, and it takes capital to become an official. Without capital, what's there to talk about?” “Become an official?” Young Hu was astonished. “How would you manage that? You're like me: you've never even earned the first examination qualification. We're ordinary men without degrees. Where would an official post come from?” “Couldn't I enter by purchasing a qualification?”

小胡默然。心里有些看不起王有龄。捐官的情形不外乎两种，一种是做生意发了财，富而不贵，美中不足，捐个功名好提高身价，象扬州的盐商，个个都是花几千两银子捐来的道台，那一来便可以与地方官称兄道弟，平起平坐，否则就不算“缙绅先生”，育事上得公堂，要跪着回话。再有一种，本是官员家的子弟，书也读得不错，就是运气不好，三年大比，次次名落孙山，年纪大了，家计也艰窘了，总得想个谋生之道，走的就是“做官”的这条路，改行也无从改起，只好卖田卖地，拜托亲友，凑一笔去捐个官做。象王有龄这样，年纪还轻，应该刻苦用功，从正途上去巴结，不此之图，而况又穷得衣食不周，却痴心妄想去捐班，岂不是没出息？

Young Hu said nothing, but inwardly he thought rather less of Wang. There were generally two kinds of people who bought official status. One was the merchant who had made his fortune but felt that wealth without rank left something to be desired. He bought a title to raise his standing. The salt merchants of Yangzhou, for example, had each spent several thousand taels to acquire the rank of circuit intendant. That enabled them to address local officials as brothers and meet them as equals. Without such standing, they did not count as members of the gentry and had to kneel when answering questions in court. The other kind was the son of an official: well educated, perhaps, but unlucky enough to fail the examinations every three years. As he grew older and his family resources dwindled, he had to find a way to earn a living. Official service was the only career for which he had been prepared; he could scarcely begin again in another trade. So he sold fields and property, sought help from relatives and friends, and scraped together enough money to buy a qualification. Wang Youling, however, was still young. Surely he ought to study hard and work his way up through the regular examinations. Instead, though he could barely feed and clothe himself, he was dreaming of buying his way into office. Did that not show a lack of ambition?

王有龄看出他心里的意思，有几杯酒在肚里，便不似平时那么沉着了，“小胡！”他说，“我告诉你一句话，信不信由你，先父在日，替我捐过一个‘盐大使’。”小胡最机警，一看他的神情，就知道决非假话，随即笑道：“唷！失敬，失敬，原来是王老爷，一直连名带姓叫你，不知者不罪。”“不要挖苦我了！”王有龄苦笑道，“说句实话，除非是你，别人面前我再也不说，说了反惹人耻笑。”“我不是笑你。”小胡放出庄重的神态问道，“不过，有一层我不明白，既然你是盐大使，我们浙江沿海有好几十个盐场，为什么不给你补缺？”“你只知其一，不知其二……”

Wang guessed what he was thinking. With several cups of wine inside him, he was less restrained than usual. “Young Hu,” he said, “I'll tell you something, whether you believe it or not. While my father was alive, he bought me a qualification as a salt-station superintendent.” Young Hu was exceptionally quick-witted. One look at Wang's expression told him this was no invention. He immediately smiled. “Oh! My apologies! So you are His Honour Wang. And all this time I've been calling you by your full name. You must forgive an ignorant man.” “Don't make fun of me.” Wang smiled bitterly. “To tell the truth, you're the only person I'd say this to. Anyone else would only laugh.” “I'm not laughing at you.” Young Hu grew serious. “But there's one thing I don't understand. If you're qualified to be a salt-station superintendent, why haven't you been appointed? There must be dozens of saltworks along the Zhejiang coast.” “You know only half the story...”

捐官只是捐一个虚衔，凭一张吏部所发的“执照”，取得某一类官员的资格，如果要想补缺，必得到吏部报到，称为“投供”，然后抽签分发到某一省候补。王有龄尚未“投供”，哪里谈得到补缺？讲完这些捐官补缺的程序，王有龄又说：“我所说的要‘本钱’，就是进京投供的盘缠。如果境况再宽裕些，我还想‘改捐’。”“改捐个什么‘班子’？”“改捐个知县。盐大使正八品，知县正七品，改捐花不了多少钱。出路可就大不相同了。”“怎么呢？”“盐大使只管盐场，出息倒也不错，不过没有意思。知县虽小，一县的父母官，能杀人也能活人，可以好好做一番事业。”

Purchasing office meant, in the first instance, purchasing an empty title. A certificate issued by the Board of Civil Appointments made its holder eligible for a particular category of post. To obtain an actual appointment, he first had to report to the Board for registration and then draw lots for assignment to a province, where he would wait for a vacancy. Wang had not even completed the registration. How could he expect a substantive post? After explaining the procedure, Wang added, “The ‘capital’ I mean is the money to travel to the capital and register. If I had a little more to spare, I'd also like to purchase a different qualification.” “For what sort of post?” “A county magistracy. A salt-station superintendent holds the principal eighth rank; a county magistrate holds the principal seventh. Changing qualifications wouldn't cost very much, but the prospects would be entirely different.” “How so?” “A salt-station superintendent manages a saltworks, nothing more. The income can be quite good, but the work has little meaning. A county magistrate may be a minor official, yet he is responsible for the welfare of an entire county. He has the power to take lives or save them. He can accomplish something worthwhile.”

这两句话使得小胡肃然起敬，把刚才看不起他的那点感想，一扫而空了。“再说，知县到底是正印官，不比盐大使，说起来总是佐杂，又是捐班的佐杂，到处做‘磕头虫’，与我的性情也不相宜。”“对，对！”小胡不断点头，“那么，这一来，你要多少‘本钱’才够呢？”“总得五百两银子。”“噢！”小胡没有再接口，王有龄也不再提，五百两银子不是小数目，小胡不见得会有，就有也不见得肯借。

These words filled Young Hu with respect, sweeping away every trace of his earlier contempt. “Besides, a county magistrate is the chief official of his jurisdiction. A salt-station superintendent is only a subordinate, and one who has purchased his qualification at that. He has to bow and scrape wherever he goes. That doesn't suit my temperament.” “Quite right!” Young Hu nodded repeatedly. “Then how much ‘capital’ would you need?” “At least five hundred taels of silver.” “Oh.” Young Hu said no more, and Wang let the subject drop. Five hundred taels was no small sum. Young Hu might not possess it; even if he did, he might not be willing to lend it.

两人各有心事，吃闷酒无味，天也黑上来了，王有龄推杯告辞，小胡也不留他，只说：“明天下午，我仍旧在这里等你，你来！”“有事吗？”王有龄微感诧异，“何不此刻就说？”“我有点小事托你，此刻还没有想停当。还是明天下午再谈。你一定要来，我在这里坐等，不见不散。”看他如此叮嘱，王有龄也就答应了。到了第二天下午，依约而至，不见小胡的踪影。泡一碗茶得好几文钱，对王有龄来说，是一种浪费，于是沿着山路一直走了过去。城隍山上有好几座庙，庙前有耍把戏的，打拳卖膏药的，摆象棋摊的，不花钱而可以消磨时光的地方多得很。他这里立一会，那面看一看，到红日衔山，方始走回原处，依旧不见小胡。

Each was now absorbed in his own thoughts. Drinking in silence had lost its charm, and darkness was gathering. Wang pushed his cup away and rose to leave. Young Hu did not try to detain him. He merely said, “I'll be here again tomorrow afternoon. Come and meet me.” “Is there something you want?” Wang asked, slightly surprised. “Why not tell me now?” “There's a small favour I'd like to ask, but I haven't quite thought it through. Let's talk tomorrow afternoon. You must come. I'll wait here until you do.” Young Hu was so insistent that Wang agreed. The next afternoon he arrived as promised, but there was no sign of his young acquaintance. A bowl of tea cost several copper coins, which Wang considered an unnecessary extravagance, so he continued along the hillside path. There were several temples on City God Hill. Before their gates stood acrobats, performers demonstrating martial arts to sell their medicinal plasters, and men with chessboards set up for challengers. There were plenty of ways to pass the time without spending money. Wang stopped here and watched there, returning to the meeting place only when the red sun touched the hills. Young Hu was still nowhere to be seen.

是“不见不散”的死约会。王有龄顿感进退两难，不等是自己失约，要等，天色已暮，晚饭尚无着落。呆了半天，越想越急，顿一顿足，往山下便走，心中自语：明天见着小胡，非说他几句不可！他又不是不知道自己的境况，在外面吃碗茶都得先算一算，何苦捉弄人？走了不多几步，听见后面有人在叫：“王有龄，王有龄！”转身一看，正是小胡，手里拿着手巾包，跑得气喘吁吁，满脸是汗。见着了他的面，王有龄的气消了一半，问道：“你怎么这时候才来？”“我知道你等得久了，对不起，对不起！”小胡欣慰地笑着，“总算还好，耽迟不耽错。来，来，坐下来再说。”王有龄也不知道他这话是什么意思？默默地跟着他走向一副设在橱下的座头，泡了两碗茶。小胡有些魂不守舍似的，目送着经过的行人，手里紧捏住那个手巾包。

They had expressly agreed to wait until they met. Wang found himself in a quandary. If he left, he would be breaking his word; if he stayed, night would soon fall, and he had made no provision for supper. He stood there for some time, growing more anxious with every thought. Finally, he stamped his foot and started downhill. Tomorrow, he told himself, he would certainly give Young Hu a piece of his mind. The young fellow knew perfectly well how things stood with him: even a bowl of tea away from home had to be carefully considered. Why torment him like this? He had gone only a few steps when someone called from behind, “Wang Youling! Wang Youling!” He turned to see Young Hu running towards him, a handkerchief bundle in his hand, breathless and drenched in sweat. Half of Wang's anger vanished at the sight. “Why are you only arriving now?” he asked. “I know you've been waiting a long time. I'm sorry, truly sorry.” Young Hu smiled with relief. “Still, all's well. I'm late, but I haven't missed you. Come, let's sit down and talk.” Wang had no idea what he meant. He followed silently to a table beneath the eaves, where they ordered two bowls of tea. Young Hu seemed distracted. He watched the passers-by while gripping the handkerchief bundle tightly.

“小胡！”王有龄忍不住问了：“你说有事托我，快说吧！”“你打开来看，不要给人看见。”他低声地说，把手巾包递了给王有龄。他避开行人，悄悄启视，里面是一叠银票，还有些碎银子，约莫有十几两。“怎么回事？”“这就是你做官的本钱。”王有龄愣住了，一下子心里发酸，眼眶发热，尽力忍住眼泪，把手巾包放在桌上，却不知怎么说才好。

“Young Hu!” Wang could contain himself no longer. “You said you wanted a favour. What is it?” “Open this and look. Don't let anyone see,” he whispered, handing over the bundle. Shielding it from passing eyes, Wang opened it discreetly. Inside was a stack of bank drafts and some broken silver, perhaps a little over ten taels. “What's all this?” “The capital you need to become an official.” Wang was stunned. His heart suddenly ached and his eyes grew hot. Fighting back tears, he put the bundle on the table, unable to find a word to say.

“你最好点一点数。其中有一张三百两的，是京城里‘大德恒’的票子，认票不认人，你要当心失落。另外我又替你换了些零碎票子，都是有名的‘字号’，一路上通行无阻。”小胡又说：“如果不为换票子，我早就来了。”这里王有龄才想出来一句话：“小胡，你为什么待我这么好？”“朋友嘛！”小胡答道，“我看你好比虎落平阳，英雄末路，心里说不出的难过，一定要拉你一把，才睡得着觉。”“唉！”王有龄毕竟忍不住了，两行热泪，牵连不断。“何必，何必？这不是大丈夫气概！”这句话是很好的安慰，也是很好的激励、王有龄收拾涕泪，定一定神，才想起一件事，相交至今，受人绝大的恩惠，却是对他的名氏、身世，一无所知，岂不荒唐？

“You'd better count it. One draft is for three hundred taels, issued by Dadeheng in the capital. It's payable to whoever presents it, so be careful not to lose it. I've also changed some of the money into smaller drafts, all from well-known firms. You'll be able to use them all along the way.” Young Hu added, “If it hadn't taken so long to change the drafts, I'd have been here much earlier.” Only then did Wang find something to say. “Young Hu, why are you so good to me?” “Because we're friends!” Young Hu replied. “Seeing you like this—a tiger stranded on the plain, a hero with nowhere to turn—makes me more miserable than I can say. I couldn't sleep unless I gave you a hand.” “Ah!” Wang could hold back no longer. Tears streamed down his face. “Come now, come now! Where's your courage?” The words comforted him and restored his spirits. Wang wiped his tears away and composed himself. Only then did something occur to him: he had received an immense kindness from a man whose full name and background he did not even know. How absurd!

于是他微有窘色地问道：“小胡，还没有请教台甫？”“我叫胡光墉，字雪岩，你呢，你的大号叫什么？”“我叫雪轩。”“雪轩，雪岩！”胡雪岩自己念了两遍，抚掌笑道：“好极了，声音很近，好象一个人。你叫我雪岩，我叫你雪轩。”“是，是！雪岩，我还要请教你，府上……”这是问他的家世，胡雪岩笑笑不肯多说：“守一点薄产过日了，没有什么谈头。雪轩，我问你，你几时动身？”“我不敢耽搁。把舍间咯略安排一番，总在三、五日内就动身。如果一切顺利，年底就可以回来。雪岩，我一定要走路子，分发到浙江来，你我弟兄好在一起。”“好极了。”胡雪岩的“好极了”，已成口头禅，“后天我们仍旧在这里会面，我给你饯行。”

Slightly embarrassed, he asked, “Young Hu, I haven't yet had the honour of learning your courtesy name.” “My name is Hu Guangyong, and my courtesy name is Xueyan. What about yours?” “Xuexuan.” “Xuexuan, Xueyan!” Hu repeated the names twice, then clapped his hands and laughed. “Splendid! They sound so alike we might almost be the same person. You call me Xueyan, and I'll call you Xuexuan.” “Yes, yes! Xueyan, there's something else I'd like to ask. Your family...” He was asking about Hu's background, but Hu merely smiled and declined to say much. “I live on a little property. There's nothing worth mentioning. Tell me, Xuexuan, when will you leave?” “I dare not delay. Once I've put things in order at home, I'll set off within three to five days. If all goes well, I should be back by the end of the year. Xueyan, I'll do everything I can to secure an assignment to Zhejiang, so that we two brothers can be together.” “Splendid!” The word was already becoming Hu's habitual response. “Let's meet here again the day after tomorrow. I'll give you a farewell dinner.”

“我一定来。”到了第三天，王有龄午饭刚过，就来赴约。他穿了估衣铺买的直罗长衫，亮纱马褂，手里拿一柄“舒莲记”有名的“杭扇”，泡着茶等，等到夭黑不见胡雪岩的踪影，寻亦没处寻，只好再等。天气热了，城隍山上来品茗纳凉的，络绎不绝。王有龄目迎目送着每一个行人，把脖子都摆得酸了，就是盼不着胡雪岩。夜深客散，茶店收摊子，这下才把王有龄撵走。他已经雇好了船，无法不定，第二天五更时分上船，竟不能与胡雪岩见一面话别。

“I'll certainly come.” On the appointed day, Wang came just after lunch. He wore a gauze gown and a glossy gauze riding jacket bought at a second-hand clothes shop, and carried one of the famous Hangzhou folding fans made by Shulianji. He ordered tea and waited. Darkness fell without any sign of Hu. There was nowhere he could go to look for him, so he could only keep waiting. The weather had grown hot, and a steady stream of people came up City God Hill to drink tea and enjoy the cool air. Wang watched every arrival and departure until his neck ached, but the man he hoped to see never appeared. Late at night, when the customers had gone and the teahouse was closing, Wang finally had to leave. He had already hired a boat and could not cancel his departure. He boarded in the hours before dawn the next morning, without having had a chance to see Hu and say goodbye.

在王有龄北上不久，浙江的政局有了变化：巡抚常大淳调湖北，云南巡抚黄宗汉改调浙江，未到任以前由布政使——通称“藩司”、老百姓尊称为“藩台”的旗人椿寿署理。黄宗汉字寿臣，福建晋江人。他是道光十五年乙未正科的翰林，这一榜人才济济，科运甚隆，那年，咸丰二年，当到巡抚的就有三个，广东叶名琛、江西张芾，当到二品大员的有何桂清、吕贤基、彭蕴章、罗惇衍，还有杭州的许乃钊，与他老兄许乃普，都当内阁学士。这黄宗汉据说是个很能干的人，但是关于他的操守与治家，批评极坏。到任以后，传说他向椿寿索贿四万两银子，椿寿没有买他的帐，于是多事了。

Shortly after Wang left for the north, Zhejiang's provincial administration changed. Governor Chang Dachun was transferred to Hubei, and Huang Zonghan, governor of Yunnan, was reassigned to Zhejiang. Until Huang arrived, the acting governorship was held by Chunshou, a bannerman serving as provincial treasurer—a post commonly called fansi and respectfully referred to by ordinary people as fantai. Huang Zonghan, whose courtesy name was Shouchen, came from Jinjiang in Fujian. He had entered the Hanlin Academy after passing the regular metropolitan examination in the fifteenth year of the Daoguang reign, the yiwei year. That examination produced a remarkable crop of talented men whose careers prospered. By the year in question, the second year of Xianfeng, three had become provincial governors: Huang himself, Ye Mingchen in Guangdong, and Zhang Fu in Jiangxi. Others who had reached the second official rank included He Guiqing, Lü Xianji, Peng Yunzhang, and Luo Dunyan. Xu Naizhao of Hangzhou and his elder brother Xu Naipu had both become grand secretariat academicians. Huang was said to be highly capable, but his personal integrity and the management of his household were subjects of severe criticism. After taking office, he was said to have demanded a bribe of forty thousand taels from Chunshou. Chunshou ignored the demand, and trouble followed.

其时漕运正在改变办法。因为海禁已开，而且河道湮淤，加以洪杨的起事，所以江苏的苏、松、太各属改用海运，浙江则是试办，椿寿既为藩司，又署理巡抚，责无旁贷，当然要亲自料理这件公事。漕运的漕，原来就是以舟运谷的意思。多少年来都是河运，先是黄河，后来是运河，而运河又有多少次的变迁兴作，直到康熙年间，治河名臣靳辅，于成龙先后开“中河”，历时千余年的运河，才算大功告成。这条南起杭州，北抵京师，流经浙江、江苏、山东、河北四省，全长两千多里的水道，为大清朝带来了一百五十年的盛运。不幸的是，黄河的情况，越来越坏，有些地方，河底积淤，高过人家屋脊，全靠两面堤防约束，“春水船如天上行”，真到了束手无策的地步。而运河受黄河的累，在嘉庆末年，几乎也成了“绝症”。于是道光初年有海运之议。

At the time, the system for transporting the grain tax was undergoing change. Maritime restrictions had been lifted, river channels were silting up, and the rebellion led by Hong Xiuquan and Yang Xiuqing had broken out. The jurisdictions of Suzhou, Songjiang, and Taicang in Jiangsu therefore switched to sea transport, while Zhejiang was preparing to try it. As both provincial treasurer and acting governor, Chunshou could not evade responsibility. He had to oversee the matter personally. The word cao in caoyun, the grain-tax transport system, originally meant carrying grain by boat. For centuries the grain had travelled by river, first on the Yellow River and later on the Grand Canal. The canal itself had undergone repeated changes and construction. Only during the Kangxi reign, when the celebrated river administrators Jin Fu and Yu Chenglong successively opened sections of the Middle Canal, was this waterway, more than a thousand years in the making, finally considered complete. Running from Hangzhou in the south to the capital in the north, through Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Shandong, and Hebei, the waterway extended for more than two thousand li and had brought the Qing dynasty a century and a half of prosperity. Unfortunately, conditions on the Yellow River grew steadily worse. In places, accumulated silt raised its bed above the rooftops of nearby houses, leaving the river confined only by the embankments on either side. “On spring waters, boats seem to sail through the sky”: matters had truly reached the point where no remedy seemed possible. Burdened by the Yellow River's troubles, the Grand Canal itself was almost beyond cure by the end of the Jiaqing reign. Hence the proposal, early in the Daoguang reign, to ship the grain by sea.

在嘉应末年时有齐彦槐其人，著有一篇《海运南僧议》，条分缕析断言“一举而众善备”，但地方大吏不愿轻易更张。直到湖南安化的陶文毅公陶澍，由安徽巡抚调江苏，锐意革新，消除盐、漕两事的积弊，齐彦槐的建议，才有一个实验的机会。这次实验由陶澍亲自主持，在上海设立“海运总局”，他亲自雇好专门运载关东豆麦的“沙船”一千艘，名为“三不象”的海船几十艘，分两次运米一百五十多万石到天津，结果获得极大的成功，省时省费，米质受损极微。承运的船商，运漕而北，回程运豆，一向漕船南下“回空”，海船北上“回空”，现在平白多一笔收入，而且出力的船商，还“赏给顶戴”做了官，真正是皆大欢喜。但是到了第二年，这样的好事竟不再做下去！依然恢复河运。因为，不知道有多少人靠这条运河的漕船来剥削老百姓，他们不愿意革新！

Towards the end of the Jiaqing reign, a man named Qi Yanhuai wrote an essay setting out the case for shipping the southern grain tax by sea. He examined the matter point by point and declared that a single measure would secure a whole range of benefits. Senior provincial officials, however, were reluctant to change established arrangements. The proposal received a trial only after Tao Shu of Anhua, Hunan—later honoured with the posthumous title Wenyi—was transferred from the governorship of Anhui to that of Jiangsu. Determined to reform the administration, Tao set about eliminating longstanding abuses in both the salt system and grain transport. Tao personally supervised the trial. He established a Sea Transport Bureau in Shanghai and hired a thousand flat-bottomed sand junks normally used to carry beans and wheat from the northeast, together with several dozen seagoing vessels of the type known as sanbuxiang, or “unlike any of the three.” In two shipments, they transported more than one and a half million shi of rice to Tianjin. The experiment was an outstanding success: it saved both time and money, and very little of the rice deteriorated. The shipowners carried grain-tax rice north and beans on the return journey. Previously, canal grain boats had returned south empty, while the coastal vessels had sailed north without cargo. Now an additional source of income had appeared. Shipowners who distinguished themselves were even rewarded with official rank and the right to wear the corresponding hat insignia. Everyone was delighted. Yet the very next year this excellent arrangement was abandoned, and river transport resumed. Countless people depended on the canal grain boats as a means of exploiting the population. They had no desire for reform.

漕运的弊端与征粮的弊端是不可分的，征粮的权责属于州县，这七品的正印官，特称为“大老爷”，在任两件大事：刑名、钱谷。延请“绍兴师爷”至少亦得两名：“刑名师爷”和“钱谷师爷”。县大老爷的成名发财，都靠这两个人。钱谷师爷的本事不在算盘上，在于能了解情况，善于应付几种人，第一种是书办，世代相传，每人手里有一本底册，哪家有多少田？该纳粮多少？都记载在这本册子上，为不传之秘。第二种是“特殊人物”，他们所纳的粮，都有专门名称，做过官的绅士人家的“衿米”，举人、秀才、监生是“料米”，这两种米不能多收，该多少就多少，否则便有麻烦。再有一种名为“讼米”，专好无事生非打官司的讼棍所纳的粮，也要当心。总而言这一名话，刁恶霸道，不易对付的那班“特殊人物”，必须敷衍，分量不足，米色粗劣，亦得照收不误。甚至虚给“粮串”——纳粮的凭证，买得个安静二字。

Abuses in grain transport were inseparable from abuses in grain collection. Responsibility for collecting the tax lay with the departments and counties. A county magistrate, a seventh-rank chief official respectfully called “His Honour,” had two major concerns: criminal justice and revenue. He needed at least two of the professional advisers known as Shaoxing secretaries, one specialising in legal cases and the other in money and grain. His reputation and his fortune depended on these two men. A revenue adviser's chief skill lay not in his use of the abacus but in his grasp of local conditions and his ability to deal with several kinds of people. The first were the clerks, whose positions passed down through their families. Each kept a master register recording how much land each household possessed and how much grain tax it owed. These records were jealously guarded secrets. The second group consisted of people with special standing. Even the grain they paid had distinctive names. Grain from gentry households whose members had held office was called jinmi; that paid by holders of provincial examination degrees, lower examination qualifications, or Imperial Academy status was called liaomi. Neither group could be made to pay more than its proper assessment without causing trouble. Another category, songmi, was the grain paid by habitual litigants who delighted in stirring up lawsuits. They too had to be handled carefully. In short, the troublesome, overbearing people who were difficult to manage had to be kept satisfied. Their grain had to be accepted even if it was short in weight or poor in quality. Sometimes tax receipts were issued without payment at all, simply to buy peace.

有人占便宜，当然有人吃亏，各种剥削耗费，加上县大老爷自己的好处，统统都出在良善小民头上，这叫做“浮收”，最“黑”的地方，“浮收”到正额的一半以上，该纳一石米的，起码要纳一石五斗。于是有所谓“包户”，他们或者与官吏有勾结，或者能挟制官吏，小户如托他们“包缴”，比自己到粮柜上去缴纳，便宜得多。

If some people gained, others inevitably lost. Every exaction and expense, together with the magistrate's own share of the proceeds, fell on the honest, ordinary taxpayer. This was called “excess collection.” In the worst places, the excess amounted to more than half the official assessment: a household owing one shi of rice had to deliver at least one and a half. Hence the rise of tax contractors. Some colluded with the officials; others could bring pressure to bear on them. For small households, paying through these contractors was much cheaper than delivering their grain directly to the tax office.

第三种就是漕船上的人。漕船都是官船，额定数字过万，实际仅六千余艘，分驻运河各地，一地称为一帮。这就是游侠组织“青帮”之帮的出典。帮中的管事及水手，都称为帮丁，其中又有屯丁、旗丁、尖丁之分。尖丁是实际上的头目，连护漕的千总、把总都得听他的指挥。州县衙门开仓怔粮，粮户缴纳，漕船开到，验收装船，名为“受兑”。一面征粮，一面受兑，川流不息，那自然是再顺利不过的事，但是这一来漕船上就玩不出花样来了。

The third group consisted of the men who worked on the grain boats. These were government vessels, officially numbering more than ten thousand but in reality only a little over six thousand. They were stationed in groups along the canal, each local group being called a bang. This was the origin of the word bang in the name of the Green Gang, the fraternity of men who lived by their own code. Both the managers and the sailors in these associations were known collectively as transport servicemen. Among them were men attached to military farming settlements, ordinary registered boatmen, and headmen. The headmen were the real leaders: even the military captains and lieutenants assigned to escort the grain had to follow their instructions. When local offices opened their granaries for tax collection, households delivered their grain, and the transport boats arrived to inspect and load it. This process was called acceptance and transfer. If collection and loading proceeded continuously together, everything could hardly have gone more smoothly. But such efficiency would have left the boatmen no opportunity to play their tricks.

他们的第一个花样是“看米色”。由于漕船过淮安时，漕运总督要“盘粮”点数，到通州起岸入仓时，仓场侍郎要验看米质，如有不符，都由漕船负责，因此，他们在受兑时，验看米色，原是分所当为。但米色好坏，仅凭目视，并无标准，这样就可以挑剔了，一廒一廒看过去，不是说米色太杂，就是不够干燥，不肯受兑。以一般的情况而言，开仓十日，所有的仓厥就都装满了，此时如不疏运上船，则后来的粮户，无仓可以贮米，势必停征。粮户也就要等待，一天两天还不要紧，老百姓无非发发牢骚而已，日子一久，废时失业，还要贴上盘缠，自然非吵不可，这叫做“闹潜”，是件极严重的事，地方官往往会得到极严厉的处分。倘或是个刮地皮的贪官，这一闹漕就不定就会激起民变，更是件可以送命的大祸。因此，钱谷师爷，便要指挥书办出来与“看米色”的旗丁讲斤头，倘或讲不下来，而督运的委员，怕误了限期，催令启程，那些帮丁就不问兑足不兑足，只管自己开船。这时的州县可就苦了，必须设法自运漕米，一路赶上去补足，称为“随帮交兑”。

Their first trick concerned the inspection of the rice. When the boats passed Huai'an, the director-general of grain transport checked the cargo quantities; when the grain was landed and stored at Tongzhou, the granary vice-minister inspected its quality. The boats were responsible for any discrepancy. It was therefore perfectly proper for their crews to examine the rice before accepting it. Quality, however, was judged by sight alone, without an objective standard. That left ample room for fault-finding. The inspectors went from one granary compartment to another, claiming that the rice was too mixed or insufficiently dry, and refused to accept it. Under normal circumstances, all the granary compartments would be full within ten days of the start of collection. Unless the grain was moved out and loaded onto boats, later arrivals would have nowhere to deposit their rice, and collection would have to stop. The taxpayers would be left waiting. A day or two might bring no more than grumbling, but a longer delay kept them from their work and forced them to spend money on lodging and food. Trouble was inevitable. Such disturbances over the grain tax were a serious matter and often brought severe punishment upon local officials. If the magistrate was especially rapacious, a disturbance might even develop into a popular revolt—a disaster that could cost him his life. The revenue adviser would therefore send the clerks out to bargain with the boatmen responsible for inspecting the rice. If no agreement could be reached and the transport commissioners, anxious about the deadline, ordered the fleet to leave, the crews would sail regardless of whether their full quota had been loaded. That left the local authorities in a miserable position. They had to arrange their own transport, pursue the fleet, and deliver the outstanding grain along the way, a practice known as “following the fleet to complete delivery.”

幸而取得妥协，漕米兑竣，应该出给名为“通关”的收据，这时尖丁出面了，先议“私费”，就是他个人的“好处”，私费议妥，再议“通帮公费”，是全帮的好处。这些看米色所受的勒索，以及尖丁私费、通帮公费，自然羊毛出在羊身上，由浮收来支付。这以后，就该帮丁受勒索了，首先是“过淮”投文过堂，照例有各种陋规，一帮船总要花到五六百两到一千两银子。这一关一过，沿路过闸过坝，处处要送红包，大概每一艘船要十几两银子。最后到了通州，花样更好，要投四个衙门的文，有人专门代办，每船十三两银子，十两铺排四个衙门，三两是代办者的酬劳。等漕米上岸入仓，伸手要钱的人数不清，总要花到三五十两。所以帮丁勒索州县，无非悖入悖出。

If a compromise was reached and the grain finally accepted, the crews were supposed to issue a clearance receipt. At this point the headman appeared. First came negotiations over his “private expenses”—his personal reward. Once that had been settled, there were the “public expenses of the whole association,” a payment for everyone's benefit. The money extorted during inspection, the headman's private reward, and the association's collective payment all came, of course, from the same source: the excess grain collected from the taxpayers. After that, it was the boatmen's turn to be extorted. First came the formal submission of documents and appearance before the authorities at Huai'an, accompanied by the customary unofficial fees. A fleet generally had to spend between five or six hundred and a thousand taels to pass this stage. Beyond it, every lock and dam required another payment, adding up to a dozen or more taels per boat. At Tongzhou, the arrangements were still more elaborate. Documents had to be submitted to four government offices. Professional intermediaries handled the process for thirteen taels a boat: ten to distribute among the offices and three for themselves. When the rice was finally landed and stored, so many hands reached out for money that another thirty to fifty taels would be spent. What the boatmen extorted from the counties was, in effect, taken from them in turn.

帮丁的苦楚犹不止此，一路还要受人的欺侮。在运河里，遇到运铜运铅的船，以及木排，千万要当心，那是在运河是蛮不讲理出了名的，撞沉了漕船，他们可以逃散，帮丁则非倾家荡产来赔不可，因为如此，帮丁便格外团结，以求自保，“青帮”之起因如此，所以，他们的“海底”名为“通漕”，并不是世俗所称的“通草”。

Their troubles did not end there. They also suffered intimidation along the route. On the canal, they had to be especially wary of vessels carrying copper or lead and of timber rafts, whose crews were notorious for their lawlessness. If they rammed and sank a grain boat, they could scatter and disappear, while the grain boat's crew might have to sacrifice everything they owned to compensate for the loss. The boatmen consequently formed unusually close bonds for their own protection. This was how the Green Gang began. Its secret code was therefore called Tongcao, meaning “the grain-transport connection,” rather than the similarly pronounced name of the rice-paper plant, as people commonly supposed.

一度行之有效，但以积习已深，惯于更张的南漕海运，终于咸丰元年旧事重提。这出于两个原因，第一个是人，第二个是地。这个人是两江总督陆建瀛，湖北人，极能干，而且善于结交，所以公卿延誉，负一时物望。他颇有意步武陶澍，留一番政绩。陶澍改盐法，淮北行之大效，而淮南依旧，陆建瀛在淮南继陶未竟之功。漕运也是如此，他得到户部尚书孙瑞珍的支持，准备恢复海运。适逢其会的是，运河出了问题，在徐州附近的丰县以北决口，“全河北趋，由沛县之华山、戚山分注微山、昭阳等湖，挟清水外泛，运河闸、坝、纤堤，均已漫淹”，朝廷一方面拨巨款抢救，一方面也加强了改用海运的决心。海运之议，奉旨由两江总督陆建瀛、江苏巡抚杨文定、浙江巡抚常大淳，会同筹划。结果决定咸丰二年江苏的苏州，松江、常州、镇江、太仓等四府一州的溜米，改用海运。浙江则是试办，但其间又有反复，未成定议。

Sea transport for the southern grain tax had proved effective once, only to be abandoned because entrenched habits resisted change. In the first year of Xianfeng, it was finally proposed again. Two factors brought this about: one concerned a person, the other the physical circumstances. The person was Lu Jianying, the governor-general of Liangjiang. A native of Hubei, he was highly capable and skilled at cultivating connections. Senior officials praised him, and he enjoyed considerable public esteem. He wished to follow Tao Shu's example and leave a record of achievement. Tao's salt reforms had worked well north of the Huai River, while the old system remained in place to the south. Lu took up Tao's unfinished work there. He intended to do the same with grain transport and, with the support of Sun Ruizhen, president of the Board of Revenue, prepared to restore sea shipments. At just this moment, disaster struck the canal. A breach opened north of Fengxian, near Xuzhou. “The entire river turned north; from Huashan and Qishan in Peixian it poured into Weishan, Zhaoyang, and the other lakes, driving their waters outward until the canal's locks, dams, and towpath embankments were all submerged.” The court allocated substantial funds for emergency repairs, while its resolve to adopt sea transport grew stronger. The emperor ordered Lu Jianying, Governor Yang Wending of Jiangsu, and Governor Chang Dachun of Zhejiang to draw up a joint plan. They decided that in the second year of Xianfeng the grain from Suzhou, Songjiang, Changzhou, and Zhenjiang prefectures, together with Taicang department, would travel by sea. Zhejiang was to conduct a trial, but repeated changes of direction there prevented a final decision.

就在这段期间中，椿寿由湖南布政使调浙江。当朝命初下时，黄宗汉是掌理一省司法的浙江按察使，通称“臬司”，等椿寿到任时，他已经调差了。第二天，洪军由广西而湖南，湖北吃紧，清文宗把善于“捕盗”的常大淳，调为湖北巡抚。浙江巡抚由藩司椿寿署理。

It was during this period that Chunshou was transferred from the provincial treasury of Hunan to that of Zhejiang. When the appointment was first announced, Huang Zonghan was serving as Zhejiang's provincial judicial commissioner, commonly known as the niesi, responsible for the administration of justice throughout the province. By the time Chunshou arrived, Huang had already been transferred elsewhere. The next day, with Hong's forces moving from Guangxi into Hunan and the situation in Hubei becoming urgent, the Xianfeng emperor transferred Chang Dachun, known for his skill at suppressing bandits, to the governorship of Hubei. Chunshou became acting governor of Zhejiang.

椿寿的运气太坏。这年的浙江，省城杭州及附近各州县，自五月以后，雨量稀少，旱荒已成，于是对他发生两大不利，第一是钱粮征收不起，第二是河浅不利于舟行，影响漕运。江苏的海运，非常顺利，四府一州的漕粮，糙米三十二万多石，白米二万七千余石，于三月间出海北上，安然运到。而浙江的漕米，到九月间还未启运，这是前所未有现象。在此以前，也就是渐江正闹旱灾的五月间，为了军事上的需要，各省巡抚有个小小的调整，云南巡抚张亮基调湖南，遗缺由甘肃布政使黄宗汉接充。他不愿意去云南，经过一番活动，很快地改调浙江。不过一年的功夫，重回杭州时，已非昔比。署理巡抚椿寿交卸以后，仍旧干他的藩司。据说黄宗汉在第一天接见椿寿时，就作了个暗示，椿寿的“纱帽”在他手里，如果想保全，赶快送四万两银子的“红包”过去。黄宗汉敢于作此勒索，就因为椿寿在漕运上，已经迟延，如果上司肯替他说话。可以在天灾上找理由，有处分，亦属轻微。否则，耽延了“天瘦正供”，将获严谴。

Chunshou's luck could hardly have been worse. From the fifth month onward, little rain fell in Hangzhou or the surrounding districts, and drought had taken hold. This created two serious difficulties for him. Tax revenues could not be collected, and shallow waterways obstructed navigation, delaying the grain shipments. Jiangsu's sea transport proceeded smoothly. More than 320,000 shi of unpolished rice and over 27,000 shi of polished rice from the four prefectures and one department sailed north in the third month and arrived safely. Zhejiang's grain, however, had still not been dispatched by the ninth month—an unprecedented situation. Earlier, in the fifth month, while Zhejiang was suffering from drought, military needs had prompted a small reshuffle of provincial governors. Zhang Liangji, governor of Yunnan, was transferred to Hunan, and Huang Zonghan, then provincial treasurer of Gansu, was appointed to succeed him. Huang did not want to go to Yunnan. After some manoeuvring, he soon secured a reassignment to Zhejiang. Barely a year had passed, yet when he returned to Hangzhou, his position was greatly changed. After handing over the acting governorship, Chunshou resumed his duties as provincial treasurer. It was said that at their first meeting Huang hinted that Chunshou's official cap—his position—was in Huang's hands. If he wished to keep it, he should promptly send a gift of forty thousand taels. Huang dared make this demand because Chunshou was already behind schedule with the grain shipments. A sympathetic superior could plead the natural disaster on his behalf and keep any penalty light. Otherwise, delaying the grain due to the imperial storehouses could bring severe punishment.

椿寿没有理会他，于是黄宗汉想了个极狠毒的手法来“整”人。他认为本年漕粮，启运太迟，到达通州交仓，粮船不能依照限期“回空”，这样便要影响下一年的漕运。就在这个言之成理的说法上来整椿寿。心里已有成算，表面丝毫不露，把椿寿请到抚院来谈公事，问起清运的情形。

Chunshou ignored him, so Huang devised a particularly cruel way to deal with him. This year's grain, he argued, had been dispatched so late that, after delivering it at Tongzhou, the boats could not return empty within the prescribed time. That would delay the following year's shipments. It was upon this plausible argument that he built his attack. With his plan already formed, Huang revealed nothing. He invited Chunshou to the governor's office to discuss business and asked how the grain transport was progressing.

一提到这上面，椿寿自己先就紧张，“回大人的话，”他说，“今年浙江的漕运，无比如何要担处分了！”“谁担处分啊？”黄宗汉故意这样问。“自然是司里。”藩、臬两司向巡抚回话，照例自称“司里”。“这也不担处分的事。”黄宗汉用这句话先做一个伏笔，却又立即撇开不谈，“贵司倒先说说看，究竟因何迟误？”“自然是因为天旱水浅，河道干淤。已经奏报过的。”“天旱是五月以后的事。请问，照定例，本省漕船，每年什么时候开，什么时候‘过淮’，什么时候‘回空’？”一连三问，把椿寿堵得哑口无言。照定例，江西和浙江的漕船，限在二月底以前尽数开行。年深日久，定例有变，但至迟亦不会过四月。现在秋风已起，漕船开行的还不过一半，这该怎么说呢？

The subject immediately made Chunshou uneasy. “Your Excellency,” he replied, “there is no avoiding a penalty over Zhejiang's grain transport this year.” “A penalty for whom?” Huang asked deliberately. “For this office, naturally.” When addressing the governor, both the provincial treasurer and the judicial commissioner customarily referred to themselves as “this office.” “It is not simply a question of a penalty.” Having planted this warning, Huang immediately moved on. “First tell me exactly what caused the delay.” “The drought, naturally. The water is low and the channels have dried up and silted over. This has already been reported to the throne.” “The drought began after the fifth month. Tell me, under the regulations, when should this province's grain boats depart, when should they pass Huai'an, and when should they return empty?” The three questions in succession left Chunshou speechless. Under the regulations, all grain boats from Jiangxi and Zhejiang were supposed to sail before the end of the second month. Time had modified the practice, but even the latest departures should not extend beyond the fourth month. Now the autumn winds were blowing, and scarcely half the boats had left. How could he explain that?

他迟迟不答，黄宗汉也不开口，是逼着他非说不可。椿寿无奈，只好这样答道：“大人也在浙江待过，漕帮的积弊，还有什么不明白的？漕丁有种种花样，譬如说陈漕带私货罗……”椿寿的话未完，抚台便一个钉子碰了过来：“天下乌鸦一般黑，各省漕丁都是一样的。”“今年略微不同，因为奉旨筹议南漕海运，漕帮不免观望，这也是延误的原因之一。”“观望什么？”黄宗汉大声问道，“议办海运是来年新漕之事，跟今年何干？”振振有词一问，椿寿语塞，既然来年有此改变之议，漕丁自不免有所瞻顾，以致鼓不起劲来，但身为藩司，署理抚院，这些地方正该督催，否则便是失职，所以椿寿无同可解。

Chunshou hesitated, and Huang remained silent, forcing him to answer. At last he said, “Your Excellency has served in Zhejiang before. Surely you know the longstanding abuses in the transport associations. The boatmen have all sorts of tricks—for instance, carrying private cargo with the previous season's grain...” Before he could finish, the governor cut him short. “Crows are black everywhere. The boatmen are the same in every province.” “This year is somewhat different. Since the imperial order to consider shipping the southern grain by sea, the associations have been waiting to see what will happen. That is also one reason for the delay.” “Waiting to see what?” Huang demanded loudly. “The proposed sea shipments concern next year's new grain. What have they to do with this year's?” The forceful question left Chunshou without an answer. Naturally, the prospect of change the following year made the boatmen uncertain and dampened their enthusiasm. But as provincial treasurer and acting governor, he ought precisely to have pressed them to proceed. Otherwise, he had neglected his duty. There was no defence he could offer.

“现在怎么办呢？”黄宗汉又忧形于色地说，“事情总要办通才行啊！”“是，是！”椿寿赶紧答道，“司里尽力去催，总在这个把月里，一定可以全数启运。”“个把月？”黄宗汉皱着眉说，“说老实话，这上面我还不大弄得清楚。反正本年漕运，自前任常中丞调任以后，都由老兄一手经理。以后该如何办理，等我商量了再说。“他这段话有两层用意，第一是说目前还不甚了解漕运的情况，等了解了又当别论，留下翻覆的余地，第二是“一手经理”四个字，指明了全部责任。

“What are we to do now?” Huang went on, assuming a worried expression. “The business must be carried through somehow.” “Yes, indeed!” Chunshou answered quickly. “This office will press them as hard as possible. Within a month or so, all the boats will certainly be on their way.” “A month or so?” Huang frowned. “To be frank, I am not yet very familiar with this business. In any event, since Governor Chang's transfer, this year's grain transport has been entirely under your management. As for what should be done next, let me consult others before deciding.” His words served two purposes. By saying that he did not yet fully understand the situation, he left himself free to take a different position once he had learned more. And by stressing that Chunshou had managed it entirely himself, he placed the whole responsibility on him.

椿寿原是“上三旗”的公子哥儿，这几年在外面历练了一番。纨袴的积习，固已大减，而人心的险，却无深知，哪里去理会得黄宗汉的深意？还只当抚台语气缓和，事无大碍，所以连声应诺，辞出抚院，赶紧召集手下，商议如何设法把未走的船，能够早日开行，只要一出浙江省境，责任就轻得多了。于是椿寿即刻召集督粮道和其他经办漕运的官员，一面宣达了抚台的意思，一面力竭声嘶地要大家“各秉天良”，务必在最短期间内，设法让漕船全数开出。别处都还好办，麻烦的是湖属八帮，浙江湖川府是东南膏腴之区，额定漕粮三十八万八千余石，关系重大，偏偏这八帮的漕船，一艘都动弹不得。

Chunshou had grown up as a privileged young gentleman of the Upper Three Banners. Several years of provincial service had done much to reduce his idle, aristocratic habits, but he still knew little of the treachery people were capable of. How could he grasp Huang's real meaning? He took the governor's milder tone as a sign that nothing was seriously wrong. Assenting repeatedly, he left the governor's office and hurried to summon his subordinates. They must find a way to get the remaining vessels moving as soon as possible. Once the boats crossed Zhejiang's border, his responsibility would be considerably reduced. He immediately assembled the grain intendant and the other officials involved in transport. After conveying the governor's views, he urged them, almost shouting himself hoarse, to act according to their consciences and find a way to dispatch every boat with the least possible delay. Most places presented manageable difficulties. The real problem lay with the eight associations under Huzhou. Huzhou prefecture occupied some of the richest land in the southeast and had an assessed grain quota of more than 388,000 shi, making it especially important. Yet not one boat belonging to its eight associations could move.

椿寿看看情势严重，不得不亲自到湖州去督催。湖州运漕，有朵运河的支流，往东沿太湖南岸，入江苏省境平望的大运河。这种交流不到一百里长，但所经的双林，南浔两镇，为膏腴中的膏腴。南浔的殷富，号称“四狮八象”，海内闻名。听得藩台驾到，照例以捐班道台的身分，尽地主之谊，他们饮食起居的讲究，虽不比盐商、河工的穷奢极侈，但已远非一般宫贵之家可比。身处名匠经营的园林，坐对水陆并陈的盛馔，开宴照例开戏，南浔富家都有自己的戏班，砌末、行头，无不精美，这时集合精英，奏演名曲，而椿寿索然寡欢，却又不得不勉强敷衍，因而这样豪华享受的场台，在他反觉得受罪，耳中听着《长生殴》的《夜雨闻铃》，心里想的却是怎得下他三天三夜的大雨，运河水满，让搁浅的漕船，得以趁一帆西风，往东而去？想着漕船，椿寿无论如何坐不住了。托词“身子不爽”，向主人再三道歉告辞，回到行辕。

Recognising the seriousness of the situation, Chunshou had no choice but to go to Huzhou and supervise matters himself. Huzhou's grain boats used a branch of the canal running east along the southern shore of Lake Tai to join the Grand Canal at Pingwang in Jiangsu. The branch was less than a hundred li long, but it passed through Shuanglin and Nanxun, two towns that were rich even by the standards of this fertile region. The wealth of Nanxun's great families, known collectively as the “Four Lions and Eight Elephants,” was famous throughout the country. On learning that the provincial treasurer was coming, they extended the hospitality expected of local men who had purchased the rank of circuit intendant. Their refinements in food and daily living might not match the extravagant excesses of the salt merchants or river-administration circles, but they far surpassed those of ordinary official and aristocratic households. Chunshou found himself in gardens designed by master craftsmen, seated before sumptuous dishes drawn from land and water. As was customary, the banquet began with an opera performance. Nanxun's wealthy families maintained their own troupes, with exquisite props and costumes. Now their finest performers were brought together to present celebrated scenes. Yet Chunshou took no pleasure in any of it and had to force himself to be polite. What should have been a luxurious entertainment felt instead like an ordeal. As he listened to “Hearing Bells in the Night Rain” from The Palace of Eternal Life, he could think only of how welcome three days and nights of heavy rain would be. If the canal filled, the stranded grain boats could catch a west wind and sail eastward. The thought of the boats made it impossible for him to remain seated. Pleading indisposition, he repeatedly apologised to his hosts and returned to his temporary headquarters.

行辕里已经有许多人在等着。这些人分为三类，一类是漕帮中的“领运千总”，名义上算是押运的武官，照原来的传统，多由武举人中选拔，一类是临时委派的押运官，大多为候补州县，走路子钻上这个差使，多少弄几文“调剂调剂”，再一类就是各帮中真正的头脑：“尖丁”。“尖丁”的身分是小兵，这还是明朝“卫所”演变下来的制度。小兵与二品大员的藩台，身分相差不知几许？照平日来说，连见椿寿的面都难，但此刻也顾不得这些官派了！要设法能让漕船开动，非找尖丁来谈，才商议得出切实的办法，所以椿寿吩咐，一体传见。行辕借在一家富户的两进屋子，时已入夜，轩敞的大厅上，点起明晃晃的火油灯，照出椿寿的满面愁容！他居中坐在红木炕床上，两旁梨花木的“太师椅”上，坐的是候补州县身分的押运官，千总和尖丁便只有站的份儿了。

Many people were already waiting there. They fell into three groups. First were the captains charged with leading the grain convoys, military officers traditionally selected from men who had passed the provincial military examination. Second were the temporarily appointed escort officials, mostly expectant local magistrates who had used their connections to secure an assignment that might bring them a little money to ease their circumstances. Third were the real leaders of the associations: the headmen. In official status, a headman was merely a common soldier, a survival of the military settlement system of the Ming dynasty. The gulf between such a man and a provincial treasurer of the second rank was immense. Ordinarily, he would scarcely have been allowed into Chunshou's presence. Now, however, official dignity had to yield. Any practical plan for moving the boats required the headmen's participation, so Chunshou ordered that everyone be admitted. His temporary headquarters occupied a wealthy family's house with two successive courtyards. Night had fallen. Bright oil lamps lit the spacious hall and illuminated the worry on Chunshou's face. He sat in the centre on a mahogany couch. The escort officials awaiting local appointments occupied the pearwood armchairs on either side; the military captains and headmen had to stand.

在鸦雀无声的沉重的气氛中，椿寿扯开嘶哑的嗓子说道：“今年的漕粮，到底还运得出去，运不出去？”这一同大家面面相觑，都要看一看对方的脸色。最有资格答话的是尖丁，但以身分关系，还轮不到他们开口。“我在抚台面前，拍了胸脯的，一个月当中，一定全数开船。现在看了实在情形，我觉得我的话说得过分了。今天一定先要定个宗旨出来，船能动是动的办法，不能动是不能动的办法。这样子一天一天等下去，非把脑袋等掉了不可。”这是提出了要砍脑袋的警告，在座的人，无不悚然！坐在左首太师椅上的一名候补州县，便欠身说道：“总得仰仗大人主持全局，属下便赔上性命，也得把漕船开出去。漕粮关乎国家正用，今年天旱水浅，纵然耽迟，还有可说，倘或不走，那就是耽错了。”“耽迟不耽错”这一说，凡是坐在大师椅上的，无不齐声附和。这些候补州县，没有一个不闹穷，有些在省城住了十几年，始终没有补上一个缺，穷得只剩下一叠当票，好不容易才派上这一个押运的差使，指望着漕船一动，便好先支一笔公费安家。至于这一去什么时候才能到达通州，他们不必担心，迟延的处分，落不到他们头上。

In the heavy silence, Chunshou raised his hoarse voice. “Are we going to get this year's grain shipped or not?” They looked at one another, each trying to read the others' expressions. The headmen were best qualified to answer, but their low status gave them no right to speak first. “I gave the governor my word that every boat would leave within a month. Now that I've seen the actual conditions, I fear I promised too much. Today we must decide on a course of action. If the boats can move, we need one plan; if they cannot, we need another. If we go on waiting day after day, we'll wait until we lose our heads.” The warning that heads might fall sent a shiver through everyone present. One of the expectant magistrates in the armchairs on the left leaned forward. “We must rely on Your Excellency to direct the whole affair. Even at the cost of our lives, we must get the grain boats moving. This grain is essential to the state's regular needs. With this year's drought and shallow water, delay may still be excused. But if the boats do not leave at all, that will be failure.” Everyone seated in an armchair promptly endorsed this distinction between delay and failure. These expectant magistrates were all short of money. Some had lived in the provincial capital for more than ten years without obtaining a substantive appointment, until their possessions had dwindled to a stack of pawn tickets. At last they had secured escort duties. Once the boats moved, they hoped to draw an advance on official expenses to provide for their families. They had no need to worry about when the fleet would reach Tongzhou: any punishment for delay would fall on someone else.

倘说漕船不走，他们便回不得省城，因为船不走，便无所谓押运，不仅万事全休，而且比不得这个差使还要坏——不得这个差使，不必借了盘缠来到差。现在两手空空回杭州，债主那里如何交代？椿寿当然明白他们的用心，而且也知道这些人无足轻重，既出不了什么力，也担不了什么责任，所以不理他们的话，望着站在他们身后的“领运千总”说：“他们有什么主意，说出来商量。”“领运千总”的想法，与那些候补州县差不多，只是他们不能胡乱作主，凡事要听尖丁的招呼，因而有个年纪大些的便这样回答：“请大人作主！”“如果我说不走呢？”大家都不响，没有一个人赞成他的主意，只是不敢驳回。但这样不作声，也就很明显地表示出反对的意思了。

If the boats did not sail, however, they could hardly return to the provincial capital. No sailing meant no escort duty. They would not merely lose everything they had hoped for; they would be worse off than if they had never received the assignment. Without it, they would not have borrowed money to travel to their posts. How could they return to Hangzhou empty-handed and face their creditors? Chunshou understood their motives. He also knew that they carried little weight: they could neither contribute much nor assume any real responsibility. Ignoring their remarks, he looked towards the convoy captains standing behind them. “Tell us what ideas you have, so that we can discuss them.” The captains thought much as the expectant magistrates did, but they could not make decisions on their own. In everything they had to follow the headmen's lead. One of the older captains therefore answered, “We ask Your Excellency to decide.” “And if I say the boats are not to go?” No one spoke. None agreed with the suggestion, but none dared oppose it. Their silence, however, made their opposition plain enough.

在座的一个实缺同知，此时忍不住开口：“跟大人回话，还是让他们推出一两个人来，看看有何话说？”“他们”是指尖丁，椿寿点点头，对那些尖丁说：“我看也非你们有句话不可。”“是！”有个“有头有脸”的尖丁答应一声，请个安说：“请大人先休息。我们商量出一个宗旨，再跟大人回禀。”“好，好，你们商量。”椿寿坐在炕床上咕噜噜吸水烟，八帮的尖丁便退到廊下去悄悄商议，好久尚无结论，因为各帮的情况不同，看法各异，牵涉的因素很多。今年的漕运，吃力不讨好是公认的看法，但走与不走，却有相反的主张，一派认为赔累已不可免，不加不走，还省些事，一派则以在漕船上带着许多私货，不走则还要赔一笔，“公私交困”，简直要倾家荡产了。

A substantive deputy prefect present could no longer hold back. “Your Excellency, perhaps they should choose one or two representatives and let us hear what they have to say.” By “they,” he meant the headmen. Chunshou nodded and turned to them. “I think we must hear from you after all.” “Yes, Your Excellency!” replied one of the more prominent headmen, making a respectful salute. “Please rest a little while we confer. Once we have agreed on a proposal, we will report to you.” “Very well. Go and discuss it.” Chunshou sat drawing on his water pipe, which gurgled softly, while the headmen of the eight associations withdrew to the corridor to confer in low voices. A long time passed without agreement. Conditions differed from one association to another, opinions varied, and many interests were involved. Everyone agreed that this year's transport would mean hard work with little reward, but they disagreed over whether to sail. One faction argued that losses were inevitable, so they might as well stay and spare themselves the trouble. The other pointed out that the boats carried substantial private cargoes. Failing to sail would bring losses on those goods too. Caught between public obligations and private business, they could be ruined.

谈来谈会，莫衷一是，椿寿已经派人来催了，只好听凭上面云决定走与不走。不过总算也有了一点协议“那就是走也好，不走也好，各帮的赔累，只能一次，不能两次。“如果不走，本年的漕粮便要变价缴纳，户部定章是每石二两银子，现在市价多少？”椿寿问。“这要看米的成色。”被推定去回话的那个尖丁答道：“总在七钱到八钱这个数目之间。”“船上的漕粮有多少？”“一共二十七万六千石。”“那么，”椿寿问道，“就算每石赔一两二钱银子，共该多少？”那尖丁的心算极快，略略迟疑了一下，便报出确数：“共该三十三万一千二百两银子。”

They argued back and forth without reaching a common view. When Chunshou sent someone to hurry them, they could only leave the decision to their superiors. They had, however, agreed on one point: whether the boats sailed or not, the associations could bear one round of losses, but not two. “If the boats do not sail, this year's grain must be sold and the proceeds remitted,” Chunshou said. “The Board of Revenue fixes the rate at two taels per shi. What is the current market price?” “That depends on the quality,” answered the headman chosen to speak for them. “Somewhere between seven and eight tenths of a tael.” “How much grain is on the boats?” “A total of 276,000 shi.” “Then,” Chunshou asked, “if we lose one tael and two tenths on every shi, what does that come to?” The headman was quick at mental arithmetic. After scarcely a pause, he supplied the exact figure. “331,200 taels.”

“如果漕船不走，奏请变价缴银，上头一定会准的。不过，”椿寿面色凝重地问，“这三十三万两银子，该谁来赔？”“大人晓得的，湖属八帮是‘疲帮’，力量实在够不上。总要请大人格外体恤，留漕丁一条命。”“哼！”椿寿冷笑，“你们要命，难道我的命就可以不要？”这是双方讨价还价，有意做作。漕帮有“屯田”，有“公费”，遇到这种情形，便得从公众的产业和收入中，提出款子来赔，赔累的成数，并无定章，但以上压下，首先要看帮的好坏，公产多的“旺帮”便赔得多，负债累累的“疲帮”便赔得少。说也奇怪，越是富庶的地区，漕帮越疲，第一疲帮是江苏松江府属各帮，溯州府属八帮的境况也不见得好，这因为是越富庶的地区，剥削越多的缘故。这赔累的差额，除了漕帮以外，主要的使得由藩司从征收漕粮的各种陋规和“浮收”中，提成分赔。所以处理这件棘手的案子，实际上只是藩台衙门和湖属八帮间的事。椿寿软哄硬逼，总算把分赔的成数谈好了。

“If the boats cannot sail, the throne will certainly approve a request to sell the grain and remit silver instead. But”—Chunshou's face grew grave—“who is to make up those three hundred and thirty-odd thousand taels?” “Your Excellency knows that the eight Huzhou associations are financially exhausted. We simply haven't the means. We must beg you to show special consideration and leave the boatmen a chance to survive.” “Hmph!” Chunshou gave a cold laugh. “You want to save your lives. Is mine the only one that does not matter?” Both sides were bargaining, putting on a show. The transport associations possessed agricultural estates and collective funds. In circumstances like these, they had to draw on their common property and income to cover losses. There was no fixed rule governing their share. Pressure came from above, and the first consideration was an association's financial condition. Prosperous associations with substantial communal assets paid more; exhausted associations burdened with debt paid less. Curiously enough, the richer the district, the poorer its transport associations tended to be. Those under Songjiang in Jiangsu were the most impoverished, and the eight Huzhou associations were not much better off. The explanation was that richer districts suffered heavier exactions. Beyond the associations' contribution, most of the shortfall had to be met by the provincial treasurer out of a share of the unofficial fees and excess collections associated with the grain tax. In practice, therefore, this difficult affair concerned only the provincial treasury and Huzhou's eight associations. By alternately coaxing and pressuring them, Chunshou finally secured agreement on how the loss would be divided.

然而这也不过是万不得已的退路。眼光总是朝前看的，能够把漕船开出去，交了差，也免了赔累，何乐不为？所以椿寿又回过头来问：“照你们看，漕船到底能不能动呢？能动还是照开的好。”这一句话自然大受欢迎，在座的候补州县，一看事有转机，无不精神夏振，纷纷颂赞椿寿的明智。惟有那名代表漕帮说话的尖丁，大摇其头。不过他首先声明，他自己有点意见，并有代表漕帮，不知该说不该说？“说，说！集思广益，说出来商量。”

That, however, was merely a last resort. It was still better to look for a way forward. If the boats could sail, the obligation would be fulfilled and the loss avoided. Why not try? Chunshou therefore returned to the original question. “In your judgement, can the boats move at all? If they can, it would still be better to send them.” The suggestion was warmly welcomed. Seeing a turn for the better, the expectant magistrates brightened at once and competed in praising Chunshou's wisdom. Only the headman who had spoken for the associations shook his head firmly. Before explaining, however, he made it clear that he had a personal opinion and was not speaking on behalf of the associations. He was unsure whether he ought to express it. “Go on! We need everyone's ideas. Tell us, and we can discuss it.”

照那尖丁个人的看法，漕船要能开行，首先得要疏浚河床，同时在各支流加闸，提高运河中的水位，然后另雇民船分载漕米，减轻漕船的载重，这样双管齐下，才有“动”的可能。“那就这样办啊！有何不可呢？”有个押运官兴奋地说。那尖丁苦笑了一下，没有作声。椿寿却明白他的意思，以讥嘲的口吻答道：“老兄说得容易！可知道这一来要多少钱？”“于其赔累，何不把赔累的钱，花在疏浚河床和雇用民船上？不但交的差，而且治理了运河，也是大人的劳绩。”这两句话说动了椿寿的心，点着头沉吟，“这倒也是一说。”他自语似的问：“就不知道要多少日子？”

In the headman's personal view, getting the boats moving would first require dredging the channel and installing gates on the tributaries to raise the water level in the canal. Civilian boats would then have to be hired to take part of the grain and lighten the official vessels. Only by combining these two measures would movement be possible. “Then let us do precisely that! What is there to prevent us?” one escort official exclaimed excitedly. The headman gave a bitter smile and said nothing. Chunshou, however, understood. “You make it sound easy,” he replied sarcastically. “Have you any idea what it would cost?” “Rather than pay compensation, why not spend the money on dredging and hiring boats? That would not only fulfil the transport obligation but improve the canal as well. It would be an achievement to Your Excellency's credit.” The argument appealed to Chunshou. He nodded thoughtfully. “There is something in that,” he murmured. “The question is how long it would take.”

疏浚的计划，施工的日程，要多少工、多少料，都要仔细计算，才能知道确数，在这样人多口杂的场台中，是不可能得到结果的，所以椿寿叫大家散一散，别外找了些实际能负责，能办事的人来重作商量。这个少数人的集议，首先要谈的就是工料的来源。这实在也只有一个字：钱。漕帮中被推派出来说话的那名尖丁，以久历江湖的经验，预感到此举不妥，但人微言轻，无法扭转椿寿的“如意算盘”，便很干脆地答应了所派的经费，而且保证漕帮一定全力支持这件事。不过他也很郑重地声明，漕帮出了这笔钱，漕船不管如何非走不可。如果再出了什么花样。漕帮不能负责。于是疏浚河道的计划，很快地便见诸实际行动。这件事地方官原来也有责任，只是湖州府和运河所经的乌程、归安、德清三县，要办这件事惟有派工派料。公文往返，以及召集绅士磋商，需要好久才能动工，未免缓不济急。为了与天争时，自己拿钱出来征雇民工是最切实的办法。等这一切安排好了，预计八月底以前，漕船一定可以开行。这样，椿寿才算松了一口气，动身回省。走的那天，秋风秋雨，一般行旅闷损不乐的天气，在椿寿却大为高兴，心里在想，这雨最好落大些，连下几天，前溪水涨，起漕的时间，还好提前。

The dredging plan, the construction schedule, and the labour and materials required all had to be carefully calculated before a definite answer could be given. Nothing of the sort could be settled in a crowded meeting where everyone was talking. Chunshou therefore dismissed the gathering and summoned a smaller group of people who could actually take responsibility and get things done. Their first concern was where the labour and materials would come from. In the end, that came down to a single word: money. Drawing on his long experience of the world, the headman who had represented the associations sensed that the undertaking would end badly. But a man of his low standing had little influence and could not overturn Chunshou's hopeful calculations. He therefore readily accepted the sum assigned to the associations and promised their full support. At the same time, he made one solemn condition: once they paid this money, the boats had to sail, come what might. If anything else went wrong, the associations would accept no further liability. The dredging scheme was soon put into effect. The local authorities had responsibilities in the matter too, but Huzhou prefecture and the three counties through which the canal passed—Wucheng, Gui'an, and Deqing—could act only by requisitioning labour and materials. The exchange of official documents and consultations with local gentry would take so long that work could not begin soon enough to meet the emergency. With time running out, the most practical solution was to supply the money themselves and hire local labourers. Once these arrangements were made, it was estimated that the boats would certainly be able to sail before the end of the eighth month. Only then did Chunshou breathe more easily and set off for the provincial capital. On the day he left, autumn wind and rain made the weather miserable for most travellers. Chunshou, however, was delighted. Let the rain grow heavier, he thought, and continue for several days. If the streams ahead rose, the grain boats might depart even sooner.

* * *回到省城，他第一件事便是去见抚台黄宗汉。听完报告，黄宗汉还夸奖了一番，说他实心办事。还告诉他一些京里来的消息，说朝廷已有旨意，严饬直隶总督和驻北通州的仓场侍郎，自天津杨村地方，调派一千五百艘驳船到山东临清，准备驳运漕粮。不过直隶总督已经复奏，怕杨村的驳船，到达临清，河水已经结冰，所以这样请求：江浙的漕粮在临清、德州一带卸下来，暂时存贮，到明年开春解冻，再转漕北上，这个请求，能不能奉准，尚不可知。

On returning to the provincial capital, his first act was to call on Governor Huang Zonghan. After hearing his report, Huang actually praised his conscientious work. He also passed on news from the capital. The court had ordered the governor-general of Zhili and the granary vice-minister stationed at North Tongzhou to send fifteen hundred barges from Yangcun near Tianjin to Linqing in Shandong, ready to take over the grain. The governor-general of Zhili had replied, however, that the river might be frozen by the time the Yangcun barges reached Linqing. He proposed that the grain from Jiangsu and Zhejiang be unloaded around Linqing and Dezhou and stored there until the spring thaw, then sent north. Whether the request would be approved was not yet known.

椿寿认为这是个好消息，他原有顾虑，怕北地天寒，到了十月以后，河里结冰，漕船依旧受阻。现在既有直隶总督据实奏陈，等于为他把心里想说的话说了出来，格于事实，朝廷不能不准，这样就只要到了临清，便算达成任务。倘说迟延，则各地情形相同，处分的案子混在一起，变成“通案”就不要紧了。椿寿吃了这颗定心丸，对于疏浚河道的工程，进度不甚理想，就不太着急。他最关心的是直隶总督那个复奏的下文，等漕船开出，才看到明发上谕：“浙江嘉杭等帮米石，如能拨船赶运，当仍遵前旨，酌拨杨村船只，趁此天气晴和，迅往拨运。设或沿途必须截卸，临情、德州等仓，是否足资容纳？着仓场侍郎、直隶总督、漕运总督、山东巡抚各将现在应办急务，迅速妥为办理，毋得听任属员推诿恶习，各分畛域，再赴贻误。懔之！“

Chunshou regarded this as good news. He had been worried that the northern cold would freeze the rivers after the tenth month and leave the grain boats obstructed once again. Now the governor-general of Zhili had reported the facts, effectively saying what Chunshou himself had wanted to say. Faced with reality, the court could scarcely refuse. Reaching Linqing would therefore count as completion of the task. As for any delay, conditions were the same for everyone. The disciplinary cases would be combined into a general case, and the matter would lose its danger. With this reassurance, Chunshou became less anxious about the unsatisfactory progress of the dredging. What interested him most was the response to the governor-general's memorial. Only after the boats had sailed did he see the publicly circulated imperial edict: “If the rice carried by the Jiaxing, Hangzhou, and other Zhejiang fleets can be expedited by allocating additional vessels, the previous order shall remain in force. A suitable number of boats from Yangcun shall be sent promptly to transfer it while the weather remains fine and mild. Should unloading en route prove unavoidable, do the granaries at Linqing, Dezhou, and elsewhere have sufficient capacity? The granary vice-minister, the governor-general of Zhili, the director-general of grain transport, and the governor of Shandong shall each promptly and properly attend to the urgent duties before them. They must not permit their subordinates to persist in shirking responsibility, confining themselves to their own jurisdictions, and causing further delay. Let them take heed!”

“亏得赶运出去。”椿寿心里在想，“照上谕来看，在临清、德州截卸，暂时存贮，已经准了。不过粮仓恐怕不够，湖帮的漕米到了那里，倘或无仓可储，倒是棘手之事。”于是，他“上院”去见抚台。黄宗汉一见他就说：“啊，来得正好。我正要叫‘戈什哈’去请你，有件要紧事商量。”“请大人吩咐。”“不，不！你有事你先说。”椿寿便说明来意，意思是想请抚台出奏，浙江湖属八帮的漕米，已出省境北上。如果到了临清，无法驳运，需要截卸时，请饬下漕运总督及山东巡抚，预留空仓。他是怕湖属八帮的漕船最后到达，仓位为他帮捷足先登，所以有此要求。

“Thank goodness we got the boats away,” Chunshou thought. “The edict appears to allow them to unload at Linqing and Dezhou for temporary storage. But there may not be enough granary space. If the Huzhou grain arrives and there is nowhere to put it, we shall have a difficult problem.” He therefore went to see the governor. As soon as Huang saw him, he said, “Ah, just in time! I was about to send an attendant for you. There is an important matter we must discuss.” “Please give me your instructions, Your Excellency.” “No, no. If you have business, speak first.” Chunshou explained why he had come. He wished the governor to submit a memorial stating that the grain boats of Huzhou's eight associations had left Zhejiang and were heading north. If they reached Linqing but could not transfer their cargo and had to unload it, the director-general of grain transport and the governor of Shandong should be instructed to reserve empty granaries. Chunshou feared that the Huzhou boats would arrive last, after other fleets had occupied all the available space.

黄宗汉一面听，一面不断摇头，等他说完，俯身向前问道：“漕运一事，贵司内行，而且今年由贵司一手料理，我要请问，可曾计算过‘回空’的日子？”原来是这一层顾虑，椿寿略略放了心，“回大人的话，”他说，“回空自然要衍期……”“衍期多少时候？”黄宗汉不待辞毕，枪着问道，“请贵司算与我听一听。”“这要看临清的情形。如果在那里截卸，等明年开冻驳运，又要看前面漕船的多寡，多则慢，少则快。“最快什么时候？”“总要到明年四月。”“回空呢？”“也要两个月。”“这就是说，漕船明年夏天才能回家，还要经过一番修补，又得费个把月，最快也得在七月里才能到各县受兑漕米。请问贵司，明年新漕，不是又跟今年一样，迟到八九月才能启运吗？”

Huang shook his head repeatedly as he listened. When Chunshou finished, he leaned forward. “You are the expert on grain transport, and this year you have managed it entirely yourself. May I ask whether you have calculated when the empty boats will return?” So that was his concern. Chunshou felt a little relieved. “Your Excellency, their return will naturally be delayed...” “By how long?” Huang interrupted. “Please work it out for me.” “That depends on conditions at Linqing. If the grain is unloaded there and transferred after the thaw next year, it will also depend on the number of boats ahead of ours. The more there are, the longer it will take.” “What is the earliest possible date?” “Not before the fourth month of next year.” “And the return journey?” “That would take another two months.” “So the boats will not return home until next summer. They will then need repairs, which will take another month or so. At the earliest, they will not be ready to collect grain from the counties until the seventh month. Tell me, then: will next year's new grain not be delayed until the eighth or ninth month, just like this year's?”

“是！”椿寿答道，“不过明年改用海运，亦无关系。”“什么叫没有关系？”黄宗汉勃然变色，“你说得好轻巧。年年把漕期延后，何时始得恢复正常？须知今年是贵司责无旁贷，明年就完全是我的责任。贵司这样子做法，简直是有意跟我过不去呀！”椿寿一看抚台变脸，大出意外，他亦是旗下公子哥儿出身，一个忍不住，当即顶撞了过去：“大人言重了！既然我责无旁贷，该杀该剐，自然由我负责，大人何必如此气急败坏？”“好，好！”黄宗汉一半真的生气，一半有意做作，脸上一阵青，一阵红地说：“你负责，你负责！请教，这责任如何负法？”“本年漕运虽由我主管，但自从大人到任，凡事亦曾禀命而行。今年江苏试办海运，成效甚佳，请大人出奏，明年浙省仿照江苏成例，不就行了吗？““哼，哼！”黄宗汉不断冷笑，“看贵司的话，好象军机大臣的口吻，我倒再要请教，如果上头不准呢？”“没有不准之理。”

“Yes,” Chunshou answered. “But next year we shall use sea transport, so it will not matter.” “What do you mean, it will not matter?” Huang's expression changed sharply. “You speak very lightly of it. If the schedule slips later every year, when will it ever return to normal? This year's responsibility rests squarely on you; next year's will be entirely mine. The way you are handling this, one would think you were deliberately trying to make trouble for me!” The governor's sudden anger took Chunshou completely by surprise. He too had been raised as a privileged bannerman, and for a moment he lost his restraint. “Your Excellency goes too far! If the responsibility is mine, then I shall answer for it, even if I must be executed. There is no need for Your Excellency to lose his temper in this fashion.” “Very well! Very well!” Huang was partly angry and partly acting. His face alternated between pallor and a flush. “You will answer for it, will you? May I ask how?” “Although I have supervised this year's grain transport, since Your Excellency arrived I have acted only after reporting to you. Jiangsu's trial of sea transport has been highly successful this year. If Your Excellency requests permission for Zhejiang to follow its example next year, would that not settle the matter?” “Hmph! Hmph!” Huang kept giving cold little laughs. “You speak as though you were a grand councillor. Then let me ask another question: what if the throne refuses?” “There is no reason it should refuse.”

“又是这样的口吻！”黄宗汉一拍炕几，大声呵斥，“你到底是来议事，还是来抬杠？”椿寿做了二十几年的官，从未见过这样的上司，心里在想：你是科甲出身，我亦不是捐班佐杂爬上来的，受惯了气的，论宦途经历，我放浙江藩司，你还不过是浙江臬司，只不过朝中有人，道光十五年乙未那一榜……转念到此，椿寿打了个寒噤，暗叫一声：大事不好！黄宗汉的同年，已有当了军机大臣的，那是苏州的彭蕴章。还有户部两侍郎，一个是福建的王庆云，最爱照应同乡，另一个又是他的同年，而且是好友的伺桂清。俗语说得好，“朝里无人莫做官。”黄宗汉敢于如此目中无人，无非仗着内有奥援，而且听说他今年进京，皇上召见六次之多，圣眷正隆，自己无论如何碰不过他。这些念头雷轰电掣般闪过心头，顿感气馁，只得忍气吞声地陪个罪。

“That tone again!” Huang struck the small table on the couch and shouted, “Have you come to discuss business or to argue with me?” In more than twenty years of official service, Chunshou had never encountered such a superior. You entered through the examinations, he thought, but I am not some subordinate who bought his way in and has grown accustomed to being insulted. As for our careers, when I was appointed provincial treasurer of Zhejiang, you were still only its judicial commissioner. You merely have people at court—the men from that yiwei examination of the fifteenth year of Daoguang... At that thought, Chunshou shuddered. This was serious! One of Huang's examination classmates, Peng Yunzhang of Suzhou, had already become a grand councillor. There were also the two vice-presidents of the Board of Revenue. One, Wang Qingyun of Fujian, was particularly eager to help men from his home province. The other was He Guiqing, another examination classmate of Huang's and a close friend. The saying was true: “Without someone at court, do not seek office.” Huang's arrogance rested on powerful backing in the capital. Moreover, it was said that during his visit there that year the emperor had received him no fewer than six times. He was enjoying exceptional imperial favour. Chunshou could not possibly prevail against him. These thoughts flashed through his mind like thunder and lightning, draining his courage. Swallowing his anger, he could only apologise.

“大人息怒。我岂敢跟大人抬杠？一切还求大人维持。”这一说，黄宗汉的脸色才和缓了一些，“既为同僚，能维持总要维持。不过，“他使劲摇着头，一字一句地说：”难，难！“椿寿的心越发往下沉，强自镇静着问道：“大人有何高见？要请教诲。”“岂敢，岂敢。等我想一想再说吧！”说完，端一端茶碗，堂下侍候的戈什哈便拉开嗓子：“送客！”

“Please do not be angry, Your Excellency. How could I dare argue with you? I can only ask for your protection in this matter.” Only then did Huang's expression soften a little. “We are colleagues. Naturally, I shall support you wherever I can. But...” He shook his head emphatically and pronounced each word with care. “Difficult. Very difficult.” Chunshou's heart sank further. Forcing himself to remain calm, he asked, “What course would Your Excellency advise? I beg you to instruct me.” “Oh, I could hardly presume. Let me think it over first.” With that, Huang lifted his teacup. An attendant waiting below called out loudly, “Show the guest out!”

这送客等于逐客。椿寿出了抚台衙门，坐在轿子里，只催轿扶加快，急急赶回本衙门，让听差把文案请到“签押房”，关上房门，细说了上院的经过，惊疑不定地问道：“各位看看，黄抚台这是什么意思？”“黄抚台外号‘黄阎罗’，翻脸不认人是出名的，这件事要好好铺排一下。”“唉！”椿寿摇摇头，欲言又止，失悔在黄抚台刚到任，不理他索贿的暗示。“ ‘天大的公事，地大的银子’，”有个文案说很很率直，“先去探探口气看，院上到底打的什么主意？”于是连夜走路子去打听，总算有了确实的消息，据说黄宗汉为了明年的新漕得以早日受兑装载，照限期抵达通州，决定上奏，把湖属八帮的瘤船追了回来，漕米卸岸入仓，连同明年的新漕，一起装运。

This dismissal amounted to an expulsion. Chunshou left the governor's office and, once in his sedan chair, kept urging the bearers to go faster. Back at his own headquarters, he summoned his secretaries to his private office and shut the door. After describing the interview in detail, he asked in alarm and uncertainty, “What do you think Governor Huang means by all this?” “His nickname is Huang the King of Hell. He is notorious for turning on people without warning. This business will need careful handling.” “Ah!” Chunshou shook his head, beginning to speak and then stopping. He regretted having ignored Huang's hint about a bribe when the new governor first arrived. “‘A mountain of official business can be moved by a mountain of silver,’” one secretary said bluntly. “We should first sound him out and learn what he really has in mind.” They used their connections that very night to make inquiries and eventually obtained definite news. It was said that, to ensure next year's new grain could be accepted and loaded early and reach Tongzhou on time, Huang had decided to petition the throne to recall the boats of Huzhou's eight associations. Their grain would be unloaded and stored, then shipped together with next year's collection.

这样做法，只苦了漕帮，白白赔上一笔疏浚河道的费用。其次，那些奉委押运的候补州县，没有“公费”可派，一笔过年的盘缠便落空了。椿寿心中虽有不忍，但到底是别人的事，藩司能够不赔，已是上上大吉，只好狠一狠心不理他们了。果然，第二天抚台衙门来了正式公事，惟恐影响来年新漕的期限，“所有本年湖属八帮漕船，仰该司即便遵照，全数追回，候命办理。”椿寿不敢怠慢，立即派出人去，把湖属八帮的漕船截了回来，同时上院去见抚台，请示所谓“候命办理”是如何办法？黄宗汉一直托病不见。过了有五、六天，一角公文送到，拆开一看，椿寿几乎昏厥，顿足骂道，“黄寿臣，黄寿臣，你好狠的心！我与你问冤何仇，你要置我于死地！“黄宗汉的手段，的确太毒辣了，他以一省最高行政长官的地位，统筹漕运全局的理由，为了使来年新漕的输运，如期完成，以期此后各年均得恢复正常，作了一个决定，本年湖属八帮的漕米，留浙变价，全部漕米二十七万六千石，照户部所定价格，每石二两银子，共该五十五万二千两，限期一个月报缴。

Such a measure would chiefly hurt the associations, which would have paid for the dredging to no purpose. The expectant magistrates appointed as escorts would suffer too: without official expense allowances to draw upon, the money they had hoped to take home for the New Year would vanish. Chunshou felt sorry for them, but their troubles were, after all, their own. If the provincial treasury could escape a loss, that was already the best he could hope for. He would have to harden his heart and leave them to it. Sure enough, an official order arrived from the governor's office the next day. To avoid disrupting the schedule for the following year's grain, “all boats belonging to this year's eight Huzhou associations are to be recalled without delay, in full compliance with this order, and held pending further instructions.” Chunshou dared not hesitate. He immediately sent men to intercept the boats and bring them back, then went to the governor's office to ask what the “further instructions” would be. Huang repeatedly declined to see him, pleading illness. Five or six days later, another official dispatch arrived. Chunshou opened it and almost fainted. Stamping his foot, he cried, “Huang Shouchen! Huang Shouchen! How can you be so cruel? What wrong have I done you that you must drive me to my death?” Huang's methods were indeed ruthless. Invoking his authority as the province's highest administrative official and his responsibility for the entire grain-transport system, he had issued a decision. To ensure that the following year's grain arrived on schedule and that regular arrangements could thereafter be restored, this year's grain from Huzhou's eight associations would remain in Zhejiang and be sold. The total quantity was 276,000 shi. At the Board of Revenue's prescribed rate of two taels per shi, the proceeds due were 552,000 taels, to be remitted within one month.

这是椿寿与尖丁早已算过了的，市价与部价的差额，一共要三十三万两银子。如果在他第一次到湖州开会之前，抚台就作了这个决定，那么漕帮赔大部分，藩司赔小部分，这笔小部分的赔款，也还可以在浮收的款项中拨付，说起来只是今年白吃一场辛苦，没有“好处”而已。但现在的情况完全不同了，漕帮负担了疏浚河道的全部经费，事先已经声明，出了这笔钱，漕船非走不可，于今截回不定，已觉愧对漕帮，再要他们分赔差额，就是漕帮肯赔，自己也难启齿，何况看情形是决无此可能的。至于浮收的“好处”，早已按股照派，“分润”有夫人员，哪里再去追索？即使追索得到，也不过五、六万银子，还差着一大截呢！

Chunshou and the headman had already worked out what this meant: the gap between the market price and the official rate was some 330,000 taels. Had the governor made this decision before Chunshou's first meeting in Huzhou, the associations would have borne the larger share and the provincial treasury the smaller. The treasury's portion could still have been drawn from the excess collections. In effect, everyone would merely have worked for nothing that year and forfeited their customary gains. Now the situation was entirely different. The associations had paid the full cost of dredging and had explicitly stipulated that, once the money was spent, the boats must sail. Recalling them already left Chunshou ashamed. He could hardly ask them to contribute towards the price shortfall as well, even if they were willing—which, from all appearances, they certainly were not. As for the profits from excess collection, those had already been divided into shares and distributed among the people concerned. How could they now be recovered? Even if recovery were possible, they amounted to only fifty or sixty thousand taels, far short of what was needed.

事情的演变，竟会弄得全部责任，落在自己一个人头上。椿寿悔恨交并，而仍不能不拼命作最后的挣扎，愁眉苦脸地召集了亲信来商议，大家一致的看法是：“解铃还须系铃人”，惟有去求抚台，收回“变价”的成命，应解的二十多万石漕米，随明年新漕一起启运。就这样起卸入仓，从船上搬到岸上，明年再从岸上搬到船上，来回周折的运费、仓费，以及两次搬动的损耗，算起来也要赔好几万两银子，而且一定还会受到处分，但无论如何总比赔三十三万两银子来得好。两害相权取其轻，椿寿只得硬着头皮上院，把“手本”送了进去，门上出来答道：“上头人不舒服，请大人回去吧！上头交代，等病好了，再请大人过来相叙。”棒寿愤不可遏，吩咐跟班说：“回去取铺盖！抚台不见我不走，就借官厅的炕床睡。”

Events had somehow left the entire burden resting on Chunshou alone. Tormented by regret and anger, he nevertheless had to make one last desperate effort. With a miserable expression, he called his trusted advisers together. They all reached the same conclusion: only the man who had tied the knot could untie it. He must beg the governor to withdraw the order to sell the grain and allow the more than two hundred thousand shi to be shipped with the next year's collection. Unloading it now, storing it on shore, and loading it again the following year would still cost tens of thousands of taels in handling charges, storage fees, and losses from repeated movement. A disciplinary penalty would also be unavoidable. Even so, it would be better than finding 330,000 taels. Choosing the lesser of two evils, Chunshou steeled himself and went to the governor's office. He sent in his visiting card, but the gatekeeper returned with a message. “His Excellency is unwell and asks you to go home. He says he will invite you to call when he has recovered.” Chunshou's anger became uncontrollable. “Go back and fetch my bedding,” he told his attendant. “If the governor will not see me, I shall not leave. I can sleep on the couch in the reception hall.”

门上一看，这不象话，赶紧陪笑道：“大人不必，不必！想来是有急要公事要回，我再到上房去跑一趟。”于是椿寿就在官厅中坐等，等了半个时辰，黄宗汉出来了，仰着头，板着脸，一见面不等椿寿开口，就先大声问道：“你非见我不可？”“是！”椿寿低声下气地回答：“大人贵恙在身，本不该打搅，只是实在有万分困难的下情上禀。”“如果是湖属漕米的事，你不必谈。已经出奏了。”这句话就如焦雷轰顶，一时天旋地转，不得不颓然坐倒，等定定神看时，黄宗汉已无踪影，抚院的戈什哈低声向他说道：“大人请回吧！轿子已经伺侯半天了。”椿寿闭上眼，眼角流出两滴眼泪，拿马蹄袖拭一拭干净，由听差扶掖着，一步懒似一步地走官厅。

Seeing how unseemly the situation was becoming, the gatekeeper hastened to placate him. “There is no need, Your Excellency, no need at all! You must have some urgent official matter to report. I shall go to the inner quarters once more.” Chunshou waited in the reception hall. After about an hour, Huang appeared, head raised and face stern. Before Chunshou could speak, he demanded loudly, “You insist on seeing me?” “Yes,” Chunshou replied humbly. “I ought not to disturb Your Excellency during your illness, but I am in the greatest difficulty and must explain my circumstances.” “If it concerns the Huzhou grain, there is no need to say anything. I have already reported the decision to the throne.” The words struck him like a thunderbolt. The room seemed to spin, and he collapsed into a seat. When he recovered enough to look around, Huang had vanished. An attendant said softly, “Please go home, Your Excellency. Your chair has been waiting for some time.” Chunshou closed his eyes. Two tears escaped from their corners. He wiped them away with the curved cuffs of his robe and, supported by an attendant, left the hall, each step more reluctant than the last.

就在这天晚上，椿寿在藩司衙门后院的签押房里，上吊自杀。第二天一早为家人发觉，哭声震动内外，少不得有人献殷勤，把这个不幸的消息，飞报抚台。黄宗汉一听，知道闯了祸，逼死二品大贝，罪名不轻。但转念想起一重公案，觉得可以如法炮制，心便放了一半。

That night, Chunshou hanged himself in the private office behind the provincial treasury. His household discovered him early the next morning, and their cries of grief filled the compound. Someone inevitably seized the opportunity to curry favour by rushing the unhappy news to the governor. Huang knew at once that he was in trouble. Driving an official of the second rank to his death was no minor offence. Then he remembered an earlier case. If he could follow the same procedure, perhaps he would be safe. The thought eased half his fear.

他想起的是陕西蒲城王鼎尸谏的往事，这重公案发生在十年以前，王鼎与奸臣穆彰阿，同为大学士值军机。这位“蒲城相国”性情刚烈，嫉恶如仇，而遇到穆彰阿是阴柔奸险的性格，每在御前争执，一个声色俱厉，一个从容自如，宣宗偏听不明，总觉得王鼎不免过分。

The case he recalled was that of Wang Ding of Pucheng, Shaanxi, who had taken his own life as a final remonstrance ten years earlier. Wang and the corrupt minister Mujangga had both been grand secretaries serving on the Grand Council. Wang, known as the grand secretary from Pucheng, was fierce in temperament and hated wrongdoing. Mujangga was subtle, yielding in manner, and treacherous. When they argued before the Daoguang emperor, one spoke with visible anger while the other remained composed. The emperor, swayed by one side and failing to see clearly, invariably felt that Wang had gone too far.

道光二十二年，为了保荐林则徐夏用，王鼎不惜自杀尸谏，遗疏痛劾穆彰阿。那时有个军机章京叫陈孚恩，是穆彰阿的走狗，一看王鼎不曾入值，亦未请假，心里一支，借故出宫，赶到王鼎家一看，听得哭声震天，越发有数。趁王鼎的儿子，翰林院编修王抗骤遭大故，五中昏瞀的当儿，劝他把王鼎的尸首解下来，同时把遗疏抓到手里，一看内容，不出所料，便又劝王抗以个人前程为重，不必得罪穆彰阿，又说“上头”对王鼎印象不佳，而大臣自杀，有伤国体，说不定天颜震怒，不但王鼎身后的恤典落空，而且别有不测之祸。这一番威胁利诱，教王抗上了当，听从穆彰阿更改遗疏，并以暴疾身故奏报。宣宗也有些疑心，但穆彰阿布置周密，“上头”无法获知真相，也就算了。

In the twenty-second year of Daoguang, Wang Ding pressed for Lin Zexu's restoration to office and went so far as to hang himself in remonstrance, leaving a memorial that bitterly denounced Mujangga. A Grand Council secretary named Chen Fu'en, one of Mujangga's henchmen, noticed that Wang had neither attended duty nor asked for leave. Growing suspicious, he found an excuse to leave the palace and hurried to Wang's residence. The sound of overwhelming grief confirmed his fears. Wang's son, Wang Kang, a compiler in the Hanlin Academy, was dazed by the sudden calamity. Taking advantage of his confusion, Chen urged him to have the body lowered and at the same time secured the final memorial. Its contents were exactly what he had expected. He then advised Wang Kang to think of his own future and avoid offending Mujangga. He added that the emperor already held a poor opinion of Wang Ding and that a senior minister's suicide damaged the dignity of the state. If the emperor became angry, the family might lose not only the customary posthumous honours but also face unforeseen disaster. Threats and promises together deceived Wang Kang. He accepted Mujangga's alteration of the final memorial, and his father's death was reported as the result of sudden illness. The Daoguang emperor had some suspicions, but Mujangga had arranged matters so thoroughly that the truth could not reach the throne. The affair was allowed to rest.

陈孚恩帮了穆彰阿这个大忙，收获也下小，不久，穆彰阿就保他当山东巡抚。而王抗则以不能成父之志，为他父亲的门生，他自己的同年，以及陕甘同乡所不齿，辞官回里，郁郁以终。穆彰阿是道光十五年乙未科会试的大主考，黄宗汉是他的门生，颇为巴结这位老师。秦桧门下有“十客”，穆彰阿门下有“十子”，黄宗汉与陈孚恩都在“穆门十子”之数，自然熟知其事。所以，一遇椿寿的变故，他立即遣派亲信，以釜底抽薪的宗旨，先设法把椿寿的遗嘱弄到手，然后亲自拜访驻防的将军和浙江学政，因为这两个人是可以专折奏事的，先要把他们稳住，才可以不使真相上闻。

Chen Fu'en profited handsomely from this service. Before long, Mujangga recommended him for the governorship of Shandong. Wang Kang, on the other hand, was despised for failing to fulfil his father's purpose—by his father's protégés, by his own examination classmates, and by fellow natives of Shaanxi and Gansu. He resigned, returned home, and died in sorrow. Mujangga had been chief examiner at the metropolitan examination in the yiwei year, the fifteenth year of Daoguang. Huang Zonghan was therefore one of his examination protégés and had diligently cultivated his patron. Just as Qin Hui had once had his “Ten Guests,” Mujangga had his “Ten Sons.” Huang and Chen Fu'en both belonged to that circle, so Huang knew the Wang Ding affair very well. As soon as Chunshou's death occurred, he sent trusted men to secure Chunshou's final statement, aiming to remove the danger at its source. He then personally visited the commander of the local banner garrison and Zhejiang's education commissioner. Both were entitled to submit independent memorials to the throne. He first had to secure their cooperation if the truth was to be kept from the emperor.

当然，另一方面他还要间接拜托旗籍的官员，安抚椿寿的家属，然后奏报藩司出缺。上吊自杀是瞒不住的，所以另外附了个“夹片”，说是“浙江钱漕诸务支出，本年久旱岁歉，征解尤难，该司恐误公事，日夜焦急，以至迫切轻生。”把湖属八帮应运漕米，留浙变价的事，只字不提，同时录呈了经过修改的椿寿的遗嘱。咸丰帝此时初登大宝，相当精明，看遗嘱内有“因情节所逼，势不能生”两句话，大为疑惑，认为即令公事难办，何至遽尔自尽？是否另有别情，命令黄宗汉“再行详细访察，据实奏闻，毋稍隐饰。”接着，浙江学政万青藜也有专折奏报，说椿寿身后，留有遗嘱，“实因公事棘手，遽行自尽。”与黄宗汉的奏折，桴鼓相应。皇帝批示：“已有旨，令黄宗汉详查具报。汝近在省垣，若有所闻，亦可据实具奏。”

Meanwhile, he used intermediaries to ask banner officials to placate Chunshou's family, then reported that the provincial treasurer's post had fallen vacant. The suicide itself could not be concealed. Huang therefore attached a supplementary note: “The administration of Zhejiang's revenues and grain entails many expenditures. This year, prolonged drought and a poor harvest have made collection and remittance especially difficult. Fearing that official business would suffer, the treasurer grew anxious day and night until, overcome by desperation, he took his own life.” He made no mention of the order to retain and sell the Huzhou grain. He also submitted an altered copy of Chunshou's final statement. The Xianfeng emperor, newly enthroned and still quite discerning, was struck by two phrases in it: “Driven by the circumstances, I can no longer live.” Even if official business was difficult, why should it lead so abruptly to suicide? Suspecting another cause, he ordered Huang to “make further detailed inquiries and report the facts, concealing nothing.” Zhejiang's education commissioner, Wan Qingli, then submitted his own memorial. He stated that Chunshou had left a final declaration and “had indeed taken his own life because official business had become intractable.” This neatly echoed Huang's account. The emperor replied: “An order has already been issued directing Huang Zonghan to investigate thoroughly and report. Since you are in the provincial capital, you too may submit a factual report if anything comes to your attention.”

看来事情要闹得很大，但事态真正严重的关键所在，只有黄宗汉自己知道。因为椿寿的自尽，如果真的是由于他的措施严峻、则虽良心有愧，亦不过课以道义上的责任，在公事上可以交代得过，那就不必有所畏惧。而事实上并非如此，椿寿之死，是死在他虚言恫吓的一句话上。所谓“留浙变价”，原是黄宗汉有意跟椿寿为难的一种说法，暗地里他并不坚持这样做，不但不坚持，他还留着后手，以防椿寿无法做到时，自己有转圜的余地。由于在军机处和户部都有极好的关系，所以黄宗汉对来年新漕改用海运，以及本年湖属各帮漕米，不能如限北运的处置办法，都有十足的把握，私底下书函往还，几乎已有成议。但这些情形，椿寿无从知道，他亦瞒着不说。以改用海运并无把握，河运粮船难以依限回空的理由，下令截回漕船，留浙变价，这一套措施与他所奏报的改革办法，完全不符。他向椿寿所说的，留浙变价一事，“已经出奏”，事情到了推车撞壁的地步，再也无可挽回，这才使椿寿感到已入绝路，不能不一死了之。其实，“已经出奏”这句话，根本是瞎说。

The affair seemed likely to grow serious, but only Huang knew where the real danger lay. If Chunshou had truly killed himself merely because Huang's administrative measures were severe, Huang might feel guilty and bear moral responsibility, yet he could still defend his official conduct. There would have been little to fear. But that was not what had happened. Chunshou had died because of a false statement Huang had used to intimidate him. The proposal to retain and sell the grain in Zhejiang had been devised to make Chunshou's life difficult. In private, Huang did not insist upon it. Indeed, he had kept another course in reserve so that, if Chunshou could not comply, he himself would still have room to manoeuvre. With excellent connections in both the Grand Council and the Board of Revenue, Huang was entirely confident about securing sea transport for the following year's grain and about the arrangements for this year's Huzhou grain, which could not be sent north within the prescribed time. Private correspondence had already brought these matters close to agreement. Chunshou had no way of knowing this, and Huang deliberately kept him in the dark. Claiming that sea transport remained uncertain and that the canal boats could not return on schedule, Huang ordered the fleet recalled and the grain sold in Zhejiang. These measures were wholly at odds with the reforms he had actually proposed to the throne. When he told Chunshou that the decision to sell the grain had “already been reported,” he made it appear that matters had reached an immovable barrier and that no remedy remained. That was what persuaded Chunshou that he had reached a dead end and could only die. In fact, the claim that the decision had already been reported was a complete fabrication.

就凭这句谎言，黄宗汉便得对椿寿之死，负起全部责任。因而他必须多方设法掩饰遮盖，不使真相上闻，一面活动万青藜等人，帮着他瞒谎，一面遣派亲信，携带巨贤，到京师活动。当然，象军机大臣彭蕴章那里，是不必也不能行贿的，只有以同年的身份，拜托关顾照应。不过这样一件案子，也不是轻易压得下去的。椿寿是“上三旗”的旗人，亲戚之中，颇有贵官，认为他的死因可疑，自然要出头为他讲话，这样军机处要帮黄宗汉的忙，就不能不费一番手脚，来遮人耳目。

That lie alone made Huang fully responsible for Chunshou's death. He therefore had to do everything possible to conceal the truth from the throne. While persuading Wan Qingli and others to help sustain the deception, he sent trusted men to the capital with large sums of money to work on his behalf. With someone like Grand Councillor Peng Yunzhang, of course, bribery was neither necessary nor possible. Huang could only appeal to their bond as examination classmates and ask for his protection. Even so, a case like this could not easily be suppressed. Chunshou belonged to the Upper Three Banners and had highly placed relatives. They suspected that his death had a hidden cause and naturally spoke up for him. If the Grand Council was to help Huang, it would have to take some pains to conceal what it was doing.

照一向的惯例，类似这种情况，一定简派大员密查。既称密查，自然不能让被查的人知道，可是一二品的大员出京，无论如何是件瞒不住的事，于是便有许多掩护其行踪及任务的方法，一种是声东击西，譬如明发上谕：“着派某某人驰往江苏查案”，这人便是“钦差”的身分，所经之处，接待的礼节极其隆重。这样一路南下，到了济南，忽然不定了，用钦差大臣的关防，咨会山东巡抚，开出一张名单，请即传提到案，迅雷不及掩耳地展开了查案的工作。再有一种是暗渡陈仓，乘某某大员外放到任的机会，密谕赴某处查案。这道密谕照例不发“邸抄”，被查的省分，毫无所知，行到目的地，拜访总督或巡抚，出示密谕，于是一夕之间，可以掀起大狱。查黄宗汉逼死椿寿一案，就是用的这一种办法，所以在表面上看不出黄宗汉出了毛病的痕迹。这当然又是军机处帮他的忙。

In such circumstances, established practice called for a senior official to conduct a secret investigation. Naturally, the person under investigation was not supposed to know. Yet the departure from the capital of a first- or second-rank official could hardly be hidden. Various methods were therefore used to disguise his route and assignment. One was to announce one destination while aiming at another. A public edict might, for instance, order a named official to proceed urgently to Jiangsu to investigate a case. Travelling as an imperial commissioner, he would receive elaborate honours along the way. On reaching Jinan, however, he would suddenly stop. Using his commissioner's seal, he would send the governor of Shandong a list of people to be summoned immediately, launching the investigation before anyone had time to prepare. Another method was to use an outwardly ordinary appointment as cover: a senior official leaving the capital to take up a provincial post would receive secret instructions to investigate a case elsewhere on the way. Such instructions were not published in the official court gazette, so the province concerned knew nothing of them. On reaching his destination, the investigator called on the governor-general or governor and produced the secret edict. A major judicial inquiry could thus erupt overnight. This was the method chosen to investigate Huang's role in driving Chunshou to his death. Outwardly, there was therefore no sign that Huang was in trouble—another favour done him by the Grand Council.

这位钦差名叫何桂清，是黄宗汉的同年。在他们乙未一榜中，何桂清的年纪比较轻，仪表清俊，吐嘱渊雅，人缘极好。这年秋天，由户部侍郎外放江苏学政，在京里饯行送别的应酬甚多，所以一直迟迟不能启程。就在这殷摒挡行囊，准备到任的期间内，出了椿寿这件案子，彭蕴章和他一些在京同年商量的结果，奏请密派问桂清于赴江苏学政途中，顺道查办。“上头”只对椿寿的死因怀疑，不曾想到是他所信任的黄宗汉干的好事，自然不会以何桂清与黄是同年为嫌，便准了军机处的建议。这个消息，很快、很秘密地传到了杭州，黄宗汉等于服下一位定心丸。何桂清以钦命在身，不敢耽搁，也就在岁暮之际，出京南下。

The commissioner chosen was He Guiqing, Huang's examination classmate. Among the successful candidates of their yiwei year, He was relatively young. Handsome, refined in speech, and graceful in manner, he was exceptionally well liked. That autumn he had been transferred from a vice-presidency at the Board of Revenue to the post of education commissioner in Jiangsu. So many farewell engagements awaited him in the capital that his departure was repeatedly delayed. Chunshou's case arose while he was making ready to leave. After consulting other examination classmates in the capital, Peng Yunzhang proposed that He be secretly instructed to investigate it on his way to Jiangsu. The emperor suspected something about Chunshou's death, but had not imagined that Huang, whom he trusted, was responsible. He therefore saw no reason to object to He's relationship with Huang and approved the Grand Council's recommendation. The news reached Hangzhou swiftly and in strict secrecy. To Huang, it was profoundly reassuring. With an imperial commission now entrusted to him, He Guiqing dared delay no longer. As the year drew to its close, he left the capital and headed south.

附：本批精读方向（学习提示，不属于原文）

阅读：先梳理王有龄与胡雪岩的关系，再梳理椿寿与黄宗汉的矛盾。尤其区分“叙述事实”“据说”“人物的判断”与“人物对外的说法”。
完形：关注 however、therefore、otherwise、even if、in effect 等词前后的语义关系；同时记录 rely on、bear responsibility、make up the shortfall 等搭配。先确定上下文关系，再记单词。
段落排序：梳理“征收粮税—验米拒收—仓满停征—百姓等待—官员被迫议价”的因果链，以及“提议不走—核算亏损—提出疏浚—附加条件—官员翻案”的转折链。注意 the first / the second / the third 等结构标志和 this / that 的回指。
翻译：选取含 although、unless、whether、so that、as for 的长句，先独立英译汉，再对照中文。中文原文与英文句子不必逐词对应，应检查主干、逻辑、指代及语气是否保留。
写作：优先积累能脱离故事背景使用的表达，如 fulfil an obligation、take responsibility、reach an agreement、leave room to manoeuvre。官场称谓、历史单位和人物对话不宜直接搬进议论文。
