CHAPTER FIFTEEN

With his anxiety relieved, Hu Xueyan once more devoted all his attention to business. There was little to do aboard the boat, so it was a good opportunity to go over his accounts. When he worked out the total, he could scarcely believe it himself. In barely half a year, as much as five hundred thousand taels had passed through his hands. With business in Hangzhou, Huzhou, and Shanghai, he had certainly built up a lively operation, and everything was going smoothly. Yet if anything unexpected happened, a tug at one thread could disturb the whole fabric. He would not fall alone: many others would be dragged down with him—first Wang Youling, then Fat Zhang, then Yu Si, and then You Laowu. At this thought, the autumn wind on the river felt especially cold. Pushing aside his abacus, he sat alone before the lamp and considered everything, growing deeply cautious. He must not rely on his quick mind and flexible methods to expand without restraint. One man's energy was limited. Anything he overlooked could open a gap that would rapidly widen; by the time he discovered it, matters might already be beyond repair.

Two conclusions followed naturally. First, he must conserve his energy and stop meddling in unnecessary disputes. Second, he must find more help. Liu Qingsheng had been a good choice and could now take charge independently. Chen Shilong had promise, but Hu had not yet made proper use of him. During the remaining journey to Hangzhou, he would teach Chen the tricks and practices of business, then put him in charge of the Shanghai affairs. Before teaching him the trade, however, he must arrange his marriage. That would also settle Hu's own relationship with A’zhu and remove another worry: a further way of conserving his energy. After considering the matter for some time, he decided how to begin.

The boat set off early the next morning. Apart from Old Zhang, who was helping at the helm in the stern, everyone was at leisure. Hu deliberately called Chen into the cabin for a talk. Since boarding, A’zhu had spent most of her time in the after cabin, saying little even when they ate together. Chen, of course, often went back to see her. Hu felt certain that she would listen carefully from there to whatever the two men said in the middle cabin. He therefore meant to appear to speak casually while addressing his words to her. “Shilong, my business has taken shape now. But one man can't eat all the rice himself: we must all lend a hand. Tell me, would you rather be in Huzhou or Shanghai?” Unaware that Hu had a settled plan and was asking deliberately, Chen thought he really was being offered a choice. Shanghai might be prosperous, but he was unsure of himself there. Huzhou was home, and he could stay close to A’zhu. Naturally he preferred Huzhou. “I'd like to get the silk firm properly established in Huzhou first.” “I knew you'd choose Huzhou.” With his back to the after cabin, Hu could make a face without A’zhu seeing. He winked emphatically at Chen, warning him that the next words had a purpose. “You can't bear to leave A’zhu!” Chen was quick to understand and gave an embarrassed smile of acknowledgment. With one man making the assertion and the other accepting it, the relationship among all three was settled in that single exchange—unless A’zhu herself came out and plainly declared that she would not marry Chen Shilong.

Listening from the after cabin, A’zhu was furious. They were treating a person like a bale of silk: a few words between them, and the transaction was complete! How could they take everything upon themselves like that? Yet, for all her angry thoughts, she remained where she was, scarcely breathing as she listened. Hu was speaking again. “My idea is that the silk firm has your father-in-law and mother-in-law there...” At this, A’zhu was astonished. Whom did he mean by “father-in-law and mother-in-law”? Listening on, she could not mistake it. Who could Chen's parents-in-law be but her own parents? Bewilderment, shame, and resentment mingled with an excitement she could not describe. Her thoughts raced like ten thousand horses, and only by repeatedly mastering herself could she make out what was being said. “To tell the truth, A’zhu's mother hasn't quite the right idea. Because I helped the family, she thinks she ought to give me her daughter in marriage as repayment. But those are separate matters. I didn't help them because I wanted their daughter.” Hypocrite! A’zhu cursed inwardly. Remembering the many ways Hu had once tried to win her affection, she felt her face grow warm and silently added, Shameless!

What followed made her feel rather better. “In all conscience,” Hu said, “do I like A’zhu? Of course I do. But I won't put her at a disadvantage. Her character is spotless, and even a young lady of a great family might not equal her in looks and qualities. Shilong, even marrying you would be beneath her.” “I know.” Chen nodded with a sense of his own unworthiness. “It's good that you know. You must be especially considerate towards her.” Chen repeated the same answer. “I know.” He kept agreeing as though he had already married her! A’zhu deeply resented it. She was puzzled too. From their tone, it sounded as though they had long since made a private bargain behind her back. She must first discover exactly what had happened, then consider how to get her own back. This time Chen spoke. “Mr Hu, what do you think I ought to do about the marriage? I have nothing—no means at all.” “You have me!” Hu answered readily. “I have three matches to arrange at present. One has already succeeded; another needs more consideration; and then there's yours. A word from me will settle Old Zhang. Your mother-in-law is easier still: she always listens to me. A’zhu is devoted to her parents. Once they agree, we needn't fear she won't.” So that was it! They meant to use her parents to pressure her. No wonder they were so confident. They thought nothing of her at all. Her first step must be to speak to her father and prevent him from agreeing too readily. Then they could stand there looking helpless!

She had scarcely settled this plan when she was startled again: Hu seemed to read her thoughts aloud. “But A’zhu is extremely proud. Kindness moves her; force doesn't. We must allow for that too. Even if she obeyed her parents unwillingly, with a thousand resentments in her heart, she wouldn't treat you particularly well afterwards. In the end, what matters most is that both people should be willing. Always remember what I've told you: A’zhu responds to kindness, not force. Give way to her in everything, and you'll have happiness all your life.” These words left a mixture of sweetness and pain in her heart. They also took the wind out of her sails: she could no longer summon any resistance. Yet she still resented being managed in this way and felt that Chen ought not to have things so easily. “My plan,” Hu continued, “is to see whether we can hold the wedding before the year's end. If there isn't time, we can complete the formal betrothal this year and have you marry into her household next year...” “Marry into her household!” Chen interrupted loudly. His voice alone showed his reluctance, and A’zhu's heart tightened as she listened. “You aren't being asked to change your surname to Zhang. The two households will simply become one. There's nothing disgraceful about it.” “As long as I don't have to change my surname.”

“Don't fail to appreciate your good fortune!” Hu deliberately pitched this at A’zhu. “Even if you wanted to change your surname, she might not think you good enough.” Chen showed his white teeth in an embarrassed smile. Through a crack in the partition, A’zhu saw it and felt a pleasant sense of triumph. “A good wife is all very well,” Hu continued, “but a man who never moves a step from her apron strings won't amount to much. With your parents-in-law at the Huzhou silk firm, they can manage it perfectly well. I want you to help me in Shanghai. Learn the foreign language properly from Old Gu, and it will serve you all your life.” “Gladly!” Chen was excited. “Mr Gu speaks it wonderfully well. I'll make sure I learn it from him.” “That's the spirit!” Hu sounded pleased. “Fortunately, the silk business will bring you back to Huzhou often. You'll have plenty of time to show A’zhu your affection.” The wretch! Her face flushed crimson. She did not know whether to sit or stand, yet dared make no sound for fear that the men would discover her eavesdropping. She tiptoed back to her berth and, with a hand over her racing heart, carefully considered all they had said.

She became so absorbed that she lost track of time. Only when Old Zhang called did she come to herself. Looking out, she saw the sun overhead: it was noon already. “Come and eat! A’zhu, what have you been doing? I haven't seen you all morning.” “I fell asleep!” She was pleased with that answer. Being asleep meant that she could not have heard Hu and Chen talking, which would make it easier to pretend ignorance when she faced them. They were pretending too. At the table, both men behaved as usual. It was Old Zhang who had much to say. The boat would stop at Deqing that afternoon, where they would part: Hu and Chen would continue south to Hangzhou, while Old Zhang took his daughter north towards home in the same boat. Naturally, various things had to be settled. They reached Deqing early that afternoon. As soon as the boat was moored, Hu invited Old Zhang ashore for a stroll. A’zhu immediately guessed that they had something concerning her to discuss. While lying in her berth that morning, she had decided that, whatever happened, she must first return to Huzhou and talk to her mother about this lifelong matter. If her father agreed now, there would be no room left for discussion. Unwilling to let others arrange everything for her, she resolved to remain beside him all day and give Hu no opportunity to speak. What could she do now? There was only one way: follow them. Surely Hu would not openly discuss matchmaking with her standing there!

“Father, I'm coming too!” she called. Naturally Hu did not want her along. Preventing it was easy; he did not even need to think. “A’zhu, would you do me a favour and pack my odds and ends?” “Yes!” Honest Old Zhang agreed. “We're changing boats. Everyone's things need sorting out. You stay here.” This gave Hu another opening. “Exactly! Shilong, go through the presents that are to be taken to Huzhou, and explain everything to A’zhu. We mustn't have any mistakes.” With that, he strode ashore with Old Zhang, deliberately leaving Chen behind to confide his feelings to her. A’zhu could scarcely contain her indignation. She felt like Monkey in Journey to the West: however she tried, she could never escape Hu Xueyan's palm. For the moment, Chen would naturally have to bear the brunt of her frustration.

“What have they gone ashore for?” she demanded angrily. Chen was naturally clever. During his time with Hu, he had also picked up many ways of handling people and situations simply by watching and listening. In something affecting his entire future, he could hardly afford carelessness. Rather than answer at once, he looked up and considered her carefully, determined to understand her attitude first. That question proved she had heard their whole conversation that morning. Now she suspected that Hu had gone ashore to discuss her marriage with her father. If so, why had she not come forward to speak that morning? Why fly into a temper now? Clearly, this was a fit of temper. He understood that too: the more he opposed her, the fiercer she would become. The best course was to leave her nothing on which to vent her anger. Smiling apologetically, he said, “I really don't know. Shall I run after them and ask?” “How obliging of you!” One wave subsided, and another rose. “And what were you and your master talking about all morning? What was all that nonsense?” With the secret out, how could he deny it? But how should he acknowledge it? A smile without an answer would annoy her; a full account would undo the effect of Hu's carefully planned words, which had reached her heart so precisely. Neither course would do. He was at a loss, and his silence seemed about to anger her again.

Urgency brought him an idea. It was like building an embankment: after days of labour by many hands, the moment came to close the final breach. Then one had to see clearly, act quickly, and seize the opportunity. At a critical closing, an official in charge might leap into the gap himself and use his body to resist the flood. Moved by such selfless courage, the others would put forth all their strength, and the work would succeed. Chen too must dare that leap if this great undertaking was to succeed. Without hesitation, he dropped onto both knees and knelt upright before her. “Since you've heard everything, I needn't say much more. A’zhu, my whole life is in your hands.” She had not expected this. Her heart raced with alarm: what if someone saw them? “What are you doing?” she demanded in a low voice. “Get up, quickly!” “Getting up is easy. Say one word, and I will.” She knew what that word was. But even if she agreed in her heart, she could not say it. She must first try frightening him. “The more you behave like this, the less I'll say! Get up, or I'll never speak to you again.” Chen had resolved to settle the matter once and for all. His voice allowed no argument. “Until you say it, I'll kneel here for ever.”

“I've never known anyone like you!” A’zhu said resentfully. “What do you want me to say?” “You know.” “Exactly! You know, and I know. Isn't that enough?” At those words, Chen's heart settled. Smiling, he asked, “Then is it really settled?” “Stop pestering!” Her face grew stern. “If you don't get up, even what's settled will be unsettled!” He could no longer refuse to obey, but he wanted one more test. “I'll get up. Give me a hand.” “I won't! Why should I pull you up?” She drew a finger down her cheek to shame him. “‘A man's knees are worth gold.’ Apparently yours are worth nothing.” “For the sake of that gold beneath my knees, help me up!” As he spoke, he held out his hand.

A’zhu truly meant to ignore him, yet her right hand seemed independent of her intentions. Somehow it reached out, and once Chen had taken it, he would not let go. Rising, he kept hold of her hand and sat beside her. His other hand reached swiftly for the window. With a clatter, the wooden shutter closed, plunging the cabin into darkness. “What are you doing?” she asked sharply but quietly. “Nothing! I only want to kiss you.” “Don't you dare!” Before she had finished, he ignored her objection and became more intimate than she was prepared to accept. She felt embarrassed, anxious, and conflicted. Beyond the dark cabin it was still broad daylight, and the thought of being discovered made her deeply uneasy. “Let go,” she finally managed to say, turning her head aside. “Just one more kiss!” “Again?” she said angrily. “Don't make me afraid of you!” It was a serious warning. Chen stopped, released her, and helped her sit up. “Shall I open the window?” he asked gently. “Of course.” She reached out and opened it herself. As the light came in, she quickly withdrew into a corner hidden from anyone outside, smoothed her hair, straightened her clothes, and sat with closed lips and lowered eyes, looking as though she had been hurt.

“A’zhu...” “Don't pester me again!” she interrupted. “Behave yourself and talk sensibly, or kindly leave.” Chen said nothing. He merely smiled and kept looking at her, making her both embarrassed and annoyed. “Would you stop staring at me like that?” She gave him a reproachful glance. “It's not as though you don't know me.” “I'm sorry,” he said with a smile. “I can't bear to stop looking.” The words stirred something different in her heart, and her tone softened. “You can keep your affection in your heart. Must you display it all on your face? You're thick-skinned and don't mind being laughed at, but you ought to think of someone else.” Chen looked away. Almost at once, however, his gaze returned. Without sight of her face, he felt as though he had mislaid something important and could not be at ease until he found it again.

During this brief silence, A’zhu felt her heart grow calmer. Touching her face, she found it no longer so hot. “I want to ask you some serious questions,” she said. “Whether you answer honestly is a matter for your conscience.” “Very well!” Chen answered firmly. “I'll answer in good conscience. Ask me.” “You and your master discussed me long ago?” “Yes, long ago.” “What did you say?” “That's hard to explain. We said so much that I don't know where to begin.” “Then heaven knows how many times you've plotted about me!” Recalling the hateful way they had disposed of her privately, she complained, “You could probably sell me to a foreign country without my knowing it.” “Who would dare plot against you?” Chen assumed a solemn air. “I'd be the first to object!”

“Hmph!” A’zhu curled her lip. “You're such a good man! If you're so good, why haven't you let a word slip all these days?” “It wasn't that I wouldn't. I didn't dare.” “Why not?” “I was afraid you'd reject me outright. After that, it would be hard to say anything more.” On reflection, that was probably true. But she also realised that, although her girl friends considered her formidable, she was no match for Hu or Chen. If they really intended anything heartless, they could manoeuvre her into a position with no escape. What, then, could she rely on? Chen's heart. Only by winning that could she feel secure. She must combine kindness with firmness. Consideration mattered, but she must also establish rules and refuse to give way. That was for the future, however. For now, she must learn more about what concerned her. “What exactly did Mr Hu say about me?” To Chen, “Mr Hu” was an entirely new form of address from her. He had never heard her call him that before. The change suggested both that her relationship with Hu had reached its end and that she would follow her husband's lead, using the same address Chen used. This understanding delighted him, and once again he stared at her with a foolish smile. She had never seen such an expression on Hu's face. Like the smile of a mischievous big boy, it had a charm of its own. This time she felt like reaching out to touch his face.

“What did you just ask?” Chen suddenly said. Her thoughts elsewhere, A’zhu was startled. But in such situations she had a way of turning the accusation back on the other person. “Look at you!” she complained. “You forget everything the moment it's said. How can you claim to care about me?” “I'm sorry!” He smiled apologetically. “I'm so happy that I've rather lost my head.” During this delay, she remembered her question and repeated it. “I asked what Mr Hu said about me.” “You heard for yourself! A thousand words, all meaning one thing: you're wonderful.” He was referring to her eavesdropping. She could hardly deny it. “I mean at other times. Surely he's said more than that.”

“Don't inquire into that any more.” Chen waved a hand. “Let's talk about ourselves.” “Yes!” The word escaped her, followed by another question. “What have they gone ashore to discuss? Me?” “Certainly.” “Then why did you play innocent just now and say you didn't know?” “Then was then, and now is now. Now I needn't call you A’zhu. I can call you my wife!” “Shame on you!” she said, blushing. “Don't be so embarrassing.” “It's really extraordinary!” Chen sounded full of wonder. “First I called you Miss Zhang, then A’zhu, and now my wife. So much has changed in only a few months!” Thinking it over, she felt the same. Life was astonishing. From the time she had met Hu to this moment, how many new and strange experiences had she had? These six months had certainly been eventful.

“I was thinking,” Chen continued, “a person depends entirely on luck. Meeting Mr Hu was the day my luck began.” “Don't put it like that! Nobody can depend on luck alone. Luck lasts only a while; you must always try to improve yourself.” There was something admonishing in her words, and Chen found them a little disagreeable. Then he reflected that she spoke this way precisely because she already thought of them as husband and wife, sharing the same fortunes. His slight resentment quickly disappeared. He said no more, and neither did she. Their silence did not mean that they had nothing to say. As they looked at one another, his lingering gaze and her swiftly averted glances conveyed more than words could tell.

“It's dark!” A’zhu exclaimed. “Why hasn't Father come back?” “They must be drinking in town. It'll be a while yet.” “Are you hungry?” “I'm not. Are you?” “Neither am I. But...” She stopped, thinking. If she was not hungry, she was not hungry. What could that “but” mean? Chen could not resist asking. “But what?” “Let's go outside.” A’zhu stood up. “What sort of scene is this, the two of us sitting here in the dark?” Chen would have preferred to remain close beside her in the darkness, talking softly. To oblige her, however, he answered against his wishes, “Yes, let's go out, light the lamp, and wait for them.”

In the middle cabin, they lit the kerosene lamp and found four dishes, four sets of cups and chopsticks, and a pot of wine already on the square table. When had the boatman come in? Only one partition away, they had known nothing about it. It was astonishing. Thinking a little further, A’zhu blushed again. “Look!” she reproached Chen quietly. “They must have heard everything we said in there.” He understood too. While laying out the meal, the boatman must have heard their private conversation in the after cabin and chosen not to disturb them. He had left the dishes without lighting the lamp or calling them to eat, allowing events to take their course. Evidently he was a very tactful man. “We said nothing we need be ashamed of. It doesn't matter if they heard,” Chen said reassuringly. “They'll find out sooner or later anyway, and there's no need to hide it. If you simply take no notice, like the Seventh Lady, people won't tease you.”

Mention of the Seventh Lady aroused both concern and curiosity in A’zhu, along with an unpleasant feeling she could not describe. “Let me ask you something. How do you feel when she keeps calling you A’long?” Before considering his own feelings, Chen detected a trace of jealousy in the question and immediately became cautious. “If she chooses to call me that, I can only answer. To tell the truth...” The words were on his lips, but he felt they would be unkind. He waved a hand. “Oh, let's not discuss it.” “Why?” A’zhu pressed him. “Why not?” “It's nothing to do with us. Why talk about it?” “There's no harm in talking.” Seeing how intent she was, Chen had to answer, though against his conscience. “Honestly, it makes me rather uncomfortable.” She smiled faintly, showing that the reply pleased her, and asked no more.

Chen felt as though a weight had been lifted. He helped A’zhu with the lamp, and they sat opposite each other to eat. Ordinarily each had attended to their own needs. Even if he cared for her, he could hardly show his attentions openly. Now things were different. He served her rice and selected dishes for her, laughingly calling himself a “big-footed maid”—a Hangzhou expression he had heard used to mock a man who liked waiting on women. The meal lasted an hour. Chen talked a great deal about one thing and another, though seldom about himself, and A’zhu listened with absorbing interest. “They're back!” he suddenly called. She turned and saw two lanterns approaching slowly. At once she became anxious. What expression should she wear when facing her father and Hu? Naturally, she could not hide in the after cabin. All she could do was try to look calm, clear the table, and respond as circumstances required once they came aboard. Chen hurried out, helped the boatman lay the gangplank, and supported Old Zhang across. When he turned to help Hu, Hu squeezed his hand firmly, signalling that the great matter had been settled.

“Well!” Hu began teasing as soon as he entered. “How long have you two been eating this meal?” “We've been waiting for you all this time.” Seeing both men's red faces and smelling the wine on them, A’zhu issued a warning in advance. “Don't come talking nonsense because you're drunk!” “We shan't!” Hu replied with an unmistakably drunken air. “No nonsense. Serious matters.” “What serious matters can you discuss when you've been drinking? I'll make a strong pot of tea. Have some and go to sleep—that would be best.” She called the boatman to clear away the food, then took the porcelain teapot to make tea. Though outside the cabin, her thoughts remained within it. She listened for what Hu would say, but heard Old Zhang's voice instead. “Shilong!” “Yes!” Chen answered emphatically. That call and response alone made her anxious. One hand held a handful of tea leaves; the other was damp with nervous sweat. What would her father say? Yet he fell silent again, leaving his daughter more bewildered and uneasy than ever.

“Old Zhang,” Hu said, breaking the unbearable silence, “you talk to A’zhu. I'll talk to Shilong.” “Yes, yes! I don't know what to say to him. You do it!” Then Old Zhang called, “A’zhu! A’zhu!” From his tone, her father must already have agreed. This was something to discuss quietly, she thought. Why shout it out? A little annoyed, she answered loudly, “I'm making tea!” “Come out when you've finished. I have something to tell you.” “Can't you come in and tell me?” “I'm coming.” He left the cabin. He had drunk rather too much and stumbled unsteadily as he walked.

A’zhu hurried to support him. “You ought to drink less of that yellow stuff! Why have you had so much?” “Because I'm happy!” Old Zhang answered. “Life only seems worthwhile on an evening like this.” His fatherly love showed in every word. She was moved and felt truly fortunate, though her reply naturally retained the affectionate complaining tone of a daughter accustomed to indulgence. “Drunken talk from the first word! I've never heard you speak of ‘life’ before. All that elegant language is embarrassing.” Despite her words, she remained devoted to him. She helped him sit down, brewed the tea, and brought him the first cup. Catching her hand and looking up, Old Zhang said, “A’zhu, you must thank Mr Hu.” “Why?” “He's arranged a good match for you.” Lowering his voice to say it, he nodded repeatedly. “This is the best way. The very best.”

She found it rather funny but could hardly show it. Her feelings were contradictory. On the one hand, she wanted her father to stop before he embarrassed her further. On the other, she wanted to hear exactly what Hu had told him. Naturally, Old Zhang went on. “A’zhu,” he said solemnly, “Mr Hu is the matchmaker, and I've given him my consent. I hope you'll live happily together and grow old side by side.” After all this, whom did he mean? Although she knew, and knew too that her father was no speaker, she still felt anxious. She wanted to hear the name “Chen Shilong” before she could be at ease. What she said, however, was full of bashfulness. “Father, I say you're talking drunkenly, and still you won't admit it. Enough! Don't say any more.” “Yes. You understand already, so it doesn't matter if I say nothing. But marriage is an important business. I ought to tell you.” He was rambling and hardly reaching the point. Hu, outside, could no longer bear it. “Will you two come out? I have a few things to say.” “Yes, yes!” Old Zhang called back. “You had better say them after all.” He rose and pulled his daughter along. Too shy to come out willingly, she could not resist his insistence and at last entered the middle cabin. Her lively eyes passed briefly over Chen's face, then withdrew. She stood by the doorway with her head lowered.

“A’zhu, you've always been perfectly natural. There's no need for embarrassment,” Hu said. “I've arranged the match, your father has agreed, and Chen could wish for nothing better. We're waiting only for your consent. Then I'll have him bow to your father and establish the relationship. Tell us plainly: do you like Shilong or not?” “I don't know.” Her answer was loud and quick. She turned her face aside, looking almost resentful. “You must be too embarrassed to say. Then give us a sign. If you don't like him, go outside. If you do, sit here.” What a tormentor Hu was! She cursed him inwardly. Naturally she did not want to leave, yet she could not bring herself to sit. She could only continue pretending not to understand. “I don't know what you mean.” “Saying you don't understand means you do!” Hu laughed. “Very well. We've had our joke. Now I'll ask you seriously. If you feel too shy to tell me, tell your father and let him report it. Shilong is my pupil, so I am both the matchmaker and the elder representing him. Marriage is for life, no childish game. Whatever there is to say should be made clear now. What do you require of the bridegroom's family?” This showed Hu's experience. He knew that the couple were deeply attached and willing, and that his services to the Zhang family gave him considerable influence as matchmaker. Even so, he asked clearly, so that the bride's family would have no grievance afterwards.

Old Zhang thought the question unnecessary. “Nothing, nothing!” he said, waving his hands. “How could we make demands? Whatever our honoured matchmaker says, we shall follow.” “It's precisely because you think that way that I must ask.” Hu turned again. “A’zhu, this concerns your whole life. You mustn't be embarrassed. Tell me what you want, and I'll see whether it can be done. If it can't, I shall have nothing more to do with the matter.” This was a way of putting pressure on her from the opposite direction. If she really refused to speak, he might say he would withdraw. Then a happy prospect could fall through, leaving a deadlock that was difficult to resolve. Alarm overcame her shyness. With her head down, she said softly, “I don't ask anything. I only want him to try to get ahead and never change his feelings.” “Did you hear that, Shilong? If you refuse to improve yourself and become idle and self-indulgent, or if prosperity later leads you to bring home a concubine and upset A’zhu, you'll be deliberately disgracing me as your matchmaker.” “Time reveals a man's heart, Mr Hu. You shall see.” “Good. I believe you. A’zhu, be at ease. With me supervising him, he won't dare neglect his future. As for a change of heart, if anything like that happens, tell me. I'll stand up for you.” She wanted to thank him but felt too shy. Instead, she looked at him and nodded slightly to show her gratitude.

“Very well! Shilong, bow to your father-in-law. You shall use the new form of address from today.” A’zhu hurried away at once. Old Zhang repeatedly protested that it was unnecessary, but Chen knelt, touched his forehead firmly to the floor, and called with a broad smile, “Father!” “Please get up!” Old Zhang was both delighted and uneasy, grinning as he clumsily tried to help him rise. Chen stood, then knelt again to bow to Hu. At Hu's instruction, he fetched a presentation box. Hu had planned ahead and bought four kinds of jewellery in Shanghai: a pair of jadeite earrings, a pair of gold bracelets, two pearl flowers, and four gemstone rings. Together they must have been worth five or six hundred taels. They were the betrothal gifts for the bride's family. Old Zhang naturally felt overwhelmed, but there was no need for formal refusals. After thanking Hu, he called loudly, “Come and look! You're fortunate indeed. Your mother has never worn jewellery as fine as this.”

Hidden in the after cabin and peering through a crack, A’zhu was already deeply moved. At those words from her father, something swept through her heart, and hot tears suddenly covered her face. She could not suppress her sobs. “Why, what's the matter...?” “Don't question her.” Hu waved a hand, interrupting Old Zhang. “She's probably feeling sorry for her mother.” The words touched A’zhu's heart exactly. Whether she felt gratitude towards her parents or towards Hu, she could not have said. She simply lay down on her berth and wept. The more she cried, the more relieved and confident she became. When she had finished, she wiped her eyes and walked openly into the cabin. She was still too embarrassed to smile, however, and sat with a solemn face, waiting for her father or Hu to speak so that she could join in. Hu obliged. “A’zhu, where's the tea you made us?” “Oh dear, I'd forgotten!” She rose. “I'm afraid it's cold.” “Cold tea is just what we want. Bring it here.” She fetched it and poured a cup for Hu, then one for her father. Chen sat shyly to one side, looking very much like a bridegroom. After a little hesitation, she poured him a cup too. She neither carried it over nor spoke, merely pushing it towards the edge of the table to indicate that he should take it himself.

“Look for yourself. Do you like them?” Hu opened the presentation box. Faced with the glitter of pearls and gems, A’zhu was astonished. Were these really hers? She could scarcely believe it. “Never display your wealth!” Old Zhang's long experience of the world made him genuinely uneasy. “Put them away carefully. You can look when you get home.” She had to obey, though reluctantly. Closing the box, she lowered her head and said softly, “Mr Hu, thank you.” “A mere trifle!” Hu smiled. “When Shilong prospers, he'll buy you diamonds.” Old Zhang too was moved, and his words now came more readily. “Shilong, you must never forget how well Mr Hu has treated you both. You must repay him.” Chen nodded earnestly. While he was trying to find words that would express his gratitude, Hu spoke first. “That isn't entirely right, Old Zhang. There's no question of repayment. I've asked you to help me, and naturally I regard you as family. We share good fortune and bad. If we run the business well, everyone benefits. Now...” He gave Old Zhang a meaningful glance. “Let's go to bed. A’zhu and Shilong can sort out our things so that we can part tomorrow morning.”

He deliberately gave them a chance to be alone and exchange a few heartfelt words. Chen took a lamp and saw the men to their berths. Returning, he first shut the cabin window, then placed a mirror on the table. “Do try on the jewellery,” he said gently. Nothing could have pleased her more. She opened the box and first put on the jadeite earrings shaped like autumn leaves. Then she slipped a gold bracelet onto each wrist and put on a ruby ring. Only the pearl flowers could not be worn: they belonged in a woman's hair bun, and she still wore her hair in a braid. Seated opposite, Chen watched with a delighted smile. A’zhu had never looked particularly like a girl from a modest household. Adorned with jewellery, her oval face seemed still more gracious and distinguished. She did not look at all like a boatman's daughter. Turning this way and that before the mirror, she suddenly stopped smiling. Slowly, she removed the ornaments and put each away. Chen was puzzled. What had made her lose interest?

“You...” he began awkwardly. “You don't seem very happy.” “It's not that I'm unhappy. It's a pity, that's all.” “What's a pity?” he asked anxiously. “Surely you don't think a woman like you is unworthy of wearing such things?” “That isn't what I mean. ‘A capable woman doesn't keep wearing the clothes she married in.’ It's a pity you didn't buy these for me.” Her words struck him deeply. His thoughts turned over and over: how could he prove himself worthy of her? After remaining lost in thought for some time, he finally forced out an answer. “You have ambition, and so do I. But unless you're willing to share a few years of hardship with me, I shan't be able to buy you jewellery like this one day.” “Do you think I can't bear hardship? Wait and see!” “I believe you.” Chen smiled. “In truth, how could I bear to make you suffer? As things are, the business is going very smoothly, and we should live comfortably. We owe it all to Mr Hu's help.” “We must never forget how we began.” At last A’zhu voiced a heartfelt thought. “Our first duty is to look after every part of his business. Only then shall we deserve what he's done for us.” The night was late and still. Even though they spoke quietly, Hu could just hear them from his berth. He felt that he had managed this affair extremely well. Pleased with the result and proud of himself, he fell peacefully asleep, his mind free of care.
