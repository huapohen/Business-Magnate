谈完正事，一席盛宴，亦近尾声，端上来四样“压桌菜”，只好看看，倒是小碟子装的八样酱菜，一扫而空，胡雪岩喝了一碗香梗米粥，抚抚肚子站起来说：“我要告辞了，大概明天动身，不再来向各位辞行，等过了端午，我一定设法抽空，亲自来接芝香兄，那时候再叙吧！”潘叔雅还要留他多坐，吴季重和陆芝香又要请他吃晚饭。胡雪岩觉得对这班“大少爷”，不必过于迁就，所以一律托词拒绝，厚犒了潘家的婢仆，仍旧坐着那乘装饰华美的四人大轿出阊门。这时不过午后两点钟，胡雪岩一面在轿中闭目养神，一面在心里打算，这一下午只剩下一件事，就是立阿巧姐恢复自己之身的那张笔据，一杯茶的工夫就可了事。余下来的工夫，都可用来陪嵇鹤龄，等下进城，不妨到慕名已久，据说还是从明朝传下来的一家“孙春阳”南货店去看看。

By the time they finished discussing business, the sumptuous meal was nearly over. Four substantial dishes were brought out to conclude the feast, but they could do no more than look at them. The eight little plates of pickles, however, were swept clean. Hu Xueyan drank a bowl of fragrant rice porridge, patted his stomach, and rose. “I must take my leave. I shall probably set off tomorrow, so I will not come round to say goodbye again. After the Dragon Boat Festival, I shall certainly try to find time to come and escort Brother Zhixiang myself. We can talk further then.” Pan Shuya wanted him to stay a little longer, while Wu Jizhong and Lu Zhixiang both invited him to dinner. Hu felt there was no need to indulge these wealthy young gentlemen too much. He found excuses to decline all their invitations, tipped the Pan household’s maids and servants generously, and rode out through Chang Gate in the same splendid four-bearer sedan chair. It was only two in the afternoon. Resting with his eyes closed, he considered his plans. There was just one thing left to do that afternoon: have the document drawn up restoring A’qiao’s freedom. That would take no longer than a cup of tea. He could spend the rest of his time with Ji Heling. When he went into the city, he might also visit Sun Chunyang, the southern-goods shop he had long heard of, which was said to date from the Ming dynasty.

打算得倒是不错，不想那顶四人大轿害了他，阊门外是水陆要道，金间栈成了名符其实的“仕宦行台”，而苏州因为江宁失守，大衙门增多，所以候补的、求差的、公干的官员，平空也添了许多，近水楼台，都喜欢住在金阊栈，看见这顶四乘大轿，自然要打听轿中是哪位达官？胡雪岩性情随和，出手豪阔，金阊栈的伙计，无不巴结，于是加油添酱，为他大大吹嘘了一番，说他是浙江官场上的红人，在两江也很吃得开，许巡抚是小同乡，何学使是至交，亲自来看过他两次。总督佑大人派了戈什哈送过一桌燕菜席，这顶四人大轿是苏州城里第一阔少，一生下来就做了道台的潘大少爷派来的。把胡雪岩形容成了一个三头六臂、呼风唤雨的“通天教主”。恰好潘、吴、陆三家又讲究应酬，送路菜的送路菜，送土仪的送土仪，派来的又都是衣冠整齐的俊仆，这一下越显得胡雪岩交游广阔，伙计所言不虚。于是纷纷登门拜访，套交情，拉关系，甚至还有来告帮的，把个胡雪岩搞碍昏头搭脑，应接不暇。直到上灯时分，方始略得清静。

It was a sound enough plan, but the four-bearer chair proved his undoing. The district outside Chang Gate was a major junction of land and water routes, and Jinchang Inn had become a veritable residence for traveling officials. Since the fall of Jiangning, more major government offices had been established in Suzhou, bringing a corresponding increase in officials awaiting appointments, seeking commissions, or traveling on official business. For convenience, many preferred to stay at Jinchang Inn. The sight of the imposing sedan chair naturally set them asking what eminent official it carried. Hu’s easy manner and generous spending had won over every attendant at the inn. Eager to please him, they embellished his accomplishments without restraint. He was an influential figure in Zhejiang official circles, they said, and equally well connected in Liangjiang. Governor Xu came from the same locality as he did; Education Commissioner He was an intimate friend and had visited him twice in person. Governor-General Yi had sent a guardsman with a whole banquet of bird’s-nest dishes. The chair belonged to Young Master Pan, Suzhou’s richest young gentleman, who had held the rank of circuit intendant from the day he was born. They made Hu sound like the all-powerful Patriarch Tongtian, with three heads and six arms, able to command the winds and rain. As it happened, the Pan, Wu, and Lu families were all punctilious about social courtesies. They sent dishes for his journey and gifts of local produce, carried by handsome, smartly dressed servants. This seemed to confirm both the breadth of his connections and every word the attendants had said. Visitors streamed in to make his acquaintance, cultivate a connection, or even beg his assistance. Hu was overwhelmed and scarcely knew which way to turn. Only when the lamps were lit did he get a little peace.

“胡先生！”周一鸣提出警告：“你老在这里住不得了！”“是啊！”胡雪岩苦笑着说，“这不是无妄之灾？”“潘倒不是这样说。有人求还求不来这洋的场面，不过你老不喜欢这样子招摇。我看，搬进城去住吧！”“明天就要走了。一动不如一静，只我自己避开就是了。”好在最要紧的一件大事，已经办妥，于是胡雪岩带着阿巧姐的那张笔据，与周一鸣约了第二天再见，然后进城，一直去访嵇鹤龄。谈起这天潘叔雅的晚宴，嵇鹤龄大为惊奇，自然也替他高兴。“真正是‘富贵逼人来’！雪岩，我真想不到你会有这么多际遇！”

“Mr Hu,” Zhou Yiming warned, “you cannot go on staying here.” “Indeed I cannot,” Hu replied with a rueful smile. “Isn’t this a calamity I have done nothing to deserve?” “I would not quite put it that way. Some people could not attract this sort of attention however hard they tried. It is simply that you do not care for such a display. I think you should move to an inn inside the city.” “I am leaving tomorrow. Better to stay put than make another change. I need only keep out of the way myself.” Fortunately, the most important piece of business had already been settled. Taking the document concerning A’qiao with him, Hu arranged to meet Zhou again the next day, then went into the city and straight to Ji Heling’s lodgings. His account of that day’s banquet at Pan Shuya’s astonished Ji, who was naturally delighted for him. “Wealth and distinction really are forcing themselves upon you! Xueyan, I never imagined you would meet with so many opportunities.”

不过嵇鹤龄是读书人，总忘不了省察的工夫，看胡雪岩一帆风顺，种种意想不到的机缘，纷至沓来，不免为他忧虑，所以接下来便大谈持盈保泰的道理，劝他要有临深履簿的警惕，处处小心，一步走错不得。话是有点迂，但胡雪岩最佩服这位“大哥”，觉得语重心长，都是好话，一字一句，都记在心里。最后便谈到了彼此的行期。“动身的日子一改再改，上海也没有信来，我心里真是急得很！”胡雪岩问，“不知道大哥在苏州还有几天耽搁？如果只有一两天，我就索性等你一起走。”“不必。我的日子说不定。你先走吧！我们在杭州碰头。”“那也好！”胡雪岩说，“明天上午我要到孙春阳看一看，顺便买买东西。铁定下午开船。明天我就不来辞行。”“我也不送你的行。彼此两免。”桩鹤龄说，“提起孙春阳，我倒想起在杭州临走以前，听人谈起的一个故事，不妨讲给你听听。这个故事出在方裕和。”

Ji was a scholar, however, and never forgot the need for self-examination. Hu was enjoying such smooth sailing, with unforeseen opportunities presenting themselves one after another, that Ji could not help worrying about him. He went on at length about the need to preserve prosperity once it had been attained, urging Hu to proceed as warily as though approaching a deep abyss or treading on thin ice. He must be careful at every turn: one false step could not be afforded. The advice was somewhat pedantic, but Hu admired this elder brother above all others. He heard the affection and gravity behind the words, considered every one of them sound, and took them all to heart. At last they turned to their travel plans. “I keep putting back my departure, and there has been no letter from Shanghai. I am terribly anxious,” Hu said. “How many more days will you be in Suzhou, Elder Brother? If it is only a day or two, I might as well wait and travel with you.” “There is no need. My date is uncertain. You go first, and we shall meet in Hangzhou.” “Very well. Tomorrow morning I shall look round Sun Chunyang and buy a few things. I shall definitely sail in the afternoon, so I will not come to say goodbye tomorrow.” “And I shall not see you off. We can dispense with both formalities,” Ji replied. “Speaking of Sun Chunyang reminds me of a story I heard just before leaving Hangzhou. Let me tell you. It concerns Fang Yuhe.”

方裕和跟孙春阳一样，是一家极大的南北货行，方老板是有“徽骆驼”之称、专出典当朝奉的徽州人，刻苦耐劳，事必躬亲，所以生意做得蒸蒸日上，提起这一行业，在杭州城内首屈一指。哪知道从两年以前，开始发生货色走漏的毛病，而且走漏的是最贵重的海货、鱼翅、燕窝、于贝之类，方老板明查暗访，先在店里查，伙计中有谁手脚不干净？再到同行以及馆子里去查，看哪家吃进了来路不明的黑货？然而竟无线索可寻。到了最近，终于查到了，是偶然的发现，发现有毛病的是“火把”——用于竹子编扎的火炬，寸许直径三尺长，照例论捆卖，贵重的海货，就是藏在火把里，走漏出去的。方老板头脑很清楚，不能找买火把的顾客，说他勾结店中的伙计走私，因为顾客可以下承认，反咬一口，“诬良为盗”，还得吃官司。考虑结果，声色不动，那捆有挟带的火把，亦依旧摆在原处。

Like Sun Chunyang, Fang Yuhe was a very large dealer in goods from both northern and southern China. Its proprietor, Fang, came from Huizhou, a district famous for producing pawnshop appraisers, whose people were known as the “Huizhou camels” for their capacity for hard work and endurance. He attended to everything himself, and his business flourished until it ranked first in its trade in Hangzhou. Yet two years earlier goods had begun to disappear, and the missing stock consisted of the most expensive delicacies: shark’s fins, edible bird’s nests, dried scallops, and the like. Fang investigated both openly and in secret. First he examined his own staff to discover who might be light-fingered. Then he made inquiries among other dealers and restaurants, looking for anyone who had bought stolen goods of uncertain origin. He found no trace. Only recently had he discovered the method, quite by chance. The trouble lay in the torches: bundles of bamboo bound together, about a cun across and three chi long, normally sold by the bundle. The valuable delicacies were hidden inside these and carried out of the shop. Fang kept his head. He could not simply accuse a customer buying torches of conspiring with his staff to steal goods. The customer could deny it and counter by accusing him of falsely branding an honest man a thief, leaving Fang facing a lawsuit. After considering the matter, he betrayed nothing and left the bundle containing the stolen goods where it was.

不久，有入来买人把，去接待“顾客”的，是他最信任的一名伙计，也是方老板的同宗，不但能干，而且诚实。这一下方老板困惑了，这个人忠诚可靠，决不会是他走私。也许误打误撞，一时巧合，决定看一看再说。过了几天，又发现火把中有私货，这次来买火把的是另一个人，但接待的却仍是那方姓伙计。这就不会是巧合了，他派了个小徒弟，暗中跟踪那名“顾客”，一跟跟到漕船上。这就很容易明白了，怪不得本地查不出，私货都由漕船带到外埠去了。于是有一天，方老板把他那同宗的伙计找来，悄悄地问道：“你在漕船上，有朋友没有？”“没有。”说是这样说，神色之间，微微一惊，方老板心里明白，事无可疑了，如今要想的是处置的办法。谈到这里，嵇鹤龄问道：“雪岩，换了你做方老板，如何处置？”

Before long someone came to buy torches. The clerk who attended to this “customer” was one of Fang’s most trusted employees, a member of his own clan, both capable and honest. Fang was puzzled. Surely so loyal and reliable a man could not be the thief? Perhaps he had merely happened to serve the customer by chance. Fang decided to wait and see. A few days later he found stolen goods in the torches again. This time a different man came to buy them, but the same clerk named Fang attended to him. That could no longer be coincidence. Fang sent a young apprentice to follow the “customer” in secret, and the boy trailed him all the way to a grain-transport boat. The explanation was now plain. No wonder nothing had been found locally: the stolen goods were being carried to other towns aboard the grain boats. One day Fang called his kinsman aside and quietly asked, “Do you have any friends on the grain boats?” “No.” So he said, but a slight look of alarm crossed his face. Fang now had no doubt. The question was what to do about it. At this point Ji asked, “Xueyan, if you had been the proprietor, how would you have handled the matter?”

“南北货这一行，我不大熟悉。不过看这样子，店里总还有同伙勾结。”“是的，有同伙勾结。”胡雪岩略想一想说：“南北货行的规矩，我虽不懂，待人接物的道理是一样的。我有我的处置办法，你先说，那方老板当时怎么样？”方老板认为他这个同宗走私，能够两年之久，不被发觉，是个相当有本事的人，同时这件事既有同伙勾结，闹出来则于信誉有损，而且势必要开除一班熟手，生意亦有影响，所以决定重用此人，升他的职位，加他的薪水。这一来，那方伙计感恩图报，自然就不会再有什么偷漏的弊病发生。听嵇鹤龄讲完，胡雪岩点点头说：“那个老板的想法不错，做法还差一点。”嵇鹤龄大为诧异，在他觉得方老板的处置，已经尽善尽美，不想在胡雪岩看，还有可批评之处，倒有些替方老板不服气。“噢！我倒要看看，还有什么更好的办法？”

“I do not know much about the northern- and southern-goods trade. But from the sound of it, there must have been accomplices in the shop.” “Yes, there were.” Hu considered for a moment. “I may not know the rules of the trade, but the principles of dealing with people are the same. I have my own way of handling it. First tell me what Fang did.” Fang reasoned that a man who could carry on stealing for two years without being discovered must be exceptionally capable. Besides, since others were involved, exposing the affair would damage the firm’s reputation. He would also have to dismiss a number of experienced employees, which would hurt the business. He therefore decided to give the man greater responsibility, promote him, and raise his pay. Grateful for this kindness and eager to repay it, the clerk naturally put an end to the thefts. When Ji finished, Hu nodded. “The proprietor had the right idea, but his way of carrying it out could have been better.” Ji was astonished. He had thought Fang’s handling of the matter could scarcely be improved upon. To hear Hu still find fault made him feel rather indignant on Fang’s behalf. “Oh? Then I should like to hear what better method you have.”

“做贼是不能拆穿的！一拆穿，无论如何会落个痕迹，怎么样也相处不长的。我放句话在这里，留待后验，方老板的那个同宗，至多一年工夫，一定不会再做下去。”“嗯，嗯！”嵇鹤龄觉得有些道理了，“那么，莫非不闻不问？”“这怎么可以？”胡雪岩说，“照我的做法，只要暗中查明白了，根本不说破，就升他的职位，加他的薪水，叫他专管查察偷漏。莫非他再监守自盗？”“对！”嵇鹤龄很兴奋他说，“果然，你比哪个生意人都高明。‘羚羊挂角，无迹可寻’，这才是入于化境了。”“不过话要说回来，除非那个人真正有本事，不然，这样；做法，流弊极大、变成奖励做贼。所以我的话也不过是纸上谈兵。大哥，”他说，“我常常在想到你跟我说过的那句话：”用兵之妙，存乎一心‘！做生意跟带兵打仗的道理是差不多的，只有看人行事，看事说话，随机应变之外，还要从变化中找出机会来！那才是一等一的本事。““我看你也就差不多这个本事了。”嵇鹤龄又不胜惋惜地说，“你就是少读两句书。”说到此事，胡雪岩只有摇头，嵇鹤龄倒是想劝他折节读书，但想想他那样子忙法，何来读书的工夫？也就只好不作声了。

“You must never unmask a thief. Once you do, it leaves a mark that cannot be rubbed away, and however you manage things, you cannot continue working together for long. I will make a prediction now, and time will test it: that kinsman of Fang’s will leave within a year at most.” “Hmm.” Ji began to see some sense in this. “Then would you simply turn a blind eye?” “How could I?” Hu replied. “My method would be to establish the facts in secret, then promote him and raise his pay without ever saying I knew. I would put him in sole charge of detecting theft. Would he still steal the very goods he was appointed to guard?” “Exactly!” Ji exclaimed. “You really are cleverer than any other merchant. ‘The antelope hangs by its horns and leaves no trace.’ That is mastery so complete that the art disappears.” “Still, there is another side to it. Unless the man really has ability, the consequences could be very bad: it would amount to rewarding theft. So what I have said is only an exercise in strategy on paper. Elder Brother, I often think of something you once told me: ‘The art of command lies in the commander’s mind.’ Business and warfare are much alike. You must act according to the person and speak according to the situation. Beyond adapting to changes, you must find opportunities within them. That is the highest skill of all.” “I think you have very nearly reached it,” Ji said, then added with deep regret, “If only you had read a few more books.” Hu could only shake his head. Ji wanted to urge him to devote himself seriously to study, but, considering how busy he was, where could he find the time? There was nothing more to say.

到了第二天，刚刚起身，又有个浙江到江苏来公差的佐杂官儿，投帖来拜。胡雪岩一看这情形，果真应了周一鸣的话。此地不能再住了，因此托客栈去通知他的船老大，当天下午启程，自己匆匆忙忙避了出去，临走时留下话，如果周一鸣来了，叫他到城内吴苑茶馆相会，不见不散。坐上轿子，自觉好笑，世间的麻烦，有时是意想不到的，自己最不愿做官，偏偏有人拿官派套上头来，这是哪里说起？自然，他也有些懊恼，一清早在自己住处存不住身，想想真有些不甘心。这样怏怏然进了城，便觉意兴阑珊，只在吴苑喝茶，听隔座茶客大谈时事。那人是浓重的湖南口音，相当难懂，而且声音甚大，说话的神态，亦颇不雅，指手画脚，口沫横飞，胡雪岩深为不耐。但看他周围的那些听众，无不聚精会神，十分注意，不由得有些好奇，也耐着心细听。

The next morning Hu had scarcely risen when a minor official from Zhejiang, visiting Jiangsu on government business, sent in his card. Zhou Yiming had been right: he could no longer stay here. He asked the inn to notify his boatman that he would leave that afternoon, then hurried out to escape. Before going, he left instructions that if Zhou came, he should be sent to meet him at Wuyuan Teahouse inside the city. Hu would wait until he arrived. Once in his sedan chair, he found the situation almost laughable. Life could produce the most unexpected troubles. He was the last man who wanted an official career, yet people insisted on treating him as though he possessed all an official’s importance. What was one to make of that? He was also irritated, of course. To be driven out of his own lodgings first thing in the morning was difficult to accept. He entered the city in this disgruntled mood and lost interest in doing anything. He sat drinking tea at Wuyuan, listening to a man nearby hold forth on current affairs. The speaker’s thick Hunan accent was hard to follow. He spoke very loudly, gesticulating and spraying saliva in a thoroughly ungraceful manner, and Hu found him exasperating. Yet everyone around the man was listening with rapt attention. Curiosity got the better of Hu, and he made himself listen carefully too.

慢侵听懂了，是谈曾国藩在湖南省城长沙城外六十里的靖港，吃了败仗，愤而投水，为人所救的情形。湖南的藩司徐有王、臬司陶恩培本来就嫌曾国藩是丁忧在籍的侍郎，无端多事，办什么团练，分了他们的权柄，所以会衔申详巡抚骆秉章，请求出奏弹劾曾国藩，同时遣散他的部队。骆秉章还算是个明白人，而且他刚请到一位襄办军务的湘阴名士左宗棠，认为曾国藩已经上奏自劾，不可以再落井下石，而且战事正紧，也不是裁军的时候，所以骆秉章断然拒绝了徐、陶两人的要求。哪知就在第二天，归曾国藩节制的长沙协副将塔齐布。败太平军于湘潭。湖南的提督鲍起豹，上奏自陈战功，朝廷拿曾国藩自动与鲍起豹表功的奏招一比较，知道吃败仗的应该奖励，“打胜仗”的根本不曾出兵，于是一道上谕，免了鲍起豹的官，塔齐布则以副将越过总兵这一阶，超擢为指挥一省绿营的湖南提督。

Gradually he understood. The man was describing Zeng Guofan’s defeat at Jinggang, sixty li outside Changsha, the Hunan provincial capital. In his anguish Zeng had thrown himself into the water, but had been rescued. Hunan’s provincial treasurer, Xu Youwang, and judicial commissioner, Tao Enpei, had already resented him. Here was a vice-minister living at home in mourning for a parent: why must he meddle by organizing militia forces and encroaching on their authority? They jointly petitioned Governor Luo Bingzhang to impeach Zeng and disband his troops. Luo was a sensible man, and had recently engaged Zuo Zongtang, a distinguished scholar from Xiangyin, to assist with military affairs. Zeng had already submitted a memorial impeaching himself, Luo reasoned; it would be wrong to kick a man when he was down. Nor, with the fighting so urgent, was this the time to reduce the army. Luo firmly rejected Xu and Tao’s request. The very next day, however, Taqibu, deputy commander of the Changsha brigade serving under Zeng, defeated the Taiping forces at Xiangtan. Hunan’s provincial commander, Bao Qibao, submitted a memorial claiming the victory for himself. Comparing Zeng’s self-impeachment with Bao’s claim to military glory, the court saw that the man who had lost a battle deserved reward, while the man who had “won” had never even sent out troops. An imperial edict dismissed Bao. Taqibu was promoted from deputy brigade commander directly to Hunan provincial commander, skipping the rank of brigade commander and taking command of the province’s Green Standard forces.

部将尚且如此，主帅的地位决不会动摇，自可想可知。徐有王和陶恩培大为不安，深怕曾国藩记仇，或者塔齐布要为他出气，随便找他们一个错处，参上一本，朝廷一定准奏。因而两个人约好了，到长沙南门外高峰寺，曾国藩驻节之处，磕头道贺兼道歉。这是一大快事，听的人无不抚掌，“曾恃郎吃了这个败仗，反而站住脚了。”那人说道，“士气反比从前好，都是朝廷明见万里，赏罚公平的缘故。”“正是，正是！‘”好些人异口同声地附和。由此开始，谈话便乱了，你一言，我一语，胡雪岩只觉得意气激昂，心里暗暗在想：真叫“公道自在人心”，看样子洪杨的局面难以久长。一旦战局结束，抚辑流亡，百废俱举，那时有些什么生意好做？得空倒要好好想它一想，须抢在人家前面，才有大钱可赚。于是海阔天空地胡恩乱想，及至警觉，自己不免好笑，想得太远了！再抬头看时，茶客寥寥无几，早市已经落场，辰光近午，周一鸣不知何以未来？这一上午就此虚耗，胡雪岩叹口气站起身来，付过茶帐，决定到孙春阳去买了土产，回客栈整顿行装上船。

If a subordinate received such advancement, his commander’s position must plainly be secure. Xu Youwang and Tao Enpei became deeply uneasy. They feared Zeng might bear a grudge, or Taqibu might seek to avenge him. Either could find some fault with them and submit an impeachment that the court would surely uphold. The two therefore arranged to visit Zeng at his headquarters in Gaofeng Temple outside Changsha’s south gate, where they kowtowed, offering congratulations and apologies together. It was a most satisfying story, and the listeners all applauded. “Vice-Minister Zeng’s defeat has actually secured his position,” the man said. “His troops’ morale is better than before, thanks to the court’s far-seeing judgment and its fairness in rewards and punishments.” “Exactly! Exactly!” several voices agreed. Then the conversation broke up into a confusion of remarks. Hu felt his spirits rise. People truly did have a sense of justice, he thought. From the look of things, the regime of Hong and Yang could not last. Once the war ended, the displaced would need to be resettled and every neglected enterprise revived. What business opportunities would there be then? He must think it through when he had a moment. To make a great deal of money, he would have to move before everyone else. His thoughts roamed far and wide until he caught himself and laughed: he was getting much too far ahead. Looking up, he found only a few customers left. The morning’s tea trade was over, and it was nearly noon. Why had Zhou still not come? He had wasted the entire morning. With a sigh, he rose and paid his bill, deciding to buy some local specialties at Sun Chunyang, then return to the inn, pack, and board his boat.

刚走出吴苑，劈面遇着周一鸣，彼此叫应，胡雪岩问道。“哪里来？”“我从闸门来。”周一鸣答道：“一早先到潘家去看阿巧姐，约好明天上午到木读。阿巧姐要我陪她到金间栈，才知道你老进城了。”“喔，那么阿巧姐呢？”“她在客栈里收拾东西，叫我来接胡先生。”周一呜说，“听客栈里的人说，你老今天动身，所以有些行李已经发到船上去了。”“噢。”胡雪岩问道：“孙春阳在哪里，你知道不知道？”“知道。在吴趋坊。”于是周一鸣领路，安步当车到了吴趋坊以北的孙春阳，门口一株台抱不交的大树，光秃秃的却有几枝新芽，证明不是桔树。周一鸣告诉胡雪岩说，这株老树还是明朝留下来的，此地原是唐伯虎读书之处。

He had just stepped out of Wuyuan when he met Zhou Yiming face to face. They greeted each other, and Hu asked, “Where have you come from?” “Chang Gate. I went to the Pan household first thing this morning to see Sister A’qiao. We arranged to go to Mudu tomorrow morning. She asked me to escort her to Jinchang Inn, and only there did we learn that you had come into the city.” “Oh? Where is she now?” “At the inn, packing. She sent me to fetch you. When she heard from the attendants that you were leaving today, she had some of the luggage sent aboard already.” “I see. Do you know where Sun Chunyang is?” “Yes, in Wuqufang.” Zhou led the way, and they walked at an easy pace to the shop just north of Wuqufang. A huge tree stood outside, too broad for a man to encircle with his arms. Its bare branches bore a few new shoots, proving that it was not dead. Zhou told Hu that the tree dated from the Ming dynasty, and that Tang Bohu had once studied on this spot.

胡雪岩对这个古迹，不感兴趣，感兴趣的是孙春阳的那块招牌，泥金的底子，已经发黑，“孙春阳”三字，亦不甚看得清楚，然而店它却有朝气，一眼望去，各司其事，敏捷肃穆。有个白胡子老头，捧着管水烟袋，站在店堂中间，左右顾眼，拿着手里的纸媒儿，指东指西，在指挥伙计、学徒招呼客人。奇怪的是有顾客，不见货色，顾客交易，付了钱手持一张小票，往后走去，不知是何花样？“孙春阳的规矩是这样，”周一鸣为他解释，“办事分六房，下是衙门里吏、户、礼、兵、刑、工六帚，是南货、北货、海货、腌腊、蜜饯、蜡烛六房。前面付钱开票，到后面凭票取货。”“顾客看不见货色，怎么挑？或者货色不合，怎么办？”“用不着挑的，说啥就是啥，货真价实。”周一鸣说：“孙春阳做出牌子，货色最道地，斤两最足，老少无欺。如果这里的货色不满意，就没有再好的货色了。”“牌子做到这么硬，倒也不是件容易的事。”

Hu had no interest in the historic site. What interested him was Sun Chunyang’s signboard. Its gilt ground had darkened, and the three characters of the name were barely legible, yet the shop itself was full of life. At a glance he saw everyone attending to his duties, briskly and solemnly. A white-bearded old man stood in the middle of the shop holding a water pipe. Looking from side to side, he pointed with the paper spill in his hand, directing clerks and apprentices as they served customers. The curious thing was that there were customers but no goods in sight. A customer would make a purchase, pay, and then walk toward the back with a small slip of paper. What was the arrangement? “That is Sun Chunyang’s system,” Zhou explained. “The business is divided into six departments. Not Personnel, Revenue, Rites, War, Punishments, and Works, as in government, but southern goods, northern goods, delicacies from the sea, cured meats, preserved fruits, and candles. You pay at the front and get a ticket, then collect your goods at the back.” “But if customers cannot see the goods, how do they choose? What if they are unsuitable?” “There is no need to choose. You get exactly what you ask for: genuine goods at fair prices. Sun Chunyang has earned its reputation. Its goods are the finest, its weights are full, and it cheats neither young nor old. If you are dissatisfied here, you will not find anything better elsewhere.” “It cannot have been easy to build such an unassailable reputation.”

于是胡雪岩亲自上柜，买的是茶食和蜡烛，也买了几条火腿，预备带回杭州跟金华人腿去比较优劣。付款开票，到货房交涉。要店里送到金阎栈。孙春阳的牌子真是“硬”，说是没有为客送货的规矩，婉词拒绝。“这就不对了！”胡雪岩悄悄对周一鸣说：“店规不是死板板的。有些事不可通融，有些事要改良，世界日日在变，从前没有外国人，现在有外国人，这就是变。做生意贵乎随机应变。孙春阳从明朝传到现在，是因为明朝下来，一直没有怎么变，现在不同了，海禁大开，时势大变，如果还是那一套几百年传下来的古规矩，一成不变，我看，孙春阳这块招牌也维持不久了。”周一鸣也觉得大宗货色，店家不送，是件说不通的事。听了胡雪岩的话，心里好好体会了一番，因为他晓得这是胡雪岩在教导，以后跟着他做生意，得要记住他这番话，随机应变，处处为顾客打算。

Hu went to the counter himself and bought tea snacks and candles, along with several hams that he intended to take back to Hangzhou and compare with Jinhua hams. Having paid and received his ticket, he went to the appropriate department and asked for his purchases to be delivered to Jinchang Inn. Sun Chunyang proved unassailable in another sense too. They politely refused: delivering purchases to customers was not their practice. “That is a mistake,” Hu said quietly to Zhou. “A shop’s rules should not be rigid. Some things allow no compromise; others need improvement. The world changes every day. Once there were no foreigners here; now there are. That is change. What matters in business is the ability to adapt. Sun Chunyang has survived from the Ming dynasty because until now very little has changed since those days. Things are different today. The restrictions on maritime trade have been thrown open, and circumstances are changing profoundly. If they cling unchanged to the same old rules handed down for hundreds of years, I doubt this famous name will survive much longer.” Zhou also thought it unreasonable for a shop to refuse delivery of such a large purchase. He carefully considered Hu’s words, knowing that Hu was teaching him. When he followed him into business, he must remember this lesson: adapt to circumstances and always consider the customer’s needs.

照胡雪岩的打算，本想在城里吃了午饭再回金阊栈，现在因为有几大篓的茶食之类的拖累，不得不雇个挑伕，押着出城。到了金阊栈，只见阿巧姐已将他的箱笼什物，收拾得整整齐齐， 堆在一边，只等船家来取。于是唤来金阊栈的伙计，一面准备午饭，一面吩咐结帐。等吃了饭，付过帐，阿巧姐送胡雪岩到船上，送到船上，却又说时候还早，不妨坐一回。周一鸣知趣，托词避到岸上去了。胡雪岩归心如箭，急待开船，但阿巧姐不走，却不便下逐客令。看她站在那里，默然有所思的神气，又不免诧异，当即问道：“可是还有话要跟我说？”阿巧姐在想心事，一时未听清他的话，眨着眼强笑道：“你说啥？”“我说：你是不是还有话要跟我说？”“话？”她迟疑了一下，“又象有，又象没有。”这就是说，不过不忍舍去，想再坐一会。胡雪岩觉得她的态度奇怪，不弄弄清楚，一路回去，想起来心里就会有个疙瘩，所以自己先坐了下来，歪身过去，拉开一张骨牌凳，示意她也坐下。

Hu had intended to have lunch in the city before returning to Jinchang Inn. Now, encumbered with several large baskets of snacks and other purchases, he had to hire a porter and accompany the load out of the city. At the inn he found that A’qiao had packed his trunks and belongings neatly and stacked them to one side, ready for the boatmen to collect. He called an attendant, ordering lunch and asking for his account to be prepared. After the meal he paid the bill, and A’qiao escorted him to the boat. Once aboard, however, she said it was still early and she might as well sit for a while. Zhou tactfully found an excuse to go ashore. Hu was eager to be home and impatient to set off, but with A’qiao still there he could hardly order her away. Her silent, preoccupied air puzzled him. “Is there something else you want to tell me?” he asked. Absorbed in her own thoughts, she had not quite heard. She blinked and forced a smile. “What did you say?” “I asked whether you had something else to tell me.” “Something to tell you?” She hesitated. “Perhaps I have. Perhaps I haven’t.” What she meant was merely that she could not bear to leave and wanted to stay a little longer. Hu found her manner strange. Unless he understood it, he knew the puzzle would trouble him all the way home. He sat down first, leaned sideways, and pulled out an oblong stool, indicating that she should sit too.

一个是在等她开口，一个是在找话好说，想来想去，想到有件事要问：“昨天，潘家三少请你吃饭，到底为啥？是托你在上海买地皮、造房子？”“你已经晓得了。”“晓是晓得，不太清楚。”于是胡雪岩很扼要地把昨天聚晤的情形，约略说了一遍。“照这样说，你过了节还要到苏州来？”“不一定，要着我有没有工夫。我看是来不成功的，将来总是让老周辛苦一趟。”“那时候……，”阿巧姐说，“我不晓得在哪里？”这是变相的询问，问她自己的行止归宿？胡雪岩便说：“到那时候，我想一定有好消息了。”“好消息？”阿巧姐问：“什么好消息？”这是很明白的，自然是指何桂清筑金屋，胡雪岩不知道她是明知而装傻，还是真的没有想到？心里不免略有反感，便懒得理她，笑笑而已。

He waited for her to speak while she searched for something to say. At last a question occurred to her. “Why exactly did Young Master Pan invite you to lunch yesterday? Was he asking you to buy land and build houses in Shanghai?” “So you know already.” “I know something about it, but not very clearly.” Hu gave her a brief account of their meeting. “Then you will be coming back to Suzhou after the festival?” “Not necessarily. It depends on whether I have time. I doubt I shall manage it; I shall probably have to ask Old Zhou to make the journey.” “By then…” A’qiao said. “I wonder where I shall be?” This was an indirect inquiry about her own future: where was she to go, and with whom would she settle? Hu replied, “By then, I think there is sure to be good news.” “Good news? What good news?” The meaning was plain enough. He was naturally referring to He Guiqing’s setting her up in a home of her own. Hu could not tell whether she was pretending not to understand or had really failed to think of it. He felt a flicker of irritation and could not be bothered to explain. He merely smiled.

“有工夫，你最好自己来！”“为什么呢？”“到那时候，我也许有话要跟你说。”“什么话？何不此刻就说？”“自然还不到时候。”阿巧姐又说，“也许有，也许没有，到时候再说。”言词闪烁，越发启人疑窦。胡雪岩很冷静地将她前后的话和恋恋不舍的神态，合在一起来想，终于明白了她的心思。此刻她还在彷徨，一只手已经抓住了那一何，这一只手却还不肯放弃这一胡。然而这倒不是她取巧，无非这几日相处，易生感情，遽难割舍罢了。意会到此，自己觉得应该有个表示，但亦不宜过于决绝，徒然刺伤她的心，所以用恳切规劝的语气说道：“你不要胡思乱想了！终身已定，只等着享福就是了。”“唉！”阿巧狙忽然幽幽地叹了口气，“啥地方来的天官赐？”胡雪岩一愣，旋即明白，苏州人好说缩脚语，“天官赐”是隐个“福”字，于是笑道：“你真是得福不知，好了，好了，”他摆出不愿再提此事的神态，“你请上岸吧！我叫老周送你回去。”

“If you have time, you had better come yourself.” “Why?” “I may have something to tell you then.” “What? Why not tell me now?” “Because the time has not come yet.” A’qiao added, “Perhaps I shall, perhaps I shan’t. We shall see when the time comes.” Her evasiveness only deepened his suspicions. Hu calmly put her remarks together with her reluctance to leave, and at last understood what was in her mind. She was still wavering. One hand had already taken hold of He, while the other was unwilling to let go of Hu. Yet this was not a clever attempt to gain an advantage. They had spent these days together, affection had grown easily, and she could not cut it off all at once. Understanding this, Hu felt he should make his position clear, but not so harshly as to wound her needlessly. In a tone of earnest persuasion, he said, “You must stop letting your thoughts run away with you. Your future is settled. All you need do now is enjoy your good fortune.” “Ah,” A’qiao sighed softly. “Where am I to find any ‘Heavenly Official Bestows’?” Hu stared, then understood. Suzhou people liked to leave the last word of a familiar phrase unspoken. “The Heavenly Official Bestows” omitted the word “blessings.” He smiled. “You really do not know how fortunate you are. There, there.” His manner showed that he wished to discuss it no further. “Please go ashore. I shall have Old Zhou take you back.”

“还早！”阿巧姐不肯走，同时倒真的想起一些话，要在这时候跟胡雪岩说。算了，算了！胡雪岩在心里说，多的日子也过去了，何争这一下午？倒要看看她，究竟有些什么花样。所以索性取出孙春阳买的松子糖之类的茶食，一包包打开，摆满了一桌子说：“你慢慢吃着谈。”阿巧姐笑了，“有点生我的气，是不是？”“我改了主意了。今天不走！”胡雪岩又说，“不但请吃零食，还要请你吃了晚饭再走。”“这还不是气话？”“好了，好了！”胡雪岩怕真的引起误会，“我怎么会生你的气，而且也没有什么可气的。你一定还有许多话，趁我未走以前，尽量说吧！”

“It is still early!” A’qiao refused to go. Besides, she now really had remembered some things she wanted to say to him. Never mind, Hu told himself. So many days have passed already; why begrudge her one afternoon? He would see what she was up to. He took out the pine-nut sweets and other tea snacks he had bought at Sun Chunyang, opened the packets one by one, and covered the table with them. “Take your time. You can eat while you talk.” A’qiao smiled. “You are a little annoyed with me, aren’t you?” “I have changed my mind. I am not leaving today. I shall give you dinner before you go, as well as these snacks.” “And you say you are not speaking in anger?” “There, there!” Afraid she might really misunderstand, Hu added, “How could I be angry with you? There is nothing to be angry about. You must still have a great deal to say. Tell me everything while I am here.”

“这倒是真话，我要托你带两句话到上海。”阿巧姐拈了颗杨梅脯放在嘴里，“请你跟二小姐说……”说什么呢？欲言又止，令人不耐，胡雪岩催问着：“怎么样，要跟老二说啥？”“我倒问你，尤五少府上到底怎么样？”阿巧姐补了一句：“我是说尤五奶奶，是不是管五少管得很紧？”问到这话，胡雪岩便不必等她再往下说，就明白了她的意思，“你是想劝者二，跟尤五少说一说，让他接口家去，是不是？”他问。“是啊！外面借小房也不是一回事。”“这件事，用不着你操心，有七姑奶奶在那里，从中自会安排。”胡雪岩说，“五奶奶人最贤慧，不管尤五少的事。”“那么，为什么不早早办了喜事呢？”这自然是因为尤五的境况，并不顺遂，无心来办喜事。不过这话不必跟阿巧姐说，他只这样答道：“我倒没有问过他，不知是何缘故。我把你的话带给老二就是了。”

“That is true. I want you to take a message to Shanghai.” A’qiao picked up a preserved yangmei berry and put it in her mouth. “Please tell Second Miss…” Tell her what? Her hesitation was irritating. “Well?” Hu prompted. “What do you want to tell her?” “Let me ask you something. What exactly are things like in Young Master You’s household? I mean Mrs You. Does she keep him on a very tight rein?” Before she could go further, Hu understood. “You want to advise Second Miss to ask him to take her into his household, don’t you?” “Yes. Keeping her in a rented place outside is hardly a proper arrangement.” “You need not worry about that. The Seventh Lady is there; she will arrange things between them. Mrs You is a most accommodating wife and does not interfere with his affairs.” “Then why have they not celebrated the marriage already?” The reason, of course, was that You’s circumstances had not been going well, and he was in no mood for a wedding. There was no need to tell A’qiao this. Hu merely replied, “I have not asked him, so I do not know why. I shall pass your message on to Second Miss.”

说到这里，只见舱门外探进一个人来，是船老大来催开船，说是天色将晚，水关一闭，就得明天早晨才能动身。“不要紧，”胡雪岩说，“我有何学台的名片，可以‘讨夫’。”这意思是只等阿巧姐一走，哪怕水关闭了，他也要开船。意会到此，她实在不能再逗留了，便站起身来说：“我要走了！”胡雪岩也不留，一面派人上岸招呼周一鸣来接，一面送客。等阿巧人袅袅娜娜地上了岸，船老大油去跳板，正侍开船，忽然周一鸣奔了来，大声喊道，“慢慢，慢慢！”胡雪岩就站在舱门口，随即问道：“还有什么话？”“阿巧姐有个戒指，掉在船里了。”

Just then a head appeared at the cabin door. It was the boatman, urging them to depart. It was getting late, he said; once the water gate closed, they could not leave until morning. “Never mind,” Hu replied. “I have Education Commissioner He’s visiting card. I can request official assistance.” The implication was that as soon as A’qiao left, he intended to sail, even if the water gate had closed. Once she understood that, she really could not linger any longer. She rose. “I am going.” Hu did not ask her to stay. He sent someone ashore to fetch Zhou while he saw her off. When she had stepped gracefully onto the bank, the boatman withdrew the gangplank and was preparing to cast off when Zhou came running. “Wait! Wait!” he shouted. Hu, who was standing at the cabin door, asked at once, “What else is it?” “Sister A’qiao has dropped a ring on the boat.”

于是重新搭起跳板，让阿巧姐上船，胡雪岩问她，是掉了怎么样的一个戒指？她支支吾吾地，只是在般板中低头寻找。这就令人可疑了。胡雪岩故意不理，不说话也不帮她找，只站着不动。他是出于好玩的心理，要看她如何落场？阿巧姐却以为胡雪岩是看出她说假话，心中不快，有意造成僵局，不免有些恼羞成怒了。于是，她仰起身子站定脚，用女孩子赌气的那种声音说：“寻不着这个戒指，我不走！”说完，气鼓鼓地坐了下来，眼睛偏到一旁去望，是气胡雪岩漠不相关的态度。这让他诧异了，莫非真的掉了一个戒指？看样子是自己弄错了。因而赔笑说道：“你又不曾说明白，是怎样一个戒指，我想帮你寻，也无从寻起。”这话道理欠通，阿巧姐便驳他：“戒指总是戒指，一定要说明白了，你才肯劳动贵手，帮我去寻？”

The gangplank was put back and A’qiao came aboard again. Hu asked what sort of ring she had lost. She gave evasive answers and kept her head down, searching the deck. It looked suspicious. Deliberately ignoring her, Hu stood still, neither speaking nor helping her search. He found it amusing and wanted to see how she would bring the performance to an end. A’qiao, however, thought he had seen through her lie, was displeased, and was deliberately creating an impasse. Her embarrassment began to turn to anger. She straightened up, planted her feet, and declared with a girl’s petulance, “I will not leave until I find that ring!” Then she sat down sulkily and looked away, furious at his indifference. This surprised him. Had she really lost a ring? Perhaps he had misjudged her. With an apologetic smile he said, “You have not told me what sort of ring it is. Even if I wanted to help, I would not know what to look for.” The reasoning was weak, and A’qiao challenged it. “A ring is a ring. Must I describe it exactly before you will trouble your precious hands to help me?”

“好，好！”胡雪岩摇摇手说：“我都要走了。何必还斗两句口。”他定神想了想，只有用“快刀斩乱麻”的办法，“走，我们上岸！”“上岸？”阿巧姐愕然相问：“到哪里去？”“进城。”胡雪岩说，“你的戒指也不要寻了，我赔你一个，到珠宝店里，你自己去挑。”这一下就象下象棋“将军”，一下子拿阿巧姐“将”住了，不知如何应付？支支吾吾地答道：“算了，算了，我也不要你赔。”胡雪岩回答得极快：“那也就不要寻了！你就再坐一会儿，让老周送你回潘家。我到了上海，自会写信给你。”能够再与胡雪岩相聚片刻，而且又听得这样一句话，她觉得也可满意了，所以刚才那种绷紧了脸的神情，不知不觉的消失，重重的钉了一句：“你自己说的，要写信来！看你守不守信用。”

“All right, all right!” Hu waved a hand. “I am about to leave. Why must we have a quarrel as well?” He steadied himself and considered. The only solution was to cut the tangle with one stroke. “Come, let us go ashore.” “Ashore?” A’qiao asked in surprise. “Where are we going?” “Into the city. Stop looking for your ring. I shall replace it. You can choose one yourself at a jeweler’s.” It was like a sudden cry of “Check!” in a game of chess: A’qiao was trapped and did not know how to respond. She faltered, “Never mind. I do not want you to replace it.” Hu answered instantly. “Then stop looking. Sit a little longer, and let Old Zhou take you back to the Pan household. I shall write to you when I reach Shanghai.” A little more time with him, and a promise like that, were enough to satisfy her. The tense look on her face faded without her noticing. She made certain of his promise. “You said it yourself: you will write. We shall see whether you keep your word.”

“一定会守。我自己没有空写信，请古大少写，或者请七姑奶奶写。”“七姑奶奶通文墨？”“好得很呢！她肚子里着实有些墨水。”胡雪岩说，“我都不及她。”这在阿巧姐听来，好象是件极新鲜有趣的事，“真看不出！”她还有些不信似的，“七姑奶奶那副样子，不象是通文墨的人。”“你是说地不够‘文气’是不是？”胡雪岩说：“人不可貌相！七姑奶奶的为人行事，另有一格，你们做梦都想不到的。”接着，他讲了七姑奶奶的那段“ 妙事”，有意灌醉了古应春，诬赖他“酒后乱性”，以至于逼得古应春指天发誓，一定要娶七姑奶奶，决不负心。阿巧姐听得目瞪口呆，“这真正是新闻了。哪里有这样子做事的？”她说、“女人的名节最重，真有这样的事还要撇清，没有这样的事，自己拿烂泥抹了一脸。这位七姑奶奶的心思，真是异出异样！”“是啊，她的心思异出异样。不过厉害也真厉害，不是这样，如何叫老古服服帖帖？”胡雪岩掉了一句文：“欲有所取，先有所予，七姑奶奶的做法是对的。”

“I certainly shall. If I have no time to write myself, I shall ask Young Master Gu, or the Seventh Lady.” “The Seventh Lady can read and write?” “Very well indeed. She has a good deal of learning. More than I do.” A’qiao found this a fascinating discovery. “You would never guess!” She still sounded a little doubtful. “The way she looks, she does not seem a literary sort of person.” “You mean she lacks a scholarly air? You must not judge by appearances. The Seventh Lady has a way of doing things entirely her own. You could never dream of it.” He told her the Seventh Lady’s remarkable exploit: deliberately getting Gu Yingchun drunk, then falsely claiming that he had slept with her while drunk, until he was forced to swear by heaven that he would marry her and never prove faithless. A’qiao listened in astonishment. “That really is something new. Who ever heard of doing such a thing? A woman’s reputation is more important than anything. Even if something of that kind had happened, she would try to clear herself. Here nothing happened, and she smeared her own face with mud! The Seventh Lady certainly has an extraordinary mind.” “Indeed she does. But she is formidable too. How else could she have made Old Gu so obedient?” Hu produced a learned quotation. “‘Before you can take, you must first give.’ The Seventh Lady went about it the right way.”

阿巧姐不作声，脸色慢慢转为深沉，好久，说了一句：“我就是学不到七姑奶奶那样的本事。”那副神色加上这么句话，言外之意就很深了，胡雪岩笑笑，不肯搭腔。见此光景，阿巧姐知道胡雪岩是“吃了秤砣——铁心”了，再挨着不走，也未免大自轻自贱！所以霍地站了起来，脸扬在一边，用冷冷的声音说：“我要走了！”胡雪岩不答她的话，只向外高喊一声：“搭跳板！”跳板根本没有撤掉，而且他也是看得明明白白的，是有意这样喊一声。阿巧姐心里有数，这就是俗语说的：“敲钉转脚”，将她离船登岸这回事，弄得格外牢靠，就算她改变心意，要不走也不行了。做出事来这么绝！阿巧姐那一片微妙的恋意所转化的怨恨，越发浓了，“哼！”她冷笑一声，“真正气数，倒象是把我当作‘瘟神’了！就怕我不走。”

A’qiao fell silent, her expression slowly growing somber. After a long while she said, “I could never learn to be as clever as the Seventh Lady.” Her look, together with those words, carried a good deal of unspoken meaning. Hu smiled and declined to take it up. Seeing this, A’qiao knew that he had, as the saying went, swallowed an iron scale weight: his heart was set like iron. To linger any longer would be humiliating. She sprang to her feet, turned her face aside, and said coldly, “I am going.” Hu did not answer her. He merely called out, “Put down the gangplank!” The plank had not been removed, and he could see that perfectly well. He had called out deliberately. A’qiao understood: he was driving in the nail and bending its point over, as the saying went. He was making her departure doubly certain. Even if she changed her mind, she could no longer stay. How ruthlessly he was behaving! The delicate affection she felt for him had turned to resentment, which now grew still stronger. “Hmph!” she sneered. “You are infuriating. Anyone would think I was the god of pestilence. You are so afraid I might not go!”

这一骂，胡雪岩亦只有苦笑，一只手正插在袋里，摸着表链子上系着的那只“小金羊”，突然心潮起伏，几乎想喊出来：“阿巧，不要走！”然而她已经走了，因为负气的缘故，脚步很急也很重，那条跳板受了压力，一起一伏在晃荡，她虽握着船老大伸过去的竹稿当扶手，到底也是件危险的事！胡雪岩深怕她一脚踩空，失足落水，瞠目张口，自己吓自己，什么话都忘记说了。等他惊魂一定，想要开口说句什么，阿巧姐已经上了轿，他只有高声叫道：“老周，拜托你多照料！”“晓得了！请放心。”周一鸣又扬扬手说，“过几天我就回上海，有要紧事写信，寄到金阎栈转好了。”

Under this reproach, Hu could only give a bitter smile. One hand was in his pocket. His fingers touched the little golden goat attached to his watch chain, and suddenly emotion surged through him. He almost cried out, “A’qiao, don’t go!” But she was already leaving. In her anger she walked quickly and heavily, making the gangplank dip and sway beneath her. Though she held the bamboo pole the boatman extended as a handrail, it was still dangerous. Terrified that she would miss her footing and fall into the water, Hu stared with his mouth open, frightening himself until he forgot every word he had meant to say. By the time he recovered and tried to speak, she was already in her sedan chair. He could only call out, “Old Zhou, please take good care of her!” “I will. Do not worry.” Zhou waved again. “I shall return to Shanghai in a few days. If anything urgent comes up, write to me care of Jinchang Inn, and they will forward it.”
