第二十六章｜CHAPTER TWENTY-SIX

胡雪岩到了上海，仍旧在投大兴客栈，行李还不曾安顿好，就写条子叫客栈专人送到七姑奶奶的寓所，请古应春来相会。不到一个钟头，古应春亲自驾着他的那辆“亨斯美”赶到大兴客栈，一见面叫应了，什么话不说，先仔细打量胡雪岩的行李。“怎么回事，老古！”“阿巧姐呢？”“没有来！”胡雪岩说，“事情大起变化，你想都想不到的。”“怎么样呢？”“说来话长。回头有空再谈。喂，”他问，“五哥回来了没有？”“还没有。”古应春又问：“阿巧姐呢？怎么事情起了变化？你要言不烦说两句。”胡雪岩不知道他何以对阿巧姐特别关心，便反问一句：“你是不是派人到木渎去谈过？”“你先不用管这个，只说阿巧姐怎么样了？”“名花有主，是我一手经理。不久，就是何学台的姨太太了。”接着，便讲移植这株名花的经过，胡雪岩虽长于口才，但经过太曲折，三言两语说不完，站着讲了一刻钟，才算说清楚。“这样也好！”古应春拉着他的袖子说，“走！去晚了，七姐的急性子，我是晓得的，又要埋怨我。”“慢来，慢来！”胡雪岩按住他的手说，“我的话告诉你了，你一定也有话，怎么不告诉我？”“当然要告诉你的。到家再说。”等坐上马车，古应春承认曾派人到木渎去谈过阿巧姐的事，但一场无结果，派去的人下会办事，竟连未能成功的原因何在，都弄不清楚。“我倒比你清楚。阿巧姐吃了一场惊吓，由此让我还交了三个朋友，都是苏州的阔少，有一大笔款子要我替他们用出去。”胡雪岩笑道：“老古，我这一趟苏州，辛苦真没有白吃，谈起个中的曲折，三天三夜都谈不完。”事情大多，东一句，西一句，扯来扯去，古应春一时也听不清楚，只知道他这趟大有收获。彼此在生意上休戚相关，胡雪岩有办法，他自然也感到兴奋。

On reaching Shanghai, Hu Xueyan again put up at the Daxing Inn. Before he had even settled his luggage, he wrote a note and had a man from the inn take it to the Seventh Lady’s lodgings, asking Gu Yingchun to call. In less than an hour Gu arrived, driving his Hengsimei carriage himself. After their first greetings, he said nothing but looked Hu’s luggage carefully over. “What is it, Gu?” “Where is A’qiao?” “She hasn’t come,” Hu replied. “Things have taken a turn you could never have imagined.” “What has happened?” “It’s a long story. We’ll talk when we have time. Tell me, has Brother Five come back?” “Not yet.” Gu returned to his question. “What about A’qiao? What has changed? Give me the gist of it.” Unable to understand this particular concern for her, Hu asked a question of his own. “Did you send someone to Mudu to discuss her affairs?” “Never mind that for the moment. Just tell me what has happened to her.” “That lovely flower has found her owner, and I arranged the whole thing. Before long she will be Education Commissioner He’s concubine.” He went on to explain how he had transplanted this celebrated flower. Eloquent as he was, the affair had too many twists to be disposed of in a few words. He stood talking for a quarter of an hour before he had made it clear. “That’s a good outcome too!” Gu took him by the sleeve. “Come along. I know Seventh Sister’s impatience. If we’re late, she’ll be blaming me again.” “Not so fast!” Hu pressed down his hand. “I’ve told you my story. You must have something to tell me. Why keep it to yourself?” “Of course I’ll tell you. When we get home.” Once they were in the carriage, Gu admitted that he had sent someone to Mudu about A’qiao. Nothing had come of it, and his messenger had proved so incompetent that he could not even explain why he had failed. “I know more about it than you do. A’qiao had quite a fright, and through that affair I made three friends, all wealthy young men from Suzhou, with a large sum they want me to put to work for them.” Hu laughed. “Gu, the trouble I took on this trip to Suzhou was certainly not wasted. Three days and three nights would hardly be enough to tell you all the twists and turns.” There was so much to relate that he darted from one subject to another. Gu could not immediately make sense of it all, but he understood that the journey had yielded a great deal. Their business fortunes were bound together, and Hu’s resourcefulness naturally excited him too.

转眼间到了七姑奶奶寓所，马蹄声音是她听熟的，亲自下楼来开门，老远就在喊：“小爷叔，你回来了。”“回来了，回来了！”胡雪岩说：“先告诉你一桩开心的事，你总说苏州的糖食好吃，我替你带了一大篓来，放在‘石灰缸，里，包你半年都吃不完。”“谢谢，谢谢！”七姑奶奶口中是对胡雪岩说话，眼睛却看着古应春。“阿巧姐不来了！”古应春轻声对她说，“她也不会姓胡了。”“怎么闹翻了？”“不是，不是。你不要乱猜，回头再跟你说。总而言之，可以放心了！”“嗯，嗯！”七姑奶奶很高兴地拍拍胸。胡雪岩听他们这番对答，越觉困惑，“老古，”他用低沉的声音问：“到底是怎么回事？什么事可以放心？”“现在不会‘白板对煞，了，”七姑奶奶搭腔，“大家都可以放心。小爷叔，快上楼来，看看哪个来了？”上楼掀帘一看，合笑凝睇的竟是芙蓉，胡雪岩惊喜之余，恍然大悟所谓“白板对煞”作何解。“你是怎么来的？”“我跟三叔一起来的。”芙蓉说，“一到就住在七姐这里。本来要写信告诉你，七姐说不必，你就要回来的。”

They soon reached the Seventh Lady’s lodgings. Recognising the sound of the horse’s hooves, she came downstairs to open the door herself and called out from a distance, “Little Uncle, you’re back!” “Yes, I’m back! First, something to make you happy. You’re always saying how good Suzhou sweets are, so I’ve brought you a whole big hamper. Keep them in a lime jar, and I guarantee you won’t finish them in half a year.” “Thank you, thank you!” Though she was speaking to Hu, her eyes were on Gu. “A’qiao isn’t coming,” Gu told her quietly. “And she won’t be taking the name Hu.” “Have they quarrelled?” “No, no. Don’t start guessing. I’ll tell you later. At any rate, we can stop worrying.” “Good, good!” She patted her chest with evident relief. This exchange left Hu more puzzled than ever. “Gu,” he asked in a low voice, “what is all this about? What can we stop worrying about?” “There won’t be a head-on collision now,” the Seventh Lady put in. “We can all relax. Come upstairs, Little Uncle, and see who’s here.” He went up and lifted the curtain. It was Furong who gazed at him with a smile. Amid his delighted surprise, the meaning of that “head-on collision” suddenly became clear. “How did you get here?” “I came with Third Uncle,” Furong replied. “I’ve been staying with Seventh Sister ever since we arrived. I meant to write and tell you, but she said there was no need, as you would soon be back.”

“那么三叔呢？”“他就住在不远一家客栈。”古应春笑道：“这位先生真是妙人！从他一来，你晓得哪个最开心？”“哪个最开心？”胡雪岩想了想说：“照我看，只有他自己。”大家都笑了，“还有一个，”古应春指着七姑奶奶：“她！”这一说，胡雪岩又大惑不解了，“何以七姐最开心？”“你想呢？我们这位姑奶奶一刻都静不下来的，现在听了你小爷叔的话，要学做千金小姐，大门不出，二门不迈，叫她怎么坐得住？刘三爷一来算救了她了，他每天到各处去逛，看了希奇古怪的花样，回来讲给她听，真好比听大书。”“听大书都没有听刘三叔说笑话来得发噱。”七姑奶奶也爽郎地笑着，“这个人真有趣。”“来了，来了！”古应春说，“他的脚步声特别。”

“And where is Third Uncle?” “At an inn quite close by.” Gu laughed. “He really is a character. Do you know who has been happiest since he arrived?” “Who?” Hu considered. “As far as I can see, only the man himself.” Everyone laughed. “There’s someone else.” Gu pointed to the Seventh Lady. “Her!” Once again Hu was mystified. “Why should Seventh Sister be the happiest?” “What do you think? Our lady here cannot sit still for a moment. Now that she has taken your advice and means to behave like a sheltered young lady, never stepping beyond the outer gate or the inner door, how can she bear to stay put? Third Master Liu has been her salvation. Every day he goes about seeing all sorts of strange and wonderful things, then comes back and tells her about them. It is as good as listening to a storyteller.” “No storyteller could make me laugh as much as Third Uncle Liu does,” she said, laughing freely herself. “He is wonderfully entertaining.” “Here he comes,” Gu said. “There’s no mistaking his footsteps.”

因为有此一句话，胡雪岩便先注意门帘下的脚，原来刘不才着的是一双只有洋人用的黑色革履，上了油，擦得闪闪发亮。身上只穿长袍，未着马褂，那件袍子纯黑，非绸非缎，细细看去，才知是洋人用来做礼服的呢子，刘不才别出心裁，做成长袍，配上水钻的套扣，显碍相当别致，也相当轻佻。“喔！”刘不才先开口，“你总算回来了！人象胖上点。”胡雪岩先答他的话，忍着笑将他从头看到底，“刘三爷，”他又似嘲弄，又似佩服他说：“你真正时髦透顶了！”“刘三爷真开通。”古应春也说：“叫我就不敢穿了这一身奇装异服，招摇过市。”“这有啥要紧？人穿衣服，不是衣服穿人。”七姑奶奶帮刘不才说话，“ ‘女要俏，一身孝，男要俏，一身皂’，刘三爷这身打抢真叫俏！看上去年纪轻了十几岁。”

At that remark Hu looked first at the feet below the door curtain. Liu Bucai was wearing the sort of black leather shoes otherwise worn only by foreigners, dressed and polished to a brilliant shine. He wore a long robe without a riding jacket. The robe was jet black, neither silk nor satin. Only on closer inspection did Hu realise that it was made from the woollen cloth foreigners used for formal dress. With characteristic ingenuity Liu had had it made into a Chinese robe, complete with rhinestone clasps. The effect was strikingly unusual and decidedly frivolous. “Ah!” Liu spoke first. “You’re back at last! You seem to have put on a little weight.” Hu answered him, then surveyed him from head to foot, suppressing a laugh. “Third Master Liu,” he said, half in mockery and half in admiration, “you are the very height of fashion!” “Third Master Liu is wonderfully open-minded,” Gu added. “I would never dare parade through the streets in an extraordinary outfit like that.” “What does it matter? People wear clothes; clothes don’t wear people,” the Seventh Lady said in Liu’s defence. “‘For a woman to look fair, let her wear mourning white; for a man to look fine, let him dress in black.’ Third Master Liu looks handsome indeed! It takes more than ten years off him.”

这一说大家都笑了，“闲话少说，”古应春问道：“我们是下馆子，还是在家吃饭？”“在家吃吧！”胡雪岩说，“我不想动了。”于是七姑奶奶和芙蓉都下厨房去指挥娘姨料理晚餐，胡雪岩开始畅谈此行的经过，因为有刘不才在座，关于阿巧姐的曲折，自然是有所隐讳的。“照此看来，刘不才来得正好，”等听完了，古应春异常兴奋他说，“五月初七去接陆芝香，就请刘三爷去。”“是的。”胡雪岩点点头，“我也这么想，将来陪他们吃喝玩乐，都是刘三爷的事。何学使经过上海，也归刘三爷接待。”“好的！”刘不才欣然答应，“都交给我。包管伺候得他们服服帖帖。”“你这身衣服，”古应春说，“陆芝香或许不在乎，在何学使一定看不顺眼。”“我懂，我懂！”刘不才说，“陪啥人穿啥衣裳，我自己有数。”

Everyone laughed at this. “Enough talk,” Gu said. “Shall we eat out or at home?” “At home,” Hu replied. “I don’t feel like going out again.” The Seventh Lady and Furong went down to the kitchen to direct the maidservant’s preparations for dinner, while Hu began an expansive account of his journey. With Liu present, he naturally concealed some of the complications concerning A’qiao. “In that case, Liu Bucai has come at just the right moment,” Gu said excitedly when he had finished. “Let Third Master Liu go to meet Lu Zhixiang on the seventh day of the fifth month.” “Yes.” Hu nodded. “That was my thought too. In future, entertaining them—food, drink and amusements—will all be Third Master Liu’s responsibility. He can also receive Education Commissioner He when he passes through Shanghai.” “Gladly!” Liu agreed. “Leave them all to me. I guarantee they’ll be perfectly satisfied with my attentions.” “As for those clothes of yours,” Gu said, “Lu Zhixiang may not mind, but Commissioner He is certain to disapprove.” “I know, I know! I know how to dress for the company I’m keeping.”

“我在想，”胡雪岩说，“将来刘三爷跟官场中人打交道，甚至到家里去的机会都有，有个功名在身上，比较方便得多。我看，捐个官吧？”“最好不捐。一品老百姓最大。”胡雪岩很机警，听出刘不才的意思，不捐官则已，要捐就要捐得象样，不过自己也不过“州县班子”，不能替刘不才捐个“知府”，所以这样说道：“我们是做生意，不是做官，大小不在乎，只为了做生意方便。譬如说逢关过卡，要讨个情，一张有官衔的名帖投进去，平坐乎起，道弟称兄，比一品老百姓，就好说话很多了。”“小爷叔的话不错，我也想捐一个，捐他个正八品的县丞，”“那也不必，都是州县班子好了，弄个‘大老爷’做做。”接着胡雪岩的话，那边笑了；七姑奶奶手里捧着一瓶洋酒，高声说道：“各位‘大老爷，请上桌吧！”

“I was thinking,” Hu said, “that Third Master Liu will be dealing with officials in future, and may even be invited into their homes. An official qualification would make things much easier. Why not purchase a rank?” “Better not. A first-rank commoner outranks them all.” Hu was quick to detect his meaning: if Liu were to buy a rank at all, he wanted a respectable one. Yet Hu himself belonged only to the class of subprefectural and county magistrates, and could hardly buy Liu the rank of prefect. He therefore said, “We are in business, not in government service. High rank or low doesn’t matter; it is simply to make business easier. Suppose you need a favour at a customs post or checkpoint. Send in a visiting card with an official title, and you can meet on equal terms and call one another brother. That makes it far easier to speak than if you were a first-rank commoner.” “Little Uncle is right. I’ve been thinking of buying a rank too—a county assistant magistrate, full eighth rank.” “There’s no need for that. Let us all join the magistrates’ class and enjoy being addressed as ‘Your Honour.’” Laughter came from across the room as Hu finished. Holding a bottle of foreign wine, the Seventh Lady called out, “Your Honours, dinner is served!”

“啊呀！”古应春突然说道，“我倒忘记了，有位仁兄应该请了他来。”“谁啊？”胡雪岩问。“裘丰言。”“喔，他也来了。这可真有得热闹了。”胡雪岩笑着说了这一句，却又摇摇头：“不过今天不必找他。我们还有许多事要谈。”生意上的许多机密，只有他们俩可以知道，连刘不才都不宜与闻，因此饭桌上言不及义，只听刘不才在大谈这天下午所看的西洋马戏，马背上的金发碧眼的洋美女，如何婀娜多姿，大露色相。别人倒都还好，英蓉初涉洋场，听了目瞪口呆，只是不断他说：“哪有这样子不在乎、不顾脸面的？我不信！”

“Oh dear!” Gu exclaimed suddenly. “I’ve forgotten someone we ought to have invited.” “Who?” Hu asked. “Qiu Fengyan.” “Oh, he’s here too. We really shall have a lively time.” Hu smiled, then shook his head. “But there’s no need to fetch him tonight. We still have a great deal to discuss.” Much of their business was confidential to the two of them; even Liu Bucai ought not to hear it. At dinner, therefore, they avoided serious matters and listened to Liu describe the Western circus he had seen that afternoon: a blonde, blue-eyed beauty performing on horseback, displaying her charms and her figure with astonishing freedom. The others took it in their stride, but Furong, on her first visit to this foreign world, listened in open-mouthed amazement. “How could anyone be so shameless, so careless of appearances?” she kept saying. “I don’t believe it!”

“百闻不如一见。”胡雪岩说，“你明天自己去看一次就晓得”对的！“七姑奶奶的兴致也来了，“明天我们也去看一场，”“女人也许看吗？”“女人难道不是人？为啥不许！”“有没有女人去看？”英蓉问她三叔。“有，有。不但有，而且还跟不认识的男人坐在一起……”“三叔又要瞎说了。”芙蓉老实不客气的指责，“这话我绝对不信。”“我话没有说完，你就怪我！”刘不才说，“我说的是西洋女人。”古应春衔杯在口，忍俊不住一口酒喷了出来，亏得脸转得快，才没有喷到饭桌上，但已呛了嗓子，又咳又笑好半天才能静下来。“小爷叔！”七姑奶奶也笑着对胡雪岩说：“我们这位刘三爷跟‘酒糊涂’裘大老爷，真正是‘宝一对’，两个人唱双簧似他说起死后来，简直把人肚肠都要笑断。我情愿每天备了好酒好菜请他们吃，听他们说说笑话，消痰化气、延年益寿。”“你倒真阔！”古应春笑道，“请两位州县班子的大老爷做清客。”

“Seeing once is better than hearing a hundred times,” Hu told her. “Go and see for yourself tomorrow, and you’ll know it’s true.” The Seventh Lady was interested too. “Let’s go to a performance tomorrow.” “Are women allowed to watch?” “Aren’t women people? Why shouldn’t they be?” “Do women actually go?” Furong asked her uncle. “Yes, yes. Not only do they go, they sit beside men they don’t even know—” “There goes Third Uncle talking nonsense again,” Furong said bluntly. “I simply don’t believe that.” “You blame me before I’ve finished! I mean Western women.” Gu, with his cup at his lips, could not contain himself and spat out a mouthful of wine. Only a quick turn of his head saved the dinner table. As it was, he choked, coughed and laughed for quite some time before recovering. “Little Uncle,” the Seventh Lady said to Hu, laughing too, “our Third Master Liu and His Honour Qiu, that Wine Muddlehead, are a perfect pair of treasures. When they put on their double act and start joking, they nearly make you split your sides. I would gladly provide them with good wine and good food every day just to hear their jokes. It would clear the phlegm, settle the spirits and lengthen one’s life!” “You do live grandly,” Gu laughed. “Two gentlemen of magistrate rank as your entertaining companions!”

“我倒想起来了。”七姑奶奶问道：“刚才你们在谈，是不是刘三爷也要捐个官做？”“老古也是！”胡雪岩接口，“老古槽通洋务，现在刚正吃香的时候，说不定将来有人会借重，真的挂牌出来，委个实缺。七姐，那时候你就是掌印夫人了。”“谢谢！”七姑奶奶撇着嘴说，“我才不要做啥官太太。”“老古！”胡雪岩先是当笑话说，转一转念头，觉得倒不是笑话，“说真的！考古，我看你做官，倒是蛮好一条路子。于你自己有益，对我们大家也有好处。“七姑奶奶口快，紧接着问：“对老古自己有没有益处，且不去说它，怎么说对大家都有好处？”“自然罗！”胡雪岩答道，“你只看王雪公，他做了官，不是我们都有好处？”“喔，我懂了，是仰仗官势来做生意。既然如此，老古为朋友，倒不妨打算打算。”“你啊！”古应春叹口气说，“得着风，就是雨。晓得的人，说你热心，不晓得的人，当你疯子。”七姑奶奶听了胡雪岩的劝，脾气已改得好多了，受了古应春的这顿排揎，笑笑不响。“小爷叔！”古应春转脸又说，“我样样佩服你，就是你劝我做官这句话，我不佩服。我们现在槁到兴兴头头，何苦去伺候贵人的颜色？”胡雪岩很知趣，见这上头话不投机，就不肯再说下去，换了个话题说：“从明天起，我们又要大忙特忙了。今天早点散吧！”

“That reminds me,” she said. “Were you saying just now that Third Master Liu means to purchase an official rank?” “Gu too,” Hu put in. “Gu understands foreign affairs, and that is just the knowledge people need nowadays. Someone may come to value his services and actually give him a substantive appointment, with an official signboard of his own. Seventh Sister, then you will be the wife of an official who holds a seal.” “Thank you very much!” She curled her lip. “I have no wish to be an official’s wife.” “Gu!” Hu had begun in jest, but on reflection the idea seemed worth taking seriously. “Really, Gu, an official career might be a very good path for you. It would benefit you and the rest of us as well.” Quick as ever, the Seventh Lady asked, “Never mind for the moment whether it would benefit him. How would it benefit everyone?” “Naturally it would,” Hu replied. “Look at Mr Xuexuan. Haven’t we all benefited from his holding office?” “Oh, I understand. We would have official influence behind our business. In that case Gu might consider it for his friends’ sake.” “You!” Gu sighed. “A puff of wind, and you proclaim a downpour. Those who know you call you enthusiastic; those who don’t would think you mad.” Since taking Hu’s advice, the Seventh Lady had greatly moderated her temper. Under this scolding she merely smiled and said nothing. Gu turned back to Hu. “Little Uncle, I admire you in everything, but I cannot admire this advice to become an official. We are enjoying what we do now. Why put ourselves at the mercy of great men’s moods?” Hu tactfully let the subject drop when he saw that they could not agree. “From tomorrow we shall be extremely busy again. Let us break up early tonight.”

“对！”七姑奶奶看一看胡雪岩和芙蓉笑道，“你们是小别胜新婚，早点去团圆，我也不留你们多坐。吃了饭就走好了。”于是止酒吃饭。古应春拿起挂在门背后的一支西洋皮马鞭，等在那里，是预备亲自驾车送他们回大兴客栈的样子。“你住得近，不必忙走！就在这里陪七姑奶奶谈谈闲天解解闷。”胡雪岩向刘不才说。虽然七姑奶奶性情脱略，但道理上没有孤身会男客的道理，所以刘不才颇现踌躇，而古应春却懂得胡雪岩的用意，是怕刘不才跟到大兴栈去，有些话就不便谈了。因而附和着说：“刘三爷，你就再坐一会好了。”既然古应春也这么说，刘不才勉强答应了下来。古应春陪着胡雪岩和芙蓉下楼，戴着顶西洋鸭舌帽的小马伕金福，已经将马车套好，他将马鞭子递了过去，命金福赶车，自己跨辕，以便于跟胡雪岩谈话。“先到丝栈转一转，看看可有什么信？”先到裕记丝栈，管事的人不在，古应春留下了话，说是胡大老爷已从苏州回到上海，如有他的信，直接送到大兴客栈。然后上车又走。

“Quite right!” The Seventh Lady glanced at Hu and Furong with a smile. “A short separation makes a reunion sweeter than a wedding. Go and enjoy yours. I won’t keep you here. Leave as soon as you’ve eaten.” They stopped drinking and finished their meal. Gu took a Western leather horsewhip from behind the door and waited, apparently intending to drive them back to the Daxing Inn himself. “You live close by, so there’s no hurry for you to leave,” Hu told Liu. “Stay and chat with the Seventh Lady to keep her amused.” Free and easy as she was, propriety did not allow her to receive a male guest alone. Liu therefore hesitated. Gu, however, understood Hu’s purpose: if Liu followed them to the inn, there would be things they could not discuss. He added, “Third Master Liu, do stay a little longer.” With Gu saying so too, Liu reluctantly agreed. Gu accompanied Hu and Furong downstairs. Jinfu, the young groom in his Western peaked cap, had already harnessed the horse. Gu handed him the whip and told him to drive, taking a seat astride the carriage shaft himself so that he could talk to Hu. “Let’s stop at the silk warehouse first and see whether there are any letters.” At the Yuji Silk Warehouse the manager was out. Gu left word that His Honour Hu had returned from Suzhou to Shanghai, and that any letters for him should be sent straight to the Daxing Inn. Then they drove on.

到了客栈，芙蓉便是女主人，张罗茶烟，忙过一阵，才去检点胡雪岩从苏州带回来的行李。胡雪岩使向古应春问起那笔丝生意。刚谈不到两三句，只听芙蓉在喊：“咦！这是哪里来的？”转脸一看，她托着一方白软缎绣花的小包袱走了过来，包袱上是一给头发，两片剪下来的指甲。“头发上还有生发油的香味，”芙蓉拈起那一络细软而黑的头发，闻了一下说，“铰下来还不久。”胡雪岩很沉着地问：“你是在哪里寻出来的？”“你的那个皮包里。”不用说，这是阿巧姐替他收拾行李时，有意留置的“私情表记”，胡雪岩觉得隐瞒、分辩都不必要，神色从容地点点头说：“我知道了！回头细细告诉你。”芙蓉看了这两样东西，心里自然不舒服，不过她也当得起温柔贤慧四个字，察言观色，见胡雪岩是这样地不在乎，也就愿意给他一个解释的机会，仍旧收好原物，继续整理其他的行李。

At the inn, Furong assumed the role of hostess, seeing to tea and tobacco before turning to the luggage Hu had brought from Suzhou. Hu began asking Gu about their silk venture. They had scarcely exchanged a few sentences when Furong called out, “Why, where did this come from?” He turned to see her approaching with a small embroidered wrapping of soft white satin laid on her palm. On it lay a lock of hair and two fingernail clippings. She picked up the fine black hair and sniffed it. “It still smells of hair oil. It can’t have been cut long ago.” Hu remained composed. “Where did you find it?” “In that leather bag of yours.” Plainly A’qiao had deliberately left this token of their private attachment while packing his things. Hu saw no need either to conceal the matter or to defend himself. He nodded calmly. “I see. I’ll explain it all to you later.” Furong was naturally displeased at the sight, but she deserved her reputation for gentleness and good sense. Reading his expression and observing how untroubled he was, she was willing to give him a chance to explain. She put the objects away again and continued sorting the rest of the luggage.

“洋人最近的态度，改变过了。”古应春也继续谈未完的生意，“听说，英国人和美国人都到江宁城里去看过，认为洪秀全那班人搞的花样，不成名堂，所以有意跟我们的官场，好好坐下来谈。苦的是‘上门不见土地’。”“这叫什么话？”“找不着交涉的对手。”古应春说，“历来的规矩，朝廷不跟洋人直接打交道，凡有洋务，都归两广总督兼办，所以英国、美国公使要见两江总督，督署都推到广州，拒而下见。其实，人家倒是一番好意。”“何以见得？”“这是有布告的。英、美、法三国领事，会衔布告，通知他们的侨民，不准接济小刀会刘丽川。”古应春又说，“我还有个很靠得住的消息，美国公使麦莲，从香港到了上海，去拜访江苏藩司吉尔杭阿，当面声明，并无助贼之心。只是想整顿商务、税务，要见两江怡大人。此外又听说英、美、法三国公使，会衔送了一个照会，为了上海新设的内地海关，提出抗议。”“这是什么意思？”“多设一道海关，多收一次税，洋商自然不愿。”

“The foreigners have changed their attitude lately,” Gu continued their interrupted discussion. “I hear that both the British and the Americans have been into Jiangning. They think Hong Xiuquan and his followers are making a poor business of things, so they would like to sit down and talk properly with our officials. The trouble is that they come to the shrine and cannot find the god.” “What do you mean?” “They cannot find anyone to negotiate with. It has always been the rule that the court does not deal directly with foreigners. All foreign affairs fall to the Governor-General of Liangguang as an additional duty. So when the British and American ministers ask to see the Governor-General of Liangjiang, his office refers them to Guangzhou and refuses to receive them. Yet they really do come with good intentions.” “How do you know?” “There is a public notice to prove it. The British, American and French consuls have issued a joint proclamation forbidding their nationals to assist Liu Lichuan of the Small Swords Society.” Gu went on, “I also have very reliable information that the American minister, McLane, came from Hong Kong to Shanghai and called on Ji’erhang’a, the Jiangsu provincial treasurer. He declared in person that they had no intention of helping the rebels. They merely wanted to put trade and taxation in order, and wished to see Governor-General Yi of Liangjiang. I have also heard that the British, American and French ministers have sent a joint diplomatic note protesting against the new inland customs office in Shanghai.” “What is that about?” “One more customs office means another levy. Naturally the foreign merchants object.”

胡雪岩很用心地考虑了一会，认为整个形势，都说明了洋人的企图，无非想在中国做生意，而中国从朝廷到地方，有兴趣的只是稳定局势，其实两件事是可以合起来办的，要做生意，自然要求得市面平静，要求市面平静，当然先要在战事上取胜，英美法三国公使，禁止他们的侨民接济刘丽川，正就是这个意思。当今最好的办法，是开诚布公，跟洋人谈合作的条件。当他陈述了自己的意见，古应春叹口气说：“小爷叔，要是你做了两江总督就好了，无奈官场见不到此，再说一句，就是你做了两江总督也不行，朝廷不许你这样做也是枉然，我们只谈我们自己的生意。”他提醒他说：“新丝快要上市了。”新丝虽快上市，不准运到上海与洋人交易，则现有的存货，依然奇货可居。疑问是这样的情势，究竟可以维持多久？板高不售，一旦禁令解除，丝价下跌是一可虑，陈丝品质不及新丝，洋人要买一定买新丝，陈丝的身价更见下跌，说不定卖不出去是二可虑。胡雪岩意会到此，矍然而惊，当即问道：“考古，照你看，我们的货色是卖，还是不卖？”古应春不作声。这个决定原是很容易下的，但出入太大，自己一定要表现出很郑重的态度，才能说动胡雪岩，所以他的沉默，等于盘马弯弓，实际上是要引起胡雪岩的注意和重视。“你说一句啊！”胡雪岩催促着。“这不是一句话可以说得尽的，贵乎盘算整个局势，看出必不可易的大方向，照这个方向去做，才会立于不败之地。”胡雪岩一面听，一面点头，“不错。”他说，“所谓眼光，就是要用在这上头。照我的看法洪杨一定失败，跟洋人一定要合作。”“对！我也是这样的看法。既然看出这个大方向，我们的生意应该怎么做，自然就很明白了。”“迟早要合作的，不如放点交情绪洋人，将来留个见面的余地。”胡雪岩很明确他说：“老古，丝我决定卖了！你跟洋人去谈。价钱上当然多一个好一个。”

Hu considered this carefully. To him, the whole situation showed that the foreigners wanted nothing more than to trade in China, while the Chinese authorities, from the court down to the local administrations, wanted only to restore stability. The two aims could in fact be pursued together. Trade required peaceful markets, and peaceful markets required victory in the war. That was precisely the purpose of the three countries’ ministers in forbidding their nationals to support Liu Lichuan. The best course now was to speak frankly with the foreigners and agree on terms of cooperation. When he explained his view, Gu sighed. “Little Uncle, if only you were Governor-General of Liangjiang! Unfortunately our officials cannot see that far. Come to think of it, even you could do nothing in that post if the court forbade it. Let us confine ourselves to our own business.” He reminded Hu, “The new silk will soon be coming onto the market.” New silk might be coming onto the market, but so long as it could not be brought to Shanghai for sale to foreigners, the stock already there would retain its scarcity value. The question was how long that situation could last. If they held out for a high price and the ban was suddenly lifted, falling silk prices would be one danger. Another was that old silk was inferior to new: the foreigners would certainly prefer the fresh crop, depressing the value of the old stock still further and perhaps making it impossible to sell. Realising this, Hu started in alarm. “Gu, in your opinion, should we sell our stock or hold it?” Gu said nothing. The decision itself was easy enough, but its consequences were so considerable that he had to appear to weigh it with great seriousness if he was to persuade Hu. His silence was like a horseman wheeling about with his bow drawn: its real purpose was to command Hu’s attention and impress on him the importance of the matter. “Say something!” Hu urged. “It cannot be settled in a word. We must consider the whole situation and identify its unalterable direction. Only by following that direction can we make our position secure.” Hu nodded as he listened. “Exactly. This is where foresight matters. In my view, Hong and Yang are bound to fail, and cooperation with the foreigners is inevitable.” “Yes. That is my view too. Once we recognise that general direction, the course for our business is quite clear.” “Since we shall have to cooperate sooner or later, we may as well show the foreigners some goodwill and leave ourselves room for future dealings.” Hu spoke decisively. “Gu, I have decided to sell the silk. Go and negotiate with them. Naturally, every extra bit we can get on the price is welcome.”

古应春只点头，不说话。显然的，怎样去谈，亦须有个盘算。古应春想了想说：“这样做法，不必瞒来瞒去，事情倒比较容易办。不过‘操纵’二字就谈不到了。”这句话使得胡雪岩动容了，他隐隐然觉得做生意这方面，在古应春面前象是差了一着，然而那股好胜之心，很快地被压了下去。做生意不是斗意气！他这样在想，见机最要紧。“ ‘操纵，行情，我何尝不想？不过当初我计算的时候，没有想到最要紧的一件事，这件事，洋人占便宜，我们吃亏。所以要想操纵很难，除非实力厚得不得了。”“哪一件事！”古应春间，“洋人占便宜的是，开了兵船来做生意……”“着啊！”胡雪岩猛然一拍手掌，“我说的就是这件事，洋人做生意，官商一体，他们的官是保护商人的，有困难，官出来挡，有麻烦，官出来料理。他们的商人见了官，有什么话也可以实说。我们的情形就不同了，官不恤商艰，商人也从来不敢期望官会替我们出面去论斤争两。这样子的话，我们跟洋人做生意，就没有把握了，你看这条路子走得通，忽然官场中另出一个花样，变成前功尽弃。譬如说，内地设海关，其权操之在我，有海关则不便洋商而便华商，我们就好想出一个办法来，专找他们这种‘不便’的便宜，现在外国领事提出抗议，如果撤消了这个海关，我们的打算，岂不是完全落空？”

Gu merely nodded. Plainly the approach to the negotiations still required thought. After a pause he said, “That will make things easier: we shall no longer have to conceal things from one another. But there can be no question of manipulating the market.” Hu’s expression changed. He had a vague sense that, in business, Gu had shown himself a move ahead of him. Yet he quickly suppressed his competitive impulse. Business was not a contest of pride; the essential thing was to recognise an opportunity and adapt. “Do you think I don’t want to control prices? But in my original calculations I overlooked the most important factor of all, one that favours the foreigners and puts us at a disadvantage. It makes controlling the market very difficult unless we have enormous resources.” “What factor? The foreigners’ advantage is that they come to trade with warships—” “Exactly!” Hu clapped his hands. “That is just what I mean. When foreigners trade, their officials and merchants work together. Their officials protect the merchants: if difficulties arise, the officials fend them off; if there is trouble, the officials deal with it. Their merchants can also speak frankly to their officials. With us it is quite different. Officials have no sympathy for merchants’ difficulties, and merchants never dare expect them to bargain on our behalf. That leaves us with no certainty in trading with foreigners. You see a promising course, and suddenly the authorities devise some new measure that wipes out all your previous efforts. Take an inland customs office. We control it, and its existence disadvantages foreign merchants while favouring Chinese ones. That gives us scope to devise a plan that profits from precisely those disadvantages. Now that the foreign consuls have protested, what if the customs office is abolished? Wouldn’t our whole scheme come to nothing?”

胡雪岩知道他在动脑筋，这笔生意，脑筋不灵活是无法去做的，跟洋人打交道已经不容易，还有一批丝商散户要控制。主意是胡雪岩所出，集结散户，合力对付洋人，并且实力最强的庞二这个集团，亦已由于胡雪岩的交情和手腕，联成了一条线。而指挥这条线的责任，却落在古应春的身上。以前为了说服大家一致行动，言语十分动听，说是只要团结一致，迫得洋人就范，必可大获其利，如今这句话必得兑现，倘或丝价不如预期之高，一定要受大家的责难。其中还有一部分是垫借了款子的，丝价不好，垫出去的钱不能十足收回，就非吃赔帐不可。这样考虑了好一会，盘算了坏的这方面，又盘算了好的这方面，大致决定了一个做法，“小爷叔”，他说，“我想先跟洋人去谈，开诚布公说明白，大家一起来维持市面，请他们开个底价给我。这个底价在我们同行方面，不宜实说，留下一个虚数，好作讨价还价的余地。你看我这样子做，是不是妥当？”“洋人这方面的情形，我没有你熟。”胡雪岩说，“不过我们自己这方面的同行，我觉得亦用得着‘开诚布公’这四个字。”“你是说，洋人开价多少，我们就实说多少？”“对，我就是这个意思。”胡雪岩说，“这趟生意，我们赚多赚少在其次，一定要让同行晓得，我们的做法是为大家好，决不是我们想利用小同行发财。”“小爷叔是眼光看得远的做法，我也同意。不过，”古应春说，“当初为了笼络散户，垫出去的款子，成数很高，如今卖掉了丝，全数扣回，所剩无几，只怕他们有得罗嗦。”

Hu knew that Gu was thinking hard. This venture demanded an agile mind. Dealing with the foreigners was difficult enough; there was also a collection of silk merchants and small independent dealers to keep in line. It had been Hu’s idea to unite the small dealers against the foreigners, and his connections and skill had brought Pang Er’s group, the strongest of them all, into the same alliance. Yet the responsibility for directing it fell on Gu. To persuade everyone to act together, they had made enticing promises: if they united and forced the foreigners to yield, they were certain to reap large profits. Those promises now had to be fulfilled. If silk fetched less than expected, everyone would blame them. Some of the dealers had also received advances; if prices were too low to recover those advances in full, a loss would be unavoidable. Gu considered the bad possibilities and the good for some time before broadly deciding on a course. “Little Uncle, I propose to speak frankly to the foreigners first. We can explain that we should all work together to sustain the market and ask them to give us a firm price. We should not reveal that actual figure to our own fellow merchants, but leave ourselves a margin for bargaining. Do you think that would be sound?” “I don’t know the foreigners’ circumstances as well as you do,” Hu replied. “But I think frankness would serve us with our own fellow merchants too.” “You mean that whatever price the foreigners offer, we should tell them exactly that?” “Yes. How much we make on this venture comes second. We must make our fellow merchants understand that we are acting for everyone’s good, not trying to get rich at the smaller dealers’ expense.” “That is a far-sighted approach, Little Uncle, and I agree. But the advances we made to bring the small dealers in were a very high proportion of the value of their silk. If we sell now and deduct the advances in full, very little will remain for them. I’m afraid they may complain.”

“不要紧！”胡雪岩说：“我在路上已经算过了，有庞家的款子，还有苏州潘家他们的款子，再把这票丝卖掉，手上的头寸极宽裕，他们要借，就让他们借。”“慢慢！”古应春挥着手说：“是借，是押，还是放定金？”这句话提醒得恰是时候，借是信用借款，押是货色抵押，放定金就得“买青”——买那些散户本年的新丝。同样一笔钱，放出去的性质不一样，胡雪岩想了想说：“要看你跟洋人谈下来的情形再说，如果洋人觉得我们的做法还不错，愿意合作，那就订个合约，我们今年再卖一批给他们。那一来，就要向散户放定金买丝了。否则，我们改做别项生意，我的意思，阜康的分号，一定要在上海开起来。”“那是并行不悻的事，自己有了钱庄，对做丝只有方便。”“这样子说，就没有什么好商量的了。你拿出本事去做，你觉得可以做主的，尽由自己做主。”将胡雪岩的话从头细想了一遍，古应春发觉自己所顾虑的难题，突然之间，完全消失了。明天找洋人开诚布公去谈，商量好了一个彼此不吃亏的价钱，然后把一条线上的同行、散户都请了来，问大家愿不愿意卖？愿意卖的最好，不愿意卖的，各自处置，反正放款都用栈卑抵押，不至于吃倒帐。生意并不难做。

“Never mind,” Hu said. “I worked it out on the journey. With the Pang family’s funds, the money from the Pan family and the others in Suzhou, and the proceeds of this silk, we shall have ample funds in hand. If they want to borrow, let them.” “Wait!” Gu waved a hand. “Are we to lend on credit, lend against security, or pay deposits?” The reminder came at exactly the right moment. A loan meant unsecured credit; a secured advance required goods as collateral; a deposit meant buying the crop in advance—the small dealers’ new silk for the year. The same sum could be put out on very different terms. Hu thought for a moment. “That will depend on what you arrange with the foreigners. If they think well of our conduct and are willing to cooperate, we can sign a contract to sell them another batch this year. Then we shall need to pay the small dealers deposits for their silk. Otherwise we can turn to other business. In any case, I am determined to open a Shanghai branch of Fukang.” “The two things can proceed together perfectly well. Having a bank of our own will only make the silk business easier.” “Then there is nothing more to discuss. Put your abilities to work. Whenever you think you can decide a matter yourself, go ahead.” Reviewing Hu’s remarks from the beginning, Gu realised that all the difficulties troubling him had suddenly vanished. Tomorrow he could talk openly with the foreigners and agree on a price that left neither side at a disadvantage. Then he would invite all the merchants and small dealers in their alliance and ask whether they wished to sell. If they did, so much the better; if not, they could make their own arrangements. The loans were secured by warehouse receipts in any event, so there was no danger of losing them through default. Business need not be so difficult after all.

这样想了下来，神色就显得轻松了，“小爷叔，”他笑道，“跟你做事，真正爽快不过。”“你也是爽快人，不必我细说。总而言之，我看人总是往好处去看，我不大相信世界上有坏人。没有本事才做坏事，有本事一定会做好事。既然做坏事的人没有本事，也就不必去怕他们了。”古应春对他的这套话，在理路上一时还辨不清是对还是错，好在这是闲话，也就不必去理他。起身告辞，要一个人去好好筹划，明天如何踉洋人开谈判？

His face relaxed as this reasoning became clear to him. “Little Uncle,” he said with a smile, “working with you really is a pleasure.” “You’re straightforward too. I needn’t explain everything. In short, I always look for the good in people. I scarcely believe there are bad people in the world. People do bad things because they lack ability; capable people are sure to do good. And since wrongdoers have no ability, there is no need to fear them.” Gu could not immediately decide whether the logic of this was sound. Fortunately, it was only casual conversation and required no answer. He rose to take his leave, wanting to be alone to work out how he would open negotiations with the foreigners the following day.

等古应春一走，胡雪岩才能把全副心思摆到英蓉身上。小别重逢，自然有一番体己的话，问她在湖州的日常生活，也问起他的兄弟。芙蓉告诉他，决计叫他兄弟读书上进，附在一家姓朱的书香人家读书，每个月连柬脩和饭食是三而银子，讲好平日不准回家。胡雪岩听见这话，大为惊异，想不到芙蓉那样柔弱的性情，教养她的兄弟，倒有这样刚强的处置。“那么小兔儿呢？”他问，“一个人住在朱家，倒不想家？”“怎么不想？到了朱家第三天就逃了回来，让我一顿手心又打回去了，”“你倒真狠得下这个心？”“你晓得我的心，就晓得我狠得下来了！”“我只晓得你的心好，不晓得你心狠。”胡雪岩已估量到她有个很严重的说法，为了不愿把气氛弄得枯燥严肃，所以语气中特地带着点玩笑的意味。芙蓉最温柔驯顺不过，也猜到胡雪岩在这时刻只愿享受温情笑谑，厌闻什么一本正经的话，所以笑笑不响，只把从湖州带来的小吃，烘青豆、酥糖之类摆出来供他消闲。

Only after Gu had left could Hu give Furong his undivided attention. Reunited after a short separation, they naturally had intimate things to discuss. He asked about her daily life in Huzhou and about her younger brother. She told him she had resolved that the boy should study and make something of himself. She had placed him with a scholarly family named Zhu, where his lessons and meals together cost three taels a month, on the understanding that he was not to come home on ordinary school days. Hu was astonished. He had never imagined that Furong, so gentle in temperament, could be so firm in bringing up her brother. “And Little Rabbit? Doesn’t he get homesick, living alone with the Zhus?” “Of course he does. On his third day there he ran home. I gave him a beating on the palms and sent him back.” “Could you really harden your heart like that?” “If you understood my heart, you would know why I could.” “I know you have a good heart. I didn’t know you had a hard one.” Hu sensed that she had something very serious to say. Unwilling to make their conversation solemn and dry, he deliberately spoke with a touch of teasing. Furong, gentle and compliant as ever, guessed that just then he wanted warmth and laughter rather than serious discussion. She smiled and said nothing, bringing out the snacks she had brought from Huzhou—roasted green beans, flaky sugar sweets and the like—for him to enjoy at his leisure.

她将他的心思倒是猜着了，但也不完全对，胡雪岩的性情是什么时候都可以说笑话，也什么时候都可以谈正经，而且谈正经也可以谈出谐谑的趣味来，这时便又笑道：“你是啥个心，怎么不肯说？是不是要我来摸？”说着顺手捞住芙蓉的一条膀子，一摸摸到她胸前，芙蓉一闪，很轻巧地避了开去。接着便发现窗外有人疾趋而过，看背影是大兴客栈的伙计。显然的，刚才他的那个轻桃的动作，已经落入外人眼中，即令芙蓉温柔驯顺，也忍不住着恼，手一甩尘到一边，扭着头不理胡雪岩。一时忘形，惹得她不快，他自然也感到歉疚，但也值不得过去赔笑说好话，等一会事情也就过去。所以只坐着吃烘青豆，心里在想着，湖州有哪些事要提出来问她的？偶然一瞥之间，发觉芙蓉从腋下钮扣押出一条手绢，正在擦眼泪，不由得大惊失色，奔过去，捧有她的脸一看，可不是泪痕宛然？“这，这是为什么？”“没有什么！”芙蓉醒醒鼻子，擦擦眼泪，站起来扯了扯衣襟，依旧坐了下来，要装得没事人似的。“一定有缘故。”胡雪岩待为这样说：“你不讲，我要起疑心的。”“我自己想想难过！不怨别人，只怨自己命苦。”她将脸偏到一边，平静他说，“如果是平起平坐的夫妇，上床夫妻，下床君子，你一定也要尊重人家，不会这样动手动脚，叫不相干的人看轻了我，”

She had understood him, but not entirely. Hu could joke at any time, discuss serious matters at any time, and even find humour in a serious discussion. Now he smiled again. “What sort of heart have you got, then? Why won’t you tell me? Must I feel for myself?” He caught her by the arm and let his hand wander to her breast. She slipped lightly away, then noticed someone hurry past the window. From the man’s back she recognised a servant of the Daxing Inn. Evidently that frivolous gesture had been seen by an outsider. Gentle and compliant though she was, she could not help taking offence. She flung her hand aside, sat apart and turned her head away from Hu. He felt guilty for having displeased her in a moment of forgetfulness, but did not think the matter serious enough to go over and coax her with apologies. It would soon pass. He sat eating roasted beans and considering what he ought to ask her about Huzhou. Then a chance glance showed him that she had drawn a handkerchief from the fastening under her arm and was wiping away tears. Alarmed, he hurried over and took her face in his hands. There was no mistaking the tear marks. “What—what is the matter?” “Nothing.” She sniffed, wiped her eyes, stood and straightened her clothes, then sat down again, trying to appear unaffected. “There must be a reason,” Hu said deliberately. “If you won’t tell me, I shall begin to suspect things.” “I was thinking of something that makes me sad. I blame no one but my own unhappy fate.” Turning her face aside, she spoke calmly. “If we were husband and wife on equal terms—husband and wife in bed, but proper and respectful out of it—you would surely treat me with respect. You would not paw me about like that and let strangers look down on me.”

越是这样怨而下怒的神态，越使得胡雪岩不安，解释很难，而且也多余，唯一的办怯是认错。“我不对！”他低着头说，“下次晓得了。”忠厚的芙蓉反倒要解释了，“我也不是说你不尊重我，不过身分限在那里，也是没有办法的事。”她又说，“你现在应该想得到了，我为什么对小兔儿狠得下心来，我要他争气！要他忘记了有我这样一个姐姐！”“这……”胡雪岩颇感不安，“你也把这一点看得太重了！男人家三妻四妾，也是常事，我又没有看轻过你。”“话不是这么说。”芙蓉也觉得这身分上的事，再谈下去也无味，所以避而不谈，只谈她兄弟，“我一个人前前后后都想过了，小兔儿在我身边，一定不会有出息，为啥呢，第一，不愁吃，不愁穿，他要啥，我总依他，只养不教，一定不成材；第二，有三叔在那里，小兔儿学不到好样，将来嫖赌吃着，一应俱全。我们刘家就再没有翻身的日子了！”

Her resentment, expressed without anger, made Hu all the more uneasy. Explanation would be difficult and unnecessary. The only course was to admit his fault. “I was wrong,” he said, lowering his head. “I shall remember next time.” It was kind-hearted Furong who then felt obliged to explain. “I am not saying that you don’t respect me. But my position sets a limit, and there is nothing to be done about it.” She continued, “Now you ought to understand why I can be hard on Little Rabbit. I want him to make something of himself. I want him to forget that he has a sister like me.” “Well…” Hu was deeply uncomfortable. “You attach too much importance to that. It is perfectly ordinary for a man to have several wives and concubines. I have never looked down on you.” “That is not what I mean.” Furong too felt that further discussion of her status would be pointless, so she turned back to her brother. “I have thought it all through on my own. If Little Rabbit stays with me, he will never amount to anything. Why? First, he has no worries about food or clothing, and I give him whatever he wants. If I merely support him without teaching him, he will be useless. Second, with Third Uncle about, he will learn no good example. In time he will take to women, gambling, fine food and clothes—the whole lot. Then our Liu family will never rise again.”

这番话说得胡雪岩半晌作声不得，口虽不言，心里却有许多话，最想说的一句是：“我把你看错了！”他一直看芙蓉是个“面人儿”，几块五颜六色的粉，一把象牙刻刀，要塑捏成怎样一个人，就是怎样一个人。此时方知不然！看似柔弱，其实刚强，而越是这样的人，用的心思越深，做出来的事，说出来的话，越是出人意外。从今以后，更不可以小觑任何人了！不然就可能会栽大跟斗。由于这样的警惕，他更加不肯轻易答腔，站起来一面踱方步，一面回味她的话，越想越深，把她未曾说出来的意思都琢磨到了。“难为你想得这么深！”他站定了脚说，“不过，我倒要劝你，你这样子不是福相！我实在替你担心。你什么事放不开，一个人在肚子里用功夫，耗心血的，怪不得人这么瘦！”芙蓉颇有自知之明，知道自己怎么样在肚子里用功夫，也抵不上他脑筋略为一转，就凭这两句话，便可以想见他已了解自己所不曾说出来的一番意思——如果她是他明媒正娶的结发糟糠，小兔儿这个小舅子，他就会当自己同胞的小弟弟看待，自然而然地负起教养之责，惟其他念不及此，所以只有靠她做姐姐的，自己要有决断。只要他知道了就好，他一定会有办法！茎蓉这样在想，先不必开口，且听他说些什么？

For a long while Hu could say nothing. Though silent, he had much on his mind, above all the words, “I have misjudged you.” He had always thought of Furong as a little dough figurine: with a few lumps of coloured dough and an ivory modelling knife, one could shape her into whatever one wished. Now he saw his mistake. Outwardly weak, she was inwardly strong. Such people thought deeply, and their words and actions could be all the more surprising. From now on he must never underestimate anyone, or he might suffer a disastrous fall. This warning made him still more reluctant to answer hastily. He rose and paced slowly, turning her words over in his mind. The more he considered them, the more deeply he understood, until he had grasped even what she had left unsaid. “You have thought a long way ahead,” he said, coming to a stop. “But let me advise you: this is no way to lead a fortunate life. I really am worried about you. You cannot let anything go; you keep working away at everything inside. It drains your heart and mind. No wonder you are so thin.” Furong knew herself well enough to realise that all her inward brooding could not match a quick turn of his mind. Those few remarks showed that he had understood what she had not said: had she been his formally married first wife, the wife who had shared his hardships, he would have treated Little Rabbit, his young brother-in-law, as his own younger brother and naturally taken responsibility for his upbringing. Since he had not thought to do so, she, the boy’s sister, had had to make the decision herself. It was enough that he understood now. He would surely find a way. Thinking this, Furong kept silent and waited to hear what he would say.

“这是我不对！我没有想到小兔儿。不过，话说回来，是我没有想到，不是不管他。我的事情实在太多，就算是我自己的兄弟，只怕也没有工夫来管。所以，你不要怨我，只要你跟我提到，我一定想办法，尽责任。”胡雪岩停了一下说，“你就只有这么一个亲骨肉，只要你舍得，事情就好办了，你倒说，你希望小兔儿将来做啥？做官？”“也不一定是做官，总巴望他能够自立。”芙蓉想了想，低眉垂眼，是那种不愿说而又非说不可的神态，“无论如何，不要象三叔那种样子。”胡雪岩明白，这是她感怀身世，痛心疾首的一种感慨。如果不是刘不才不成材，她即使相信算命算相的话，生来是偏房的命，但不能为人正室，不嫁也总可以！只力有了一个兄弟，又不能明望叔父能教养侄儿成人，终于不得不做人的偏房，而委屈的目的，无非是为了小兔儿。其情哀，其志苦，胡雪岩对她不但同情，而且钦佩，因而也愈感到对小兔儿有一份必须要尽的责任。“你的意思我懂了。”他说，“你三叔虽不是败子回头金不换，也有他的道理，将来会发达的。你不要太看轻了他。”“我不是看轻他，他是我叔叔，一笔写不出两个刘字，我总尊敬他的。不过……“芙蓉忽然摇摇手，”这也不去说他了。我只望你拿小免儿当自己人。““当然。不是自己人是啥？”胡雪岩说、“闲话少说，你倒说，你将来希望小兔儿做啥？”“自然是巴望他荣宗耀祖。”“荣宗耀诅，只有做官。象我这样捐来的官不希奇，要考场里真刀真枪拼出来的才值钱。”胡雪岩平静他说，“只要小兔儿肯替你争气，事情也很好办，我替你请个最好的先生教他读书。”

“That was my fault. I didn’t think of Little Rabbit. But remember, I failed to think of him; that does not mean I refuse to look after him. I have so much to do that even if he were my own brother, I might have no time for him. So don’t blame me. You need only mention it, and I shall find a way and do my duty.” Hu paused. “He is your only close blood relation. If you can bear to let him go, the rest is easy. Tell me, what do you want him to become? An official?” “Not necessarily. I just hope he can stand on his own feet.” She thought for a moment, her eyes lowered, plainly reluctant to say what she felt she must. “Whatever happens, I don’t want him to turn out like Third Uncle.” Hu understood the painful reflection on her own life behind those words. Had Liu Bucai been more capable, then even if she believed the fortune-tellers and physiognomists who said she was born to be a concubine, she could at least have remained unmarried rather than accept a secondary wife’s place. But she had a younger brother, and no prospect of relying on their uncle to bring his nephew up. In the end she had been obliged to become a concubine, and the purpose of that sacrifice was simply to provide for Little Rabbit. Her circumstances were pitiable and her resolve hard-won. Hu felt not only sympathy but admiration, and all the more strongly acknowledged his duty towards the boy. “I understand,” he said. “Your uncle may not be a reformed prodigal worth more than gold, but he has his own ways and will prosper in time. Don’t think too little of him.” “I don’t. He is my uncle; our surname Liu is the same word, however you write it. I always respect him. But…” She suddenly waved a hand. “Let us leave him out of it. I only hope you will treat Little Rabbit as one of your own.” “Of course. What else could he be?” Hu replied. “Enough of that. Tell me what you want him to become.” “Naturally, I hope he will bring honour to his ancestors.” “Then he must become an official. A purchased rank like mine is nothing remarkable. It is the rank won by real combat in the examination hall that counts.” Hu spoke calmly. “Provided Little Rabbit is willing to work hard and do you credit, it can be arranged. I shall find you the best possible teacher for him.”

为了表示不是信口敷衍，胡雪岩当时就要笔墨纸张，给王有龄写信，请他代为托“学老师”，觅一个饱学秀才“坐馆”。当然，他也还有许多事要跟王有龄谈，文墨上的事，胡雪岩不大在行，有些话，象跟何桂清见面的经过，又非亲笔不可，所以这封信写到钟敲十二下，还没有写完。芙蓉倒觉得老大过意不去，先是当他有些负气，后来看看不象，长篇大套在写，当然是谈别的事。不过因头总是由小兔儿身上而起，这样慎重其事，未免令人难安。“好歇歇了！”她温柔他说，“莲子羹都煮成泥了，吃了点心睡吧，明天再说。”“马上就好，马上就好。”胡雪岩头也不抬他说。说是这样说，仍旧又很费劲地写了一个钟头才罢手，他把头一张信纸，递了给芙蓉。芙蓉是识得字的，接过来念道：“雪公太守尊兄大人阁下，敬禀者，”念到这里笑了，“好罗嗦的称呼！”“你看下去。”于是芙蓉又念：“套言不叙。今有内弟刘小兔，”到这里，芙蓉又笑了，“你怎么把小兔儿的小名也写了上去？”“那要什么紧，又不是官场里报履历，我跟王大老爷通家至好，就写小名也不要紧。”恩想也不错，她便笑道：“说来说去，总说不过你。”“不用你说，我自己晓得，你看，”他指着“内弟”二字。“这你总没话说了吧？”这是不拿芙蓉视作妾媵，她自然感激，却不便有何表示，只静心看下去，见胡雪岩对聘师的要求是学问好、性情好，年纪不宜过大，如愿就聘，柬脩从优。这见得他是真为自己跟小兔儿打算，心头由热而酸，不知不党的滚下两滴眼泪。

To show that this was no casual promise, Hu immediately called for writing materials and began a letter to Wang Youling, asking him to approach the educational official and find a learned licentiate willing to serve as a private tutor. Of course, he had many other things to discuss with Wang. Literary composition was not his strength, and some matters, such as his meeting with He Guiqing, had to be set down in his own hand. When the clock struck twelve, the letter was still unfinished. Furong began to feel very apologetic. At first she had thought he might be writing in a fit of pique; later, seeing the length of the letter, she realised he must be discussing other matters. Still, Little Rabbit had prompted it, and such solemn attention made her uneasy. “Do take a rest,” she said gently. “The lotus-seed soup has boiled to a paste. Have your supper and go to bed. You can finish tomorrow.” “Almost done, almost done,” Hu answered without raising his head. Despite his words, he laboured over the letter for another hour before putting down his brush. He handed Furong the first sheet. She could read, and began aloud: “‘To His Excellency the Prefect, my honoured elder brother Mr Xuexuan, I respectfully beg to report—’” She laughed. “What a long-winded address!” “Read on.” She continued, “‘Dispensing with formal compliments, I have a younger brother-in-law, Liu Xiaotu—’” Again she laughed. “Why have you written down Little Rabbit’s childhood name?” “What does it matter? This is not an official statement of his career. Prefect Wang and I are intimate family friends. A childhood name is quite all right.” On reflection she agreed. Smiling, she said, “However much I argue, I can never get the better of you.” “You needn’t tell me. I know that already. Look here.” He pointed to “younger brother-in-law.” “You can have no complaint about that, can you?” He was declining to treat her as merely a concubine. Naturally she was grateful, though she could not easily express it. She read on quietly. The tutor, Hu wrote, must be learned, good-tempered and not too old; a generous fee would be offered to anyone willing to accept. It was clear that he was thinking seriously of her and Little Rabbit. Warmth rose in her heart and turned to an ache; two tears rolled down before she knew it.

“我想想又不对了！”她揩一揩眼睛说，“怕小兔儿福薄，当不起！再说，这样费事，我心也不安。”这话让胡雪岩没奈何了，“算命看相，可以相信，不过一个人也不要太迷这些花样。”他搔搔头说，“你样样都好，就是这上头看不开。”“我看，还是先附在人家馆里的好。”“为啥呢？”为来为去，还是为了芙蓉怕小兔儿没有那种专请一位先生来教导的福分，她最相信八字，连自己的终身，都相信是注定了偏房的命。胡雪岩意会到此，便有了办法。“我看这样，你先去替小兔儿排个八字看，到底福命如何？若是注定要做官的，就照我的话做，不然就随便你。”“这话说得好！你倒提醒我了。明天就替他去排个八字看。”美蓉去找了一张红纸，“劳动你把小兔儿的生辰八字写下来。”

“Now I think perhaps this isn’t right,” she said, wiping her eyes. “Little Rabbit may not have enough good fortune to bear it. Besides, all this trouble makes me uneasy.” Hu could only feel helpless. “One may believe in fortune-telling and face-reading, but there is no need to become so obsessed with them.” He scratched his head. “You are good in every other way. It is only here that you cannot take a broader view.” “I think he had better go on sharing another family’s tutor for the time being.” “Why?” Whatever form her reasoning took, it came back to the fear that Little Rabbit did not possess the good fortune needed to support having a teacher all to himself. She placed immense faith in the eight characters of a birth horoscope, even believing that her own fate had destined her to be a concubine. Once Hu understood this, he found a solution. “Then do this. First have Little Rabbit’s horoscope drawn up and see what sort of fortune he has. If he is destined to become an official, follow my advice. Otherwise, do as you please.” “That is a good idea! You have reminded me. I shall have it done tomorrow.” Furong found a sheet of red paper. “Please write down Little Rabbit’s birth particulars for me.”

写完小兔儿的生辰八字，也吃了消夜，上床在沈头上，芙蓉还有一桩“官司”要审，就是那方白缎绣花小包袱中，包着的一绺黑发，两片指甲。“这是哪里来的？”她说，“你用不着赖，也用不着说假话。”“听你的口气，当我一定要赖，一定要说假活。那，我就最好不说话，说了真话，你也一定不相信。”“我说不过你！”芙蓉有些着恼，“你不说，那包东西我不还你。”“你尽管拿去好了，不管拿它烧掉、摔掉，我决不过问。”“你不觉得心疼？”“心疼点啥？”胡雪岩泰然自若地，“你要不相信，我当面烧给你看！”“唉！”芙蓉叹口气说，“ ‘痴心女子负心汉’，我真替那个送你这些东西的人难过。”这句话却发生了意想不到的效用，胡雪岩大为不安，“你说”我别样，我都不在乎，就是这一样不能承认。“他加重语气分辩，”我决不是没有良心的人，对朋友如此，对喜欢过的女人，也是如此。“

After he had written down the particulars and they had eaten their late supper, they went to bed. On the pillow, Furong still had a “case” to try: the lock of black hair and the two nail clippings wrapped in embroidered white satin. “Where did they come from?” she asked. “There is no need to deny it or tell lies.” “From your tone, you seem certain that I shall deny it and lie. In that case I had better say nothing. Even if I tell the truth, you won’t believe me.” “I can’t argue with you!” Furong was a little cross. “If you won’t tell me, I shan’t give the packet back.” “Keep it by all means. Burn it or throw it away; I won’t ask what you have done with it.” “Wouldn’t that hurt you?” “What is there to hurt?” Hu was perfectly composed. “If you don’t believe me, I’ll burn it before your eyes.” Furong sighed. “‘A devoted woman and a faithless man.’ I really feel sorry for whoever gave you those things.” Her words had an unexpected effect. Hu became deeply troubled. “You can call me anything else and I won’t mind, but I cannot accept that.” He defended himself emphatically. “I am certainly not a man without a conscience, either towards my friends or towards the women I have loved.”

“这样说起来，你对这个女人是喜欢过的？”“不错。”胡雪岩已经从芙蓉的语气，料准了她不会吃醋，觉得直言不妨，所以又说，“就是前不久，我喜欢过，现在已经一刀两断。她不知道怎么，忽然‘冷镬里爆出热栗子’，在我决不能捡‘船并旧码头，的便宜。所以对这两样东西，我只当做不曾看见。”“你的话我弄不明白。”芙蓉问，“她叫啥名字，啥出身？”“叫阿巧姐。是堂子里的，七姑奶奶也见过。”芙蓉深为诧异：“七姑奶奶这样直爽的人，跟我无话不谈，怎么这件事不曾提起？”“你说话叫人好笑，直爽的人，就该不管说得说不得，都要乱说？”胡雪岩提醒她：“七姑奶奶真正叫女中豪杰，不要看她疯疯癫癫，胸中着实有点丘壑，你不要看错了她！“

“So you did love this woman?” “Yes.” Judging from Furong’s tone, Hu felt certain she would not be jealous and saw no harm in speaking plainly. “It was quite recently, but now I have made a clean break. Somehow she has suddenly made hot chestnuts pop in a cold pan. I certainly cannot take advantage of returning to an old berth. So I shall simply treat these things as though I had never seen them.” “I don’t understand you,” Furong said. “What is her name, and what is her background?” “Sister A’qiao. She comes from a pleasure house. The Seventh Lady has met her too.” Furong was very surprised. “The Seventh Lady is so outspoken and talks to me about everything. Why has she never mentioned this?” “What a funny thing to say. Must an outspoken person blurt out everything, whether it ought to be said or not?” Hu cautioned her, “The Seventh Lady really is a heroine among women. Do not be deceived by her wild ways. She has a good deal of judgement and strategy in her head. Don’t misjudge her.”

“好了，好了！你不要把话扯开去。你倒讲讲看，你们怎么样好法？”“就是这佯子！”胡雪岩翻个身，一把抱住芙蓉。“哼！”芙蓉冷笑，“看你这样子，心里还是忘不掉她，拿我来做替身！”说着，便要从他怀抱中挣扎出来，无奈他的力气大，反而拿她抱得更紧了，“我不是拿你做她的替身，我是拿你来跟她比一比。”他说，“她的腰没有你细，皮肤没有你滑。说真的，我还是喜欢你。”这两句话等于在醋罐里加了一大勺清水，酸味冲淡了，“少来灌米汤！”她停了一下又说，“你把跟她的事，从头到尾，好好讲给我听。”“讲起来话长！”胡雪岩从枕头下掏出表来看了一下说，“两点钟了！再讲就要讲到天亮，明天再说。““你不讲就害我了！”“这叫什么话？”“你不讲，害我一夜睡不着。”“好，我讲。”等把阿巧姐的故事，粗枝大叶讲完，胡雪岩又说，“这一来，你可以睡得着了，不许再罗嗦！”

“Enough, enough! Don’t change the subject. Tell me how close you two were.” “Like this!” Hu rolled over and gathered Furong into his arms. “Hmph!” She gave a cold laugh. “I can see that you still cannot forget her. You’re using me as her substitute.” She tried to free herself, but he was stronger and held her all the tighter. “I’m not using you as a substitute. I’m comparing you with her,” he said. “Her waist isn’t as slender as yours, and her skin isn’t as smooth. To tell you the truth, I like you better.” Those words were like a great ladle of water poured into a vinegar jar: they diluted the sourness. “Stop feeding me flattery!” After a pause she added, “Tell me the whole story, properly, from beginning to end.” “It’s a long one.” Hu took his watch from beneath the pillow and looked at it. “Two o’clock! If I start now, we shall be talking till dawn. Tomorrow.” “If you won’t tell me, you’ll make me suffer.” “What do you mean?” “You’ll keep me awake all night.” “Very well, I’ll tell you.” When he had given her a rough account of A’qiao’s story, he said, “Now you can sleep. No more questions!”

“问一句话可以不可以？”“可以。不过只许一句。”“照你看，”芙蓉问，“事情会不会起变化？”“什么变化？”“阿巧姐只怕不肯嫁何学台了。”芙蓉从容分析，“照你的说法，她先对你也不怎么样，等到见了年纪轻、人又漂亮、官又做得大的何学台，心里就有了意思。照规矩说，她自己也要有数，是人家何家的人了，在你面前要避嫌疑，怎么又在替你收拾行李的时候，私底下放了这两样‘私情表记，？而且送你上了船，推三阻四，不肯下船，恨不得跟你一起回来。这你难道看不出来，她的心又变过了。““我怎么看不出来？不理她就是了。”“你倒说得容易！可见你不懂女人的心。”这一下，胡雪岩使不能不打破自己的戒约，往下追问：“女人的心怎么样？”“男人是没良心的多，见一个，爱一个，爱一个，丢一个，女人不同，一颗心飘来飘去，不容易有着落，等到一有着落，就象根绳子一样，捆得你紧紧地、再打上个死结，要解都解不开。现在你是让她捆住了，自己还不晓得，说什么‘不理她就是’，有那么容易？你倒试试看！”芙蓉讪笑地又说，“真正是‘吃的灯草灰，放的轻巧屁’！”

“May I ask just one?” “Yes, but only one.” “Do you think,” Furong asked, “that things might change?” “What sort of change?” “I’m afraid A’qiao may refuse to marry Commissioner He.” Furong analysed the matter calmly. “From your account, she wasn’t especially attached to you at first. Then she saw Commissioner He, young, handsome and highly placed, and took a fancy to him. By rights she ought to recognise that she belongs to the He household now and avoid any appearance of impropriety with you. Why, then, did she secretly put these two tokens of affection into your luggage? And when she saw you onto the boat, she found one excuse after another not to get off. She plainly wanted to come back with you. Can’t you see that her heart has changed again?” “Of course I can. I simply won’t take any notice of her.” “You make it sound so easy! It shows how little you understand a woman’s heart.” Hu could no longer keep to his own limit on questions. “Then what is a woman’s heart like?” “Most men have no conscience. They love every woman they see, then cast aside every woman they love. Women are different. A woman’s heart drifts about and rarely finds somewhere to settle. Once it does, it is like a rope that binds you tight and is tied in a knot you can never undo. She has bound you already, and you don’t even know it. You say, ‘I simply won’t take any notice of her.’ Is it that easy? Try it and see!” With a mocking laugh Furong added, “You’ve eaten lamp-wick ash and now you’re passing airy wind—such weightless talk!”

这一番话把胡雪岩的瞌睡虫赶得光光的，睁大了眼，望着帐顶，半晌做声不得。“你说，我的话错不错？”“岂但不错！还要谢谢你，亏得你提醒我。”胡雪岩不安地问，“你看，该怎么办？”“自然是把她接了回来。”这是句反话，如果在平时，胡雪岩一定又会逗她拈酸吃醋，开开玩笑，此时却无这种闲逸的心情，一本正经他说：“这是决不会有的事。我现在就怕对何学台没有交代，好好一件事，反弄得人家心里不痛快，对我生了意见，说都说不明白了！“芙蓉是有心试探，看他这样表示，心头一块石头落地，便全心全意替他策划：“你现在要抢在前面，不要等她走在你前面叫明了，事情就会弄僵，人人要脸，树树要皮，话说出口，她怎么收得回去？”“这话对！”胡雪岩说，“我现在脑筋很乱，不晓得怎么快法？”“无非早早跟何学台说明，把阿巧接了回去，生米煮成熟饭，还有啥话好说。”“话是有道理。不过官场里有样规矩你不懂，做哪个地方的官，不准娶哪个地方的女子做妾，麻烦就在这里。”

This drove every trace of sleep from Hu. His eyes widened as he stared at the top of the bed curtains, unable to speak for a long while. “Well? Am I right?” “More than right. I ought to thank you. It’s fortunate you warned me.” Uneasily, he asked, “What do you think I should do?” “Naturally, bring her back here.” It was an ironic answer. Ordinarily Hu would have teased her into jealousy for his own amusement, but now he had no leisure for such play. He replied in complete seriousness, “That will certainly never happen. What worries me is being unable to account for it to Commissioner He. Here is a perfectly good arrangement that might end by upsetting him and turning him against me. I should never be able to explain.” Furong had deliberately tested him. His answer lifted a weight from her heart, and she devoted herself fully to helping him devise a plan. “You must act first. Don’t wait for her to get ahead of you and announce her refusal. Then everything will be deadlocked. People need face just as trees need bark. Once she has said it aloud, how can she take it back?” “That is true. My thoughts are all in confusion now. I don’t know how to act quickly enough.” “Simply tell Commissioner He at once and have him take A’qiao into his household. Once the raw rice is cooked, what more can anyone say?” “That makes sense. But there is an official rule you don’t understand: an official may not take a woman from the area he governs as a concubine. That is where the difficulty lies.”

谈到官场的规矩，芙蓉就无法置喙了。但即使如此，她的见解对胡雪岩仍旧是个很大的帮助。第二天一早醒来，首先想到的也就是这件事，大清早的脑筋比较清醒，他很冷静地考虑下来，认为“生米”虽不能一下子就成“熟饭”，但米只要下了锅，就不会再有变化，于今为计，不妨托出潘叔雅做自己的代表，先向何桂清说明白，事成定局，阿巧姐自会死心，这就是将“生米”下锅的办法。不过，这件事还要个居问奔走的人。现成有个周一鸣在那里，不然还有刘不才，也是千这路差使的好材料。好在事情一时还下会生变，不妨等周一鸣回来了再说。

Furong could offer no opinion on the rules of officialdom. Even so, her insight had been a great help. When Hu woke early the following morning, this was the first matter on his mind. His head was clearer at that hour. Considering it calmly, he concluded that although the raw rice could not immediately become cooked rice, once it was in the pot there would be no turning back. The best course was to ask Pan Shuya to act as his representative and explain the arrangement to He Guiqing first. Once the matter was settled, A’qiao would resign herself to it. That would put the raw rice in the pot. Someone would still be needed to carry messages between them. Zhou Yiming was ready to hand; failing him, Liu Bucai was also well suited to that kind of errand. Since the situation was unlikely to change immediately, Hu could wait until Zhou returned before proceeding.
