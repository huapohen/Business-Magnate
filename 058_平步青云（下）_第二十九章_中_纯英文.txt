A Swift Rise to Prominence, Volume III — Chapter Twenty-Nine, Part Two

Hu therefore kept Gu Yingchun with him and used Miaozhu’s dressing table as a desk. She was as good as her word. After providing paper, brushes and tea, she sent the maids to bed and withdrew into the adjoining room herself. “Gu,” Hu said quietly, sitting on the edge of the bed, “this whole amnesty business was only settled tonight, when Changgen came back. I’ll tell you everything that happened. Please write a letter to Education Commissioner He for me, and tomorrow morning Old Zhou can take it there specially.” “Aren’t you about to return to Suzhou yourself? Wouldn’t it be better to speak to him in person?” “When the situation was uncertain and nothing was settled, it was better not to leave anything in writing. As matters stand now, a formal written account is needed to show that we take it seriously. Besides, Commissioner He still has to discuss it with the military affairs office. An oral message might be misunderstood. Put it clearly on paper, and there can be no mistake.” By the time the long letter was finished, the clock was striking three. It was near the summer solstice, when the days were longest and the nights shortest. A faint dawn was showing outside, though the deep night was still cool as water. Imagining Miaozhu fast asleep, Hu did not want to wake her and suggested that he and Gu share the large bed. “Why should we?” Gu laughed. “With such warm, fragrant beauty waiting to fill your arms, it would be a shame to waste Heaven’s gifts. Naturally, I shall take the small bed and you two the large one.” The remark stirred Hu, and he changed his mind. “You take the large bed yourself. I’ll squeeze in beside her.” “There are pleasures in being squeezed together too. As you please.” Gu undressed and got into bed.

Hu quietly pushed open the adjoining door. A flame still flickered, and the gauze curtains hung half drawn. Wrapped in a lined coverlet, Miaozhu lay facing the wall, snoring faintly. He tiptoed in, closed the door softly, undressed, put out the light and felt his way into bed beside her. He had no wish to disturb her, but could not settle his own thoughts. In her hair she wore a string of pearl orchids, flowers particularly suited to a pillow: mingling with a woman’s hair oil, their scent grew fuller, rich and alluring enough to set the heart astir. Crowded into the little bed, Hu also recalled his time with A’zhu aboard Old Zhang’s Wuxi fast boat. Those memories brought a sense of melancholy and left him still more restless. Without realising it, he turned from side to side and woke Miaozhu. Discovering a man beside her in her sleep naturally frightened her. As though possessed, she suddenly raised herself halfway, clasped her arms across her body and stared sideways with wide eyes. “It’s you!” She let out a breath. “You gave me such a fright.” “You might say you gave me one!” Hu laughed. “Sneaking about like a ghost! Why creep in like that?” “Creeping about makes it more amusing.” He took her arm and drew her down again. “I didn’t mean to wake you. I simply couldn’t sleep.”

“Where is Mr Gu?” “In the large bed. He has only just lain down too.” “Then he may still be awake. Keep your voice down.” She asked, “Is the letter finished?” “Of course. We wouldn’t go to bed before finishing it.” “Who was it to?” “Someone in Suzhou.” “Aren’t you going back to Suzhou? Why write, then? Does that mean you’ll stay another couple of days?” The succession of questions plainly revealed her wish to keep him. Hu thought that the truth would only upset her again, so he answered vaguely, “Well, well, nothing is fixed.”

Miaozhu began asking about his family. Since her tone suggested nothing more than idle conversation to pass the time, Hu was unguarded. He told her frankly that his elderly mother was still living, that he had a wife and a concubine, and that he had no son yet. “Third Master Liu is so shrewd and capable. I imagine your ‘Huzhou wife’ must be formidable too.” “Not in the least. She is goodness itself, a truly gentle soul.” “That is your good fortune.” “Thank you.” Hu smiled with some satisfaction. “My luck is not bad.” “It is your Huzhou wife’s good fortune too.” “Not necessarily.” “To marry someone as capable and considerate as you, Master Hu, and live comfortably without a care about food or clothing! You are so well liked, too. Wherever you go, there is lively company and every distinction. Isn’t that good fortune?” “When I’m easy to deal with, I’m very easy. When I’m difficult, I can be very difficult indeed.” “I can’t see that. You seem the most accommodating of men.” “Being accommodating has its drawbacks. It is easy to get involved with women elsewhere. Someone jealous could be driven wild.” “It is perfectly ordinary for a successful man to have several wives and concubines.” Miaozhu suddenly asked, “If you have a Huzhou wife, surely you have a Shanghai wife and a Suzhou wife too?” “Not yet. I haven’t happened to meet anyone who suits me.”

Miaozhu longed to bring her face close and ask, “What do you think of me?” But she could not bring herself to offer herself so openly, and feared that he might think less of her. She held back. Then she reflected: with all her qualities, in what way was she inferior to anyone else? For him to say in her presence that he had not met anyone who suited him was rather hard to bear. “Then what sort of person would suit you?” Hu now heard where this was leading and chose his answer carefully. “That is hard to say,” he replied, deliberately evading her. “Beauty lies in a lover’s eyes. There is no fixed rule.” That left her nothing to say. She could hardly demand, “Am I not your lover?” Even if she could bring herself to ask, what pleasure would there be in it? Her heart was wholly devoted to him, yet he treated her as a roadside willow or a flower on a wall, to be plucked and thrown away at will. It chilled her. With that thought came an inexpressible sense of injustice. Sorrow filled her heart, her eyes grew hot, and tears began to roll down.

Their cheeks were touching. Both looked up at the bed curtains, so Hu could not see her crying, but her warm tears ran onto his right cheek. He could hardly fail to notice. Turning towards her, he exclaimed in alarm, “Why, you’re crying again! What is it?” “I have something on my mind. You don’t understand.” “What has touched your feelings this time?” “I was thinking that Sister Zhen will soon have a settled home. Seventh Master Li told her that once he has accepted the amnesty and become an official, he means to live properly and accomplish something. He plans to take her into his household. We are only a year apart and have never been separated since childhood. Now she will go her way and I mine. How can I help being sad?” “So it is sisterly affection.” Hu smiled. “I have an idea. Why don’t you marry Seventh Master Li along with your sister?” It was a disastrous remark. Miaozhu’s tears poured out like a river bursting its banks. She jerked round to face the wall, pulled the coverlet over her head and began sobbing aloud. Hu regretted it bitterly, yet was too bewildered to understand why a single joke should distress her so much. There was no time to examine the question. He explained as gently as he could, then apologised, asking only that she stop crying. Instead, her sobbing grew louder. At last there was a sound at the door. Gu had been woken and had come to ask what was wrong. “Listen!” Hu nudged her. “You’ve woken him.” She paid no attention. In fact she welcomed a third person who might mediate, giving her hopes a chance of fulfilment. She cried harder still. “The more you’re supported, the drunker you get!” Helpless, Hu rose to open the door.

“What has happened?” Gu asked as he entered, studying Hu’s face. It wore an expression between laughter and tears. “How should I know? We were talking quite pleasantly, and suddenly she said she couldn’t bear her sister leaving the profession to marry, and became upset.” Before he had finished, Miaozhu threw back the coverlet. “You heartless wretch!” she said bitterly, then turned away again and covered her face to weep. She was deliberately creating a mystery for Gu to investigate, and he duly fell into the trap. “Little Uncle, how have you offended her? She seems to hate you so much. I can’t understand it.” “Neither can I.” Hu could only pretend ignorance. He did not want Gu to intervene, so he shifted aside and gestured towards the door. “It is almost daylight. Go back to bed. You haven’t much time left to sleep.” At this, Miaozhu raised her voice, as though losing the third person would leave her helpless and unable to obtain redress. Gu hesitated. “What is it really about?” “She wants to belong to me, but won’t discuss it properly. Instead, she goes through the whole performance—first tears, then a scene, then threats to hang herself. Isn’t it absurd?” Taken by surprise, Gu said without thinking, “But that is a good thing!” “Good things take time and trouble. We must talk it over slowly and work things out before we can agree.” Hu yawned and again urged him to leave. “Please go. I want to sleep too.”

Once Gu left, Miaozhu stopped crying. Hu had at least given some indication of his position, and she waited for him to talk. Instead, he blew out the lamp, got into bed and said nothing. They had reached a deadlock. Ashamed and angry, and encountering his methods for the first time, she found her love turning to resentment. Unable to turn her thoughts aside, she pursued them deeper into a blind alley. The more she thought, the angrier she grew and the less life seemed worth living. When she looked at Hu and heard him snoring loudly, her thoughts were driven to the brink. Quietly she rose, found a sash through her tears and brought a chair to the foot of the bed. She tied the sash to the rail above the bed and put her head into the loop. Hu’s snoring was feigned. He was deliberately ignoring her to escape her entreaties. He knew that she had climbed over him and got up, though not what she was doing. Suddenly the bed shook. He opened his eyes and was horrified by what he saw. Leaping out barefoot, he caught her lower body and lifted her until the loop came free. Her breath had only just begun to fail. Once she had put her head into the loop, she had realised too late that she regretted it; in that instant everything in the world had seemed precious and everyone dear. Now, rescued, she forgot his coldness. Her whole heart was filled with gratitude. She threw her arms about his head and burst into uncontrollable sobbing. This woke not only Gu but Miaozhen and the people in both courtyards. They hurried over, though, uncertain of their welcome, most remained at the doors and windows, peering in and whispering. Miaozhen alone pushed straight inside. She found her sister sobbing on the bed and Hu sitting barefoot in a white foreign-cotton undershirt and trousers, looking thoroughly embarrassed. Gu stood to one side as though faced with an impossible problem. Miaozhen could only ask him, “Mr Gu, what is all this about? Has Miaozhu offended Master Hu?” Gu did not answer. He pursed his lips and glanced upwards. Following his gaze, she saw the white silk sash tied to the vermilion bed rail. Had Miaozhu tried to take her life? She wondered, but could not bring herself to ask. It seemed too incredible.

Hu was deeply troubled and divided in his feelings, but his mind was exceptionally clear. To meet whatever difficulties might follow, he felt he must first establish a firm position on the rights and wrongs of the matter. He therefore put on a stern expression. “Sister Zhen, there is something I must ask you. We have only just met, but with Seventh Master Li connecting us, none of us is a stranger. I come to Tongli as a guest, and Miaozhu wants to involve me in a court case over a death. I really cannot understand why she should treat me like this.” Miaozhen was alarmed, and even Gu thought the accusation excessive. He hastened to intervene. “Little Uncle, you mustn’t put it like that.” “There is nothing wrong with what I said.” Hu deliberately appeared aggrieved. “Put yourself in my place. If she had stopped breathing, I would have been dragged into a case over her death for no reason at all. Is that a matter to joke about?” Miaozhen still understood only half of it. She could not fathom why her sister had tried to hang herself or how she had survived. Hearing Hu speak this way, she felt sincerely apologetic. “Master Hu, I suppose Miaozhu must have offended you. Please don’t be angry. Let me ask her. Afterwards I shall kowtow and apologise.” “Very well!” Hu took the opportunity to rise. “Ask her how I have wronged her. We have known one another for only three days. What terrible grievance could she have against me, to do this?” On the bed, Miaozhu felt both remorse and injustice, and cried still more bitterly. Gu pitied her, but did not yet understand Hu’s intentions and could say little. He merely accompanied him outside.

“Little Uncle, how did it come to this? What did you say that hurt her enough to make her try to hang herself?” “Quiet!” Hu reproached him. “You ought to be helping me keep up the performance. Why do you speak for the other side whenever you open your mouth?” Gu gave a wry smile, then said as if to himself, “Why hasn’t Changgen appeared? I’ll find him.” Hu’s silence signified consent. Gu went outside, ignoring the many idlers who drew away at the sight of him, and continued straight to Miaozhen’s courtyard. “Where is Seventh Master Li?” he asked a maid. “He didn’t stay here last night. He went back to Shengze during the night, but he’ll return by noon.” Gu had to go back. Miaozhen was now talking to Hu. Both looked at him expectantly when he entered, as though hoping for his help. “Mr Gu, you must say what is fair.” Miaozhen sounded aggrieved from the outset. “My sister has high standards. No customer has ever caught her eye. Today she was ready to give up her life for Master Hu. Surely that alone deserves some answer from him.” “Not so fast.” Hearing resentment in her tone, Gu first tried to soothe her. “We can discuss it calmly. Come over here and tell me what has happened.” She followed him aside and stated her request briefly. Miaozhu had sworn to marry no one but Hu, while Hu had refused outright and seemed unwilling to reconsider. She hoped Gu would see justice done. But how could he do that? Whatever the circumstances, he could only advise Hu, who was free to listen or not. He was puzzled too. Hu was usually so accommodating. Even if he could not accept this affection, he ought to decline gently. Why had he spoken so harshly that Miaozhen herself was angry? “Wait here. Let me ask our Little Uncle first.” Hu had another account. To him, Miaozhen’s words bordered on coercion. Besides, practical circumstances made it impossible to agree. Exhausted in body and mind by the past few days, he had no patience for further entanglement and had refused simply and directly. He too seemed to be acting partly out of resentment. Yet Miaozhen’s angry words were understandable: she loved her sister and felt her pain. Hu’s exhaustion and short temper were understandable as well. Both were in a bad mood, and afterwards they could come to understand one another, helped by someone else’s explanation. The real question was what he meant by saying that practical circumstances made agreement impossible. Gu had to ask.

“Little Uncle, tell me what the difficulty is. Is it that our Auntie at home would never agree?” “Exactly. There has been a great quarrel over Furong, and it still hasn’t been settled. Why should I invite more trouble?” Gu thought for a while. “There must be a way to arrange that. What matters most is whether you like Miaozhu.” Hu found this hard to answer. He did not want to deny his own feelings, but neither would he openly admit them. He changed the subject. “There is another thing. I brought Furong on this journey. To take up with someone else while she is here—think how she would feel. Besides, how could I explain it to Third Master Liu?” Gu, seeing more clearly from outside, understood at once. Hu did like Miaozhu. His wife in Hangzhou was no insuperable obstacle; it was Furong who prevented this happy arrangement for the moment. “You are speaking honestly. I understand.” Gu warned him, “Miaozhu is completely devoted to you. If her hopes come to nothing, she may try again. Why burden yourself with a death when you could be living happily? It would be a terrible price.” The word “death” startled Hu. Deeply uneasy, he rubbed his hands. “Are there really people so foolish that they will give up their lives?” “There may be.” Gu’s face grew serious. “If she had died the first time, that would have been one thing. If something happens a second time, you will have stood by and let her die. Your conscience will never be easy.” Hu had scarcely slept all night. After these shocks and troubles, his head felt as though it would split, and his thoughts were clouded. He told Gu frankly that he must find somewhere quiet and sleep properly. He asked him to deal with the two sisters on his behalf and leave everything until the afternoon.

For quiet, the best place was still Zhu’s house. Hu entered by the back door, went to his room, told the servant assigned to him to refuse all visitors, shut the door firmly, undressed and got into bed. He was utterly exhausted and fell asleep as soon as his head touched the pillow. He did not rise until noon. Liu was waiting over tea in the outer room. Hearing movement, he knocked. As soon as Hu opened the door, he asked, “How did things come so close to someone dying?” After a good sleep, Hu’s feelings were steadier and his mind clearer. Instead of answering, he asked about Gu. “Has Gu come back?” “Yes. He was the one who told me.” “Then Elder Yu, You Laowu and the others know too?” “Naturally. They all think you are somewhat to blame.” Anyone else can accuse me of being heartless, Hu thought, but you, of all people, cannot. Aloud he asked, “And you?” “It makes no difference to me. Your affairs have nothing to do with me.” That indicated that if Hu really did marry Miaozhu, Liu would not object. What the future might bring was uncertain, but at least a small obstacle had been removed, which was some comfort. Still, this was a side issue. Hu decided to put it off with an accommodating manner for the present. Much serious business remained. He therefore made an immediate decision. “Go and pack. We shall return to Suzhou today, settle Changgen’s important business and hurry back to Shanghai.”

“I don’t think we can go today,” Liu replied. “I heard from You Laowu that they mean to give a banquet for you together tonight.” “A banquet?” Hu was surprised. “Why?” “They must have something to say. They’re shut up together now, discussing who knows what.” This reminded Hu of something. “Has Changgen arrived?” he asked quickly. “Of course. He has been the busiest man of all these past two days. I hear he went to Shengze early this morning and hurried back specially.” Hu nodded. “Today they have brotherhood affairs to discuss. Outsiders shouldn’t intrude, and we needn’t disturb them. Find Gu. We can go somewhere to eat and talk about our own business.” When Gu arrived, he suggested returning to Miaozhen’s. She knew that she had lost her composure and was deeply anxious. She had begged him to bring Hu to lunch so that she could apologise. Avoiding her would avoid trouble, but trouble often multiplied when one tried to escape it. Hu was in quite a different mood now. After a moment’s thought he answered decisively, “Very well. Let us hear what she has to say.” The three walked leisurely to her house. Her earlier haughtiness had given way to deference. “Please don’t be angry, Master Hu,” she kept saying. “Miaozhu is young and foolish. If nothing else, for Seventh Master Li’s sake, let us treat it as though nothing happened.” This wording made Hu uneasy. “Where is Miaozhu? Why don’t I see her?” “She’ll come. She’ll come. It’s getting late. Shall I serve the meal now, or would you like some refreshments first?” “No refreshments, and no wine either. Let us eat.” Gu had come expressly to act as a conciliator and agreed. “Quite right. In this heat, leave out the wine.” “Then let us have a little yangmei liquor,” Miaozhen suggested. “I steeped it last year and have been saving it. Today I shall offer it to Master Hu.” Gu promptly changed his tone. “That would be pleasant. Yangmei liquor dispels the summer heat. A cup would do no harm.”

Miaozhen arranged their places herself at a small round table with a marble top. The silver-mounted ebony chopsticks and Jingdezhen porcelain were particularly fine. After the cold dishes had been set out, she brought a white porcelain jar, removed its cloth seal and lifted the lid. A mellow, pure fragrance rose from the liquor. It was sorghum spirit steeped with white yangmei berries from the Dongting hills. The strength of the spirit had passed into the fruit, while the deep-red liquor itself tasted sweet and mild, slipped down easily and was especially suited to an early-summer midday. As they sat down and poured, Miaozhu appeared. She wore no rouge or powder; her glossy black hair was simply dressed, with a row of jasmine blossoms. Their fragrance reached the company before she did. She greeted Liu first, then the others, and finally said softly, “Master Hu.” Her eyes moved towards him, bright with tears. Apparently remembering that this was no time or place for grief, she mastered herself, lowered her head and sat beside him. No one, Hu included, referred to the dawn incident when her life had hung by a breath. Miaozhen exerted all her powers as a hostess, handling each matter with the ease of a musician plucking the strings while watching wild geese fly. The table became lively, yet no one’s eyes could quite leave Miaozhu. Whether still resentful or merely embarrassed, she kept her head lowered, occasionally raising her brows to steal a quick look at Hu and turning away before he could notice. No one could tell what she meant. Hu’s own feelings were complicated. Remembering his words and manner that morning, he thought he had been too harsh and felt guilty. Under the table he quietly reached for her hand. Alert to his movement, she moved it aside and left him grasping nothing. This touch of resentment only deepened his feeling. Smiling, he asked, “Still angry with me?” “How would I dare?” “It is not a question of daring,” Gu put in. “Miaozhu isn’t angry at all, are you?” “Of course not,” Miaozhen added. “Why should she be? Miaozhu!” She indicated Hu’s empty cup with a movement of her lips. Miaozhu hesitated, took the silver ladle from the jar and filled the cups, beginning with Liu’s and leaving Hu’s until last.

“Everyone else has berries. Why haven’t I?” Hu demanded deliberately. Without speaking, Miaozhu ladled out two berries, put them on a little dish and pushed it towards him. “Things one has to beg for don’t taste good. I don’t want them.” “I know you don’t!” She gave a cold laugh. “You simply find me tiresome.” “Miaozhu!” Her sister’s sharp call carried a warning. Hu felt uneasy. If the elder sister tried to discipline the younger and the younger answered back, it would spoil the whole occasion. Miaozhu did not answer back. Her eyes merely reddened and filled again, making her all the more pitiable. Miaozhen sighed and seemed about to complain or reproach her, but could not bear to speak. Women of the pleasure quarters were skilled at acting, all the more so when the performance drew on real feeling. This air of grief and near despair moved everyone. Liu, whose habit was to seek enjoyment, was the first to find it unbearable. Yet his position made it difficult to speak. He signalled to Gu to take matters in hand. Gu understood, but could hardly decide another man’s affairs. Nor could he urge Hu in front of the sisters not to disappoint her love, lest they mistake his advice for a promise on Hu’s behalf. After considering it, he could offer only general comfort. “Miaozhu, it has all happened rather suddenly. It isn’t that Master Hu doesn’t like you. He has difficulties of his own. Given time, things work themselves out. So long as both have affection, a happy union may come one day.” To Gu, this was a dumpling with no filling, a distant, indefinite assurance that said almost nothing. Miaozhu, however, found enlightenment in it. She loved listening to sung storytelling and reading songbooks in seven-character lines. Her mind held countless stories of gifted scholars and beautiful women whose devoted love overcame every obstacle and ended in reunion. Gu’s words were a key opening a treasure chest she had lost and found again. Exactly! Had Hu himself not said that good things took time and trouble? She would patiently wear down his resistance. However many edges and corners he possessed, she would smooth him until he rolled easily into her arms.

Her expression changed with that thought. Head bowed and eyes lowered, she silently considered her plan, hearing none of the conversation. The others were untroubled, but Hu was like a bird startled by a bowstring. Had she formed another foolish resolution? He had often heard it said that a person taking their own life, at the instant before death, remembered the splendour of the world and bitterly regretted it. Once rescued, such a person would never think of suicide again. If they truly did still wish to die, the resolve must be exceptional; anticipating another rescue, they would devise some means others could not prevent. Then death would be certain. The thought frightened him. He looked up quickly at Miaozhu. Yet she seemed composed and at ease, hardly as though planning to die. He was puzzled. “Miaozhu.” This time she did not refuse his outstretched hand. “What are you thinking about?” he asked directly. “I’m thinking…” Suddenly she smiled beautifully. “I shan’t tell you!” The smile brought Hu immense relief. All his fears vanished. To him, such a smile could never belong to someone contemplating suicide. Gu was relieved as well and teased her. “You mean to tell him, of course, but it will be more interesting in private. Isn’t that it?” Miaozhu did not answer. She took up the ladle, filled everyone’s cup again and raised her own, looking at her sister. “Sister Zhen, have a drink.”

“The older you get, the less you understand manners!” Miaozhen seemed half amused and half annoyed. “Why toast me instead of our honoured guests?” “Naturally, I have a reason.” “Tell us. What reason?” “Drink first, and then I’ll tell you. If my reason is no good, I’ll drink two cups for your one.” “Fair enough!” Liu put in. “I’ll be the witness. Miaozhen, drink first and hear what she says.” Miaozhen drained the half-cup before her, put it down and joined the others in watching her sister. What did she have to say that required such ceremony? Certain that she had hit upon an excellent idea, Miaozhu was perfectly composed. “Sister Zhen, since our parents died, you have looked after me. Now Seventh Master Li is going to become an official, and you will soon be an official’s wife. That drink was to congratulate you.” She looked at Liu and Gu. “Wasn’t it right for her to drink it?” “Yes, yes!” they answered together. “Enough,” Miaozhen urged. “If you have something to say about yourself, say it.” “That was a drink of celebration.” Miaozhu laughed with sly brightness. “I do have something very important to say, but you must drink another cup before I can say it.” “You wretched girl!” Miaozhen was amused and exasperated. She clenched her teeth. “I won’t fall for your tricks again.”

Everyone laughed at the sisters’ exchange except Miaozhu herself. “Really, it is important. Everyone can judge whether what I say is reasonable. If it isn’t, I’ll drink three cups for your one.” “That is bold!” Liu urged her on. “Miaozhen, you mustn’t let your sister beat you.” This sort of exchange made a meal lively. Miaozhen therefore assumed a serious air. “Master Liu, I’ll take your advice. But if she has no good reason, you must judge fairly.” “Of course!” He ladled more than half a cup for her himself. When she had finished it, Miaozhu asked, “Sister Zhen, you are climbing to a higher branch. What is to become of me, left all alone?” “Quite right!” Liu said at once. “A reasonable question.” Gu and Hu agreed, but their minds moved quickly. The question was not merely reasonable; it was formidable. Hu in particular felt as though a net were descending over him. Miaozhen had indeed been thinking along those lines. Having Hu take Miaozhu into his household would provide an excellent future for her. But that was a private plan she could not openly disclose, or Hu might resent it: so she was eager to leave the profession herself, yet could not abandon her duty to her sister, and therefore meant to trap him into taking it on. He might refuse for that very reason. This concern left her momentarily speechless. Miaozhu took her opportunity. “I know you are in a difficult position, Sister Zhen. I must make some plans for myself. Let me leave first.” “Leave?” Miaozhen asked in astonishment. “Where?” “I mean to move out.” Miaozhu spoke calmly and firmly. “I have earned my living this way for the last time.” Now everyone understood. Though she had not married out of the profession, she wished to leave it first. That was a good thing, but she needed somewhere to go.

“I have thought about where to live too,” she continued. “There are plenty of convents. Surely one will let me practise devotion without taking the tonsure, and work towards a better next life.” “How can you?” Liu shook his head. “So young, and talking like this! Won’t it break your sister’s heart?” “If you truly have the resolve to give up this livelihood, I approve,” Miaozhen said slowly. “You may move out first. But there is no need to live in a convent. Master Hu, don’t you agree?” Miaozhu seemed guileless, Hu thought, yet she had no end of manoeuvres. He would put her in check and see how she answered. “I don’t believe that someone so young has seen through the world and wants to cultivate a future life. If”—the first remark was harmless; the trouble lay in that “if”—“if she really wants to practise devotion, I’ll build her a private convent.” Everyone was startled. Both sisters changed colour, and Liu and Gu were deeply uncomfortable. The words went too far. Miaozhu felt anger, but still more hatred. How cruel he was! Even her attempt to die had not changed his mind, and now he wanted to force her into a convent. Yet she too was proud. To go back on her words would invite ridicule. Very well, she thought, I will match my stubbornness against yours. Impulsively, without further reflection, she rose and curtsied. “Master Hu, that is a promise. I thank you in advance.” “He was joking!” Liu exclaimed first. “Why take it seriously?” “It is certainly no joke.” Her face was deathly pale. “I understand his wishes. He would like nothing better than for me to cut off my hair here and now. I am sorry to disappoint him. Devotion is in the heart; it does not depend on becoming a nun.” The more unreasonable her accusation, the more clearly it revealed that her whole heart was fixed on Hu. Yet the situation was hardening into an impasse from which there seemed no escape. Hu regretted his rashness and was acutely embarrassed. Miaozhen and Liu could only interrupt and change the subject. Gu, however, suddenly saw a way of bringing the whole matter together. After a moment’s thought, he made a bold decision.

“Miaozhu.” He rose and beckoned. “Come here. I want to ask you something.” “Mr Gu, if you have something to say, say it here,” she refused bluntly. “Oh dear!” Gu deliberately rubbed his forehead. “What a sharp nail I’ve run into!” Though a joke, it contained a reproach. The first rule of the pleasure quarters was never to offend a guest, so Miaozhen apologised for her. “I’m so sorry, Mr Gu. She is young and knows no better. Please forgive her.” Then she sternly scolded her sister. “Can’t you tell good from bad? If Mr Gu wants a word with you, naturally he means well. Like the dog that bit Lü Dongbin, you don’t recognise a kind heart. Apologise at once!” Miaozhu knew she had been wrong, but could not immediately change her expression into one fit for an apology. Fortunately, Gu understood. “No apology is needed. Come, let us talk over here.” Speaking as he drew her aside, he gave her a way to yield. She followed and whispered, “Mr Gu, I really am sorry. I didn’t mean it.” “I know. No need to discuss that. Tell me”—he paused, then asked very seriously—“are you absolutely determined to take the name Hu?” She raised her large eyes for a quick glance at him, lowered her head and nodded faintly. “Very well. But it cannot happen at once. It may be half a year or a year, perhaps two or three. Can you wait?” “There is nothing I cannot wait for,” she answered very softly. “Then let Master Hu build you a private convent. You will only be practising devotion without taking the tonsure. Even if you really became a nun, you could grow your hair again and return to ordinary life. Think: if you live in a house built for you by a man named Hu, aren’t you already one of his household?” His words showed her the way out of confusion. More than that, knowing he was on her side gave her confidence. Her face brightened with smiles, and she whispered her thanks repeatedly. “Mr Gu, thank you, thank you!” “Keep what I’ve said to yourself.” “Yes. I understand.”

Even so, Miaozhu was no worldly, calculating nine-tailed fox. Her new happiness appeared on her face before she knew it. Miaozhen and Liu were naturally relieved to see her at ease. Hu alone felt divided. On the one hand, she had a face that charmed in pleasure and in anger alike; freed of sorrow, she looked more radiant than ever. He saw her and loved her. On the other, he feared that Gu had acted on his own and promised whatever she wanted. If those promises were beyond Hu’s power to fulfil, how would he settle the consequences? After a long period of uncertainty, he took advantage of Liu’s discourse on gambling to ask her quietly, “What did Mr Gu say to you?” Her bright eyes glanced sideways across his face. With deliberate indifference, she replied, “Something that cannot be told to a third person.” Since she was pretending, he would tease her. “Perhaps he has taken a fancy to you? Be careful. Mr Gu has a female Zhang Fei keeping him in order.” “A female Zhang Fei?” Her curiosity was aroused. “How did she get such a name? Tell me.” “Come.” Hu took the opportunity to draw her away. Behind a screen they sat side by side on a chaise longue. Naturally, the female Zhang Fei was forgotten. Yet neither had anything else to say. He held her hand and gazed at her; she lowered her head and remained silent.

“Master Hu,” she said at last, “you promised to build me a private convent. Will you keep your word?” “I was only joking.” Hu welcomed the chance to take it back. “Would I really do such a wrong, sending someone so young into a convent to live that sort of life?” Miaozhu gave a faint, cold laugh. “A convent would cost several hundred taels. Naturally, you can’t bear to part with the money.” Shrewd as he was, Hu did not realise that she was provoking his pride. He answered at once, “A few hundred taels are nothing. Even if we had only just met, giving you that much would be no great matter, let alone when we have been close.” “Then I thank you. When the convent is built, I shall set up a tablet to pray for your long life and prosperity.” “No, no! We shall hear no more about a private convent.” Miaozhu had not truly renounced the world or decided on a religious life. In these few moments, following Gu’s guidance, she had formed another plan. “Never mind that. Simply give me a few hundred taels to build a new house to live in. Will you?” “There is no question of whether I will. If you don’t believe me, I can give you the money now.” “There is no need. Your word is enough.” She extended her slender little finger, curved like a new moon. Hu hooked his little finger through hers. Then he put an arm about her waist and spoke from the heart. “Miaozhu, I don’t understand myself. I can’t bear to leave you, yet I’m afraid of you.” “Afraid of what? I’m not a man-eating tiger.” “Not a tiger, but a…” “A what?” He had meant to say a snake that coiled round a man. Having experienced her temper, he dared not be rash. The words reached his lips and retreated. When she pressed him, he still refused to say them and merely smiled.

“I know you’re afraid of me.” She seemed full of regret. “I know what my temper is like too, but I can’t change it.” Someone with self-knowledge was easier to deal with, Hu thought. Whatever happened in future, persuading her to moderate that fierce temperament would be a good thing. He began by complimenting her. “Miaozhu, speaking honestly, from Hangzhou to Shanghai and from Shanghai to Suzhou, I have never seen anyone with qualities finer than yours. You are quite beyond criticism.” “Enough! Don’t keep putting fine hats on my head. The higher you lift me, the harder I shall fall. Next you’ll tell me my faults.” Having been forestalled, Hu could hardly continue. Once again he could only smile. “Enough of that,” she said suddenly. “What arrangement do you like in a house?” The question was so abrupt that he needed a moment to understand it. Rather than expose its meaning, he asked in return, “What about you? What arrangement do you like?” “I like high, spacious, cool rooms, with plenty of open ground in front and behind.” “Then build it as you please. If you want a great garden with pavilions and terraces, that is beyond my means. But I can still give you an ordinary house. You will live there, not I. Naturally, it should be as you like it.” The last remark was deliberate, a discreet refusal to let her draw him closer. She understood, but after Gu’s advice she had resolved to persevere and wear down his resistance. She was not disappointed. Composed as ever, she asked, “How are houses arranged in Hangzhou?”

“Ordinary houses have side rooms at the front and back, with the principal building in the middle. There is a name for it: four dishes and a soup.” She found the description novel. Closing her eyes, she pictured the house from above: side rooms at the four corners, and a hall in the middle. It did indeed make that shape. She smiled. “Good. We’ll have four dishes and a soup too.” Hu understood this almost entirely one-sided assumption. It was better to stop; the more he said, the harder it would be to make his position clear. Again he only smiled. “You do know how to smile! Once, twice, three times.” The words had no obvious purpose, yet from her lips they had a special flavour. Hu was a skilful banterer and replied with a smile, “You surpass Qiuxiang, but unfortunately there is no Tang Bohu.” This too was a covert refusal. One day, she told herself, I shall make it impossible for you to get away. A sly smile appeared on her face. It put Hu on his guard again. What was she planning? As he studied her closely, she suddenly burst into laughter. This puzzled him still more. Yet one thing was clear: their earlier resentment had gone. There was no need to waste further effort on it. He would leave it for now.

They returned to the table and resumed their lively drinking. Lunch did not finish until four in the afternoon. Miaozhu excused herself and withdrew. Soon afterwards, a trusted maid who attended Miaozhen’s rooms came in, exchanged a glance with her mistress and drew her outside. She remained away for a long time. Neglecting guests was a grave fault in a pleasure house, especially guests as important as these. Though the men continued their wide-ranging talk, each privately wondered what was happening. Near dusk, a group of customers arrived. Amid the noise, one sentence from an attendant could be heard distinctly: “Second Miss has closed her rooms.” “Second Miss” meant Miaozhu. Closing her rooms was equivalent to taking down one’s nameplate at a Shanghai changsan house: it signified leaving the profession to marry. The would-be customers, finding that this celebrated flower had an owner, could only sigh in disappointment. Some naturally asked which wealthy admirer had offered a measure of pearls for this “wonderful pearl,” Miaozhu. The attendant answered that he did not know. Others might be uncertain, but the three men in Miaozhen’s room understood perfectly. Gu smiled. “Little Uncle, you are favoured by beauty indeed. Everywhere you go, someone loses her heart.” Hu could not smile. “I am no false moralist and needn’t pretend. Naturally, she has much to recommend her. But I really have no time to enjoy this good fortune.” He looked at Liu. “Third Master, why don’t you take over?” Liu shook his head vigorously. “You must be joking. How could I do such a thing? Even if it were possible for me to accept, her heart is fixed on you. Nine oxen couldn’t turn it. I couldn’t take her over if I tried.” “What a nuisance!” Hu sounded resentful. “Gu, you must have been her wretched strategist. Tell the truth: what rotten scheme did you suggest?” Gu thought for a moment. “Little Uncle, my advice is to ignore it and let things take their course. Then you will have neither trouble nor worry.” “That makes sense.” Hu nodded gravely. “I shall do as you say.” “I’m only afraid you won’t find it easy.” Surprised and displeased at this apparent reversal, Hu said, “Now I don’t understand.” “It is simple. As the saying goes, we cannot help having feelings; who can dismiss them? I fear you cannot put her out of your heart. If so, it would be better to face the facts.” Hu considered for a while. He did indeed find it hard to let her go, and could make no reply.
