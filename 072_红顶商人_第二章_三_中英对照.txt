胡雪岩｜中英对照版
原著：高阳

红顶商人｜The Red-Topped Merchant

第二章（本章第3部分，共4部分）

阅读说明：每段中文后接对应英文；两版英文正文一致。下载站水印已清除，原始疑字在文末说明。

怎么样？萧家骥在心中自问；要脱身，此时是大好机会，但外面的情况不清楚，糊里糊涂投入枪林弹雨中，死了都只怕没人知道，岂不冤枉？然而不走呢？别的不说，起码要见李秀成，就不是一下子办得到的；耽误了工夫不说，也许陆德义就死在这一仗中，再没有这样一个讲理的人可以打交道，后果更不堪设想。就在这样左右为难之际，只见院子外面又闪过一群人，脚步轻，语声也轻，但很急促，『快，快！』有人催促，『快「逃长毛」，逃到哪里算哪里？』『逃长毛』是句很流行的话，萧家骥听胡雪岩也常将这三个字挂在口头，意思是从长毛那里逃走；而『逃到哪里算哪里』，更是一大启示。『逃！』他对自己说，『不逃，难道真的要跟李秀成做军火生意？』

What should he do? Xiao Jiaji asked himself. If he meant to escape, this was an excellent opportunity. But he knew nothing of conditions outside. To blunder into a hail of bullets and die without anyone even knowing what had happened—what a senseless end that would be! Yet if he stayed, there was at least one obvious difficulty: he could hardly expect to meet Li Xiucheng at once. Apart from the time he would lose, Lu Deyi might be killed in this very battle, leaving him without such a reasonable man to deal with. The consequences did not bear thinking about. While he wavered, another group hurried past outside the courtyard. Their footsteps and voices were light, but urgent. “Quick, quick!” someone urged. “Get away from the Longhairs! It does not matter where we end up!” “Getting away from the Longhairs” was a common expression; Xiao had often heard Hu use it. It meant escaping from their hands. But “it does not matter where we end up” was a revelation. “Escape!” he told himself. “If I do not, am I really going to sell arms to Li Xiucheng?”

打字主意，更不怠慢；不过虽快不急，看清楚无人，一溜烟出了夹弄，豁然开朗，同时闻到饭香，抬头一看，是个厨房。厨房很大，但似乎没有人。萧家骥仔细察看着，一步一步走过院落，直到灶前，才发现有个人生在灶下烤火；人极瘦，眼睛大，骤见之下，形容格外可怖，吓得他倒退了两步。那人却似一个傻子，一双虽大而失神的眼，瞅着萧家骥，什么表情都没有。『你是什么人？』他问。『你不要来问我！』那人用微弱的声音答道∶『我不逃！逃来逃去逃不出他们的手；听天由命了。』听得这话，萧家骥的心凉了一半，怔怔地望着他，半晌无语。

His mind made up, he wasted no time, though he moved quickly rather than rashly. Making sure no one was about, he slipped down the narrow passage and emerged into an open space. The smell of cooking reached him. He looked up: a kitchen. It was large and apparently deserted. He examined it carefully, crossing the courtyard a step at a time. Only when he reached the stove did he discover a man sitting beside its base, warming himself. The man was terribly thin, with enormous eyes; coming upon him so suddenly, Xiao found his appearance frightening and recoiled two paces. The man merely stared, as if half-witted, his large, lifeless eyes showing no expression. “Who are you?” Xiao asked. “Do not bother asking me,” the man replied feebly. “I am not running away. Run where you will, you never get out of their hands. I shall leave it to fate.” Half of Xiao’s courage ebbed away. He stared at the man, unable to speak for some time.

『看你这样子，不是本地人；哪里逃来的？』看他相貌和善、而且说话有气无力，生趣索然似的，萧家骥便消除一恐怕戒备之心，老实答道∶『我从上海来。』『上海不是有夷场吗？大家逃难都要逃到那里去，你怎么反投到这里来？』那人用听起来空落落的绝望的声音说∶『天堂有路你不走，地狱无门闯进来！何苦？』『我也是无法，』萧家骥借机试探，却又不便说真话，『我有个生死至交，陷在杭州，我想进城去看他。』『你发疯了！』那人说道，『杭州城里人吃人，你那朋友，只怕早饿死了；你到哪里去看他？就算看到了，你又不能救他；自己陷在里头，活活饿死。这打的是什么算盘？真正气数。』话中责备，正显得本心是好的，萧家骥决定跟他说实话，先问一句∶『你老人家贵姓？』

“By the look of you, you are not from here. Where have you escaped from?” The man had a kindly face. His voice was weak, as though life held no interest for him, and Xiao’s fear and suspicion subsided. “I have come from Shanghai,” he answered truthfully. “Is there not a foreign settlement in Shanghai? Everyone fleeing the troubles wants to get there. Why have you come into this place instead?” His voice sounded hollow with despair. “Heaven’s road lay open, and you would not take it; hell had no gate, yet you forced your way in. Why bring such trouble on yourself?” “I had no choice.” Xiao used the opportunity to sound him out, though he dared not yet tell the truth. “I have a friend in Hangzhou, a man bound to me for life and death. I wanted to go into the city to see him.” “You are mad! People are eating each other inside Hangzhou. Your friend is probably dead of starvation already. Where would you go to see him? And even if you found him, you could not save him. You would be trapped yourself and starve to death. What sort of reckoning is that? Truly, it must be fate.” His reproaches revealed a good heart. Xiao decided to tell him the truth, first asking, “May I know your name, sir?”

『人家都叫我老何。』『老何，我姓萧，跟你老人家老实说吧，我是来救杭州百姓的——也不是我，是你们杭州城里鼎鼎大名的一位善人做好事；带了大批粮食，由上海赶来。教我到城里见王抚台送信。』萧家骥略停一下，摆出一切都豁出去的神态说∶『老何，我把我心里的话都告诉你，你如果是长毛一伙，算我命该如此，今年今月今日今时，要死在这里。如果不是，请你指点我条路子。』老何听他说完，沉思不语，好久，才抬起头来；萧家骥发觉他的眼神不同了，不再是那黯然无光，近乎垂死的人的神色，是闪耀着坚毅的光芒，仿佛一身的力量都集中在那方寸眸子中似的。

“Everyone calls me Old He.” “Old He, my name is Xiao. I shall be honest with you. I have come to save the people of Hangzhou—or rather, it is not I, but a philanthropist of great renown in your city who is doing this good deed. He has brought a large quantity of grain from Shanghai and sent me to see Governor Wang with a message.” Xiao paused briefly, then assumed the manner of a man staking everything on a single throw. “I have told you what is in my heart. If you belong to the Longhairs, then it is my fate to die here, in this year, this month, this day, and this hour. If you do not, please show me a way through.” Old He listened and sat in thought without speaking. After a long time he raised his head. Xiao saw that his eyes had changed. They no longer had the dull, almost dying look of before. They shone with determination, as though all the strength in his body had gathered within them.

他将手一伸∶『信呢？』萧家骥愕然∶『什么信？』『你不是说，那位大善人托你送信给王抚台吗？』『是的。是口信。』萧家骥说，『白纸写黑字，万一落在长毛手里，岂不糟糕？』『口信？』老何踌躇着，『口信倒不大好带。』『怎么？老何，』萧家骥了解了他的意思∶『你是预备代我去送信？』『是啊？我去比你去总多几分把握。不过，凭我这副样子，说要带口信给王抚台，没有人肯相信的。』『那这样，』萧家骥一揖到地，『请老何你带我进城。』『不容易。我一个人还好混；象你这样子，混不进去。』『那末，要怎样才混得进去？』

He held out his hand. “Where is the letter?” Xiao was taken aback. “What letter?” “Did you not say that this great benefactor had sent you with a message for Governor Wang?” “Yes, but it is a spoken message. If there were black words on white paper and they fell into the Longhairs’ hands, would that not be disastrous?” “A spoken message?” Old He hesitated. “That would be rather difficult to carry.” “What do you mean, Old He?” Xiao understood him. “Were you intending to take it for me?” “Yes. I should have a better chance than you. But looking as I do, no one would believe me if I said I had an oral message for the governor.” “Then let me ask you”—Xiao bowed to the ground—“to take me into the city.” “It will not be easy. I might slip through alone, but someone who looks like you could never get in.” “Then what must I do?”

『第一、你这副脸色，又红又白，就象天天吃大鱼大肉的样子，混进城里，就是麻烦。如果，你真想进城，要好好受点委屈。『』不要紧！什么委屈，我都受。『『那好！』老何点点头，『反正我也半截入土的了，能做这么一件事，也值！先看看外头。』于是静心细看，人声依旧相当嘈杂，但枪声却稀了。『官军打败了。』老何很有把握地说，『这时走，正好。』

“First of all, your face is pink and white, as if you ate fish and meat every day. If you got inside looking like that, there would be trouble. If you really mean to enter the city, you must put up with some unpleasantness.” “That does not matter. I shall put up with anything.” “Very well.” Old He nodded. “I have one foot in the grave already. If I can do something like this first, it will have been worthwhile. Let us see what is happening outside.” They observed quietly. The voices were still noisy, but the gunfire had slackened. “The government troops have been beaten,” Old He said confidently. “This is the right time to leave.”

萧家骥觉得这是件不可思议的事，听一听声音，就能判断胜负，未免过于神奇。眼前是重要关头，一步走错不得，所以忍不住问了一句∶『老何，你怎么知道？』『我早就知道了。』老何答道∶『官军饿得两眼发黑，哪里还打得动仗？无非冲一阵而已。』这就是枪声所以稀下来的缘故了。萧家骥想想也有道理，便放心大胆地跟着老何从边门出了长毛的公馆。果然，长毛已经收队，满街如蚁，且行且谈且笑，一副打了胜仗的样子。幸好长毛走的是大街，而老何路径甚熟，尽从小巷子里穿来穿去，最后到了一处破败的财神庙，里面是七八个乞儿，正围在一起掷骰子赌钱。

Xiao found it extraordinary that the man could judge the outcome of a battle merely by listening. This was a crucial moment; a single wrong step might be fatal. He could not help asking, “How do you know, Old He?” “I knew it from the beginning. The government troops are so hungry that their sight is going black. How can they fight? All they can do is make one charge.” That explained the dwindling gunfire. On reflection, Xiao found it reasonable, and followed Old He out through a side gate of the Longhairs’ headquarters with renewed confidence. Sure enough, the rebels were returning to their quarters. They filled the main streets like ants, walking, talking, and laughing with all the air of victors. Fortunately, Old He knew the neighborhood intimately. Keeping clear of the main streets, he led Xiao through one narrow lane after another until they reached a dilapidated temple to the God of Wealth. Inside, seven or eight beggars were huddled together, gambling with dice.

『老何，』其中有一个说，『你到没有死！』老何不理他，向一个衣衫略为整齐些的人说∶『阿毛，把你的破棉袄脱下来。』『干什么？』『借给这位朋友穿一穿。』『借了给他，我穿啥？』『他把他的衣服换给你。』这一说便有好些人争着要换，『我来，我来！』乱糟糟地喊着。

“So you are not dead yet, Old He!” one of them said. Old He ignored him and addressed a man whose clothes were slightly less ragged than the others’. “A’mao, take off that tattered padded jacket.” “What for?” “Lend it to this friend.” “And what am I to wear?” “He will give you his clothes in exchange.” At that, several men clamored to make the exchange themselves. “Me! Me!” they cried in confusion.

老何打定主意，只要跟阿毛换；他的一件破棉袄虽说略为整齐些，但厚厚一层垢腻，如屠夫的作裙，已经让萧家骥要作呕了。『没有办法。』老何说道∶「不如此就叫不成功。不但不成功，走出去还有危险。不要说你，我也要换。『听这一说，萧家骥无奈，只好咬紧牙关，换上那件棉袄，还有破鞋破袜。萧家骥只觉满身虫行蚁走般肉麻，自出娘胎，不曾吃过这样的苦头，只是已穿上身，就决没有脱下来的道理。再看老何也找人换了一身衣服，比自己的更破更脏，别人没来由也受这样一分罪，所为何来？这样想着，便觉得容易忍受了。

Old He had made up his mind to deal only with A’mao. Though the padded jacket was a little more presentable than the rest, it was coated in such a thick layer of grease and filth that it looked like a butcher’s apron. The sight alone made Xiao nauseous. “There is no other way,” Old He said. “Without this, we cannot succeed. Worse than that, we shall be in danger as soon as we go out. And it is not just you: I must change too.” Xiao had no choice. Gritting his teeth, he put on the jacket, along with torn shoes and socks. His whole body crawled as if insects and ants were moving over his skin. Never in his life had he endured anything like it. Yet now that the clothes were on, there could be no question of taking them off. Looking at Old He, he saw that the man too had exchanged clothes with someone and was wearing garments even dirtier and more ragged than his own. Why was another man, with no obligation to him, taking on this misery as well? Thinking of that made it easier to bear.

『阿毛！』老何又说∶『今天是啥口令？』『我不晓得。』『我晓得。』有人响亮地回答，『老何，你问它做啥？』『自然有用处。』老何回头问萧家骥∶『你有没有大洋钱，摸一块出来。』萧家骥如言照办；老何用那块银洋买得了一个口令。但是，『这是什么口令呢？』萧家骥问。

“A’mao,” Old He asked, “what is today’s password?” “I do not know.” “I know,” someone answered loudly. “What do you want it for, Old He?” “I have a use for it, naturally.” Old He turned to Xiao. “Have you any silver dollars? Take one out.” Xiao did as he was told, and Old He bought a password with the coin. “But what is this password for?” Xiao asked.

『进城的口令。』老何答道，『城虽闭了，城里还是弄些要饭的出来打探军情，一点用处都没有。』在萧家骥却太有用了；同时也恍然大悟，为何非受这样的罪不可？走不多远，遥遥发现一道木城；萧家骥知道离城门还有一半路程。他听胡雪岩谈过杭州十城被围以后，王有龄全力企图打开一条江路，但兵力众寡悬殊，有心无力。正好张玉良自富阳撤退；王有龄立即派人跟他联络，采取步步为营的办法，张玉良从江干往城里扎营；城里往江干扎营，扎住一座，坚守一座，不求速效而稳扎稳打，总有水到渠成，联成一气打开一线生路的时候。

“For entering the city,” Old He replied. “The gates may be closed, but they still send a few beggars out to gather intelligence. Not that it does any good.” To Xiao it was immensely useful. Now he also understood why he had had to submit to this ordeal. They had not gone far when a timber fort came into view in the distance. Xiao knew they were still only halfway to the city gate. Hu had told him how, after Hangzhou’s ten gates were surrounded, Wang Youling had strained every effort to open a route to the river. Vastly outnumbered, he had lacked the strength to do it. Just then Zhang Yuliang had withdrawn from Fuyang. Wang immediately sent men to contact him and proposed an advance by fortified stages: Zhang would build camps inward from the riverbank, while the defenders built outward from the city. Each position, once established, would be held firmly. They would seek no quick result, but proceed methodically until the two lines eventually joined and a route to survival opened.

由于王有龄的亲笔信，写得极其恳切，说『杭城存亡，视此一举，不可失机误事，』所以张玉良不敢怠慢，从江干外堤塘一面打、一面扎营，扎了十几座，遭到一条河，成了障碍，张玉良派人夺围进城，要求王有龄派兵夹击；同时将他扎营的位置，画成明明白白的图，一并送上。王有龄即时通知饶廷选调派大队进城；谁知饶廷选一夜耽误，泄潜心机密，李秀成连夜兴工，在半路上筑成一座木城，城上架炮。城外又筑土墙，墙上凿眼架枪，隔绝了张玉良与饶廷选的两支人马；而且张玉良因此中炮阵亡。这是胡雪岩离开杭州的情形，如今木城依旧，自然无法通过；老何带着萧着骥，避开长毛，远远绕过木城，终于见了城门。

Wang’s handwritten appeal had been exceptionally urgent: “The survival of Hangzhou depends upon this one undertaking. We must not lose the opportunity and ruin the attempt.” Zhang Yuliang had therefore dared not delay. Fighting as he advanced, he built more than a dozen camps from the outer embankment by the river, until another waterway barred his way. He sent men through the siege lines into the city, asking Wang to launch a coordinated attack and enclosing a clear map of his positions. Wang immediately instructed Rao Tingxuan to dispatch a large body of troops into the city. But Rao delayed for a night, and the secret leaked out. Li Xiucheng set his men to work overnight and erected a timber fort halfway along the route, with cannon mounted on it. Outside stood an earthen wall pierced with firing holes for muskets. This cut off Zhang’s forces from Rao’s, and Zhang himself was killed by cannon fire. That was the position when Hu left Hangzhou. The timber fort still stood and was plainly impassable. Old He led Xiao well around it, keeping clear of the Longhairs, until at last they saw a city gate.

『这是候潮门。』『我晓得。』萧家骥念道∶『「候潮」听得「清波」响，「涌金」「钱塘」定「太平」。』这两句诗中，嵌着杭州五个城门的名称，只有本地人才知道；所以老何听他一念，浮起异常亲切之感，枯干瘦皱，望之不似人形的脸上，第一次出现了笑容，『你倒懂！』他说，『哪里听来的？』萧家骥笑笑答道∶『杭州我虽第一次来，杭州的典故我倒晓得很多。』『你跟杭州有缘。』老何很欣慰地说，『一定顺利。』

“This is Houchao Gate.” “I know.” Xiao recited, “‘Awaiting the Tide’, one hears the ‘Clear Waves’ sound; ‘Surging Gold’ and ‘Qiantang’ make ‘Great Peace’ secure.” The couplet wove together the names of five of Hangzhou’s gates, a piece of lore known only to local people. Hearing it, Old He felt a sudden warmth toward him. For the first time, a smile appeared on the withered, shrunken, wrinkled face that scarcely seemed human. “So you know that! Where did you hear it?” Xiao smiled in return. “This may be my first visit to Hangzhou, but I know a good many of its stories.” “You have a bond with Hangzhou,” Old He said, much comforted. “Everything is sure to go well.”

说着话，已走近壕沟；沟内有些巡逻，沟外却有人伏地贴耳，不知在干什事？萧家骥不免诧异却步。『这些是什么人？』『是瞎子。』老何答道，『瞎子的耳朵特别灵；地下再埋着酒坛子，如有啥声音听得格外清楚。』『噢！我懂了。』萧家骥恍然大悟，『这就是所谓「瓮器」，是怕长毛挖地道，埋炸药。』『对了！快走吧，那面的兵在端枪了。』说着，老何双手高举急步而行；萧家骥如法而施，走到壕沟边才住脚。

As they spoke, they approached a ditch. Men were patrolling on its inner side; on the outer side, others lay with their ears pressed to the ground. Xiao did not know what they were doing and stopped in surprise. “Who are those men?” “They are blind,” Old He replied. “Blind men have particularly keen hearing. Wine jars are buried underground too, so they can hear any noise all the more clearly.” “Ah, I understand!” It dawned on Xiao. “These are the listening jars I have heard of. They are afraid the Longhairs will dig tunnels and plant explosives.” “Exactly. Come along—the soldier opposite is raising his gun.” Old He lifted both hands and hurried forward. Xiao copied him, stopping only when they reached the edge of the ditch.

『口令！』对面的兵喝问。『日月光明。』那个兵不作声了，走向一座轴驴，摇动把手，将一条矗立着的跳板放了下来，横搁在壕沟上，算是一道吊桥。

“Password!” the soldier challenged them from the other side. “The sun and moon shine bright.” Without another word, the soldier went over to a windlass and turned its handle. A plank that had been standing upright came down across the ditch to form a drawbridge.

萧家骥觉得这个士兵，虽然形容憔悴，有气无力，仿佛连话也懒得说似的，但依然忠于职守，也就很可敬了；由此便想∶官军的纪律，并不如传说中那样糟不可言。既然如此，何必自找麻烦，要混进城去。想到就说∶『老何！我看我说明来意，请这里驻守的军官，派弟兄送我进去，岂不省事？』老何沉吟了一下答道∶『守候潮门的曾副将，大家都说他不错的；不妨试一试。不过，』老何提出警告∶『秀才遇着兵，有理说不清，也是实话。到底怎么回事，你自己晓得；不要前言不搭后语，自讨苦吃。』『不会，不会！我的话，货真价实；那许多白米停在江心里，这是假得来的吗？』

Though the soldier looked haggard and exhausted, as if even speaking demanded too much effort, Xiao thought his continued devotion to duty worthy of respect. Perhaps the government troops’ discipline was not quite as dreadful as people said. If so, why make trouble for himself by trying to slip into the city? He spoke as soon as the thought occurred. “Old He, would it not be easier if I explained my purpose and asked the officer commanding here to send some men to escort me inside?” Old He considered. “Everyone says Deputy Commander Zeng, who guards Houchao Gate, is a good man. You might try. But remember”—he added a warning—“a scholar confronted with soldiers may find that reason gets him nowhere. You know the facts yourself. Do not contradict yourself and invite trouble.” “I shall not. Every word I say is true. All that white rice is waiting in the middle of the river. Could anyone invent that?”

听这一说，老何翻然改计，跟守卫的兵士略说经过，求见官长；于是由把总到千总，到守备，一层层带上去，终于候潮门见到了饶廷选的副将曾得胜。『胡道台到上海买米，我们是晓得的。』曾得胜得知缘由以后，这样问道∶『不过你既没有书信，又是外路口音，到底怎么回事，倒弄不明白；怎么领你去见王抚台？』萧家骥懂他的意思，叫声∶『曾老爷！请你搜我身子，我不是刺客；公然求见，当然也不是奸细。只为穿越敌阵，实在不能带什么书信，见了王抚台，我有话说，自然会让他相信我是胡道台派来的。如果王抚台不相信，请曾老爷杀我的头。我立一张军令状在你这里。』『立什么军令状？这是小说书上的话。我带你去就是。』曾得胜被萧家骥逗得笑了；不过他的笑容比哭还难看。『是！』萧家骥响亮地答应一声，立即提出一个要求，『请曾老爷给我一身弟兄的棉军服穿！』他急于脱卸那身又破又脏的衣服；但轻快不过片刻，一进了城，尸臭蒸熏，几乎让他昏倒。

Hearing this, Old He changed his plan. He gave the sentry a brief account and asked to see an officer. They were passed up the chain from a bazong to a qianzong, then to a garrison officer, until at last, at Houchao Gate, they met Zeng Desheng, a deputy commander under Rao Tingxuan. “We know Circuit Intendant Hu went to Shanghai to buy rice,” Zeng said after hearing the story. “But you have no letter, and you speak with an outsider’s accent. I cannot make out exactly who you are. How can I take you to Governor Wang?” Xiao understood his concern. “Sir, search me. I am no assassin, and a man openly asking for an audience is surely no spy. I could not risk carrying a letter through the enemy’s lines. Once I see Governor Wang, I can tell him something that will prove Hu sent me. If he does not believe me, you may have my head. I shall leave a written military pledge with you.” “A military pledge? That is something out of a novel. I shall take you.” Xiao had made Zeng laugh, though the smile looked more painful than weeping. “Thank you, sir!” Xiao answered briskly, then immediately made a request. “Please give me a padded uniform like your soldiers’ to wear.” He was desperate to shed those filthy rags. But his relief lasted only a moment. Once inside the city, the reek of corpses rose about him so powerfully that he nearly fainted.

王有龄已经绝望了！一清早，杰纯冲过一阵——就是萧家骥听到枪声的那时刻；十几船活命的白米等着去运，这样的彭励，还不能激出士兵的力量来，又还有什么人能开粮通道，求得一线生路？因此，他决定要写遗折了∶窃臣有龄前将杭城四面被围，江路阻绝，城中兵民受困各情形，托江苏抚臣薛焕，据情代奏，不识能否达到？现在十门围紧，贼众愈聚愈多，迭次督同饥军，并密约江干各营会合夹击，计大小昼夜数十战，竟不能开通一线饷道。城内粮食净尽，杀马饷军，继以猫鼠，食草根树皮，饿殍载道，日多一日，兵弁忍饥固守，无力操戈。

Wang Youling had lost hope. Jiechun had launched an attack early that morning—the very moment when Xiao had heard the gunfire. More than a dozen boats of life-saving white rice lay waiting to be unloaded. If even that incentive could not summon the soldiers’ strength, who could possibly open the supply route and win them a chance of survival? Wang therefore decided to write his final memorial to the throne: “Your servant Youling previously entrusted Governor Xue Huan of Jiangsu with the task of reporting on my behalf that Hangzhou was surrounded on all sides, the route to the river blocked, and the troops and people trapped within the city. I do not know whether that report has reached Your Majesty. All ten gates are now closely besieged, and the rebel host grows ever larger. Repeatedly we have led our starving troops into battle and secretly arranged coordinated attacks with the camps along the riverbank. In several dozen engagements, great and small, by day and night, we have failed to open even a single supply route. The city’s food is entirely exhausted. Horses have been slaughtered to feed the soldiers, followed by cats and rats; roots and tree bark are eaten. The roads are strewn with the starved dead, whose numbers increase daily. Officers and men endure hunger and hold their posts, but they no longer have the strength to wield their weapons.”

初虞粮尽内变，经臣等涕泣拊循，均效死相从，绝无二志，臣等奉职无状，致军民坐以待毙，久已痛不欲生。

“At first we feared that the exhaustion of supplies would bring mutiny within. But after we pleaded with the men in tears and sought to comfort them, all pledged to follow us to the death, without a disloyal thought. We have discharged our duties so poorly that troops and people must sit awaiting death. Long have we suffered such anguish that life is unbearable.”

写到这里，王有龄眼痛如割，不能不停下笔来。他这眼疾已经整一年了，先是『心血过亏，肝肠上逼，脾经受克，肺气不好』，转为『风火上炎』而又没有一刻能安心的时候，以致眼肿如疣，用手一按，血随泪下；见到的人，无不大骇。后来遇到一位眼科名医，刀圭与药石兼施，才有起色；但自围城以来，旧疾复发，日重一日，王有龄深以为恨，性命他倒是早已置之度外，就这双眼睛不得力，大是苦事。

At this point the pain in Wang’s eyes became so sharp that he had to lay down his brush. He had suffered from the ailment for a whole year. At first it had been described as “an excessive depletion of the heart’s blood, upward pressure from the liver and bowels, affliction of the spleen channel, and weakness of the lung’s qi.” It had then developed into “wind and fire flaming upward.” Without a moment’s peace of mind, his eyes had swollen into wartlike lumps; when pressed, they shed blood with the tears. Everyone who saw them was horrified. Later, a renowned eye physician had treated him with medicines and other remedies, and there had been some improvement. Since the siege began, however, the old trouble had returned, worsening day by day. Wang bitterly resented it. He had long ceased to care about his life, but the failure of his eyes was a cruel affliction.

如果是其他文报，可以口授给幕友子侄代笔，但这通遗折，王有龄不愿为人所见，所以强睁如针刺般疼痛的双眼，继续往下写∶第残喘尚存，总以多杀一贼，多持一日为念，泣思杭城经去年兵燹之后，户鲜盖藏，米粮一切，均由绍贩运；军饷以资该处接济为多。金、兰这法后，臣等早经筹计，须重防以固宁绍一线饷源，乃始则饬宁绍台道张景渠，继又迭饬运司庄焕文，记名道彭斯举，各带兵勇设防，均经王履廉议格不行；又复袒庇绅富，因之捐借俱穷，固执已见，诸事掣肘。臣等犹思设防堵御，查有廖守元与湖绅赵景贤，历守危城，一载有余，调署绍兴府，竭筹布置。乃违大绅不愿设防之意，诬以通贼痛殴，履谦从旁袖手；比及城陷而走，卒致廖宗元城亡与亡，从此宁绍各属，相继失陷，而杭城已为孤注，无可解救矣！

Other official papers could have been dictated to a secretary, a son, or a nephew. But Wang did not wish anyone to see this last memorial. Forcing open eyes that felt as though needles pierced them, he continued: “While a breath remains in us, our sole thought is to kill one more rebel and hold out one more day. We reflect with tears that, after last year’s devastation by war, few households in Hangzhou retained any reserves. Rice and other provisions were all brought from Shaoxing, and much of our military funding came from there as well. After the fall of Jin and Lan, we planned at once to defend the Ningbo–Shaoxing supply line in strength. First we ordered Zhang Jingqu, intendant of the Ningbo–Shaoxing–Taizhou circuit, and later repeatedly directed Salt Commissioner Zhuang Huanwen and the nominated circuit intendant Peng Siju to lead troops and militia to establish defenses. Wang Lüqian opposed and obstructed every measure. He also shielded the wealthy gentry, exhausting all possibilities of obtaining contributions or loans. Clinging obstinately to his own views, he thwarted us at every turn. Still intent upon making defensive preparations, we noted that Liao Zongyuan, together with Zhao Jingxian of the Huzhou gentry, had held a city in peril for more than a year. We transferred Liao to act as prefect of Shaoxing and had him devote every effort to organizing its defense. But he went against the wishes of the great local gentry, who did not want defenses established. They falsely accused him of colluding with the rebels and beat him savagely, while Lüqian stood by without intervening. When the city fell, Lüqian fled; Liao Zongyuan ultimately died with the city. Thereafter, the districts of Ningbo and Shaoxing fell one after another, leaving Hangzhou an isolated last stake, beyond all hope of rescue.”

写到这里，王有龄一口怨气不出，想到王履谦携带家眷辎重，由宁波出海到福建，远走高飞，逍遥自在，而杭州全城百姓，受此亘古所无的浩劫；自己与驻防将军瑞昌，纵能拼得一死报君主，却无补于大局，因而又奋笔写道∶王履谦贻误全局，臣死不瞑目。眼下饷绝援穷，危在旦夕，辜负圣恩，罪无可逭。惟求皇上简发重兵，迅图扫荡，则臣等虽死之日，犹生之年。现在折报不通，以后更难偷达，谨将杭城决裂情形，合词备兵折稿，密递上海江苏抚臣薛焕代缮具奏。仰圣瞻天，无任痛切悚惶之至。

Wang could not release the resentment that filled him. He thought of Wang Lüqian taking his family and baggage out to sea from Ningbo and sailing away to Fujian, free and at ease, while the entire population of Hangzhou endured a catastrophe without precedent. Even if he and the garrison general, Ruichang, died to repay their sovereign, their deaths could do nothing to remedy the situation. He pressed on furiously with his brush: “Wang Lüqian has ruined the whole undertaking. I shall die unable to close my eyes. Our funds are exhausted and all hope of relief is gone; destruction may come at any moment. We have betrayed Your Majesty’s gracious trust, and our guilt admits of no pardon. We beg only that Your Majesty select and dispatch a mighty army to sweep away the rebels without delay. Then the day of our death will be as though we lived again. At present our memorials cannot pass through, and it will be still harder to smuggle them out hereafter. We therefore set forth jointly in this draft memorial the circumstances of Hangzhou’s impending collapse, for secret delivery to Shanghai, where Governor Xue Huan of Jiangsu may have it transcribed and submitted to the throne on our behalf. Looking up toward Your Sacred Majesty as toward Heaven, we are overwhelmed by anguish and fearful awe.”

翻译说明
覆盖《红顶商人》第二章 source_red.txt 第 849–967 行，全部 60 个非空源行整理为 22 个语义阅读段。王有龄遗折的正文完整保留，未压缩为内容提要。没有需要安全改写的未成年性描写。
老何 Old He、阿毛 A’mao、曾得胜 Zeng Desheng、饶廷选 Rao Tingxuan、张玉良 Zhang Yuliang；曾副将 Deputy Commander Zeng 与前批一致。把总、千总沿用旧译中出现过的 bazong、qianzong，保留军职层级，守备作 garrison officer。
候潮门 Houchao Gate。门名诗的英文与第064批统一。本段原文明确说两句嵌有“五个”城门，照译为五；不因第064批叙述曾泛称“十个”而改动本段数量。
第 855 行“打字主意”按打定主意、第 857 行“生在灶下”按坐在灶下、第 865 行“一恐怕戒备”按戒备之心、第 923 行“泄潜心机密”按泄露机密、第 925 行“萧着骥”按萧家骥、第 959 行“彭励”按鼓励理解，依据均来自本段语法与情节。
王履廉与王履谦依前后明确指代统一 Wang Lüqian。第 965 行遗折先称“廖守元”，后称“廖宗元”；独立复核第065批时发现源文第 151、153 行已反复以“廖宗元”讲述同一绍兴知府事件，故依据本书交叉证据统一 Liao Zongyuan，不另造人物。
第 965 行“金、兰这法后”有明显文本损坏；结合军情与其后的防守饷源，暂理解为金、兰失陷之后，保留缩称 Jin and Lan，未擅自扩写完整地名。第 967 行“合词备兵折稿”按联名备具折稿理解；后文明确将用王有龄和瑞昌衔名，支持“jointly”的处理。
第 963 行眼病用语按原文旧医术语叙述，“肝肠上逼”字样可疑但不擅改成其他医学诊断。“刀圭与药石兼施”泛译药物及疗法并用，没有增译为眼部手术。
金额与行动：一块银洋用于买当天进城口令；乔装、绕木城、口令过沟、求见军官、入城均已发生。遗折表达王有龄当时的绝望和对王履谦的指责，保持书信人立场，不将其控诉改写成译者断言。

综合阅读提示
“Yet if he stayed…”与前面的逃走危险形成两难选择；后面的难民呼声提供转折触发点，适合观察段落衔接。
“might / could / would”区分萧家骥所估计的风险与已经发生的行动，阅读时须跟踪语气而非只抓事件词。
遗折中“At first… But… / Thereafter…”串联最初忧虑、实际劝抚、部署受阻及后果；长句翻译可先找主干，再辨认原因、条件和补充说明。
