胡雪岩｜中英对照版
原著：高阳

红顶商人｜The Red-Topped Merchant

第六章（本章第4部分，共5部分）

这神态亦颇为可疑，胡雪岩忍不住要发怒；但一转念间冷静了，『你叫阿福来！』他说。等把阿福喊来一回，才知究竟，阿祥是在附近的一家小杂货店『白相』。那家杂货店老夫妇两个，只有一个十七岁的女儿；胡雪岩也见过，生得象『无锡大阿福』，圆圆胖胖的一张脸，笑口常开。阿祥情有所钟，只等胡雪岩一出门，便到那家杂货店去盘桓；是他家不支薪工饭食的伙计兼跑街。『老爷要喊他，我去把他叫回来。』『不必！』胡雪岩听得这段『新闻』；心里舒服了些，索性丢下阿巧姐来管阿祥的闲事，『照这样说，蛮有意思了！那家的女儿，叫啥名字？』『跟——，』阿福很吃力地说∶『跟奶奶的小名一样。』原来也叫阿巧，『那倒真是巧了！』胡雪岩兴味盎然地笑着。

Her manner too seemed suspicious, and Hu nearly lost his temper. Then he steadied himself. “Fetch A’fu.” Once the bearer had been called and questioned, the truth emerged. A’xiang was “amusing himself” at a little grocery nearby. Its elderly owners had only one child, a daughter of seventeen. Hu had seen her: she resembled a cheerful Wuxi clay doll, with a plump, round face and a smile that never left it. A’xiang had set his heart on her. As soon as Hu went out, he would linger in the shop, acting as an unpaid assistant and errand boy, without even receiving his meals. “If you want him, sir, I shall fetch him.” “No need.” The news made Hu feel rather better. He might as well leave A’qiao aside and concern himself with A’xiang’s affairs. “That sounds promising. What is the girl’s name?” “The same as—” A’fu struggled to get the words out. “The same familiar name as our mistress.” So she too was called A’qiao. “A coincidence indeed!” Hu laughed with interest.

『我跟阿祥说，你叫人家的时候，不要直呼直令地叫人家的名字；那样子犯了奶奶的讳。做下人的不好这样子没规矩。』这是知书识礼的人才会有的见解，不想出现在两条烂泥腿的轿班身上，胡雪岩既惊异又高兴；但口中问的还是阿祥。『他不叫人家小名叫啥？』胡雪岩问∶『莫非叫姐姐、妹妹？那不是太麻肉了。』『是啊！那也太肉麻。阿祥告诉我说，他跟人家根本彼此都不叫名字，两个人都是「喂」呀「喂」的。在她父母面前提起来，阿祥是说「你们家大小姐」。』『这倒妙！』胡雪岩心想男女之间，彼此都用『喂』字称呼，辨声知人，就决不是泛泛的情分了；只不知道∶『她父母对阿祥怎么样？』『她家父母对阿祥蛮中意的。』『怎么叫蛮中意？』胡雪岩问∶『莫非当他「毛脚女婿」看待？』『也差不多有那么点意思。』『既然如此，你们应该出来管管闲事，吃他一杯喜酒啊！』『阿祥是老爷买来的，凡事要听老爷作主；我们怎么敢管这桩闲事，再说，这桩闲事也管不了。』『怎么呢？』『办喜事要——。』胡雪岩会意，点点头说∶『我知道了。你把阿祥替我去叫回来。』

“I told A’xiang not to call her directly by her name. It is our mistress’s name too, and a servant ought not to be so disrespectful.” This was the sort of observation one expected from a man versed in books and propriety. Hearing it from a muddy-legged chairbearer surprised and pleased Hu. But his questions remained about A’xiang. “What does he call her, then? Sister? Little Sister? That would be rather sickly.” “Yes, far too sickly. He told me they never use each other’s names. They simply say ‘Hey!’ to one another. When he speaks of her to her parents, he calls her ‘your young lady’.” “Excellent!” Hu thought that when a young man and woman needed only “Hey!” and recognised each other by their voices, theirs was no ordinary acquaintance. But how did her parents feel? “They are very pleased with him.” “How pleased? Do they regard him as a prospective son-in-law?” “Something like that.” “Then you ought to lend a hand and earn a cup of wedding wine.” “A’xiang was bought into your household, sir. Everything must be decided by you. We would not dare interfere. Besides, we could not arrange it.” “Why not?” “A wedding needs—” Hu understood and nodded. “I see. Fetch him for me.”

用不到一盏茶的功夫，阿祥被找了回来。脸上讪讪地，有些不大好意思；显然的，他在路上就已听阿福说过，知道是怎么一回事了。『你今年十几？』『十七。』『十七！』胡雪岩略有些踌躇似的，『是早了些。』他停了一下又问∶『「他们家大小姐」几岁？』这句对阿巧的称呼，是学着阿祥说的；自是玩笑，听来却有讥嘲之意，阿祥大窘，嗫嚅着说∶『比我大两月，我是九月里生的，她的生日是七月七。』『连人家的时辰八字都晓得了！』胡雪岩有此忍俊不禁；但为了维持尊严，不得不忍笑问道∶『那家人家姓啥？』『姓魏。』『魏老板对你怎么样？』胡雪岩说，『不是预备拿女儿给你？你不要难为情，跟我说实话。』『我跟老爷当然说实话。』阿祥答道∶『魏老板倒没有说什么；老板娘有口风透露了，她说∶他们老夫妇只有一个女儿，舍不得分开。要娶她女儿就要入赘。』『你怎么说呢？』『我装糊涂。』『为啥？』胡雪岩说∶『是不肯入赘到魏家？』『我肯也没有用。我改姓了主人家的姓，怎么再去姓魏？』『你倒也算是有良心的。』胡雪岩满意地点点头，『我自有道理。』这当然是好事可谐了！阿祥满心欢喜；但脸皮到底还薄，明知是个极好的机会，却不敢开口相求，就此『敲打转脚』拿好事弄定了它。

Before a cup of tea could have been drunk, A’xiang was back. His embarrassed expression showed that A’fu had explained the matter on the way. “How old are you?” “Seventeen.” “Seventeen!” Hu hesitated slightly. “Rather early.” After a pause he asked, “And how old is ‘their young lady’?” He was playfully repeating A’xiang’s own phrase, but it sounded like mockery to the boy, who grew painfully embarrassed. “Two months older than I am. I was born in the ninth month; her birthday is the seventh of the seventh month.” “You even know her birth particulars!” Hu could scarcely keep from laughing, but restrained himself to preserve his dignity. “What is the family’s surname?” “Wei.” “How does Mr Wei treat you? Is he thinking of giving you his daughter? Do not be shy. Tell me the truth.” “Of course I would tell you the truth, sir. Mr Wei has said nothing, but his wife has dropped a hint. She says they have only one daughter and cannot bear to part with her. Whoever marries her must join their household.” “What did you say?” “I pretended not to understand.” “Why? Are you unwilling to marry into the Wei family?” “It would be no use if I were willing. I have taken my master’s surname. How could I change it again to Wei?” “You have a conscience, at least.” Hu nodded approvingly. “I shall see what can be done.” That plainly meant the happy outcome was possible. A’xiang was delighted. Yet he was still shy. He knew this was an excellent opportunity, but could not bring himself to ask and settle the matter while the chance was before him.

不说话却又感到僵手僵脚，一身不自在；于是搭讪着问道∶『老爷恐怕还没有吃饭？我来关照他们！』接着便喊∶『素香，素香！』素香从下房里闪了出来，正眼都不看阿祥；走过他面前，低低咕哝了一句∶『叫魂一样叫！』然后到胡雪岩面前问道∶『老爷叫我？』做主人的看在眼里，恍然大悟；怪不得问她阿祥在哪里？她有点懒得答理的模样！原来阿祥跟魏阿巧好了，她在吃醋。照此说来，落花有意，流水无情；阿祥倒辜负她了。这样想着，便有些替素香委屈。不过事到如今，没有胡乱干预，扰乱已成之局的道理，惟有装作不解；找件事差遣素香去做。

Saying nothing left him awkward and uncomfortable, so he found an excuse to speak. “You have probably not eaten, sir. I shall tell them.” He called, “Suxiang! Suxiang!” She emerged from the servants’ room without looking him in the face. Passing him, she muttered, “Must you call as though summoning a soul?” Then she asked Hu, “Did you call me, sir?” He saw it all and understood. No wonder she had seemed reluctant to answer when he asked where A’xiang was. She was jealous of his attachment to the Wei girl. The falling blossom was willing, but the flowing water felt nothing: A’xiang had disappointed her. Hu felt sorry for her. Yet there was no reason to interfere blindly and upset what was already taking shape. He could only pretend not to understand and find her something to do.

『我不在家吃饭了。』他嘱咐阿祥∶『你马上到张老板那里去，说我请他吃酒。弄堂口那家酒店叫啥字号？』『叫王宝和。』『我在王宝和等他。你去快点，请他马上来。』『是！』阿祥如奉了将军令一般，高声答应，急步下楼。等他一走，胡雪岩喝完一杯素香倒来的茶，也就出门了。走到王宝和，朝里一望；王老板眼尖，急忙迎了出来，哈腰曲背地连连招呼∶『胡大人怎么有空来？是不是寻啥人？』『不是！到你这里来吃酒。』王老板顿时有受宠若惊之感∶『请！请！正好雅座有空。胡大人来得巧了。』所谓雅座是凸出的一块方丈之地，一张条案配着一张八仙桌；条案上还供着一座神龛，内中一方『王氏昭穆宗亲之位』的神牌。胡雪岩看这陈设，越发勾起乡思；仿佛置身在杭州盐桥附近的小酒店中，记起与张胖子闲来买醉的那些日子了。『胡大人，我开一坛如假包换的绍兴花雕；您老人家尝尝看。』『随你。』胡雪岩问∶『有啥下酒菜？』『蛏子刚上市。还有鞭笋；嫩得很。再就是酱鸭，糟鸡。』『都拿来好了。另外要两样东西，「独脚蟹」，油炸臭豆腐干。』『独脚蟹』就是发芽豆，大小酒店必备；油炸臭豆腐干就难了，『这时候，担子都过去了。』王老板说，『还不知有没有？』『一定要！』胡雪岩固执地说，『你叫个人，多走两步路去找，一定要买来！』『是，是！一定买来，一定买来！』王老板一叠连声地答应，叫个小徒弟遍处去找，还特地关照一句∶『快去快回。』

“I shall not dine at home.” He told A’xiang, “Go straight to Mr Zhang and say I invite him for wine. What is the name of the tavern at the entrance to the lane?” “Wang Baohe.” “I shall wait for him there. Be quick, and ask him to come at once.” “Yes, sir!” The boy answered as loudly as though he had received a general’s command, then hurried downstairs. After he had gone, Hu finished the tea Suxiang had poured and went out too. As he looked into Wang Baohe, the sharp-eyed proprietor hurried forward, bowing repeatedly. “What brings Your Excellency here? Are you looking for someone?” “No. I have come for a drink.” Mr Wang was overwhelmed by the honour. “Please come in! The private table is free. You have chosen just the right moment.” The private space was a small projecting recess, about a square zhang, furnished with a narrow side table and a square dining table. On the side table stood a shrine containing a tablet to the ancestors of the Wang family. The setting deepened Hu’s homesickness. He seemed to be in a little tavern near Salt Bridge in Hangzhou, remembering the days when he and Fatty Zhang would drink together in their leisure. “Your Excellency, I shall open a jar of genuine Shaoxing Huadiao—guaranteed. Try it.” “As you please. What have you to eat with it?” “Razor clams have just come into season. There are tender whip bamboo shoots too, as well as soy-braised duck and chicken in wine lees.” “Bring them all. And two other things: one-legged crabs and fried stinking bean curd.” “One-legged crabs” meant sprouted beans, available in every tavern, large or small. The bean curd was harder. “The vendors have all passed by at this hour,” Mr Wang said. “I do not know whether any can still be found.” “I must have it,” Hu insisted. “Send someone a little farther to look. He must bring some back.” “Certainly, certainly! We shall find it.” Mr Wang sent a young apprentice searching, with particular orders to hurry back.

于是，胡雪岩先独酌。一桌子的酒菜，他单取一样发芽豆；咀嚼的不是豆子，而是寒微辰光那份苦中作乐的滋味。心里是说不出的那种既辛酸、又安慰的隽永向往的感觉。一抬眼突然发觉，张胖子笑嘻嘻地站在面前；才知道自己是想得出神了。定定神问道∶『吃了饭没有？』『正在吃酒，阿祥来到。』阿胖子坐下来问道∶『今天倒清闲；居然想到这里来吃酒？』『不是清闲，是无聊。』张胖子从未听他说过这种泄气的话，不由得张大了眼想问∶但烫来的酒，糟香扑鼻，就顾不得说话先要喝酒了。『好酒！』他喝了一口说；啧啧地咂着嘴唇，『嫡路绍兴花雕。』『酒再好，也比不上我们在盐桥吃烧酒的味道好。』『呕！』张胖子抬头四顾，『倒有点象我们常常去光顾的那家「纯号」酒店。』『现在也不晓得怎么样了？』胡雪岩微微叹息着；一仰脸，干了一碗。『你这个酒，不能这样子喝！要吃醉的。』张胖子停杯不饮，愁眉苦脸地说∶『啥事情不开心？』『没有啥！有点想杭州，有点想从前的日子。老张，「贫贱之交不可忘，糟糠之妻不下堂」；来，我敬你！』张胖了不知他是何感触？惴惴然看着他说∶『少吃点，少吃点！慢慢来。』还好，胡雪岩是心胸开阔的人，酒德甚好；两碗酒下肚，只想高兴的事。想到阿祥，便即问道∶『老张，前面有家杂货店，老板姓魏，你认不认识？』

Hu began drinking alone. Of all the dishes on the table, he touched only the sprouted beans. What he savoured was not the beans, but the little pleasures wrested from hardship in his humble days. An inexpressible mixture of pain, comfort and lingering longing filled him. Suddenly he looked up and saw Fatty Zhang smiling before him. Only then did he realise how far his thoughts had wandered. Collecting himself, he asked, “Have you eaten?” “I was just having a drink when A’xiang arrived.” Zhang sat down. “You must be at leisure today, to think of drinking here.” “Not at leisure. At a loose end.” Zhang had never heard such dispirited words from him. His eyes widened and he was about to ask, but the fragrance of the warmed wine reached him, and drinking took precedence. “Fine wine!” he declared after a sip, smacking his lips. “True Shaoxing Huadiao.” “However fine it is, it cannot taste as good as the spirits we drank at Salt Bridge.” “Oh!” Zhang looked around. “This does resemble the Chunhao tavern we used to frequent.” “Who knows what has become of it?” Hu sighed faintly and drained a bowl. “You must not drink like that. You will be drunk.” Zhang set down his own cup, looking troubled. “What has upset you?” “Nothing. I miss Hangzhou, and the old days. Old Zhang, ‘Never forget friends made in poverty; never cast out the wife who shared your bran and chaff.’ Here, I drink to you.” Zhang did not know what had stirred him and watched uneasily. “Less, less! Take it slowly.” Fortunately, Hu was broad-minded and well behaved in drink. After two bowls he wanted to think only of pleasant things. Remembering A’xiang, he asked, “Old Zhang, do you know the owner of the grocery up ahead? His name is Wei.”

『我们是同行，怎么不认识？你问起他，总有缘故吧？』『他有个女儿，也叫阿巧，长得圆圆的脸，倒是宜男之相。你总也很熟？』听这一说，张胖子的兴致来了，精神抖擞地坐直了身了，睁了眼睛看着胡雪岩，一面点头，一面慢吞吞地答道∶『我很熟，十天、八天总要到我店里来一趟。』『为啥？』『她老子进货，到我这里来拆头寸；总是她来。』『这样说，他这个杂货店也可怜巴巴的。』『是啊，本来是小本经营。』张胖子说，『就要他这样才好。如果是殷实的话，铜钾银子上不在乎；做父母的就未必肯了。』『肯什么？』胡雪岩不懂他的话。『问你啊！不是说她宜男之相？』胡雪岩楞了一下，突然意会；一口酒直喷了出来，赶紧转过脸去，一面呛，一面笑。将个张胖子搞得丈二金刚摸不着头。『啊老张，你一辈子就是喜欢自作聪明；你想到哪里去了？』『你，』张胖子嗫嚅着说，『你不是想讨个会养儿子的小？』『所以说，你是自作聪明。哪有这回事？不过，谈的倒也是喜事；媒人也还是要请你去做。』接着，胡雪岩便将阿祥与阿巧的那一段情，都说给了张胖子听。

“We are in the same trade. Of course I know him. You must have a reason for asking.” “He has a daughter called A’qiao, with a round face—the sort of face that promises sons. You must know her well too?” Zhang’s interest awakened. He sat up briskly and looked at Hu, nodding as he answered slowly, “Very well. She comes to my shop every week or so.” “Why?” “When her father needs money to restock, he borrows from me. She is always the one he sends.” “Then his little grocery must be rather hard pressed.” “Yes. He has always had very little capital. That is just what you want. If the family were comfortably off and cared little for money, her parents might not agree.” “Agree to what?” Hu did not understand. “You tell me! Did you not say she had a face that promised sons?” Hu stared, then suddenly understood. He sprayed out a mouthful of wine and hastily turned aside, coughing and laughing, while Zhang was left completely bewildered. “Old Zhang, you have spent your whole life thinking yourself clever. Where has your imagination taken you?” “Were you not thinking of taking a concubine who could bear you sons?” Zhang asked hesitantly. “That is why I say you are too clever by half. Nothing of the sort! But there is a marriage to arrange, and I still want you to be the matchmaker.” He then told Zhang the whole story of A’xiang and the Wei girl’s affection.

『好啊！』张胖子秀高兴地，『这个媒做来包定不会「春梅浆」！』『春梅浆』是杭州的俗语，做媒做成一对怨偶，男女两家都嗔怨媒人，有了纠纷，责成媒人去办交涉，搞得受累无穷，就叫『春梅浆』。老张说这话，就表示他对这头姻缘，亦很满意；使得胡雪岩越发感到此事做得惬意称心。一高兴之下，又将条件放宽了。

“Excellent!” Zhang said delightedly. “This match certainly will not become a bowl of ‘spring-plum pulp’.” That was a Hangzhou expression. If a matchmaker brought together an unhappy couple, both families blamed him. Every dispute was referred back to him for negotiation, burdening him without end; such a predicament was called “spring-plum pulp”. Zhang’s words showed that he approved of the match, which made Hu feel still more satisfied with what he was doing. In his good humour, he made the terms more generous.

『你跟魏老板去说，入赘可以，改姓不可以；既然他女儿是宜男之相，不怕儿子不多，将来他自己挑一个顶他们魏家的香烟好了。至于阿祥，我叫他也做杂货生意；我借一千银洋给他做本钱。』『既然这样，也就不必谈聘金不聘金了。嫁妆、酒席，一切都是男家包办；拜了堂，两家并作一家。魏老板不费分文，有个女婿养他们的老，有这样便宜的好事，他也该心满意足了。你看我，明天一说就成功；马上挑日子办喜事。』『那就重重拜托。我封好谢媒的红包，等你来拿。』『谢什么媒！你帮我的忙还帮得少了不成？』

“Tell Mr Wei that A’xiang may marry into his household, but may not change his surname. With her son-bearing looks, there should be no shortage of sons. Mr Wei can choose one to continue his family line. I shall put A’xiang into the grocery trade too, lending him a thousand silver dollars as capital.” “Then there is no need to discuss a separate betrothal payment. The groom’s side can provide everything—the trousseau, the feast and the rest. Once the marriage ceremony is held, the two households will become one. Mr Wei need spend nothing, and he will have a son-in-law to support them in old age. Such favourable terms should satisfy him completely. You will see: I shall settle it tomorrow, and we can choose a day for the wedding at once.” “Then I entrust it to you. I shall have a red packet ready to thank the matchmaker.” “Thank me? Have you not helped me often enough already?”

谈到这里，小徒弟捧来一大盘油炸臭豆腐干；胡雪岩不暇多说，一连吃了三块，有些狼吞虎咽的模样，便又惹得爱说话的张胖子要开口了。『看你别的菜不吃，发芽豆跟臭豆腐干倒吃得起劲！』胡雪岩点点头，停箸答道∶『我那位老把兄嵇鹤龄，讲过一个故事给我听∶从前有个穷书生，去庙里住；跟一个老和尚做了朋友。老和尚常常掘些芋头，煨在热灰里；穷书生吃得津津有味。到后来穷书生十年寒窗无人问，一举成名天下知，飞黄腾达，做了大官。衣锦还乡，想到煨芋头的滋味，特地去拜访老和尚，要尝一尝，一尝之下，说不好吃。老和尚答他一句∶芋头没有变，你人变了！我今天要吃发芽豆跟臭豆腐干，也就仿佛是这样一种意思。』『原来如此！你倒还记得，当初我们在纯号「摆一碗」，总是这两样东西下酒。』张胖子接着又问∶『现在你尝过了，是不是从前的滋味？』『是的。』『那倒难得！』张胖子有点笑他言不由衷的意味，『鱼翅海参没有拿你那张嘴吃刁？』『你弄错了，我不是说它们好吃！从前不好吃，现在还是不好吃。』『这话我就不懂了！不好吃何必去吃它？』张胖子说。『从前也不晓得吃过多少回，从来没有听你说过，发芽豆、臭豆腐干不好吃。』『不好吃，不必说；想法子去弄好吃的来吃。空口说白话，一点用都没有；反而害得人家都不肯吃苦了！』这几句话说得张胖子楞住了，怔怔地看了他好半天，方始开口∶『老胡，我们相交不是三年、五年；到今天我才晓得你的本性。这就难怪了！你由学生意爬到今天大老板的地位；我从钱庄大伙计弄到开小杂货店，都是有道理的。』一向笑嘻嘻的张胖子，忽然大生感触，面有抑郁之色。胡雪岩从他的牢骚话中，了解他不得意的心情；多年的患难贫贱之交，心里自然也很难过。

Just then the apprentice arrived with a great plate of fried stinking bean curd. Hu had no time for further talk. He ate three pieces in succession, almost wolfing them down, which gave the talkative Zhang another opening. “You leave the other dishes alone, but how you enjoy those sprouted beans and that bean curd!” Hu nodded and set down his chopsticks. “My old sworn brother Ji Heling once told me a story. A poor scholar went to live in a temple and became friends with an old monk. The monk would dig up taro roots and roast them in hot ashes, and the scholar ate them with delight. Later, after ten years of study in obscurity, the scholar won fame at a single stroke, rose rapidly and became a great official. Returning home in glory, he remembered the flavour of those taros and went specially to visit the monk and taste them again. This time he said they were no good. The monk answered, ‘The taros have not changed. You have.’ My wish for beans and stinking bean curd today means something rather like that.” “So that is it! You remember how those were always the two things we ate with a bowl of wine at Chunhao.” Zhang asked, “Now you have tasted them, do they taste as they did before?” “Yes.” “That is remarkable!” His tone suggested he doubted Hu’s sincerity. “Shark’s fin and sea cucumber have not spoiled your palate?” “You misunderstand. I did not say they tasted good. They did not then, and they do not now.” “I do not understand that! Why eat what you do not like? We ate them countless times in the old days, and I never heard you say they were no good.” “There is no need to say it. Find a way to get better things to eat. Empty talk achieves nothing; it only makes others unwilling to endure hardship.” These words left Zhang staring for a long time before he could speak. “Old Hu, we have been friends far longer than three or five years, but only today have I understood your nature. No wonder! You have climbed from apprentice to great proprietor, while I have gone from senior bank clerk to owner of a tiny grocery. There is a reason for both.” The habitually cheerful Zhang was suddenly overcome by feeling, and his face grew gloomy. Hu understood the disappointment beneath his complaint. They had shared poverty and adversity for so many years that he too felt distressed.

他真想安慰他。因而想到跟刘不才与古应春所商量的计划，不久联络好了杭州的小张和嘉兴的孙祥太，预备大举贩卖洋广杂货，不正好让张胖子也凑一股？股本当然是自己替他垫；只要他下手帮忙；无论如何比株守一爿小杂货店来得有出息。话已经要说出口了，想想不妥；张胖子嘴不紧，而这个贩卖洋广杂货的计划，是有作用的，不宜让他与闻。要帮他的忙，不如另打主意。想了一下，倒是有个主意，『老张，』他说，『我也晓得你现在委屈。不过时世不对，暂时要守一守。我的钱庄，你晓得的，杭州的老根一断，就没有源头活水了！现在也是苦撑在那里的局面。希望是一定有的；要摆功夫下去。你肯不肯来帮帮我的忙？』『你我的交情，谈不到肯不肯。不过，老胡，实在对不起，饭庄饭我吃得寒心了；你想想，我从前那个东家，我那样子替他卖力，弄到临了，翻脸不认人。如果不是你帮我一个大忙，吃官司都有份。从那时候起，我就罚过咒，再不吃钱庄饭！自己小本经营，不管怎么样，也是个老板。』说到这里，张胖子自觉失言；赶紧又作补充∶『至于对你，情形当然不同。不过我罚过咒，不帮人家做饭庄；这个咒是跪在关帝菩萨面前罚的，不好当耍。老胡，千言万语并一句∶对不对你！』说完，举杯表示道歉。

He wanted sincerely to comfort his friend. He thought of the plan he had discussed with Liu Bucai and Gu Yingchun. Once they had made contact with Little Zhang of Hangzhou and Sun Xiangtai of Jiaxing, they intended to trade on a large scale in imported and southern goods. Why not give Fatty Zhang a share? Naturally Hu would advance the capital. Zhang need only lend a hand; it would certainly offer more promise than clinging to a little grocery. He was about to speak when he thought better of it. Zhang could not keep his mouth shut, and the trading scheme served a particular purpose. He ought not to know of it. Better to find another way of helping him. After a moment, an idea came. “Old Zhang, I know that your present position is beneath you. But the times are wrong. You must hold on for a while. You know my bank: with its old roots in Hangzhou cut off, its living source of funds is gone. We too are struggling to keep it going. There is certainly hope, but work must be put into it. Would you come and help me?” “Between us, willingness does not enter into it. But forgive me, Old Hu: I have had my heart chilled by banking. Think of my former employer. I worked so hard for him, and in the end he turned against me. Without your great help, I might have faced a lawsuit. From that day I swore never to earn my living in a bank again. However small my own business, at least I am its proprietor.” Realising he had spoken awkwardly, he hurried to qualify it. “With you, naturally, it would be different. But I swore not to work in another man’s bank, and I made that oath kneeling before Lord Guan. It is not a thing to treat lightly. A thousand words come to one: I am sorry, Old Hu.” He raised his cup in apology.

『这杯酒，我不能吃。我有两句话请问你，你罚咒，是不帮人家做钱庄？』『是的。』『就是说，不给人家做伙计？』『是的！』张胖子重重地回答。『那末，老张，你先要弄清楚，我不是请你做阜康的伙计。』『做啥？』张胖子愕然相问。『做股东。等于你自己做老板！这样子，随便你罚多重的咒，都不会应了。』『做股东！』张胖子心动了，『不过，我没有本钱。』『本钱我借你。我划一万银子，算你的股份；你来管事，另外开一份薪水。』胡雪岩说，『你那家小杂货店，我也替你想好了出路；盘给阿祥，他自然并到他丈人那里。你看，这不是顺理成章 的事？』

“I cannot drink to that. Let me ask you two questions. Your oath was that you would not work in someone else’s bank?” “Yes.” “That is, you would not be another man’s clerk?” “Exactly!” Zhang answered emphatically. “Then understand this first, Old Zhang: I am not asking you to become a clerk at Fukang.” “What, then?” Zhang asked in surprise. “A shareholder. In effect, your own proprietor. However solemn your oath, that cannot violate it.” “A shareholder!” He was tempted. “But I have no capital.” “I shall lend it to you. I shall assign ten thousand taels as your share. You will manage the business and draw a separate salary. I have thought of a solution for your grocery too: transfer it to A’xiang, and he will naturally combine it with his father-in-law’s. Does that not all follow neatly?”
