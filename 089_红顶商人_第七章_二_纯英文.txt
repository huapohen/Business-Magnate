HU XUEYAN
By Gao Yang

The Red-Topped Merchant

Chapter 7 — Part 2 of 4

The Second Miss thought for a moment. “Yes. Before she came to my establishment, she worked for the Sixth Miss of Xinxianghong House. She was very friendly with A’jin, who worked there too. I know A’jin as well. She lives in Zhaofu Lane now and keeps a handsome young lover.” “What does she do nowadays?” “She runs an establishment herself.” “Then I may have guessed correctly.” The Seventh Lady described A’qiao’s secret visit to Zhaofu Lane and explained her inference that she had gone to discuss following the same path. “How strange! Why did she not consult me?” “That is a fair question, Second Sister. But let me ask you: if A’qiao wanted to do such a thing, would you approve?” “How could I? Anyone who can avoid earning a living that way should do so.” “There you have it. She knew you would not be enthusiastic. Why should she consult you?” “That is true.” The Second Miss was still puzzled. “I simply cannot understand why she should return to that wretched livelihood.”

That, the Seventh Lady felt, was precisely what they must discuss. If A’qiao intended to return to the pleasure quarters, there could be only three motives: the need to support herself, revenge upon Hu, or a wish to use the place to meet men and find a dependable partner for life. Having analyzed the possibilities, she gave her own view. “First, she need not worry about money. She told me herself that she has twenty or thirty thousand taels put away. Besides, Mr Hu will certainly give her another sum when they part. As for revenge, there is no deep hatred between them. To disgrace him, she would first have to disgrace herself. She is not so foolish. The more I think of it, the more I can see only one explanation: she wants to choose a suitable patron to marry.” “Go back into that life in order to find a husband? I have never heard of such a thing.” The Second Miss shook her head vigorously. “Unless she means to follow A’jin’s example and keep some handsome young man in a little house. She will never find a good patron to marry that way.” Her words could be understood in two senses: she still did not believe that A’qiao would adopt such a poor plan, and she certainly disapproved of it. Either way, the Seventh Lady found them reassuring.

“I too can hardly believe that A’qiao would take that road. But let us speak plainly, Second Sister. I am trying both to help Little Uncle and to do what is best for her. For Brother Five’s sake, you must help me with this.” The Second Miss smiled in embarrassment. “You deserve a penalty for saying that! Would I ever refuse something you asked? Why must you bring Fifth Master into it?” “I spoke wrongly. Do not be angry. Let us discuss the real business. I already had a plan—” The Seventh Lady intended to transplant the blossom onto another branch. She had specially asked Mr Qin at the firm to write to Dr Zhang in Ningbo, hoping to arrange a marriage between him and A’qiao. There was a long story behind it. The Second Miss listened from the beginning, learning how they had met and how the doctor, whose feelings had found no response, had returned home disappointed. She felt considerable sympathy for him.

“I approve of your plan. But whether it can succeed is another matter. Between a man and a woman, everything depends on affinity, and A’qiao does not seem to have any with him.” “That is not it. Little Uncle stood between them. Her whole heart was fixed on him, so however good the doctor might be, she would never consider him. The doctor, for his part, saw that she already belonged to someone and withdrew before the obstacle. As I see it, if she is unwilling to become a concubine, she could do no better than marry him. First, his wife has recently died. Given his devotion to A’qiao, he would be willing to take her as his new principal wife. Second, he is not very old. How old is A’qiao now?” “She was born in the year of the Sheep. This year—” The Second Miss counted on her fingers, then exclaimed in surprise. “Exactly forty!” “She looks young; one would never guess forty. But forty she is.” The Seventh Lady paused, then added apologetically, “Do not be offended, Second Sister, but a woman of forty, with her background, cannot easily become a man’s principal wife.” “Not easily? One could hardly find such a match with a lantern. Tell me seriously: how certain are you of the doctor?” “Six or seven chances in ten, at least.” “That is fairly certain. I shall see A’qiao today and ask what she means to do. If she has no intention of returning to the trade, so much the better. If she has, I must stop her. Whatever she intends, I shall act as matchmaker.” “You will represent the bride, and I the groom. We must make it succeed. It will be a good deed.”

“A good deed, certainly. Though it seems rather unfair to the doctor.” At that, the Seventh Lady remembered how, before she married Gu, Hu had arranged for Wang Youling’s mother to adopt her as a daughter. Inspiration came at once. “Since you feel that way, Second Sister, let us consider how to raise A’qiao’s standing.” Her plan was to ask Hu’s mother to adopt A’qiao. Hu would then take the part of a brother marrying off his sister, receiving the honors due to the bride’s brother. Such arrangements had often been made in former times. In their present circumstances, it seemed a particularly considerate and reasonable solution. The Second Miss naturally approved and rejoiced for A’qiao: through this, she thought, her long journey would reach a proper fulfillment.

The Seventh Lady herself was delighted with the idea. Impatient by nature and now carried away by enthusiasm, she wanted to take the Second Miss straight to A’qiao and settle the whole matter face to face. The Second Miss was more cautious. She thought they should first see A’jin, establish the facts, and invite her to join them; A’qiao would then be more easily persuaded. “Very well. Let us go and see A’jin.” “Well—” The Second Miss knew that A’jin disliked unfamiliar visitors because she kept a young lover, but she could hardly say so outright. She changed tack. “A woman of your standing should not go there. I shall send for her.” She called the young maid who had accompanied her and sent her hurrying to Zhaofu Lane, with particular instructions to hire an ordinary street carriage and bring A’jin back in it without delay. While they waited, the two women naturally talked of their own affairs. The Second Miss sounded weary of life in the pleasure quarters, yet said nothing of choosing a man to marry. Quick in both thought and speech, the Seventh Lady heard what lay beneath her words and could not resist offering candid advice.

“Second Sister, do not fix your mind on a single course and refuse to turn aside. That is too foolishly devoted. If you go on waiting for Brother Five, you may wait until your hair is white and still achieve nothing. He must have told you why. He leads a whole guild and must act in ways that earn its members’ respect. While they are too poor to feed themselves, how could he justify setting up another household? Give up that hope.” The Second Miss had no answer. Silent and tearful, she turned away to wipe her eyes. The Seventh Lady too felt an ache of unhappiness and regretted having spoken so plainly. “Truly,” she said, searching for something to ease their mutual embarrassment, “that doctor is a good man, and comfortably off. Why did I not think of it? I ought to have arranged a match for you long ago.” “Thank you. But I was born to ill fortune and took up this wretched livelihood. I cannot even become a concubine. What more is there to say?” Her words still held resentment, and the usually eloquent Seventh Lady could find nothing to add.

Fortunately, Zhaofu Lane was nearby. The carriage soon returned with A’jin. The maid had explained matters on the way, so A’jin needed no introduction before greeting the Seventh Lady courteously. “You are the Seventh Lady of the You family? What a fine-looking woman you are!” “You are too kind.” The Seventh Lady turned to her companion. “Then this must be Sister A’jin?” “Yes.” Acting as hostess, the Second Miss ordered food and drink for the newcomer, then came straight to the point. “The Seventh Lady is concerned about A’qiao and has specially asked you here. Has A’qiao been to see you these past few days?” “She comes to see me often.” “Sister A’jin,” the Seventh Lady said, “we have not met before, but Second Sister knows me: I am frank and speak my mind. If I say anything clumsy, please do not take offense.” A’jin and the Second Miss had seemed at odds as soon as A’qiao was mentioned. The Seventh Lady feared that a collision would accomplish nothing and only damage their friendship, so she made this precautionary apology. A’jin too had long experience of the world and understood its ways. She knew that her stiff answer had carried a hint of hostility and had been ill judged. “Please do not say that. I am clumsy with words. Second Sister and I are old friends, so I do not stand on ceremony with her. You must not misunderstand me.” With both sides showing humility, further explanations would only suggest that there really was a quarrel. Still, as a new acquaintance, the Seventh Lady could hardly press certain questions herself. The Second Miss would be better placed to ask them. She smiled and gave her a meaningful glance.

The Second Miss nodded and explained calmly to A’jin. “A’qiao and Mr Hu have fallen out. Even a wise magistrate cannot settle family troubles, so there is no need to decide who is right or wrong. In short, they will probably separate. The Seventh Lady has always been close to A’qiao and helped bring them together in the first place. Naturally, she cannot be indifferent to what happens now. She came specially to ask me, but I know nothing. I have not seen A’qiao for some time. Hearing that she had visited you in the last few days, I thought she must have told you something. What does she intend to do?” “Oh.” A’jin did not answer immediately. Instead, she turned to the Seventh Lady. “How are things really between A’qiao and Mr Hu?” “Not bad.”

“Then it is strange.” A’jin looked puzzled. “Whenever she comes, she complains of her unhappy fate. I ask whether Mr Hu treats her well, but she only shakes her head and refuses to say. From the look of things—” She left the next words unspoken, though their meaning was easy to guess. Now the Seventh Lady was puzzled in turn. “Why should she give that impression? This talk of parting is very recent. Mr Hu’s family is coming to Shanghai, and his principal wife will not allow him to keep a separate household. A’qiao refuses to enter the family home, so they are at an impasse. Before this, I could see no sign of disagreement between them.” A’jin nodded. “Let us leave that aside.” Her face grew gloomy. “Perhaps I am to blame. I have a great-aunt on my father’s side, from a collateral branch of the family. She is now abbess of White-Robed Convent in Fahua Town. Whenever she comes to Shanghai, she visits me. Sometimes she has met A’qiao here, and they have talked with great enthusiasm. The old abbess is always saying that suffering in this life comes from a failure to cultivate virtue in the last, and urging her to prepare for a better life to come. It was only the usual talk of a religious person. Who could have thought A’qiao would become so absorbed in it?” Only then did the Seventh Lady realize that her guesses had been entirely wrong. A’qiao’s visit might have concerned the abbess, not the opening of a courtesan establishment. “What do you mean, absorbed?” she asked urgently. “It is quite unexpected. The other day she asked me for the address of White-Robed Convent. I told her, then asked why she wanted it. At first she would not say. When I pressed her, she finally admitted that she wanted to enter the convent and leave the world.” The Seventh Lady’s face changed with shock. “Become a nun?”

“Who can tell?” A’jin answered unhappily. “I spent the whole night trying to persuade her out of it, but she never gave me a definite answer. No one can guess whether she changed her mind.” “I do not think she will do it,” the Second Miss said calmly. “For all these years she has been accustomed to comfort and has never lived a hard life. She could not endure the austerity of a convent for a single day. As I see it—” She stopped. To go on would have been unkind. The Seventh Lady too thought A’qiao might not take that road. Yet even as she comforted herself with this thought, another worry arose. It was a relief that A’qiao did not intend to return to the pleasure quarters. But such a drastic gesture also showed that she would not release her hold upon Hu. There seemed to be plenty of trouble still ahead. “What are we to do now?” she sighed. “I have no more ideas.” In all their years of friendship, the Second Miss had never seen her so helpless. Partly for her sake and partly for A’qiao’s, she felt bound to make an effort.

“A’qiao will not shave her head and become a nun. She is only disheartened. We all want what is best for her, and we must find her a way forward.” The Second Miss turned to A’jin. “She is exactly forty this year. At that age, what future has she in this world of ours? The Seventh Lady is proposing a marriage—” When A’jin had heard the story of Dr Zhang, she too grew excited. “With such a proper ending in prospect, why become a nun? How good of the Seventh Lady to take such trouble! We are A’qiao’s old companions and ought to work even harder. Bringing this match about would be a true deed of merit. There is no use sitting here talking. Let us go and see her now. I refuse to believe that the three of us cannot persuade one woman.”

Their enthusiasm restored the Seventh Lady’s confidence. After a little thought she asked, “Sister A’jin, Second Sister, are you determined to help A’qiao?” “Naturally,” the Second Miss replied, “if we can.” “Good. Then listen to me. Matters settle more smoothly when one takes time. And with sufficient effort, an iron pestle can be ground into a needle. From today, let Mr Hu stop seeing her altogether. We shall first divert her thoughts and make her forget that such a person exists. That cannot be done in two or three days, which is why I had to ask you first. If we truly mean to help, we must devote time to it. Beginning today, the three of us will keep her company constantly. Plays, storytelling, Western dinners, carriage rides, foreign circuses—wherever there is something enjoyable, we shall take her. If she refuses, we shall say that we want to go. People care about their friends’ feelings. She will be too embarrassed to refuse and too considerate to spoil everyone’s pleasure with a gloomy face. At night we shall take turns staying with her, both to watch over her and to talk and ease her mind. After fifteen or twenty days of that, her mood will change. Then we can mention Dr Zhang, and success will be much easier. As for the cost of these outings, let me boast a little: I can still afford it. I shall pay for everything.” A’jin drew a deep breath. “Second Sister, with such generosity, the Seventh Lady has said all there is to say. We can only follow her lead. But we cannot let her bear all the expense.” “Of course not.” The Second Miss turned to the Seventh Lady. “We shall do everything as you say, except on that point. Do not argue. We shall take turns as hostess, and today is mine. Come, let us invite her out to see the foreign circus.” She signed for the bill, putting a personal flourish on the account the waiter brought, a mark intelligible only to herself. This was a Western custom called “signing,” an acknowledgment of the debt. Ordinarily one wrote one’s name; an illiterate person could make any distinctive mark, provided the proper form was observed.

Each woman had brought a young maid. They crowded into two hired street carriages and drove straight to A’qiao’s lodgings. On getting out, they immediately sensed something wrong. The front door stood half open, but no one answered. The Seventh Lady raised her voice. “A’xiang! A’fu!” Neither appeared. Instead, someone hurried down the stairs, her long braid swinging, her face full of alarm. It was Suxiang, A’qiao’s maid.
