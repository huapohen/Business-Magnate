第八章｜CHAPTER EIGHT

左宗棠从安徽进入浙江，也是稳扎稳打，先求不败；所以第一步肃清衢州，作为他浙江巡抚在本省境内发号施令之地，这是同治元年六月初的事。在衢州定了脚跟，左宗棠进一步规取龙游、兰溪、寿昌、淳安等地，将新安江以南、信安江以西地区的长毛，都撵走了；然后在十一月下旬，攻克了新安、信安两江交会的严州。由此虎过山高水长的严子陵钓台，沿七里泷湖江北上，第二年二月间进围杭州南面的富阳；距省城不足百里了。钱塘江南面，洋将德克碑的常捷军；丢乐德克的常安军，在不欠以前，攻克绍兴，接着，太平军又退出萧山。整个浙江的东西南三面，都已肃清；然而膏腴之地的浙北，也就是杭州以北，太湖以南，包括海宁、嘉兴、湖州在内的这一片活土，仍旧在太平军手里。这时，左宗棠升任闽浙总督；浙江巡抚由曾国荃补授，他人在金陵城外，无法接事，仍由左宗棠兼署。为了报答朝廷，左宗棠全力反攻，谁都看得出来，杭州克复是迟早间事。

Entering Zhejiang from Anhui, Zuo Zongtang advanced cautiously, securing each position and making safety from defeat his first concern. His initial move was to clear Quzhou and establish a base within the province from which he could exercise his authority as governor. This was early in the sixth month of the first year of Tongzhi. Once firmly established there, he moved against Longyou, Lanxi, Shouchang, Chun’an, and the surrounding districts, driving the rebels out of the country south of the Xin’an River and west of the Qu River, also called Xin’an. Late in the eleventh month, he captured Yanzhou at the confluence of the two rivers. From there his forces passed Yan Ziling’s Fishing Terrace, with its lofty hills and long reaches of water, and proceeded north along the river through Qililong. In the second month of the following year they laid siege to Fuyang, south of Hangzhou and less than a hundred li from the provincial capital. South of the Qiantang River, the Changjie Army under the foreign commander d’Aiguebelle and the Chang’an Army under Diuledeke had recently captured Shaoxing; the Taiping forces then withdrew from Xiaoshan. The eastern, western, and southern regions of Zhejiang had all been cleared. But the rich northern tract, between Hangzhou and Lake Tai and including Haining, Jiaxing, and Huzhou, remained in Taiping hands. At this point Zuo was promoted to governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang. Zeng Guoquan was appointed governor of Zhejiang, but, being outside the walls of Jinling, he could not take up the post; Zuo continued to act in his place. To repay the court’s favour, Zuo threw all his strength into the counter-offensive. Everyone could see that the recovery of Hangzhou was only a matter of time.

那时攻富阳、窥杭州的主将是浙江藩司蒋益澧。左宗棠本人仍旧驻节衢州，设厂督造战船；富阳之战，颇得舟师之力。但太平军在富阳的守将，是有名骁勇的汪海洋，因而相持五月，蒋益澧仍无进展。左宗棠迫不得已，只好借重洋将，札调常捷军二千五百人，由德碑率领，自萧绍渡江，会攻富阳；八月初八终于克复。其时也正是李鸿章 、刘铭传、郭松林合力攻克江阴；李秀成与李世贤自天京经溧阳到苏州，想设法解围的时候。浙江方面，蒋益澧与德克碑由富阳北上，进窥杭州；同时分兵攻杭州西面的余杭。太平军由『朝将』汪海洋∶『归王』邓光明∶『听王』陈炳文，连番抵御，却是杀一阵败一阵。到十一月初，左宗棠亲临余杭督师，但杭州却仍在太平军苦守之中。

The commander attacking Fuyang with an eye to Hangzhou was Jiang Yili, Zhejiang’s provincial treasurer. Zuo himself remained at Quzhou, where he established workshops and supervised the building of war vessels. The flotilla proved of considerable assistance in the fighting at Fuyang. But the Taiping defender was Wang Haiyang, famous for his courage, and after five months Jiang had made no progress. Zuo was obliged to call upon the foreign commander. He ordered d’Aiguebelle to bring twenty-five hundred men of the Changjie Army across the river from Xiaoshan and Shaoxing for a combined attack. Fuyang was finally recovered on the eighth day of the eighth month. At about the same time, Li Hongzhang, Liu Mingchuan, and Guo Songlin joined forces to capture Jiangyin, while Li Xiucheng and Li Shixian travelled from the Heavenly Capital through Liyang to Suzhou, hoping to find a way to relieve the siege. In Zhejiang, Jiang and d’Aiguebelle advanced north from Fuyang towards Hangzhou, sending a separate force against Yuhang to the west. The Taiping commanders Wang Haiyang, the Court General, Deng Guangming, the Gui King, and Chen Bingwen, the Ting King, resisted repeatedly, but lost battle after battle. Early in the eleventh month, Zuo went in person to Yuhang to direct operations. Hangzhou, however, was still being stubbornly defended.

其时李鸿章 已下苏州、无锡。按照他预定的步骤，不愿往东去占唾手可得的常州，免得『挤』了曾国荃；却往浙北去『挤』左宗棠；一面派翰林院侍讲面奏调到营的刘秉璋，由金山卫沿海而下，收复了浙北的平湖、乍浦、海盐；一面派程学启由吴江经平望，南攻嘉兴。收复了浙北各地，当然可以接收太平军的辎重，征粮收税；而且仿照当年湖北巡抚胡林翼收复安徽边境的先例，以为左宗棠远在杭州以南，道理隔阻，鞭长莫及，应该权宜代行职权，派员署理浙西收复各县的州县官。这一下气得左宗棠暴跳如雷。李鸿章 不但占地盘，而且江苏巡抚这个官做到浙江来了，未免欺人太甚！但一时无奈其何，只好先全力收复了杭州再说。

By then Li Hongzhang had taken Suzhou and Wuxi. In accordance with his earlier plan, he would not move east to seize the easily obtainable prize of Changzhou, lest he encroach upon Zeng Guoquan. Instead, he moved into northern Zhejiang to encroach upon Zuo. On one side he sent Liu Bingzhang, a Hanlin lecturer whom he had personally requested from the emperor for service in his camp, south along the coast from Jinshanwei. Liu recovered Pinghu, Zhapu, and Haiyan. On the other, he sent Cheng Xueqi from Wujiang through Pingwang to attack Jiaxing from the north. Recovery of these districts naturally brought possession of Taiping baggage and stores, as well as the right to collect grain levies and taxes. Li also invoked the precedent of Hu Linyi, governor of Hubei, who had recovered districts on the Anhui border. Since Zuo was far to the south of Hangzhou, with communications obstructed and these places beyond his effective reach, Li considered it proper to exercise authority temporarily on his behalf and appoint acting magistrates to the recovered districts of western Zhejiang. Zuo was furious. Li had not merely seized territory; he was carrying his office as governor of Jiangsu into Zhejiang. It was an intolerable affront. For the moment, however, Zuo could do nothing about it. He could only concentrate on recovering Hangzhou first.

于是，胡雪岩开始计划，重回杭州；由刘不才打先锋；北去是要收服一个张秀才，化敌为友，做个内应。这个张秀才本是『破靴党』，自以为衣冠中人，可以走动官府，平日包揽讼事，说合是非，欺软怕硬，十分无赖。王有龄当杭州知府时，深恶其人；久已想行文学官，革他的功名，只是一时不得其便，隐忍在心。这张秀才与各衙门的差役都有勾结——杭州各衙门的差役，有一项陋规收入，凡是有人开设商铺，照例要向该管地方衙门的差役缴纳规费，看店铺大小，定数目高下，缴清规费，方得开张，其名叫做『吃盐水』。王有龄锐于任事，贴出告示，永远禁止；钱塘、仁和两县的差役，心存顾忌，一时敛迹；巡抚、藩司两衙门，自觉靠山很硬，不买知府的帐，照收不误，不过自己不便出面，指使张秀才去『吃盐水』，讲明三七分帐。

Hu now began planning his return to Hangzhou. Liu was to go ahead, his task being to win over a certain Licentiate Zhang, turn an enemy into a friend, and secure help from within the city. Zhang belonged to the shabby fraternity of petty scholars who exploited their status. Considering himself a gentleman entitled to enter official offices, he habitually took charge of lawsuits and meddled in disputes, bullying the weak and cringing before the strong. He was an utter scoundrel. When Wang Youling had been prefect of Hangzhou, he had detested the man and long wanted to ask the educational authorities to strip him of his degree. Lacking a suitable opportunity, he had kept his anger to himself. Zhang was in league with the runners at the various government offices. These men enjoyed a customary illicit source of income: anyone opening a shop had to pay a fee to the runners attached to the office with jurisdiction over the premises. The amount varied with the size of the shop, and the fee had to be settled before business could begin. This was called “drinking salt water.” Eager to discharge his duties, Wang posted a proclamation forbidding it for good. The runners of Qiantang and Renhe, fearful of the consequences, temporarily restrained themselves. Those at the governor’s and provincial treasurer’s offices, confident in their powerful superiors, paid no heed to the prefect and continued collecting. Since they could not conveniently appear in person, they sent Zhang, agreeing to divide the proceeds in a ratio of three to seven.

谁知运气不好，正在盐桥大街向一家刚要开张的估衣店讲斤头，讲不下来的时候，遇到王有龄坐轿路过，发现其事，停轿询问，估衣店的老板，照实陈述；王有龄大怒，决定拿张秀才『开刀』，立个榜样。当时传到轿前，先申斥了一顿；疾言厉色警告，一定要革他的功名。这一下张秀才慌了手脚，一革秀才，便成白丁，不但见了地方官要磕头，而且可以拖翻在地打屁股；锁在衙门照墙边『枷号示众』。想来想去只有去托王有龄言中计从的胡雪岩。带了老婆儿女到阜康钱庄，见了胡雪岩便跪倒在地，苦苦哀示。胡雪岩一时大意，只当小事一件，王有龄必肯依从，因而满口答应，包他无事。哪知王有龄执意不从，说这件事与他的威信有关；他新兼署了督粮道，又奉命办理团练，筹兵筹饷，号令极其重要，倘或这件为民除害的陋习不革，号令不行，何以服众？说之再三，王有龄算是让了一步。本来预备革掉张秀才的功名，打他两百小板子，枷号三月；现在看胡雪岩的份上，免掉他的皮肉受苦，出乖露丑，秀才却非革不可。说实在的，胡雪岩已经帮了他的大忙；而他只当胡雪岩不肯尽力，塘塞敷衍，从此怀恨在心，处处为难。到现在还不肯放过胡雪岩。

Unfortunately for him, Wang happened to pass in his sedan chair while Zhang was haggling over the sum demanded from a second-hand clothing shop about to open on Yanqiao Street. Wang noticed the disturbance, stopped, and made inquiries. The proprietor told him exactly what had happened. In a rage, Wang decided to make an example of Zhang. He summoned him before the chair, reprimanded him, and sternly declared that his degree would be revoked. Zhang panicked. Once stripped of his licentiate’s status, he would be a commoner. He would have to kneel before the local magistrate and could be thrown to the ground and beaten on the buttocks, or fastened in a cangue beside the screen wall of the yamen for public humiliation. After much thought, he could see only one course: appeal to Hu, whose advice Wang ordinarily followed. He brought his wife and children to Fukang Bank, fell to his knees before Hu, and pleaded desperately. Hu carelessly assumed that this was a small matter and Wang would oblige him. He promised without reservation that Zhang would come to no harm. But Wang refused to yield. His authority was at stake, he said. He had recently become acting grain intendant and had also been ordered to organize militia. In raising troops and funds, obedience to his commands was essential. If he could not abolish an abuse that harmed the people, or enforce his own proclamation, how could he command respect? After repeated persuasion, he finally made one concession. He had intended to revoke Zhang’s degree, give him two hundred strokes with the small paddle, and expose him in the cangue for three months. For Hu’s sake, he would spare the physical suffering and public disgrace. But the degree must go. In truth, Hu had done Zhang an enormous service. Zhang, however, thought he had merely made excuses instead of exerting himself. He bore a grudge thereafter, obstructing Hu wherever he could, and had not relented even now.

幸好一物降一物∶『恶人自有恶人磨』，张秀才什么人不怕，除了官就只怕他儿子。小张是个纨绔嫖赌吃着，一应俱全。张秀才弄来的几个造孽钱，都供养了宝贝儿子。刘不才也是纨绔出身，论资格比小张深得多；所以胡雪岩想了一套办法，用刘不才从小张身上下手。收服了小张，不怕张秀才不就范。到杭州的第二天，刘不才就进城去访小张——杭州的市面还萧条得很，十室九空，只有上城清河坊、中城荐桥、下城盐桥大街，比较象个样子；但是店家未到黄昏，就都上了排门，入夜一片沉寂，除掉巡逻的长毛，几乎看不见一个百姓。但是，有几条巷子里，却是别有天地；其中有一条在荐桥，因为中城的善后局设在这里，一班地痞流氓，在张秀才指使之下，假维持地方供应长毛为名，派捐征税，俨然官府；日常聚会之处，少不得有烟有赌有土娼。刘不才心里在想，小张既是那样一个脚色，当然倚仗他老子的势力，在这种场合中当『大少爷』；一定可以找到机会跟他接近。

Fortunately, everything has its master, and one rogue can be made to curb another. Apart from officials, the only person Zhang feared was his son. Young Zhang was a wastrel, devoted to whoring, gambling, and every luxury. The ill-gotten money his father scraped together all went to maintain this treasured son. Liu had himself been a rich young idler and possessed far more experience than the younger Zhang. Hu therefore devised a plan to reach the father through the son. Once the son was won over, the father could be brought to terms. On his second day back in Hangzhou, Liu went into the city to look for the young man. Business remained desperately depressed; nine houses in ten stood empty. Only Qinghefang in the upper city, Jianqiao in the middle, and Yanqiao Street in the lower city retained something of their former appearance. Even there, shops put up their shutters before dusk. After nightfall everything fell silent. Apart from patrolling rebels, scarcely a civilian was to be seen. A few lanes, however, belonged to another world. One was in Jianqiao, where the middle city’s reconstruction bureau had been established. Under Zhang’s direction, a band of local ruffians levied contributions and taxes in the name of preserving order and supplying the rebels, acting as though they were the government itself. Their usual meeting places naturally offered opium, gambling, and unlicensed prostitutes. Liu reasoned that a young man of Zhang’s character would surely exploit his father’s influence to play the grand young master in such places. There he would find an opportunity to approach him.

去的时候是天刚断黑，只见门口两盏大灯笼，一群挺胸凸肚的闲汉在大声说笑；刘不才踱了过去朝里一望，大门洞开，直到二厅，院子里是各种卖零食的担子，厅上灯火闪耀照出黑压压的一群人，一望而知是个赌局。是公开的赌局，就谁都可以进去；刘不才提脚跨上门槛，有个人喝一声∶『喂！』刘不才站住脚，陪个不亢不卑的笑，『老兄叫我？』他问。『你来做啥？』『我来看小张。』『小张！哪个小张？』『张秀才的大少爷。』刘不才不慌不忙地答道∶『我跟他是老朋友。』这下还真冒充得对了；因为张秀才得势的缘故，他儿子大为神气，除非老朋友，没有人敢叫他小张。那个人听他言语合拢，挥挥放他进门。

It was just growing dark when he arrived. Two large lanterns hung at the entrance, where a crowd of swaggering idlers were laughing and talking loudly. Liu strolled over and looked inside. The doors stood open through to the second hall. Vendors had set up their snack stalls in the courtyard, and bright lights in the hall illuminated a dense crowd. It was plainly a gambling establishment. Since it was open to the public, anyone could enter. Liu stepped over the threshold. “Hey!” someone called. He stopped with a smile that was neither servile nor arrogant. “Were you calling me, friend?” “What have you come for?” “To see young Zhang.” “Young Zhang? Which young Zhang?” “Licentiate Zhang’s son,” Liu replied unhurriedly. “An old friend of mine.” His pretence was well chosen. With his father’s rise to power, the son had grown so important that only an old friend would dare call him merely “young Zhang.” Finding the answer plausible, the man waved him in.

进门到二厅，两桌赌摆在那里，一桌牌九一桌宝；牌九大概是霉庄，所以场面比那桌宝热闹得多。刘不才知道赌场中最犯忌在人丛中乱钻，只悄悄站在人背后，踮起脚看。推庄的是个中年汉子，满脸横肉，油光闪亮；身上穿一件缎面大毛袍子，袖口又宽又大，显然的这件贵重衣服不是他本人所有。人多大概又输得急了，但见他解开大襟衣纽，一大块毛茸茸的白狐皮翻了开来，斜挂在胸前，还不住喊热，扭回头去向身后的人瞪眼，是怪他们不该围得这么密不通风，害他热得透不过气来的神情。『吴大炮！』上门一个少年说，『我看你可以歇歇了。宁与爷争，莫与牌争！』输了钱的人，最听不得这种话；然而那吴大炮似乎敢怒而不敢言，紧闭着嘴，将两个肋帮子鼓得老高，那副生闷气的神情，教人好笑。

In the second hall stood two gambling tables, one for pai gow and one for bao. The pai gow banker appeared to be losing, and his table was much livelier. Liu knew that pushing through a crowd was particularly unwelcome in a gambling house. He stood quietly behind the others and rose on tiptoe to watch. The banker was a middle-aged man with a fleshy, belligerent face shining with oil. He wore a satin-covered robe of heavy fur, with sleeves so broad and large that the expensive garment plainly did not belong to him. Whether from the crowd or the strain of his losses, he had unfastened the buttons across his chest, exposing a broad fold of white fox fur that hung diagonally down the front. Still complaining of the heat, he turned to glare at those behind him, as though they were to blame for crowding so close that he could hardly breathe. “Wu Bigmouth!” said a young man at the upper hand. “I think you should rest. Argue with a man if you must, but never with the tiles!” A losing gambler could bear no advice less easily. Yet Wu seemed afraid to answer. He kept his lips tightly shut and puffed out his cheeks in such silent resentment that he looked comical.

『好话不听，没有法子。』那少年问家∶『你说推长庄，总也有个歇手的时候；莫非一个人推到天亮？』『是不是你要推庄？』吴大炮有些沉不住气了，从身上摸出一叠银票，『这里二百两只多不少，输光了拉倒。』『银票！』少年顾左右而言，『这个时候用银票？哪家钱庄开门，好去兑银子？』『一大半是阜康的票子。』吴大炮说，『阜康上海有分号，为啥不好兑？』『你倒蛮相信阜康的！不过要问问大家相信不相信？』少年扬脸回顾，『怎么说？』『银票不用，原是说明了的。』有人这样说，『不管阜康啥康，统通一样。要赌就是现银子。』『听见没有？』少年对吴大炮说，『你现银子只有二、三十两了，我在上门打一记，赢了你再推下去；输了让位。好不好？』

“If you will not listen to good advice, there is nothing I can do,” the young man continued. “You said you would bank for a long run, but you must stop sometime. Do you mean to sit there until dawn?” “Do you want to be banker?” Wu was losing patience. He pulled out a bundle of banknotes. “There are at least two hundred taels here. I shall stop when I have lost them.” “Banknotes!” The young man looked from side to side. “Banknotes at a time like this? Which bank is open to redeem them?” “Most are Fukang notes,” Wu replied. “Fukang has a branch in Shanghai. Why should they not be redeemable?” “You certainly have faith in Fukang. But you must ask whether everyone else does.” The young man looked around. “Well?” “We agreed at the start: no banknotes,” someone said. “Fukang or any other bank, it makes no difference. Gambling means silver in hand.” “Did you hear?” the young man said to Wu. “You have only twenty or thirty taels in silver left. I shall stake against it on the upper hand. If you win, keep banking. If you lose, give up your place. Agreed?”

吴大炮想了一下，咬一咬牙说∶『好！』开门掷骰，是个『五在首』，吴大炮抓起牌来就往桌上一番，是个天杠，顿时面有得色。那少年却慢条斯理地先翻一张，是张三六；另外一张牌还在摸，吴大炮却沉不住气了，哗啦一声，将所有的牌都翻了开来，一面检视，一面说∶『小牌九没有「天九王」，你拿了天牌也没用。』刘不才在牌上的眼光最锐利，一目了然，失声说道∶『上门赢了，是张红九。』那少年看了他一眼，拿手一摸，喜孜孜地说∶『真叫得着！』翻开来看，果然是张红九，凑成一对；吴大炮气得连银子带牌往前一推，起身就走。

Wu thought, gritted his teeth, and agreed. The dice were thrown: a five at the head. Wu snatched up his tiles and turned them over at once. He had a Heaven-gong combination, and triumph appeared on his face. The young man slowly exposed one tile, a three-six, while continuing to feel the other. Unable to contain himself, Wu turned over all the remaining tiles with a clatter. Examining them, he said, “There is no Heaven-nine King in small pai gow. Even if you have a Heaven tile, it will not help.” Liu had a remarkably sharp eye for the tiles. He saw the position at once and involuntarily exclaimed, “The upper hand wins. It is a red nine!” The young man glanced at him, ran his fingers over his tile, and smiled. “You called it exactly!” He turned it over. It was indeed a red nine, completing a pair. Furious, Wu shoved his silver and tiles forwards, stood up, and began to leave.

『吴大炮。』那少年喊道，『我推庄，你怎么走了？』『没有钱赌什么？』『你的银票不是钱？别家的我不要，阜康的票子，我不怕胡雪岩少！拿来，我换给你。』吴大炮听得这一说，却不过意似的，在原位上坐了下来。等那少年洗牌时，便有人问道∶『小张大爷，你推大的还是推小的？』这小张大爷的称呼很特别；刘不才却是一喜，原来他就是张秀才的『宝贝儿子』——市井中畏惧张秀才，都称他张大爷；如今小张必是子以父贵，所以被称为小张大爷。这样想着，便整顿全神专注在小张身上。小张倒不愧纨绔，做庄家从容得很，砌好牌才回答那个人的问话∶『大牌九「和气」的时候多，经玩些。』于是文文静静地赌大牌九。刘不才要找机会搭讪，便也下注；志不在赌，输赢不大，所以只是就近押在上门。

“Wu Bigmouth!” the young man called. “Why leave now that I am banking?” “How can I gamble without money?” “Are your banknotes not money? I do not want those of other banks, but I am not afraid that Hu Xueyan will fail to honour Fukang’s. Give them to me. I shall exchange them for you.” Wu seemed a little abashed and sat down again. While the young man shuffled, someone asked, “Young Master Zhang, will it be big pai gow or small?” The form of address was unusual, but it delighted Liu: this was the licentiate’s treasured son. People in the streets, afraid of the father, called him Master Zhang. The son evidently borrowed his dignity to become Young Master Zhang. Liu concentrated all his attention on him. Zhang proved worthy of his reputation as an accomplished idler; he was perfectly at ease as banker. Only after arranging the tiles did he answer. “Big pai gow produces more ties. The play lasts longer.” They began a quiet game of big pai gow. Hoping for an opportunity to strike up a conversation, Liu also placed bets. Gambling was not his object, and he kept his stakes modest, betting on the upper hand nearest him.

这个庄推得很久，赌下风的去了来，来了去，长江后浪推前浪似的，将刘不才从后面推到前面，由站着变为坐下。这一来，他越发只守着本门下注了。慢慢地，小张的庄变成霉庄；吴大炮扬眉吐气，大翻其本——下门一直是『活门』，到后来打成『一条边』，唯一的例外，是刘不才的那一注，十两银子孤零零摆在上门，格外显眼。这有点独唱反调的意味，下风都颇讨厌；而庄家却有亲切之感，小张深深看了他一眼，眼中不自觉地流露出感动的神色。

Zhang remained banker for a long time. Bettors came and went, wave following wave, gradually pushing Liu from the back to the front until he exchanged his standing place for a seat. He consequently continued to bet only on his own hand. Slowly, the bank began to lose. Wu recovered his spirits and won back his losses with a substantial profit. The lower hand kept winning, until almost all the stakes were concentrated on that side. The sole exception was Liu’s lonely ten taels on the upper hand, conspicuous in its isolation. His persistence seemed almost an act of opposition. The other bettors disliked it, but the banker felt a corresponding warmth towards him. Zhang gave him a long look, an involuntary expression of gratitude in his eyes.

刘不才心里在说∶有点意思了！却更为沉着，静观不语。『上门那一注归下门看！』吴大炮吼着。『对不起！』小张答道∶『讲明在先的，大家不动注码。』吴大炮无奈，只好跟刘不才打交道∶『喂！喂！上门这位老兄的注码，自己摆过来好不好？配了我再贴你一半，十两赢十五两。』刘不才冷冷问道∶『输了呢？』『呸！』吴大炮狠狠向地下吐了口唾沫∶『活见鬼。』刘不才不作声；小张却为他不平，『吴大炮！』他沉下脸来说，『赌有赌品，你赌不起不要来，人家高兴赌人家的上门，关你鸟事！你这样子算啥一出？』『好了，好了！』有人打岔解劝，『都离手！庄家要下骰子了。』

Now we are getting somewhere, Liu thought. He grew more composed still, watching silently. “Count the upper-hand stake with the lower!” Wu shouted. “Sorry,” Zhang replied. “We agreed beforehand that no one would move the stakes.” Wu had to appeal to Liu. “Hey, friend at the upper hand! Why not move your own stake over here? If it wins, I shall add another half. Your ten taels will win fifteen.” “And if it loses?” Liu asked coldly. “Bah!” Wu spat savagely on the floor. “The devil take it!” Liu said nothing, but Zhang took offence on his behalf. “Wu Bigmouth!” he said, his expression darkening. “A gambler ought to have some manners. If you cannot bear to play, stay away. He likes betting on the upper hand. What bloody business is it of yours? What do you think you are doing?” “Enough, enough!” someone interposed. “Hands off the table. The banker is about to throw the dice.”

骰子一下，吴大炮一把抓住，放在他那毛茸茸的手中，咪着眼掀了几掀，很快地分成两副，一前一后摆得整整齐齐。有人想看一下；手刚伸到牌上，『叭哒』一声，挨了吴大炮一下。不问可知是副好牌，翻开来一比，天门最大；其次下门；再次庄家；上门最小。照牌路来说，下门真是『活门』。配完了下门，庄家才吃刘不才的十两银子；有些不胜歉疚地说∶『我倒情愿配你。』『是啊！』刘不才平静地答道∶『我也还望着「三十年风水轮流转」，上门会转运。现在——，』他踌躇了一会，摸出金表来，解表坠子问道∶『拿这个当押头，借五十两银子，可以不可以？』

When the dice had been cast, Wu snatched up his tiles. Holding them in his hairy hands, he narrowed his eyes and peeped at them repeatedly, then swiftly arranged them in two neat pairs, one before the other. Someone reached out for a look and received a sharp slap on the hand. Plainly Wu held something good. When the tiles were exposed, the Heaven hand ranked first, the lower hand second, the banker third, and the upper hand last. The lower hand certainly seemed charmed. After paying it, the banker collected Liu’s ten taels. “I would rather have paid you,” he said apologetically. “Yes,” Liu replied calmly. “I keep hoping that fortune will turn and the upper hand will have its day. But now—” He hesitated, then drew out his gold watch, detached its pendant, and asked, “Could I pledge this for a loan of fifty taels?”

这表坠子是一块碧绿的悲翠，琢成古钱式样，市价起码值二百两银子；但小张却不是因为它值钱才肯借∶『有啥不可以？我借五十两银子给你，要啥押头？』『不！庄家手气有关系。』刘不才固执地，『如果不要押头，我就不必借了。』其实他身上有小张所信任的，阜康的银票；有意如此做作，是要铺个进身之阶。等小张歇手，他五十两银子也输得差不多了；站起身来请教住处，说第二天拿银子来赎。『你贵姓？』小张问。『敝姓刘。』『那我就叫你老刘。』小张说，『我倒喜欢你这个朋友，东西你拿回去；好在总有见面的时候，你随便哪一天带钱来还我就是。』说着又将那块悲翠递了过来。『你这样子说，我更不好收了。府上在哪里？我明天取了银子来赎。』

The pendant was a piece of vivid green jadeite carved in the shape of an ancient coin, worth at least two hundred taels. But its value was not what moved Zhang to lend. “Why not? I shall lend you fifty taels. What need have I of a pledge?” “No. It affects the banker’s luck,” Liu insisted. “If you will not take a pledge, I need not borrow.” In fact, he had Fukang banknotes on him—the very notes Zhang trusted. He staged the transaction to create an opening for further acquaintance. By the time Zhang stopped banking, Liu had lost almost all the fifty taels. He stood up and asked where Zhang lived, saying he would bring the money and redeem the pendant the next day. “What is your surname?” Zhang asked. “Liu.” “Then I shall call you Old Liu. I like you. Take your property back. We are bound to meet again. Bring the money whenever you please.” He held out the jadeite. “Since you put it that way, I am all the less able to accept it,” Liu replied. “Where do you live? I shall fetch the silver and redeem it tomorrow.”

『说什么赎不赎？』小张有些踌躇；他一年三百六十五天，倒有三百天不在家，姓刘的『上门不见土地』，有何用处？如果为了等他，特意回家；却又怕自己把握不住自己的行踪。刘不才很机警，虽不知他心里怎么在想，反正他愿客人上门的意思，却很明显。自己有意将表坠子留在他那里，原是要安排个单独相处的机会；这不必一定到他家，还有更好的地方。『小张大爷，』他想定了就说∶『你如果不嫌弃，我们明天勺个地方见面，好不好？』『好啊！你说。』『花牌楼的阿狗嫂，你总知道？』

“Why keep talking of redemption?” Zhang hesitated. Out of three hundred and sixty-five days in a year, he spent three hundred away from home. What use would there be in Liu calling on an absent host? He might go home specially to wait, but he could not be sure of keeping to that intention. Liu was quick to understand. He did not know precisely what was in Zhang’s mind, but his reluctance to have a visitor at home was plain. Leaving the pendant had merely been a means of arranging a private meeting. That need not be at Zhang’s house; there was a better place. “Young Master Zhang,” he said, “if you have no objection, why do we not arrange to meet somewhere tomorrow?” “Gladly. Name the place.” “You know Madam A’gou at Huapailou, surely?”

译文说明
1. 本章先交代战局，再转入刘不才争取张氏父子。时间按原文的同治元年、次年及农历月日保留；文中常州方向及部分进军次序可能存在底本问题，未擅改为史书年表。
2. 新安江、信安江为两个字形不同但普通话读音相同的河名。信安江另称衢江，英文作 the Qu River, also called Xin’an 以便区分；河流交会地点仍保留小说叙述，未以现代地图改写。别称依据浙江省生态环境厅公开环评资料：https://sthjt.zj.gov.cn/module/download/downfile.jsp?classid=0&filename=d5cbbfe097e3467faebbb1cd84d998ac.pdf 。
3. 德克碑依《日意格1864年关于中国内战的日记》译文所附外文名作 d’Aiguebelle（检索出处：https://www.krzzjn.com/uploadfile/2020/1020/20201020023821381.pdf）；原文“德碑”按同一人物处理。“丢乐德克”未取得足够证据对应外文原名，暂保留 Diuledeke；常捷军、常安军分别作 Changjie Army、Chang’an Army，避免与华尔部队混淆。
4. “三七分帐”只明确比例，未说明谁得三成，译文未擅自指定。“二百小板子、枷号三月”是原定处罚；最后免去体罚与枷示，但仍革去功名。
5. 牌九使用 Heaven hand/upper hand/lower hand 区分方位；Heaven-gong、Heaven-nine King 按原文牌型转述，不补编游戏规则。十两赢十五两是吴大炮附加补贴后的提议，尚未兑现。表坠市值至少二百两，实际借款五十两，两个数字用途不同。
6. 第3073行“愿客人上门”与前后“难以守候在家、另约见面”相矛盾，疑漏“不”字；英文按连贯上下文译为 reluctance to have a visitor at home。中文对照保留底本文字，便于核查。

阅读与衔接
“Instead, he moved into northern Zhejiang to encroach upon Zuo”与前一句形成策略选择的对照；要区分军事胜利与叙述者强调的势力范围之争。
“In fact, he had Fukang banknotes on him”揭示刘不才抵押表坠的真实用意，推翻“没有其他钱”的表面解释。后文“create an opening”与之照应。

翻译与表达
“make an example of someone”在此是处罚一人以警戒其他人；“bear a grudge”指长期怀恨；“in a ratio of three to seven”表达分配比例。多义词 credit、bank、hand 在本章分别需要结合银行信用、庄家与牌位的上下文辨认。
