胡雪岩｜中英对照版
原著：高阳

红顶商人｜The Red-Topped Merchant

第八章（本章第9部分，共9部分）

阅读说明：每段中文后接对应英文；两版英文正文一致。下载站水印已清除，原始疑字在文末说明。

『这是我的真心话。大人想来看得出来。』胡雪岩又说，『除此以外，我当然也有我的打算，很想做一番事业，一个人如果要想有所成就，一半靠本事；一半靠机会。遇见大人就是我的一个机会；当然不肯轻易放过。』『你的话很老实，我就是觉得象你这路性情最投缘。你倒说与我听听，你想做的是什么事业？』这一问，很容易回答；容易得使人会觉得这一问根本多余。但照实而言，质直无味；胡雪岩虽不善于词令，却以交了嵇鹤龄这个朋友，学到了一种迂回的说法，有时便觉俗中带雅。好在他的心思快，敏捷可济腹笥的不足；此时想到一个掌故，大可借来一用。

“Those are my true feelings. I believe Your Excellency can see that,” Hu continued. “Beyond that, naturally, I have ambitions of my own. I want to build something substantial. If a man is to achieve anything, half depends on ability and half on opportunity. Meeting you is an opportunity for me. Naturally I am unwilling to let it pass lightly.” “You speak honestly. It is precisely your kind of nature that I find congenial. Tell me what sort of enterprise you wish to build.” That was an easy question—so easy that it might seem unnecessary. Yet a plain answer would be flat and tasteless. Hu was no master of rhetoric, but his friendship with Ji Heling had taught him a roundabout manner of expression that sometimes lent elegance to ordinary thoughts. Fortunately, his quick mind could make up for his limited store of learning. An anecdote occurred to him that he could put to good use.

『大人总晓得乾隆皇帝南巡，在镇江金山寺的一个故事？』左宗棠笑了。笑的原因很复杂，笑的意味，自己亦不甚分明。不称『高宗』或者『纯庙』，而说『乾隆皇帝』是一可笑；乾隆六次南巡，在左宗棠的记忆中，每次都驻驾金山寺，故事不少，却不知指的是哪一个？是二可笑∶『铜钱眼里翻跟斗』的胡雪岩，居然要跟他谈南巡故事，那就是三可笑了。可笑虽可笑，不过左宗棠仍持着宽容的心情；好比听稚龄童子说出一句老气横秋的『大人话』那样，除笑以外，就只有『姑妄听之』了。『你说！』他用一种鼓励的眼色，表示不妨『姑妄言之』。胡雪岩当然不会假充内行，老老实实答道∶『我也不晓得是哪一年乾隆皇帝南巡的事？我是听我的一个老把兄谈过，觉得很有意思，所以记住了，据说——。』据说∶有一次乾隆与金山寺的方丈，在寺前闲眺，遥望长江风帆点点；乾隆问方丈∶江中有船几许？方丈答说∶只有两艘，一艘为名；一艘为利。这是扬州的盐商，深知乾隆的性情，特意延聘善于斗机锋的和尚，承应皇差的佳话。只是传说既久，变成既俗且滥的一个故事；胡雪岩引此以喻，左宗棠当然知道他的用意，是说他的事业，只是『做大生意』图利而已。

“Your Excellency must know the story of the Qianlong Emperor at Jinshan Temple in Zhenjiang during his southern tours?” Zuo smiled. His reasons were complicated, and even he could not have defined everything in that smile. To call the ruler “the Qianlong Emperor” instead of “Gaozong” or “Emperor Chun” was one cause for amusement. Qianlong had made six southern tours and, as Zuo remembered it, stayed at Jinshan Temple each time. There were many stories; which did Hu mean? That was a second cause. Third, here was a man who spent his life turning somersaults through the hole in a copper coin, proposing to discuss imperial tours with him. Amusing as it was, Zuo remained indulgent. It was like hearing a small child utter a solemn grown-up remark: one could only smile and hear him out. “Go on.” His encouraging glance invited Hu to tell his tale. Hu naturally did not pretend to expertise. “I do not know in which year of the southern tours it happened. An older sworn brother told me, and I found it interesting enough to remember. They say—” They said that once Qianlong and the abbot were standing before the temple, gazing idly at the sails scattered over the Yangtze. The emperor asked how many boats were on the river. “Only two,” the abbot replied. “One for fame, one for gain.” The story arose from the Yangzhou salt merchants’ knowledge of Qianlong’s temperament: to receive the imperial party, they had specially engaged monks skilled in witty exchanges. Long repetition, however, had made the anecdote commonplace and stale. Zuo naturally supposed he knew Hu’s point. His enterprise was merely business on a grand scale, pursued for profit.

然而，他没有想到，胡雪岩居然另有新义，『照我说，那位老和尚的话，也不见得对。』胡雪岩很起劲地举手遥指∶『长江上的船，实在只有一艘，既为名，亦为利！』『噢！』左宗棠刮目相看了，『何以见得？』『名利原是一样东西。』胡雪岩略有些不安地，『大人，我是瞎说。』这比『既然为名，亦为利』，企求兼得的说法，又深一层了。左宗棠越感兴味；正待往下追问时，但见听差悄悄掩到他身边，低声问道∶『是不是留胡老爷便饭？』『当然。』左宗棠问道∶『什么时候了？』『未正！』未正就是午后两点，左宗棠讶然，『一谈谈得忘了时候了。』他歉然地问，『雪翁，早饿了吧？』『大人不提起，倒不觉得饿。』『是啊！我亦是谈得投机，竟尔忘食。来吧，我们一面吃，一面谈。』

But Hu unexpectedly gave it a new meaning. “In my view, the old monk was not necessarily right.” He lifted a hand and pointed into the distance with animation. “There is really only one boat on the Yangtze: it sails for both fame and gain.” “Oh!” Zuo looked at him with fresh respect. “How do you make that out?” “Fame and gain are really the same thing.” Hu seemed a little uneasy. “Your Excellency, I am only talking at random.” This went deeper than saying a boat pursued both fame and gain, hoping to obtain the two together. Zuo was increasingly interested and about to press him when an attendant slipped quietly to his side. “Will Mr Hu stay for a meal?” “Of course. What time is it?” “The middle of the wei hour.” That meant two in the afternoon. Zuo was surprised. “We have talked until I forgot the time.” He asked apologetically, “You must have been hungry for a while, Mr Xueyan?” “Until you mentioned it, I did not notice.” “Nor did I. Our conversation was so congenial that I forgot to eat. Come, we can talk over the meal.”

于是午饭就开在花厅里。左宗棠健于饮啖，但肴馔量多而质不精；一半是因为大劫以后，百物皆缺，亦无法讲求口腹之欲，席中盛馔，不过是一大盘红辣椒炒子鸡。再有一小碟腊肉；胡雪岩知道是左宗棠的周夫人，远自湖南寄来的，客人非吃不可，而且非盛赞不可，所以下箸便先挟腊肉。腊肉进口，左宗棠顾不得听他夸赞周夫人的贤德，急于想重拾中断的话题，『雪翁，』他说，『你说名利原是一样东西，这话倒似乎没有听人说过；你总有一番言之成理的说法吧？』『我原是瞎说。』胡雪岩从容答道∶『我常在想，人生在世应该先求名、还是先求利？有一天跟朋友谈到这个疑问，他说∶别的我不知道，做生意是要先求名，不然怎么叫「金字招牌」呢？这话大有道理，创出金字招牌，自然生意兴隆通四海，名归实至。岂非名利就是一样东西？』『你把实至名归这句话，颠倒来说，倒也有趣。』左宗棠又问，『除了做买卖呢？别处地方可也能用得上你这个说法不能？』

Lunch was served in the garden reception room. Zuo ate and drank heartily, but his dishes were abundant rather than refined. Part of the reason was the general shortage after the catastrophe; there was little scope for indulging the palate. The finest dish was a great platter of young chicken stir-fried with red chillies. There was also a small plate of cured pork. Hu knew it had been sent all the way from Hunan by Madam Zhou. A guest must eat it, and praise it lavishly, so it was the first thing he took with his chopsticks. Before he could properly commend her virtue, Zuo hurried back to their interrupted discussion. “Mr Xueyan, I do not think I have heard anyone say before that fame and gain are the same thing. You must have a reasoned explanation.” “I was only speaking casually,” Hu answered calmly. “I often wondered whether a man should seek fame first or profit. One day I put the question to a friend. ‘I know nothing of other matters,’ he said, ‘but in business reputation must come first. Otherwise, why do we speak of a golden signboard?’ That made excellent sense. Once you establish such a reputation, business naturally flourishes far and wide. Fame comes, and substance follows. Does that not make fame and gain the same thing?” “An interesting reversal of the saying ‘Substance comes, and fame follows’,” Zuo remarked. “But apart from trade, can your argument be applied elsewhere?”

『也有用得上的。譬如读书人，名气大了，京里的大老，都想收这个门生，还不曾会试，好象就注定了一定会点翰林似的。』说到这里，胡雪岩记起左宗棠数上春官，铩羽而归，至今还是一个举人，所以听见人谈中进士、点翰林，心里便酸溜溜地不好受；自己举这个例，实在不合时宜。好在他的机变快，就地风光，恰有一个极好的例子可举。『再譬如大人。』他说，『当年我们远在浙江，就听说湖南有位「左师爷」，真正了不起！大人名满天下，连皇上都知道，跟贵省的一位翰林说∶叫左某人出来给我办事。果不其然，不做官则已，一做便是抚台。从来初入仕途，没有一下子就当巡抚的；大人的恩遇，空前绝后。这也就是名归实至的道理。』这顶高帽子套在左宗棠头上，顿时使他起了与天相接之感，仿佛在云端里似的，飘飘然好不轻快！不自觉地拈着花白短髭，引杯笑道∶『虽蒙过奖，倒也是实情。一介举人而入仕便是封疆大吏，这个异数，老夫独叨，足令天下寒儒吐气！雪翁，来，来，我敬你一杯！』就这杯酒交欢之间，左宗棠与胡雪岩的情谊又加深了；深到几乎可以推心置腹的地步。因而说话亦越发无所隐讳顾忌。谈到咸丰曾向湖南一位翰林表示，『叫左某人出来给我办事』时；胡雪岩问说，这位翰林可是现任广东巡抚郭嵩焘？『正是他！』左宗棠的声音不自觉地高了，似乎有些激动似的。

“In some cases. Take a scholar: once he becomes famous, the great men in the capital all want him as a pupil. Before he even sits the metropolitan examination, his selection for the Hanlin Academy seems assured.” At this point Hu remembered Zuo’s repeated failures in that examination. He still held only the provincial degree, and talk of metropolitan success and Hanlin appointments left him with a sour feeling. The example was ill chosen. Fortunately, Hu’s wits were quick, and an excellent alternative lay immediately before him. “Take Your Excellency yourself. Years ago, far away in Zhejiang, we heard of an extraordinary ‘Adviser Zuo’ in Hunan. Your name was known throughout the empire, even to the emperor. He told a Hanlin scholar from your province, ‘Tell that fellow Zuo to come and work for me.’ And so it proved: either you held no office, or you became governor at once. No one begins official life as a provincial governor. The favour shown you was without precedent and will never be repeated. That too is fame bringing substance in its wake.” The compliment lifted Zuo’s spirits to the heavens. He seemed to float among the clouds, light and exhilarated. Unconsciously stroking his short, greying moustache, he raised his cup with a smile. “You praise me too highly, but it is true. To begin with only a provincial degree and enter office as a provincial chief—mine alone has been that exceptional fortune. It should hearten every poor scholar in the empire. Come, Mr Xueyan, I drink to you!” With that friendly cup, their intimacy deepened almost to the point of complete confidence. Their conversation accordingly grew freer still. Returning to the Hunan scholar whom the Xianfeng Emperor had told to summon Zuo, Hu asked whether he was Guo Songtao, now governor of Guangdong. “The very man!” Zuo’s voice rose involuntarily, as though stirred by strong feeling.

这使得胡雪岩不免困惑。因为他曾听说过，郭嵩焘救过左宗棠；对于己有恩的故交，出之以这种的异样口吻，听来真有些刺耳。左宗棠也是善于察言观色的人；而且心里也有牢骚要吐，所以很快地接下来问∶『他跟我的渊源，想来你总知道？』『知道得不多。』『那么，我来说给你听。是咸丰八年的事——。』咸丰八年春天，湖南永州镇总兵樊燮，贪纵不法，又得罪了势焰熏天的『左师爷』，因而为左宗棠主稿上奏，严劾樊燮，拜折之时，照例发炮；骆秉章坐在签押房里听见声音，觉得奇怪。看时候不是午炮，然则所为何来『听差的告诉他说∶』左师爷发军报折。『左宗棠在路秉章 幕府中，一向这样独断独行；因而又有个外号叫『左都御史』——巡抚照例挂两个衔∶一个是兵部右侍郎，便于管辖武官；一个是右副都御史，便于整饬吏治，参劾官吏。而『左师爷』的威权高过骆秉章 ，称他『左都御史』是表示右副都御史得要听他的。这一次参劾樊燮，骆秉章 事前亦无所闻；此时才要了奏折来看，措词极其严厉，但也不是无的放矢，譬如说樊燮『目不识丁』，便是实情。既已拜折，没有追回来的道理，也就算了。

Hu was puzzled. He had heard that Guo had once saved Zuo. Such an unusual tone towards an old friend and benefactor sounded distinctly jarring. Zuo too was adept at reading expressions, and had grievances he wished to air. He quickly asked, “You presumably know the connection between us?” “Not much of it.” “Then let me tell you. It happened in the eighth year of Xianfeng—” In the spring of that year, Fan Xie, brigade-general of Yongzhou in Hunan, had been greedy, lawless and unrestrained. He had also offended the immensely powerful “Adviser Zuo”. Zuo accordingly drafted a severe impeachment. When the memorial was dispatched, a cannon was fired according to custom. Luo Bingzhang heard it from his office and wondered: it was not the time for the noon gun, so what was happening? An attendant told him, “Adviser Zuo is sending a military memorial.” Zuo had always acted with such independence in Luo’s secretariat, earning another nickname, “Left Censor-in-Chief”. Governors routinely carried two additional titles: Right Vice-Minister of War, to give them authority over military officers, and Right Deputy Censor-in-Chief, to allow them to discipline and impeach officials. Since Adviser Zuo’s power exceeded Luo’s, calling him “Left Censor-in-Chief”—a play on his surname—implied that the official on the right had to obey him. Luo had heard nothing beforehand of the impeachment of Fan. Now he asked to see the memorial. Its language was severe, but the charges were not groundless. The claim that Fan could not read a single character, for example, was true. Since the memorial had already been sent, there was no recalling it. Luo let the matter rest.

其时朝廷正倚任各省带兵的督抚，凡有参劾，几乎无一不准；樊燮就此革了职。只以左宗棠挟有私怨，大为不服；便向湖广总督衙门告了一状，又派人进京向都察院呈控，告的是左宗棠，也牵连到路秉章 ，说湖南巡抚衙门是『一官两印』。这是大案，当然要查办。查办大员一个是湖广总督官文；另外一个是湖北乡试的主考官钱定青。官文左右已经受了樊燮的赌；形势对左宗棠相当不利。幸亏湖北巡抚胡林翼，与官文结上一层特殊的关系——官文的宠妾是胡老太太的义女；所以连官文都称胡林翼为『胡大哥』。这位胡老太太的义女，常对官文说∶『你什么都不懂！只安安分分做你的官，享你的福；什么事都托付给胡大哥，包你不错。』官文亦真听她的话；所以胡林翼得以从中斡旋，极力排解，帮了左宗棠很大的一个忙。

At the time, the court relied heavily on provincial chiefs commanding armies, and almost every impeachment they submitted was accepted. Fan therefore lost his post. Convinced that Zuo had acted from a private grudge, he indignantly lodged a complaint with the governor-general of Huguang and sent someone to Beijing to petition the Censorate. His charge was against Zuo, but implicated Luo as well: the Hunan governor’s office, he said, had “one post and two seals”. So grave a case naturally required investigation. The commissioners appointed were Guanwen, governor-general of Huguang, and Qian Dingqing, chief examiner of the Hubei provincial examination. Guanwen’s attendants had already accepted Fan’s bribes, and matters looked unfavourable for Zuo. Fortunately, Hu Linyi, governor of Hubei, had established a special family connection with Guanwen: the latter’s favourite concubine was the adopted daughter of Hu’s mother. Even Guanwen therefore addressed Hu as “Brother Hu”. The adopted daughter often told Guanwen, “You understand nothing. Just hold your office quietly and enjoy yourself. Leave everything to Brother Hu, and you cannot go wrong.” Guanwen genuinely listened to her. Hu Linyi could therefore mediate and work hard to settle the affair, giving Zuo great assistance.

『总而言之，郭筠仙平地青云，两年之间，因缘时会，得任封疆，其兴也暴；应该虚心克己，以期名实相称。不然，必成笑柄；甚至身败名烈！我甚为筠仙危。』说到这里，左宗棠忽然忍俊不禁了，『曾相道貌俨然，出语亦有很冷隽的时候了。前几天有人到营里来谈起，说郭筠仙责备「曾涤生平生保人甚多，可惜错保了一个毛寄云」。这话传到曾相耳里，你道他如何？』『以曾相的涵养，自然付之一笑？』『不然。曾相对人说∶』毛寄云平生保人亦不少，可惜错保一个郭筠仙！「针锋相对，妙不可言。『左宗棠说完大笑。胡雪岩亦不由得笑了；一面笑一面心里在想，郭嵩焘做这个巡抚，可说四面受敌，亏他还能撑得下去！看起来是一条硬汉；有机会倒要好好结识。左宗棠却不知怎么，笑容尽敛，忧形于色，』雪翁，『他说，』我有时想想很害怕！因为孤掌难鸣。论天下之富，苏、广并称，都以海关擅华洋之利。如今江苏跟上海有曾、李；广东又为曾氏兄弟饷源。郭筠仙虽然官声不佳，但如金陵一下，曾老九自然要得意；饮水思源，以筹饷之功，极力维持郭筠仙，亦是意中之事。照此形势，我的处境就太局促了！雪翁，你何以教我？『

“In short, Guo Yunxian has soared into the clouds. Within two years, a fortunate conjunction of circumstances has made him a provincial chief. His rise has been abrupt. He should be modest and self-disciplined, striving to make his qualities match his reputation. Otherwise he will become a laughing-stock, perhaps even ruin himself utterly. I am very anxious for him.” At this point, Zuo suddenly could not contain his amusement. “Grand Secretary Zeng looks so solemnly moral, but sometimes his remarks have a wonderfully dry edge. Someone came to camp a few days ago and reported Guo’s complaint: ‘Zeng Disheng has recommended many men in his life. Unfortunately, one of them was the ill-chosen Mao Jiyun.’ When Zeng heard it, what do you think he did?” “With his self-command, surely he merely smiled?” “Not at all. He said, ‘Mao Jiyun has also recommended many men in his life. Unfortunately, one of them was the ill-chosen Guo Yunxian.’ Point for point—exquisite!” Zuo burst out laughing. Hu laughed too, reflecting that Guo was assailed on all sides in his governorship. Remarkable that he could hold on! He seemed a tough man, worth getting to know if the opportunity arose. But for some reason Zuo’s smile vanished, and anxiety appeared. “Mr Xueyan, sometimes the thought frightens me. One hand cannot clap. For wealth, Jiangsu and Guangdong stand together, each drawing riches from Chinese and foreign trade through its maritime customs. Now Zeng and Li have Jiangsu and Shanghai, while Guangdong supplies funds to the Zeng brothers. Guo’s official reputation may be poor, but once Jinling falls, Zeng the Ninth will naturally be pleased with himself. Remembering where his resources came from, he can be expected to support Guo vigorously for his fundraising. In that situation, I shall have very little room to move. What advice can you give me?”

这番话，左宗堂说得很郑重，很深；胡雪岩亦听得很用心，很细。话外有话、意中有意；是有关左宗棠的前程，也可能有关自己利害的一件大事，不宜也不必遽尔回答，便以同样严肃的神色答道∶『大人看得很远；要让我好好想一想，才能奉答。』『好！请你好好替我想一想。』左宗棠又说，『不足为外人道。』『当然！』胡雪岩神色凛然，『我不能连这个道理都不懂。』『是，是，』左宗棠歉疚地，『我失言了。』『大人言重。』胡雪岩欠一欠身子，『等着见大人的，只怕还很多，我先告辞。』『也好！』左宗棠说，『以后你来，不必拘定时刻；也不一定要穿公服。还有，刚才我跟你谈的那件事，不必急；且看看局势再说。』

Zuo spoke gravely and with profound intent. Hu listened just as carefully. There was meaning beneath the words, purpose beneath the thought. This was a great matter touching Zuo’s future and perhaps Hu’s own interests. There was neither need nor reason to answer hastily. With equal gravity, he replied, “Your Excellency looks far ahead. I must think carefully before answering.” “Good. Please give it careful thought for me.” Zuo added, “This is not for other ears.” “Of course!” Hu’s expression was solemn. “Surely I understand that much.” “Yes, yes,” Zuo said apologetically. “I misspoke.” “You are too kind.” Hu inclined himself. “There must be many others waiting to see you. I shall take my leave.” “Very well. In future, you need not restrict your visits to fixed hours, nor must you always wear official robes. And there is no hurry about the matter we have just discussed. Let us watch how the situation develops.”
