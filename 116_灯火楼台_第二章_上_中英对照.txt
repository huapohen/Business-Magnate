第二章 深宫疑云｜CHAPTER TWO: MISGIVINGS IN THE INNER PALACE

左宗棠只睡得两个时辰，刚交子时便让老仆左贵推醒了，告诉他说：“军机徐老爷有急信。”说着，将左宗棠扶了起来，另有一仆擎着烛台，照着他看信，信封上浓墨淋漓地写着：“飞递左爵相亲钩启”，抽出信笺，上面只有八个字：“东朝上宾，请速入宫。”原来这天军机章京换班，徐用仪值夜，所以消息来得快。左宗棠遇到这种意外变故，最能沉得住气，下床看到红烛，便指着说道：“明天得换白的。”“老爷，”左贵服伺左宗棠多年，称呼一直未改，他怕自己听错了，侧耳问道：“换白蜡？”“对了。这会别多问！传轿，我马上进宫。”

Zuo Zongtang had slept for only four hours when, just after eleven that night, his old servant Zuo Gui shook him awake. “An urgent letter from Mr Xu at the Grand Council.” He helped Zuo sit up while another servant held a candlestick for him to read by. In thick, dripping ink, the envelope was addressed: “Express. To Marquis Zuo, Grand Secretary, for his personal attention.” The letter contained only eight Chinese characters: “The Eastern Empress Dowager has died. Enter the palace at once.” The Grand Council secretaries had changed shifts that day, and Xu Yongyi was on night duty, which was why the news had come so quickly. Unexpected emergencies brought out Zuo’s greatest composure. Getting out of bed, he pointed at the red candle. “Tomorrow that must be changed for a white one.” Zuo Gui had served him for many years and had never altered his form of address. Afraid that he had misheard, he bent an ear towards him. “Master, a white candle?” “Yes. No questions now. Order the sedan chair. I am going to the palace immediately.”

进宫时为丑正，乾清门未开，都在内务府朝房聚集，左宗棠一看，近支亲贵有惇亲王、醇亲王、惠亲王，御前大臣有伯彦讷漠诂、奕劻，军机大臣有宝鋆、李鸿藻、王文韶，此外便是六部尚书、“毓庆宫行走”的师傅、南书房翰林。国家大事，权在军机，军机领班的恭王不在，便该左宗棠为首。他此刻才发觉自己的地位特殊，初次当京官，朝中典故，茫然莫晓，且又遇着这样意想不到的情况，虽说他善能应变，亦有手足无措、尴尬万分之感。正要开口动问，只见徐用仪疾趋而前，借搀扶的机会，贴身说道：“听宝中堂的。”争强好胜的左宗棠，到此亦不能不退让一步，与三王略略招呼后，向宝鋆拱拱手说：“我初遇大丧，军机职司何事，都请佩翁主持。”“这是责无旁贷的事。”

It was two in the morning when he entered the palace. The Gate of Heavenly Purity was still shut, and everyone had gathered in the court waiting rooms of the Imperial Household Department. Zuo saw the close imperial kinsmen Princes Dun, Chun, and Hui; the ministers in imperial attendance Boyannemohu and Yikuang; Grand Councillors Bao Yun, Li Hongzao, and Wang Wenshao; and, besides these, the presidents of the Six Ministries, the imperial tutors serving in the Palace of Nurturing Happiness, and the Hanlin scholars of the Southern Study. Authority over great affairs of state rested with the Grand Council. In the absence of its head, Prince Gong, Zuo should take the lead. Only now did he recognize the peculiar difficulty of his position. This was his first appointment in the capital, and he knew nothing of court precedents. Faced with such an unforeseen event, even his gift for handling emergencies could not prevent him from feeling helpless and deeply embarrassed. He was about to ask a question when Xu hurried forward and, under cover of supporting him, whispered close to his body, “Follow Grand Secretary Bao.” Even Zuo, competitive and eager to prevail as he was, had to yield on this occasion. After briefly greeting the three princes, he clasped his hands towards Bao Yun. “This is my first great state mourning. Please take charge of all that falls to the Grand Council, Mr Peiheng.” “It is a duty I cannot shirk.”

一语未毕，有人来报，乾清门开了。于是惇王领头，入乾清门先到“内奏事处”。章奏出纳，皆经此处，照规矩帝后违和，派案药方亦存内奏事处，王公大臣谁都可以看的。药方一共五张，最后一张注明“西刻”，是左宗棠出宫以后请脉所开的，说是“六脉将脱，药不能下。”“宾天是什么时候？”惇王在问。“戌时。”戍时是晚上八点钟。左宗棠心里在想，接到徐用仪的信是十一点钟，计算他得知消息不会早于十点钟，相隔两个钟头，在这段辰光之中，不知道钟粹宫中是何境况？“大人！”徐用仪牵着他的袖子说：“请到南书房。”宫中定制，凡有大丧，都以乾清门内西边的南书房为“治丧办事处”。一到了那里，第一件事便是将宫帽上的顶戴与红缨子都摘了下来，然后各自按爵位官阶大小，找适当的座位坐下来。“真是想不到！”醇王向宝鋆说道：“得赶紧把六爷追回来。”“六爷”是指恭王，“已经派人去了。”宝鋆答说：“大概明天下午才能回来。”“得找个人来问一问才好。”惇王说道：“譬如有没有遗言？”“不会有的。”惠王接口：“中午的方子已经说‘神识不清’，以后牙关都撬不开，怎么能开口说话？”惇王默然，举座不语，但每人心里都有一个疑问：到底是什么病？

Before Bao Yun had finished, someone announced that the Gate of Heavenly Purity was open. Prince Dun led the way through it to the Inner Memorial Office, through which all memorials passed in and out. By established practice, when an emperor or empress was ill, the medical records and prescriptions were also kept there, available for princes and ministers to inspect. There were five prescriptions. The last, marked with the You hour, had been written after Zuo left the palace. It stated: “The pulses at all six positions are on the point of failing. Medicine cannot be swallowed.” “At what time did she pass away?” Prince Dun asked. “At the Xu hour.” That meant eight in the evening. Zuo reflected that he had received Xu’s letter at eleven, and Xu could not have learned the news before ten. Two hours had elapsed. What had happened during that interval in the Palace of Accumulated Purity? “Your Excellency.” Xu tugged at his sleeve. “Please come to the Southern Study.” Whenever there was a great imperial bereavement, palace rules made the Southern Study, west of the Gate of Heavenly Purity on its inner side, the funeral arrangements office. On arrival, the first thing everyone did was remove the buttons and red tassels from their official hats. They then took seats according to their titles and ranks. “Who could have expected this?” Prince Chun said to Bao Yun. “We must get Sixth Brother back at once.” He meant Prince Gong. “Someone has already gone,” Bao Yun replied. “He probably cannot return until tomorrow afternoon.” “We ought to find someone to question,” Prince Dun said. “Whether she left any last words, for instance.” “She could not have,” Prince Hui answered. “The noon prescription already said she was unconscious. Later they could not even prise her jaws apart. How could she speak?” Prince Dun fell silent, and no one else said a word. Yet the same question was in everyone’s mind: what had her illness been?

“要问什么病，实在没有病。”徐用仪左右看了一下，下人都在廊上，客厅中除了胡雪岩的贴身跟班以外，别无闲人，方始低声说道：“是中了毒。”此言一出，胡雪岩跟古应春互看了一眼。原来胡雪岩因为创设胡庆余堂药号，自然而然地对药性医道，都不太外行，看了从内奏事处抄出来的五张药方，又打听了慈安太后前一日御朝的情形，向古应春谈起，唯一可能的死因是中毒。此刻是证实了，只不知如何中的毒。“毒是下在点心里头的。”徐用仪说：“东太后有歇午党的习惯，睡醒以后，经常要吃甜点心。初九那天，午觉醒来，西太后派梳头太监李莲英，进了一盘松仁百果蜜糕，刚蒸出来又香又甜，东太后一连吃了三块，不到半个钟头，病就发作了。”胡雪岩骇然，“是西太后下的毒？”他问：“为什么呢？”“这话说来就长了……”

“If you ask what the illness was, there was really no illness at all.” Xu glanced about him. The servants were in the corridor; apart from Hu’s personal attendant, no outsiders remained in the reception room. Only then did he lower his voice. “She was poisoned.” Hu and Gu looked at each other. Since founding Hu Qingyu Hall, Hu had naturally acquired some knowledge of medicines and medical practice. Having examined copies of the five prescriptions from the Inner Memorial Office and enquired how Empress Dowager Ci’an had appeared at court the previous day, he had told Gu that poisoning was the only possible cause of death. To him, Xu’s account now confirmed it. What he still did not know was how the poison had been given. “It was put in a cake,” Xu said. “The Eastern Empress Dowager was accustomed to taking a nap at midday and often ate something sweet after waking. On the ninth, the Western Empress Dowager sent her hairdressing eunuch Li Lianying with a plate of honey cakes made with pine nuts and assorted dried fruits. Fresh from the steamer, they were fragrant and sweet. The Eastern Empress Dowager ate three in succession, and within half an hour the illness began.” Hu was horrified. “The Western Empress Dowager poisoned her? Why?” “That is a long story…”

慈禧太后一直有桩耿耿于怀，说什么也无法自我譬解的事，就是为什么她该低于慈安太后一等，而这一等非同小可，皇后母仪天下，生日称为“千秋”，受群臣在宫门外朝贺。下皇后一等的皇贵妃，不独无此荣耀，甚至连姓氏亦不为群臣所知。东西两宫——慈安、慈禧由“选秀女”进身，家世是一样的，慈安之父为广西左江道，慈禧之父是安徽池太广道。起初身分虽同，但当文宗元后既崩，立第二后时，选中了慈安，便使得那时封号为“懿贵妃”的慈禧，愤不能平，因为慈安无子而她有子，且是唯一的皇子，不是她的肚子争气，大清朝的帝系，将从咸丰而绝。由此可知，她是大有功于宗社的人，有功之人反遭贬损，这口气如何咽得下？可是文宗却又是一种想法，正因为她生了皇子，断送了被立为皇后的希望。原来慈禧精明能干、争胜揽权的性格，文宗已看得很清楚，自知在世之日无多，一旦驾崩，幼主嗣位，皇后成为太后，倘或骄纵不法，无人可制。

One grievance had always rankled with Empress Dowager Cixi, and no reasoning could reconcile her to it: why should she stand one degree below Empress Dowager Ci’an? That single degree mattered enormously. An empress was the mother of the realm; her birthday was celebrated as a festival of a thousand autumns, and the ministers offered congratulations outside the palace gates. An imperial noble consort, one rank lower, enjoyed none of this honour. The ministers did not even know her family name. The occupants of the Eastern and Western Palaces, Ci’an and Cixi, had both entered through the selection of palace women and came from comparable families. Ci’an’s father had been circuit intendant of Zuojiang in Guangxi; Cixi’s, circuit intendant of Chi-Tai-Guang in Anhui. Their standing had originally been equal. Yet after Emperor Wenzong’s first empress died, Ci’an was chosen as the second. Cixi, then bearing the title Noble Consort Yi, had been furious. Ci’an had no son, whereas she had borne the emperor’s only son. Had her womb not proved its worth, the direct imperial line of the Great Qing would have ended with Xianfeng. She had therefore rendered a great service to the dynasty. How could she swallow the humiliation of being placed below another woman despite that service? Wenzong, however, reasoned differently. It was precisely because she had borne an imperial son that her hope of becoming empress was extinguished. He had already recognized her intelligence and ability, her determination to win and to seize power. Knowing that he had little time left, he feared that, once he died and a child inherited the throne, an empress who became empress dowager might behave arrogantly and unlawfully with no one able to restrain her.

纵然如此，仍有隐忧，因为母以子贵，将来仍旧会成为太后，两宫并尊，而慈安赋性忠厚，必受欺侮。这重心事，偶尔与他的宠臣肃顺吐露，肃顺便劝文宗行“钩戈夫人”的故事。“钩弋夫人”是汉武帝的宠姬。当他六十三岁时，钩弋夫人为他生了一个儿子，取名弗陵，生得茁壮聪明，颇受钟爱。汉武帝晚年多病，年长诸子，看来多不成材，几经考虑，决定传位幼子弗陵，但顾虑得幼主在位，母后年轻，每每会骄淫乱政，春秋战国，不乏其例，秦始皇初年的情形，更当引以为鉴。因而狠心将钩弋夫人处死，以绝后患。文宗也觉得肃顺的建议不错，但却缺乏汉武帝的那一副铁石心肠。到得病入膏肓，势将不起时，特为用朱笔亲书密谕一道，交付慈安，大意是“西宫援母以子贵之义，不得不并尊为太后。然其人绝非可倚信者，即不有事，汝亦当专决，彼果安分无过，当始终曲全恩礼，若其失行彰著，汝可召集群臣，将联此言宣示，立即赐死，以杜后患。”不但有失谕，而且还口头叮嘱，倘或需要用这道密旨时，应该如何召集群臣，如何宣示，又如何可能有人为西宫求情，而决不可稍为之动，必须当机立断，斩草除根。慈安含泪倾听，将朱谕珍重密藏，而心里却从未想过有用得到它的一天。

Even so, an anxiety remained. A mother’s status rose with her son’s. Cixi would still become empress dowager, and the two women would share that dignity. Kind and honest by nature, Ci’an would surely be bullied. Wenzong occasionally confided this concern to his favourite minister Sushun, who advised him to follow the precedent of Lady Gouyi. She had been a favourite consort of Emperor Wu of Han. When he was sixty-three, she bore him a son, Fuling, a sturdy, intelligent child whom he loved dearly. Ill in his old age, and finding his older sons generally unpromising, Emperor Wu considered the matter repeatedly before deciding to leave the throne to Fuling. But a child emperor with a young mother often meant a licentious empress dowager disrupting government. There had been many examples in the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods; the early reign of the First Emperor of Qin provided a particularly clear warning. Emperor Wu therefore hardened his heart and put Lady Gouyi to death to forestall trouble. Wenzong approved Sushun’s suggestion but lacked Emperor Wu’s iron resolution. When his illness became incurable and death was near, he instead wrote a secret edict in vermilion and entrusted it to Ci’an. Its substance was: “The Western Palace must, by the principle that a mother’s dignity rises with her son’s, share the title of empress dowager. Yet she is by no means a person to be trusted. Even when there is no trouble, decisions must remain yours alone. If she keeps her place and commits no fault, preserve every kindness and courtesy towards her to the end. But if her misconduct becomes manifest, summon the ministers, show them these words of Mine, and order her death immediately, to prevent future calamity.” Besides the written edict, he gave detailed oral instructions: how she should summon the ministers and announce it; how someone might plead for the Western Palace; and how she must remain unmoved, decide at once, and eradicate the danger. Ci’an listened in tears and carefully hid the vermilion edict, never imagining in her heart that she would have occasion to use it.

事隔二十年，慈禧已经四十六岁，这年，光绪六年二月初，忽然得了重病，脉案对病因的叙述，含糊不清，而所开药方，则属于专治胎前产后诸症的“四物汤”，群臣皆为之困惑不解。据御医庄守和、李德立向人透露，说是“血崩”，但用血崩的药，却并不对症。于是降旨征医。直隶总督荐山东泰武临道无锡薛福辰，山西巡抚曾国荃荐太原府阳曲县知县杭州汪守正，此两人都是世家子弟，饱读医书，精研方脉，六月间先后到京，一经“请脉”，都知病根所在，不约而同的表示慈禧太后患的是“骨蒸”，其实是“蓐劳”，产后失血过多，成了俗语所说的“干血痨”，用温补甘平之法，病势日有起色。到了这年年底，已无危险，只待调养了。宅心仁厚的慈安太后，自然亦为之庆幸。有一天，就在几天以前，在她所住的钟粹宫，邀慈禧共餐，还喝了酒，到得席散，暗示宫女尽皆回避，促膝深谈，作了一番规劝。

Twenty years had passed, and Cixi was forty-six. Early in the second month of Guangxu’s sixth year, she suddenly became seriously ill. The medical records were vague about the cause, but the prescription was Four-Substance Decoction, a treatment for disorders before and after childbirth. The ministers were baffled. The imperial physicians Zhuang Shouhe and Li Deli told people that she was suffering from severe uterine bleeding, yet medicines for that condition did not suit her symptoms. An edict therefore called for doctors to be recommended. The governor-general of Zhili proposed Xue Fuchen of Wuxi, circuit intendant of Tai-Wu-Lin in Shandong; Zeng Guoquan, governor of Shanxi, recommended Wang Shouzheng of Hangzhou, magistrate of Yangqu County in Taiyuan Prefecture. Both came from established families, had read widely in medical works, and had studied prescriptions and pulse diagnosis closely. They arrived in the capital, one after the other, in the sixth month. As soon as they examined her, both understood the root of the illness and independently described it as “bone-steaming fever.” In fact it was postpartum exhaustion: excessive loss of blood after childbirth had produced what was commonly called “dry-blood wasting disease.” With warming, nourishing medicines of a sweet and mild nature, her condition improved daily. By the end of that year she was out of danger and needed only convalescence. Naturally, the kindly Ci’an rejoiced. One day, only a few days earlier, she had invited Cixi to dine in her residence, the Palace of Accumulated Purity. They drank wine as well. After the meal, she signalled all the palace women to withdraw, drew close to Cixi, and began a long, intimate conversation in which she offered some counsel.

据私下窥视的宫女所传出来的消息，说是慈安真的动了感情，首先追叙当年文宗逃难到热河的种种苦楚，文宗崩后，“孤儿寡妇”受肃顺欺侮，幸而“姐妹”同心协力，诛除权臣，转危为安。接着又谈同治十三年间的经历的大风大浪，种种苦乐，说到伤心之处，“姐妹”俩相对流涕，互为拭泪，看来慈禧也动了感情了。于是慈安慨然说道：“我们姐妹也都老了，重新同侍先帝的日子，不会太远。二十多年相处，从来没有起过什么了不得的争执，以后当然亦是平平静静过日子。有样东西是先帝留下来的，我一直以为永远也用不着，不过我怕我一死以后，有人捡到这样东西，会疑心我们姐妹表面和好，暗底下不是那回事，那就不但你我会觉得是一大恨事，先帝亦会自悔多事。这样东西，不如今天就结束了它吧！”说完，从怀中掏出一个信封，递到慈禧手里，打开来一看，慈禧脸色大变，原来就是文宗亲自以朱笔所写的那道密谕。“既然无用，就烧掉了吧！”慈安取回原件，就在烛火上点燃焚毁。慈禧作出感极而泣的神情，还需慈安多方安慰，方能收泪。但从此慈禧只要一见了慈安，便如芒刺在背，处处小心，象唯恐不能得慈安欢心似的。这一天，就是三天前的三月初九，慈安太后终于在一盘松仁百果蜜糕上送了命。

According to reports passed on by palace women who secretly watched them, Ci’an was deeply moved. She first recalled the hardships of their flight with Wenzong to Rehe and how, after his death, the “widows and orphan” had been bullied by Sushun. Fortunately the two “sisters” had acted together, destroyed the overmighty ministers, and turned danger into safety. She went on to recall the storms of Tongzhi’s thirteenth year and all their shared sorrows and joys. At the painful passages they wept together and wiped each other’s tears. Cixi too appeared moved. Ci’an then said with feeling, “We are both growing old, Sister. The day when we serve the late emperor together again cannot be far away. In more than twenty years together, we have never had any great quarrel. Of course we shall live out the rest of our days in peace as well. There is something the late emperor left me which I have always believed I should never need. But I fear that after my death someone may find it and suppose our harmony was only on the surface, concealing something quite different. That would be a lasting grief to us both, and even the late emperor would regret his unnecessary concern. Let us put an end to it today.” She took an envelope from inside her clothes and handed it to Cixi. On opening it, Cixi’s expression changed sharply: it was the secret edict Wenzong had written in vermilion with his own hand. “Since it is of no use, let us burn it.” Ci’an took back the document and held it to the candle flame until it was destroyed. Cixi made a show of weeping with overwhelming gratitude, and Ci’an had to comfort her at length before she stopped. Afterwards, however, the sight of Ci’an made Cixi feel as if thorns were pricking her back. She was careful in everything, seemingly afraid of failing to please her. Then, on the ninth day of the third month, three days before this conversation, Ci’an met her death through a plate of honey cakes with pine nuts and dried fruits.

“这样说，以后是西太后一个人作主的局面了？”胡雪岩问说，“ ”筱翁，你看事情是比以前难办呢，还是比以前容易？““我看要比以前难办。”徐用仪答说：“东太后德胜于才，军机说什么就是什么，西太后才胜于德，稍微马虎一点，她就会抓住毛病，问得人无话可说。”“这话说得不错。不过将来只要把一个人敷衍好了，事情也不至于太难。”“呃，”徐用仪不免诧异，“胡大先生，你说要敷衍哪一个人？”“李莲英。”胡雪岩说，“他立了这么大的功劳。当然会得宠。”“嗯，嗯！”徐用仪说：“我倒还没有想到。”“我也没有想到。”古应春接口说道：“我看，这条路子如果要走，就要走得早。“徐用仪不作声，意思当然是“你们要走太监的路子，另请高明”。胡雪岩体会得他的心境，便向古应春递个眼色，暗示他不必再谈李莲英。

“So from now on the Western Empress Dowager will decide everything alone?” Hu asked. “Mr Xiaoyun, do you think affairs will become harder to manage, or easier?” “Harder, I think. The Eastern Empress Dowager had more virtue than ability and accepted whatever the Grand Council said. The Western has more ability than virtue. Be the least bit careless and she will seize on the flaw and question you until you have no answer.” “Quite true. Still, if one particular person is kept happy, things should not be too difficult.” Xu was surprised. “Whom do you mean, Mr Hu?” “Li Lianying. After rendering such a service, he is bound to become a favourite.” “Yes, yes,” Xu said. “I had not thought of that.” “Nor had I,” Gu added. “If we mean to use that route, we should make a start early.” Xu said nothing. His meaning was plain: if they wanted to make connections through the eunuchs, they must find someone else to help them. Hu understood his feelings and gave Gu a look, signalling him to say no more about Li Lianying.

不过，宝鋆还是要谈的。古应春将胡雪岩准备送五万银子，而他认为其中应该留一万银子作开销，问徐用仪有何意见？“送宝中堂不必那么多，多了他反而会疑心，以为这笔借款中，又有多少好处。钱要花在刀口上，一文抵十文用，才算本事。”“那么，筱翁！”胡雪岩笑道：“你倒说说看，要怎么样才算花在刀口上？”“我亦是知其然而不知其所以然。总之，如今既然左大人打算独断独行了，空中堂那里，就不必送那么重的礼。不然就变成‘塞狗洞’了。”“ ‘塞狗洞’的事，我做过很多。”胡雪岩说：“既然筱翁不赞成，我们就来想它个礼轻意思重的办法。”“这办法不大好想。”古应春问道：“是不是跟朱铁口去谈一谈。”“没有用。这方面的行情他不懂。”

Bao Yun, however, still had to be discussed. Gu explained that Hu had been prepared to give fifty thousand taels, but that he himself thought ten thousand should be kept back for expenses. What did Xu think? “There is no need to give Grand Secretary Bao so much. Too much will only make him suspicious. He will wonder how much profit there is in this loan. Money must be spent where it counts. Making one cash do the work of ten is the real skill.” Hu smiled. “Then tell us, Mr Xiaoyun: how can we spend it where it counts?” “I know the principle, but not how to apply it. In any case, now that Lord Zuo intends to act on his own authority, there is no need to give Grand Secretary Bao such a heavy present. Otherwise it becomes a matter of ‘blocking the dog’s hole.’” “I have blocked plenty of dog holes in my time,” Hu said. “But since you disapprove, let us find a way to make a modest gift carry great meaning.” “That will not be easy,” Gu said. “Should we speak to Zhu Tiekou?” “No use. He does not understand the going rates for this sort of thing.”

三个人沉默了好一会，胡雪岩突然说道：“筱翁，你倒谈一谈，宝中堂是怎么样一个人？”“人是很念旧的……”因为念旧重情，宝鋆受了许多累。其中有件事，凡是浙江人无不知道，六、七年前轰动海内的杨乃武与小白菜一案，将因病暴毙死的小白菜之夫葛品莲，当作武大郎，而诬指小白菜谋杀亲夫，又将杨乃武比作西门庆，教唆小白菜下毒的“灭门县令”刘锡彤，就是宝鋆的乡榜同年。“宝中堂倒没有袒护刘锡彤，不过刘锡彤总以为宝中堂一向念旧，有此大军机的靠山，做借就做借了，没有什么了不起。结果是害己害人，连累宝中堂也听了好些闲话。”“这刘锡彤呢？”胡雪岩说，“充军在哪里？”“老早死掉了。”徐用仪说：“你想七十岁的人还要充军，不要说关外冰天雪地吃不消，自己想想，对不起祖宗，对不起自己，哪里还有活下去的味道？”“是啊！做人总要有味道，活下去才有劲。”胡雪岩又问：“他是哪里人？”“靠近沧州的盐山。”“家里还有什么人？”“不大清楚。”徐用仪说：“他有个儿子，本来也是牵涉在杨乃武那一案里的，后来看看事情闹大了，刘锡彤叫他回盐山，哪知坐的是福星轮。”福星轮沉没，是在中国海域中发生的第一件重大海难事件，所以徐用仪不说，也知道刘锡彤之子已经遭难。“哪里有什么一路福星？”古应春说道，“祸福无门，唯人自召。刘锡彤居心可恶，才会遭祝。不过报应也太惨了。”

The three were silent for some time before Hu suddenly asked, “Tell us something about Grand Secretary Bao himself, Mr Xiaoyun. What sort of man is he?” “He is very loyal to old friendships…” That loyalty and attachment had brought Bao Yun many burdens. One affair was known to everyone from Zhejiang: the case of Yang Naiwu and Little Cabbage, which had shocked the whole country six or seven years earlier. Little Cabbage’s husband, Ge Pinlian, had died suddenly from illness. Yet the magistrate Liu Xitong, a “destroyer of households,” had cast him as Wu Dalang, falsely accused Little Cabbage of murdering her husband, and made Yang Naiwu into another Ximen Qing who had urged her to use poison. Liu had passed the provincial examination in the same year as Bao Yun. “Grand Secretary Bao did not shield him,” Xu said. “But Liu kept thinking that Bao’s loyalty to old friends, and his position as a leading Grand Councillor, gave him powerful backing. If he did something wrong, so what? In the end he ruined himself, harmed others, and exposed Grand Secretary Bao to a great deal of criticism as well.” “What became of Liu?” Hu asked. “Where was he sent into penal exile?” “He died long ago. Think of it: a man of seventy sent into exile. Quite apart from being unable to stand the ice and snow beyond the passes, he had only to reflect on how he had disgraced his ancestors and himself. What savour could life have left for him?” “True. Life needs some savour if a man is to have the spirit to go on. Where was he from?” “Yanshan, near Cangzhou.” “Who is left in his family?” “I am not sure. He had a son who was also implicated in the Yang Naiwu case. When things began to get serious, Liu sent him back to Yanshan. Unfortunately, he sailed on the Fuxing.” The sinking of the Fuxing had been the first major maritime disaster in Chinese waters; Xu did not need to add that Liu’s son had drowned. “So much for a lucky star lighting the whole journey,” Gu said. “Good and ill fortune have no gate: we invite them ourselves. Liu’s malicious intentions brought disaster on him. Still, the retribution was terribly harsh.”

“打听，打听。”胡雪岩说：“刘锡彤总算在我们杭州做过父母官，子孙如果没饭吃，应该做个好事。”徐用仪心想，胡雪岩哪里是为刘锡彤做过余杭县知县的香火之情，无非看在宝鋆分上，做件小小的雪中送炭之事，希望见好于宝鋆.不过他亦必须有这么个冠冕堂皇的说法，才不落痕迹，否则就会为人所讥。人情世故毕竟是他识得透。这样转着念头，不由得又想起一个人，“宝中堂有个弟弟叫宝森，”他问：“胡大先生知道不知道？”“不知道。此人怎么样？“”此人去年让言路上参了一本。参的其实不是他，是宝中堂，参宝中堂袒护亲族。不过，这一来倒楣的一定是宝森，如今境况很窘。“”呃，筱翁，你倒谈谈他倒楣的来龙去脉。“原来宝鋆之弟宝森，本是直隶的候补知县，既没有读多少书，也谈不到才具，而且理路不大清楚。靠他老兄的面子，总常有差使派他，有时州县出缺，派他去署理，坐堂问案，笑话百出，上官看宝鋆的分上，只有格外宽容。后来曾国藩由两江总督调直隶，他是讲究吏治的，看宝森实在没有用处，就想照应他亦有力不从心之感，宝森几次找宝鋆，要他写八行书给曾国藩讨差使，宝鋆怕碰钉子，不肯出信。到得真的缠不过了，宝鋆说：”你到四川去吧！“为他加捐，由候补县变成候补道，又在吏部说了情，得以分发四川。

“Make some enquiries,” Hu said. “Liu did serve as a parent-official in our Hangzhou district. If his descendants have nothing to eat, we ought to do them a kindness.” Xu thought that gratitude for Liu’s service as magistrate of Yuhang had nothing to do with it. Hu simply wanted to do a small kindness in a time of need, for Bao Yun’s sake, and win his favour. Still, a respectable explanation was necessary to conceal the purpose and avoid criticism. Hu did understand human dealings thoroughly. The thought brought another man to Xu’s mind. “Grand Secretary Bao has a younger brother, Baosen. Do you know him?” “No. What is he like?” “Last year a censorial memorial attacked him. The real target was not him but Grand Secretary Bao, who was accused of shielding his relatives. Yet Baosen was bound to suffer for it. His circumstances are now very difficult.” “Tell us how his misfortunes came about.” Baosen had been an expectant county magistrate in Zhili. He had read little, had no notable abilities, and was not particularly clear-headed. Thanks to his brother’s influence, assignments were constantly found for him. Occasionally he acted in a vacant subprefectural or county post, where his conduct of hearings produced endless absurdities. His superiors made exceptional allowances out of regard for Bao Yun. When Zeng Guofan was transferred from the Liangjiang governor-generalship to Zhili, however, matters changed. Zeng cared about sound administration. Baosen was so useless that even a wish to accommodate him could achieve little. Baosen repeatedly asked his brother to write a personal letter to Zeng seeking an appointment. Fearing a rebuff, Bao Yun refused. Finally, worn down by the pestering, he said, “Go to Sichuan!” He paid the additional contribution to raise Baosen from expectant magistrate to expectant circuit intendant, then used his influence at the Ministry of Personnel to have him assigned to Sichuan.

四川总督名叫吴棠，此人于慈禧太后未入宫以前，有援之于穿途末路的大恩。慈禧之父惠征，官居安徽池太广道，是守土有责的地方官，威丰初年，洪杨起事，舟船东下，势如破竹，惠征望风而逃，降旨革职查办，旋即一病而亡。俗语说：“太太死了压断街，老爷死了没人抬”，官场最势利不过，何况惠征是“犯官”的身分，加以外省的旗汉之别，远较京里来得分明。因此，慈橹以长女的身分，携带一妹两弟，奉母盘灵回旗时，一路遭受白眼，那种境况，真可说是凄凉万状。一天船泊江苏淮安府桃源县，忽然有人送来一份奠仪，而且颇为丰腆，白银二百两之多。慈禧再看名帖上具衔是桃源县知县吴棠，不由得纳闷，惠征从无这样一个朋友，如说是照例的应酬，隔省的官员，了无渊源，充其量送八两银子奠仪，已是仁至义尽。一送二百两，阔得出奇，慈禧判断，一定是送错了，防着人家要来索还，原封不动地摆在那里。她的判断不误，果然是送错了。吴棠一看听差送上来的回帖，大发雷霆，幸而他有个幕友，深明人情世故，便劝他说：“送错了礼没有去讨回之理，就讨，人家也未见得肯还。听说这惠道台的两位小姐，长得很齐整，而且知书识字，旗人家的闺秀，前途不可限量。东翁不如将错就错，索性送个整人情，去吊上一吊。”吴棠心想，这不失为“失之东隅，收之桑榆”的打算，当下肃其衣冠，备了祭品，传轿打道运河码头，投了帖上船祭灵。祭毕慰问家属，慈禧的两个弟弟惠祥、照样，都还年幼，只会陪礼，无从陪客，都是慈禧隔着白布灵幔，与吴棠对答，再三称谢。这一下足以证明，吴棠的奠仪并未送错，可以放心大胆地支用了。慈禧感激涕零之余，将吴棠的名帖放在梳头盒子里，跟妹妹相誓：“倘或天可怜见，咱们姐妹也有得意的一天，可千万别忘了吴大老爷这位雪中送炭的大恩人。”

The governor-general there was Wu Tang, who, before Cixi entered the palace, had helped her at a moment of utter destitution. Her father Huizheng, circuit intendant of Chi-Tai-Guang in Anhui, had been responsible for defending his territory. Early in the Xianfeng reign, when the forces of Hong and Yang swept east down the river, he fled at their approach. An edict dismissed him and ordered an investigation; soon afterwards he fell ill and died. As the saying went, “When the mistress dies, mourners block the street; when the master dies, no one will carry the coffin.” Nowhere was social calculation stronger than in official circles. Huizheng had also been an accused official, and the distinction between Bannermen and Han was much sharper in the provinces than in the capital. As the eldest daughter, Cixi accompanied her mother, a younger sister, and two younger brothers in taking her father’s coffin back to their Banner. They met cold looks all along the way and were in a pitiful state. One day their boat moored at Taoyuan County in Huai’an Prefecture, Jiangsu. Someone unexpectedly brought a generous funeral gift: two hundred taels of silver. The visiting card named Wu Tang, magistrate of Taoyuan. Cixi was puzzled. Her father had never had such a friend. If it was merely a conventional courtesy from an official of another province with no connection to them, eight taels would have been more than generous. Two hundred was astonishing. She concluded that it must be a mistake and set the silver aside untouched, in case it was reclaimed. She was right. When Wu saw the acknowledgement his servant brought back, he flew into a rage. Fortunately, a secretary who understood the world advised him, “A gift sent by mistake cannot properly be demanded back. Even if you ask, they may not return it. I hear Intendant Hui’s two daughters are very good-looking and can read and write. Young ladies of a Banner family may have limitless prospects. Why not turn the mistake to advantage, complete the kindness, and pay a visit of condolence?” Wu thought this might retrieve in the west what he had lost in the east. He dressed formally, prepared offerings, and took his sedan chair to the canal landing. After sending in his card, he went aboard to pay his respects to the dead. Cixi’s brothers Huixiang and Zhaoyang were too young to do more than return the courtesies, so Cixi herself spoke with him from behind the white mourning curtain and thanked him repeatedly. The visit reassured the family that the gift was indeed intended for them, and they could safely spend it. In tears of gratitude, Cixi put his card in her dressing box and made a vow with her sister: “If Heaven takes pity on us and our own day of good fortune comes, we must never forget Magistrate Wu, who brought us help in our hour of need.”

果然“皇天不负苦心人”，姐妹做了妯娌，不过十年的工夫，姐姐“以天下养”，妹妹亦贵为醇王的福晋。辛酉政变，两宫垂帘听政，慈禧第一件快心之事，便是报恩，这时已升知府的吴棠，官符如火，一路超，吴棠既庸且贪，而凡有参劾吴棠的折子，一概不准。不过五、六年的工夫，继骆秉章而为四川总督。他在成都，公事委诸属下，每天开筵演戏，顿顿鱼翅鸡鸭，自我豢养成一个臃肿不堪的大胖子，四川人替他起了个外号，叫做“一品肉”。宝望为老弟的打算是，惟有到“一品肉”那里当差，不必顾虑才具之短。果然，吴棠看宝是大军机，一到就派了“厘金”的差使，终吴棠之任，宝森的税差没有断过，是四川官场的红员之一。

Heaven did indeed reward their earnest hopes. The sisters married brothers. Within ten years, the elder was supported by the wealth of the whole realm, while the younger had become Princess Chun. After the Xinyou coup, when the two empress dowagers began governing from behind the curtain, one of Cixi’s first pleasures was to repay the kindness. Wu, already a prefect, rose with extraordinary speed. Though both inept and corrupt, he was protected: every memorial impeaching him was rejected. In only five or six years he succeeded Luo Bingzhang as governor-general of Sichuan. At Chengdu he left public business to his subordinates, feasted and watched plays every day, ate shark’s fin, chicken, and duck at every meal, and fed himself into a grossly swollen state. The people of Sichuan nicknamed him “First-Rank Flesh.” Bao Yun’s reasoning was that only in the service of “First-Rank Flesh” could his younger brother avoid worrying about his lack of ability. Sure enough, out of regard for the powerful Grand Councillor, Wu gave Baosen a lijin tax assignment as soon as he arrived. Throughout Wu’s tenure, Baosen never lacked a tax-collecting post and remained one of the favoured men in Sichuan officialdom.

不久，吴棠殁于任上，继任川督的是杀安德海的山东巡抚丁宝祯。安德海在两宫太后口中，称之为“小安子”，他是慈禧大后宠信的太监，在“辛酉政变”中立过功劳，升任为长春宫的总管。仗着慈禧太后的势力，招权纳贿，骄恣不法。有年夏天，打着太后的旗号，擅自出京，连直隶总督曾国藩，部只能侧目而视，不敢动他。不道丁宝帧却不买帐，等他一入山东境内，便派人严密监视，及至证实了他并未奉有赴江南采办的懿旨，便不客气地下令逮捕，飞章入奏，奉旨“毋庸讯问，就地正法”，随即提出牢来，在济南处决。安德海既为慈禧所宠信，了宝祯杀了他，就很可能得罪了慈禧。哪知事实适得其反，慈禧不但不恨，而且很感激丁宝祯，因为安德海被斩以后，丁宝侦下令暴尸三日，济南的百姓看清了安德海是没有“那话儿”的真太监。这一来，一直流传着的，安德海为慈禧面首的谣言，不攻自破。慈禧心感丁宝帧为她洗刷之德，所以吴棠出缺，将他自东抚擢为川督。当然，也有看重丁主帧清廉刚直，用他去整伤为吴棠搞坏了的四川吏治的期望在内。果然，丁宝帧一人川便大加整顿，贪庸疲软的劣员，参的参，调的调。官场气象一新。象宝森这样的人，当然也在淘汰之列，但想到他是宝釜的胞弟，不免有投鼠忌器的顾虑，处置就不一样了。

Before long Wu died in office. His successor was Ding Baozhen, the governor of Shandong who had executed An Dehai. The two empress dowagers called An “Little An.” He was a eunuch trusted by Cixi who had rendered service during the Xinyou coup and risen to chief eunuch of the Palace of Eternal Spring. Relying on her power, he gathered influence, accepted bribes, and behaved with arrogant lawlessness. One summer, claiming the empress dowager’s authority, he left the capital without permission. Even Zeng Guofan, governor-general of Zhili, dared only watch him with displeasure. Ding refused to be intimidated. As soon as An entered Shandong, he had him closely watched. Once it was established that he carried no imperial warrant to make purchases in Jiangnan, Ding ordered his arrest and sent an urgent memorial. The reply directed: “No interrogation is necessary. Execute him on the spot.” An was accordingly taken from prison and executed at Jinan. Killing Cixi’s trusted favourite might have been expected to offend her. In fact, the opposite happened. She was grateful, because Ding ordered the corpse exposed for three days, and the people of Jinan saw clearly that An was a genuine eunuch, without “the thing.” This disposed of the persistent rumour that he was Cixi’s lover. Grateful to Ding for clearing her name, Cixi promoted him to the Sichuan governor-generalship when Wu’s post became vacant. Of course she also valued his integrity and firmness and hoped he would repair the provincial administration Wu had spoiled. Ding began a thorough reform as soon as he arrived. Corrupt, incompetent, and feeble officials were impeached or transferred, and the whole atmosphere changed. A man like Baosen naturally belonged among those to be removed. Yet the fact that he was Bao Yun’s brother made Ding hesitate, afraid of damaging a precious vessel while throwing at the rat beside it. A different method was required.

象这样的情形，原有个客客气气送出门的办法，譬如督抚与两司——藩司、臬司不和，想把他们调走，而又怕伤了和气，发生纠纷，便在年终“密考”时，加上“堪任方面”的考语。既然才足以当方面之任，朝廷当然要将此人召进京去，当面察看。久而久之成了一个惯例，军机处一看督抚对两司下的是这样的考语，便知是请朝廷将两司调走，必如所请，因为封疆大吏的用人权是必须尊重的。宝森只是一个候补道，不适用此例，但亦有变通之方，即以人才特荐，奏请送部引见，意思是请朝廷考虑，此人可放实缺。那尺光绪四年年底的事。其时言路上气势很盛，除了御史、给事中这些言官以外，翰林而兼“日讲起注官”，得以专折言事者，奏议尤为朝廷所重，其中言论最犀利者四人，号称“翰林四谏”。而“四谏”中又以张佩纶的一支笔最厉害，心想宝森一无才能，只以宝鋆的关系，竟由地方大吏以人才特荐，令人不平，因而上章搏击。上谕中嘉许张佩纶“所陈绝瞻顾，尚属敢言”。至于西宝祯特荐宝森，究竟有何过人之长的实绩，命了宝祯“据实具奏，毋稍回护”。原奏又说宝森并无才能，“着李鸿章查明宝森在直隶时，官声政绩究竟如何，详细具奏”。其时宝森已经到京，兴冲冲地真的以为了宝祯够交情帮他的忙，满心打算着引见以后，靠他老兄的关系，分发到富庶的省份，弄个实缺的道员，好好过一过官瘾。正印官的气派，跟候补道毕竟是不同的。哪知跟宝鋆见了面，他第一句话就是：“你告病吧！”“为什么？”“喏，你自己看去。”很吃力地看完了张佩纶参劾的奏折，宝森倒抽一口冷气，这时才明白，丁宝祯别有用心，复奏也必是一番敷衍的空话，未见得有用。“现在言路上嚣张得很，你碰了钉子，我也帮不上你的忙。别求荣反辱吧，你先告病，过些日子，我再替你想办法。”日子过子两年了，宝森静极思动，常常跟宝鋆争吵，弟兄已有反目的模样。宝鋆经常望影而避，头痛不已。

There was an established way of politely showing someone the door. If a governor or governor-general was at odds with a provincial treasurer or judicial commissioner and wished to have him transferred without provoking a quarrel, he could write “capable of independent regional responsibility” in the confidential year-end assessment. A man so capable ought naturally to be summoned to the capital for personal examination. In time, this became a convention: the Grand Council understood such a recommendation as a request to transfer the official and granted it, since the right of the great provincial governors to choose their staff had to be respected. Baosen was merely an expectant circuit intendant, so this precedent did not apply. A variation was possible, however: a special recommendation on grounds of talent, requesting that he be sent to the Ministry of Personnel for presentation to the throne, with the implication that he deserved a substantive post. This happened late in Guangxu’s fourth year, when the censorial officials were particularly assertive. Besides censors and supervising secretaries, Hanlin scholars who also served as daily lecturers and court diarists could submit their own memorials, which the throne especially valued. Four of the sharpest were known as the “Four Hanlin Remonstrants.” The most formidable pen among them belonged to Zhang Peilun. Indignant that the wholly untalented Baosen should receive a governor-general’s special recommendation merely through his relationship with Bao Yun, Zhang impeached him. The imperial reply commended Zhang for speaking without timid consideration of personal consequences. It ordered Ding to report truthfully what exceptional achievements justified the recommendation, “without the slightest concealment or protection,” and directed Li Hongzhang to investigate Baosen’s reputation and performance while in Zhili and submit a detailed report. By then Baosen had reached the capital, delightedly believing Ding had done him a friendly service. After his audience, he expected his brother’s influence to secure him a substantive circuit post in a rich province, where he could savour being an official. Holding a real seal of office was quite different from being an expectant intendant. But his brother’s first words were, “Apply for sick leave.” “Why?” “Here. Read it yourself.” Baosen laboriously read Zhang’s memorial and drew a sharp breath. Only now did he understand Ding’s ulterior purpose. Ding’s reply to the throne would surely be no more than empty evasions and might do him no good. “The censorial officials are insufferably aggressive just now,” Bao Yun said. “If you run into trouble, I cannot help. Do not seek glory only to gain disgrace. Take sick leave first. I shall find another way for you later.” Two years had passed. Restless after his long inactivity, Baosen quarrelled with his brother so often that they seemed on the point of becoming enemies. Bao Yun avoided him whenever he caught sight of him and was thoroughly plagued by the affair.

“ 弟兄感情到了这样子，只有一个办法，把他们隔开。”胡雪岩说，“见不着面，就吵不起来了，旁人劝解，话也比较听得进去。”“胡大先生，你的话是不错，不过，请问怎么个隔法？”“那还不容易。把那位宝二爷请到哪里去住上几个月，意气慢慢化解了，弟兄到底是弟兄，终究会和好如初的。”“这倒也是个办法，可惜没有人请他。”“我请！”胡雪岩脱口而答，“如果宝二爷愿意，我把他请到上海、杭州去逛个一年半载，一切开销都是我的。”徐用仪心想，这一来宝鋆得以耳根清净，一定会领胡雪岩的情，当下表示赞成。古应春亦认为这是个别开生面的应酬宝鋆的办法，大可行得。至于胡雪岩与宝森素昧平生，看似无由一通款曲，其实容易得很，有跟胡雪岩交情深厚的文温在，便是现成的一条路子。

“When brothers reach that state, there is only one remedy: separate them,” Hu said. “If they cannot see each other, they cannot quarrel. They will also be more willing to listen to other people’s advice.” “That is true, Mr Hu, but how are they to be separated?” “Nothing easier. Invite Second Master Bao to stay somewhere for a few months. Their resentment will gradually cool. Brothers are brothers, after all; eventually they will be as close as before.” “That might work. Unfortunately, no one has invited him.” “I will!” Hu answered at once. “If he is willing, I shall invite him to Shanghai and Hangzhou for six months or a year. I shall pay all his expenses.” Xu thought Bao Yun would certainly appreciate Hu’s kindness if it gave him some peace, and approved. Gu also thought this an original and promising way to cultivate the Grand Secretary. Although Hu and Baosen had never met and appeared to have no means of approaching each other, the difficulty was easily overcome. Hu’s close friendship with Wenyu provided a ready introduction.

这天文煜宴客。本来他宦囊甚丰，起居豪奢，住处又有花木园林之胜，每逢开宴，必是丝竹杂陈，此时因逢国丧，八音遏密，同时也不便大规模宴客，以防言官纠弹，只约了少数知好，清谈小酌而已。主客是胡雪岩，其次便是宝森。主人引见以后，宝森颇道仰慕，胡雪岩更是刻意周旋，所以一见如故，谈得颇为投机。席间谈起上海“夷场”上的情形，胡雪岩与古应春大肆渲染，说得宝森向往不已。看看是时候了，古应春便即问说：“森二爷有几年没有到上海了？”“说起来寒碜。”宝森不好意思地：“我还没有去过呢！”“那可真是想不到。”古应春看看胡雪岩说：“吃花酒如果有森二爷这么有趣的人在，可就更热闹了。”宝森是所谓“旗下大爷”，吃喝玩乐，无一不精，这两年在京，全靠寄情声色，才能排遣失意，自从慈安太后暴崩，歌台舞谢，弦索不闻，正感到寂寞无聊时，听得古应春的话，自然动心。“如今是国丧，也能上堂子……”宝森突然缩住口，倒象说错了话似地。原来上海人所说的“堂子”，北方称为“窑子”。旗人口中的“ 堂子”，是皇室祭祖的所在，拿来作为窑子的别称，未免亵读，因而觉得碍口。“如今国丧，也能吃花酒？”他换了个说法。“怎么不能？”古应春答说：“一则是天高皇帝远，再则夷场是‘化外’，不管是上海道，还是松江府，都管不到，甚至于两江总督、江苏巡抚都莫奈何。”

Wenyu gave a dinner that day. His official career had made him wealthy; he lived extravagantly in a residence with fine gardens, and his banquets usually resounded with string and wind music. During the national mourning, however, all music was silenced. Large parties were also inadvisable, lest the censorial officials impeach him. He invited only a few close friends for quiet conversation over wine. Hu was the chief guest, with Baosen next in importance. Once their host had introduced them, Baosen expressed his admiration, and Hu exerted himself to please. They quickly seemed like old friends. When talk turned to Shanghai’s foreign settlement, Hu and Gu painted such glowing pictures that Baosen longed to see it. Judging the moment right, Gu asked, “How many years has it been since you visited Shanghai, Second Master Sen?” “It is embarrassing to admit,” Baosen said, “but I have never been.” “That I should never have expected.” Gu looked at Hu. “A courtesan dinner would be livelier still with someone as entertaining as Second Master Sen.” Baosen was a typical gentleman of the Banners, expert in every form of eating, drinking, and amusement. During his two years in the capital, sensual pleasures had been his only means of dispelling the bitterness of disappointment. Since Ci’an’s sudden death, music and entertainment had fallen silent. He was lonely and bored, and Gu’s words naturally tempted him. “Even during the national mourning, one may visit a tangzi…” He broke off as though he had said something improper. What Shanghainese called a tangzi was a brothel, known in the north as a yaozi. To a Bannerman, however, the Tangzi was the place where the imperial family worshipped its ancestors. Using its name for a brothel felt sacrilegious. He rephrased the question. “Even during the national mourning, one may dine with courtesans?” “Why not?” Gu replied. “For one thing, Heaven is high and the emperor far away. For another, the foreign settlement lies beyond our jurisdiction. Neither the Shanghai circuit intendant nor the prefect of Songjiang can control it. Even the governor-general of Liangjiang and the governor of Jiangsu can do nothing.”

“真的？”宝森有些不信。“我只谈一件事好了。”古应春问道：“听说森二爷票戏是人行家，有出‘张汶祥刺马’看过没有？”“听说过，可没有看过。”“那就是上海人独有的眼福、耳福，这出戏只有在上海能唱，别处是禁的。”禁演的原因是，这出戏全非事实。两江总督马新贻已经惨死在张位祥的白刃之下，而竟说他夺人之妻，有取死之道，死而被诬，冤及泉台，知道真相而稍有血性的人，无不气愤填膺。江南大吏曾谋设法禁演，但因势力不能及于夷场，徒呼负负。这一实例，说明了在京八音遏密，何以在上海可以不守国丧的规矩。宝森真是想去好好逛一逛，但有些说不出口。看出他的心情的胡雪岩，便即说道：“其实不说那些花花草草的花样，森二爷也该到上海去见识见识。如今大家都讲洋务，不到上海不知道洋务该怎么讲法？宝中堂是身分。地位把他绊住了，没有机会到上海，森二爷不妨代替宝中堂去看一看。”这为他拈出了一个正大光明的理由，宝森大为兴奋，“我也不为他，为我自己。”他说：“长点见识总是好的。将来到了上海，还要请胡大哥带一带我。”“言重了。”胡雪岩问道：“森二爷预备什么时候去？”“这还不能定。我得先跟本旗请假。”在京的旗人，不能随便出京，这个规矩在雍、乾年间，极其严格，以后慢慢地也放宽了。不过宝森因为他老兄一再告诫，诸事谨慎，所以不敢造次。

“Really?” Baosen was doubtful. “Let me give you one example. I hear you are an expert amateur actor, Second Master Sen. Have you seen the play Zhang Wenxiang Assassinates Ma?” “I have heard of it, but never seen it.” “That is a pleasure reserved for the eyes and ears of Shanghai people. The play can be performed only there. Everywhere else it is banned.” The reason for the ban was that the play was wholly untrue. Ma Xinyi, governor-general of Liangjiang, had already died horribly under Zhang Wenxiang’s blade; now the play accused him of taking another man’s wife and bringing death upon himself. Slandering a dead man, pursuing injustice beyond the grave, filled everyone who knew the truth and had any spirit with indignation. Jiangnan’s senior officials had tried to stop the performances, but their authority did not extend to the foreign settlement, and their efforts came to nothing. This example explained why Shanghai could disregard the rules of mourning while all music in the capital was silenced. Baosen truly wanted to go, but found it difficult to say so. Seeing this, Hu said, “Quite apart from such amusements, you ought to see Shanghai, Second Master Sen. Everyone talks about foreign affairs nowadays. Without visiting Shanghai, how can one know what that really means? Grand Secretary Bao is tied down by his rank and position and has no chance to go. You might see it on his behalf.” Given this entirely respectable reason, Baosen became excited. “I shall go for myself, not for him. It is always good to learn more of the world. When I reach Shanghai, Brother Hu, I must ask you to show me about.” “You are too kind. When do you intend to go?” “I cannot say yet. First I must request leave from my Banner.” Bannermen in the capital were not free to leave at will. The rule had been particularly strict under Yongzheng and Qianlong and had gradually relaxed, but Baosen’s brother had repeatedly warned him to be careful in everything, so he dared not act rashly.

这时一直未曾说话的文煜开口了：“老二，我准你的假。”原来文煜就是他正白旗的都统。“啊，啊、对了。”宝森“啪”地一下，在自己额上打了一厂，“看我这个脑筋！竟忘了本旗的长官，就在眼前。”“文大人，”胡雪岩问谊：“准他多少日子的假？”“那要问他自己。”“我想，”宝森答说：“一个月也差不多了。”“不够，不够。一个月连走马看花都谈不到，起码要三个月。”“三个月就三个月。”文烃向主森说道：“这得找个理由，你就写个呈文，说赴沪就医好了。”宝森还在踌躇，胡雪岩抢着说道：“好了！文大人准假三个月。森二爷，这三个月归我管，你一切不必费心。我大概还有五、六天耽搁，请你料理料理，我们一起走。”邂逅初逢，即使一见如故，这样被邀到纸醉金迷之地，流连三月之久而不费分文，真也可说是难得的奇遇。因为如此，反而令人有难以接受之感，主森只是搓着手，矜持地微笑着，不知道该怎么说才好。“老二，”文煜知道他的心情，忍不住开口：“你久在四川，对雪岩不熟。雪岩豪爽出了名的，只要投缘，象这么请你到南边玩上几个月，算不了什么。我看你在京里也无聊得很，不如到上海去散散心。交朋友的日子很长，你也不必觉得不好意思。”“我可真是有点儿不好意思。”宝森乘机说道：“恭敬不如从命，我先跟胡大哥道谢。”“说这话就见外了。”胡雪岩转脸对古应春，“叫惟贤明天派人到森二爷公馆去招呼，行李不必多带，缺什么在上海预备也很方便。”

Wenyu, who had remained silent, now spoke. “Second Brother, I grant you leave.” He was the commander of Baosen’s Plain White Banner. “Ah, of course!” Baosen slapped his forehead. “What a head I have! I had forgotten that my own Banner commander was sitting here.” “How much leave will you grant him, Lord Wen?” Hu asked. “That is for him to say.” “I think a month should be enough,” Baosen answered. “Not nearly enough! In one month you could scarcely even glance at things in passing. At least three months.” “Three months, then,” Wenyu said. “But you need a reason. Submit an application saying you wish to seek medical treatment in Shanghai.” Baosen was still hesitating when Hu took up the matter. “Settled! Lord Wen grants you three months. You will be in my care for those three months, Second Master Sen. You need worry about nothing. I shall probably be delayed here another five or six days. Put your affairs in order, and we shall travel together.” However quickly they had become friends, an invitation from a man just met to spend three months amid splendid pleasures without paying a cash was an extraordinary piece of good fortune. Precisely for that reason, it was hard to accept. Baosen rubbed his hands and smiled reservedly, uncertain what to say. Wenyu understood and intervened. “You have been in Sichuan so long that you do not know Xueyan. His generosity is famous. When he takes to someone, inviting him south for a few months means nothing to him. You seem thoroughly bored in the capital. Why not go and enjoy yourself in Shanghai? A friendship has many years ahead of it. There is no need to feel awkward.” “I really do feel a little embarrassed,” Baosen said, taking the opportunity. “But obedience is better than excessive courtesy. Let me thank you now, Brother Hu.” “That is too formal between friends.” Hu turned to Gu. “Have Weixian send someone to Second Master Sen’s house tomorrow to attend to his preparations. There is no need for much luggage. Anything lacking can easily be supplied in Shanghai.”

第二天午后，汪惟贤亲自去拜访宝森，执礼甚恭，自不待言，略事寒暄，谈入正题，首先问说，“森二老爷预备带几个人？”宝森不好意思，略想一一想答说：“我只带一个。”“一个怎么够？”汪惟贤屈着手指说：“打烟的一个，打杂的一个，出门跟班的一个，至少得三个人。”“我就带一个打烟的。”宝森略有些不好意思地，“有一口嗜好，没法子。”“这是福寿膏。”汪惟贤将手边一个长形布袋拿了起来，脱去布套，是个打磨得光可鉴人的紫檀长方盒，顺手递过去说：“森二老爷倒看看，这样东西怎么样？”宝森接来一看，盒盖上刻着一行填彩的隶书：“吹萧引凤”。便知是一支烟枪，抽开盒盖，果不其然。虽抽了三十年的鸦片，见过许多好烟具，这一支十三节湘妃竹的烟枪，所镶的绿玉烟嘴固然名贵，但妙处却在竹管，是用橄榄核累贯到底，核中打通，外凉内热，抽起来格外过瘾。“好东西。”宝森爱不忍释，“总得二百两银子吧？”“森二老爷中意，就不必问价钱了。请留着用吧！”汪惟贤不容他谦辞，紧接着又说：“敝东交代，森二老爷不必带烟盘。太累赘，都由我们预备。”说到这样的话，倘再客气，就变得虚伪了。宝森拱拱手说：“胡大先生如此厚爱，实在心感不尽。不过，人，我准定只带一个，带多了也是累赘。”“是，是。我们那里有人，森二老爷少带也不要紧。还有，现在是国丧，穿着朴素，森二老爷不必带绸衣服，等穿孝期满，在上海现做好了。”

The next afternoon Wang Weixian called in person. His manner was, of course, most respectful. After a few courtesies he came to business. “How many attendants do you intend to take, Second Master Sen?” A little embarrassed, Baosen considered before answering, “Only one.” “How can one be enough?” Wang counted on his fingers. “One to prepare your opium, one for general tasks, and one to accompany you when you go out. You need at least three.” “I shall take only the one who prepares my pipe,” Baosen said, still somewhat ashamed. “I have the habit. It cannot be helped.” “It is the paste of good fortune and long life.” Wang picked up a long cloth bag beside him and removed its cover, revealing a rectangular red-sandalwood box polished like a mirror. He handed it over. “See what you think of this.” The lid bore an inlaid, coloured clerical-script inscription: “Playing the Flute to Bring the Phoenix.” Baosen knew at once that it held an opium pipe. He slid the lid open, and there it was. In thirty years of smoking, he had seen many fine implements, but this pipe was exceptional. Its green-jade mouthpiece, set into a thirteen-jointed stem of mottled bamboo, was valuable enough. Its real excellence lay in the stem, lined from end to end with olive stones threaded together and bored through: cool outside and hot within, it made smoking especially satisfying. “A fine thing.” He could hardly bear to put it down. “It must cost two hundred taels?” “If it pleases you, there is no need to ask the price. Please keep it.” Before Baosen could decline, Wang continued, “My employer says you need not bring your smoking tray. It would be too cumbersome. We shall provide everything.” Further polite resistance would now have seemed insincere. Baosen clasped his hands. “I cannot express my gratitude for Mr Hu’s kindness. But I am quite determined to bring only one man. More would be cumbersome too.” “Certainly. We have people, so it does not matter if you bring fewer. And since we are in national mourning and dress plainly, you need not bring silk clothes. When the mourning period is over, they can be made in Shanghai.”

他说什么，宝森应什么。等汪惟贤一走，想一想不免得意，用新得的烟枪过足了瘾，看辰光未时已过，宝鋆已经下朝了，乘兴省兄，打算去谈一谈这件得意之事。宝鋆家的门上，一看“二老爷”驾到，立即就紧张了，飞速报到上房。宝萎刚想关照：说我头疼，已经睡了。只见宝森已大踏步闯了进来，料想挡也挡不住，只能叹口气，挥一挥手，命门上了退了下去。“你那件事，过一阵子再说。”宝鋆一见了他老弟的面就先开口，“这会儿办东太后的丧事，大家都忙得不可开交，我也不好意思跟人家提。”“哪一件？”宝森要他老兄托人情的事太多了，不知他指的是哪一件，所以如此发问。“你不是兜揽了一件帮人争产的官司吗？”“喔，那一件。”宝森答说：“如今我可没工夫管人家的事了。”原来宝森受人之托，有件庶出之子，向嫡出长兄要求分家的官司，要求宝鋆向顺天府尹说情，将庶出之子的状子驳回。他从杨乃武那一案，受刘锡彤之累，为清议抨击以后，凡是这类牵涉刑名的案件，不愿再管，无奈宝森一再纠缠，只能饰词敷衍，每一次要想不同的理由来拖延，深以为苦，因而此刻听得宝森的话，顿觉肩头一轻，浑身自在了。“我特为来跟大哥说，我要到上海去一趟，总得两三个月才能回来。”“喔，”宝鋆问道：“到上海去干什么？”“有人请我去玩两三个月。管吃管住。外带管接管送，一共是四管，自己一个子儿都不用花。”“好家伙。管你到上海玩两三个月，不要分文，谁那么阔啊？”“胡雪岩。”“原来你交上‘财神，了！”宝鋆立刻沉下脸来，“你可别胡乱许了人家什么，替我添麻烦。”宝森愕然，“人家会是事托我？”他问：“会有什么事呢？”“谁知道，此人的花样，其大无比，这一趟是来替左季高筹划借洋债，说不定就会托你来跟我罗晾。”“哼！”宝森微微冷笑，“有海岳山虏在那里，哪轮得到我来跟你罗嗦。”

Baosen agreed to everything. After Wang left, he reflected on his good fortune with pleasure, smoked his fill through the new pipe, and noticed that it was past three in the afternoon. His brother would have returned from court. In high spirits, he went to tell him the agreeable news. The doorkeepers at Bao Yun’s house grew alarmed at the arrival of “Second Master” and hurried to report it. Bao Yun was about to tell them to say that he had a headache and had gone to bed when Baosen strode in. Seeing that nothing would stop him, he sighed and dismissed the servant with a wave. “That matter of yours will have to wait,” he said before his brother could speak. “Everyone is overwhelmed by the Eastern Empress Dowager’s funeral. I cannot bring it up just now.” “Which matter?” Baosen had asked his brother to intervene in so many affairs that he did not know which one he meant. “The inheritance lawsuit you undertook to help someone with.” “Oh, that one. I have no time to manage other people’s affairs now.” A son born of a concubine had sued his eldest brother, the son of their father’s principal wife, for a division of the family property. Acting for the other party, Baosen had asked Bao Yun to influence the prefect of Shuntian to dismiss the claimant’s petition. Since Liu Xitong’s conduct in the Yang Naiwu case had exposed him to public criticism, Bao Yun had wanted nothing more to do with such judicial matters. Baosen’s persistence had forced him to invent one excuse after another for delay, to his great distress. Hearing Baosen say he no longer had time for the affair felt like a weight lifted from his shoulders. “I have come especially to tell you that I am going to Shanghai. I shall be away two or three months.” “What for?” “Someone has invited me for a holiday. Food and lodging, collection and delivery: all four provided. I shall not spend a single cash.” “Indeed! Two or three months in Shanghai without costing you anything? Who is so rich?” “Hu Xueyan.” “So you have made friends with the God of Wealth!” Bao Yun’s face darkened. “Do not go promising him things and making trouble for me.” Baosen was astonished. “Would he want anything from me? What could it be?” “Who knows? The man has endless devices. He has come this time to arrange a foreign loan for Zuo Jigao. Perhaps he will send you to pester me.” Baosen gave a faint sneer. “With Haiyue Shanfang there, what need would he have of me to pester you?”

宝鋆装作不曾听见，呼噜噜地抽了几口水烟，开口问道：“你哪一天走？”“就在这几天”宝菱点点头，喊一声：“来啊！”将听差宝福唤来吩咐：“到帐房里支二百银子，给二老爷送了去。”“谢谢大哥！”宝森请个安，又说了些闲话，高高兴兴地走了。等他的背影刚刚消失，宝福俏然而至，走到宝鋆面前说道：“朱铁口来过了，替胡大入送了一份礼来。”“哪个胡大人？”“有手本在这里。”一看手本上的名字是“胡光墉”，不同得就关切了，“送的什么？”他问。“一个成化窑的花瓶。”“大的还是小的？”“大的。”大的便是两万银子。宝望心想，胡雪岩既然送了两万银子，就大可不必再在宝森身上作人情，而居然作了，并且这个人情还不轻，看起来是个很厚道的人。同时又想到宝森一走，耳根清净，便对胡雪岩越有好感了。“朱铁口走了没有？”“还没有。”“宝望便将朱铁口传唤到上房问道：”那胡大人是怎么说的？““胡大人说想送中堂一份礼，问我有什么合适的东西？我问他打算送多重的礼？他说两万银子。我就让他买花瓶。他还托我代送，花瓶送来了，银子也交到帐房里了。”“有什么话托你转达的没有？”“没有。我倒也问过他，他说只不过佩服中堂为国贤劳，本想上门来求见请安，又怕中堂最近因为大丧太忙，不敢冒昧。”宝鋆的顾虑消释了。这两万银子可以安心笑纳，倘或附带有一句什么请托的话，反倒不便帮忙，两万银子如果舍不得退回，良心上就不免要自责。遣走朱铁口以后，宝鋆仍在考虑胡雪岩送的这笔重礼，不帮他的忙，良心上仍不免要自责，要帮他的忙呢，又觉得自己一向主张“西饷可缓，洋款不急”，忽然很热心地赞成左宗棠借这笔洋债，出尔反尔，启人疑窦。如何得以筹划出一个两全之道，成了他这天念兹在兹的一桩心事。

Bao Yun pretended not to hear. After taking several bubbling draws on his water pipe, he asked, “When are you leaving?” “In the next few days.” Bao Yun nodded and called his servant Baofu. “Draw two hundred taels from the accounts office and send them to Second Master.” Baosen made his formal thanks, chatted a little longer, and left happily. He had scarcely disappeared when Baofu quietly approached. “Zhu Tiekou has brought a present from Lord Hu.” “Which Lord Hu?” “His official visiting card is here.” Seeing the name Hu Guangyong, Bao Yun immediately became interested. “What has he sent?” “A vase from the Chenghua kilns.” “Large or small?” “Large.” A large one meant twenty thousand taels. Bao Yun reflected that, after giving him twenty thousand, Hu need not have troubled to do Baosen a kindness as well. Yet he had, and no small kindness either. He seemed a very considerate man. The prospect of peace once Baosen left made Bao Yun think still better of him. “Has Zhu Tiekou gone?” “Not yet.” Bao Yun summoned him. “What did Lord Hu say?” “He said he wished to send Your Excellency a present and asked what would be suitable. I asked how substantial a gift he intended. Twenty thousand taels, he said. So I suggested the vase. He also asked me to deliver it. The vase is here, and the silver has been handed to the accounts office.” “Did he leave any message?” “No. I asked him that too. He said only that he admired Your Excellency’s devoted service to the country. He had intended to call and pay his respects, but feared that the great mourning had made you too busy for him to intrude.” Bao Yun’s doubts vanished. He could accept the twenty thousand with an easy mind. Had a request accompanied it, helping would have been more awkward; if he could not bear to return the money, his conscience would have reproached him. After dismissing Zhu, however, he went on thinking about the gift. To do nothing for Hu would still trouble his conscience. Yet he had always argued that the western military funds could wait and that foreign borrowing was not urgent. Suddenly to support Zuo’s loan enthusiastically would contradict his earlier position and arouse suspicion. Finding a way to satisfy both considerations occupied him throughout the day.

第二天一早上朝，在轿子里忽然想起宝森告诉他的，丁宝祯当年的故事。丁宝祯以清廉知名，但身为总督，开府西南，朝延的体制不能不顾，家乡贵州的亲友，翻山越岭，千辛万苦来投靠，没有那么多闲差使可应酬，招待食罕，致送回乡盘缠的情谊不能不尽，这些都在他每个月一万两左右的“养廉银子”中支付，尽管量人为出，总也有青黄不接的时候。照一般督抚的惯例，方便得很，写张纸条，向藩库提银若干，困窘即时可解，至于亏空如何弥补，不必费心，有藩司，有榷税的候补道，甚至首府、首县为他想办法。但那一来，就谈不到整饬吏治了。于是，堂堂“制台大人”也不免要向当铺求援了。可是，他又有什么东西能当到上千上万银子？想来想去只有一个当身分、当面子的办法，取一只皮箱，随便找些旧衣服塞满上锁，再取两张封条，盖上“四川总督部堂”的大印，标明日期，在皮箱上十字交叉，满浆实贴。然后派戈什哈抬到当铺里去当。朝奉吓一跳，从来没有听说总督也会当当的，便很客气地请问：“要当多少银子？”“五千银子。”朝奉又吓一跳，五千银子不是小数目，要问一问：“是什么贵重东西，能不能看一看？”“不能看。大人亲手贴的封条，谁敢揭开来？”“那么……”“你不必多管。”戈什哈抢着说道：“你只凭封条好了。将来赎当的时候，只看封条完整，就是原封不动。你明白了没有？”朝奉自然明白了，如数照当。丁宝祯倒是好主顾，下个月藩库将养廉银子送到，立刻赎当。从此了宝祯当当，成了规矩，只凭封条不问其他。

Early next morning, on his way to court, Bao Yun suddenly remembered a story Baosen had told him about Ding Baozhen. Ding was renowned for integrity, but as governor-general in the southwest he still had to maintain the dignity of his office. Friends and relatives from his native Guizhou crossed mountains and endured great hardships to seek his help. There were not enough easy appointments to accommodate them all, but he could not neglect the obligations of offering food and lodging and providing their fares home. These expenses came out of his monthly integrity allowance of about ten thousand taels. However carefully he lived within his income, there were times when funds ran short. Most governors found this easy to remedy: a note to the provincial treasury brought whatever silver they required. They need not worry about replacing it; the provincial treasurer, expectant intendants collecting taxes, and even the prefect and county magistrate of the provincial capital would find a way. But a man who did that could hardly reform official administration. Thus even a great governor-general had to turn to a pawnshop. What could he possess that would raise thousands of taels? After much thought, he found only one solution: to pledge his rank and prestige. He filled a leather trunk with old clothes, locked it, and pasted two dated strips bearing the great seal of the Governor-General of Sichuan firmly across it in a cross. A goshha was then sent to carry it to a pawnshop. The appraiser was startled; he had never heard of a governor-general pawning things. Politely he asked how much was required. “Five thousand taels.” Startled again at the sum, he asked, “What valuable articles are inside? May I look?” “No. His Excellency sealed it himself. Who would dare break the seals?” “Then…” “You need concern yourself with nothing else,” the guard interrupted. “The seals are all you need. When it is redeemed, intact seals will show that nothing has been touched. Do you understand?” The appraiser naturally did and advanced the full sum. Ding proved a good customer. When the next month’s integrity allowance arrived from the treasury, he redeemed the trunk at once. Thereafter it became an established practice: the pawnshop lent on Ding’s seals alone, without further enquiry.

室鋆心想，左宗棠借洋债，如果照丁宝祯的办法，岂不省事？而且目前也正是一个机会。于是默默盘算了一阵，到得军机处，立刻派苏拉到“南屋”去请了徐用仪来，邀到僻处，悄悄相语。“左帅借洋款的事，接头好了没有？”“接头好了。这一回的条件，确是比以前来得好。这也是胡雪岩力盖前愆的缘故。”徐用仪又说：“本来早就想出奏了，为有东太后的大事，不能不暂缓一缓。”“也不必再缓。请你转告左相，要朝廷批准他借，必得交户部议奏，也就要算老帐了。”宝鋆突然问道：“丁稚璜当当的故事，你听说过没有？”徐用仪不知他忽有此问的用意，赔笑答道：“那是个有名的笑话，知道的人很多。”“不是笑话。”宝鋆正色说道：“如果我是朝奉，看几件破烂衣服，让他当五千银子，怎么对得起东家？外头也一走有闲话，不知道我得了人家多少好处。他只有硬吃一注，不让我掀他的底牌，我拿他没办法。左相借债也是如此，生米煮成熟饭，朝廷看他的老面子，不跟他计较。你属于我的意思不？”徐用仪怎能不懂？可是他也很圆滑，不作正面回答，只说：“中堂的美意，我相信左大人一定能够领会。”“好。不过，”宝鋆沉着脸说：“丁稚磺当当，几乎月月如此，左相借洋债，可就是只此一回，下不为例。请你千万说清楚。”“是。”

Why should Zuo not borrow abroad on the same principle? Bao Yun thought. How much trouble that would save—and the present moment offered just the opportunity. He considered it silently, then, on reaching the Grand Council, sent a sula attendant to the southern office for Xu and drew him aside. “Have the arrangements for Marshal Zuo’s foreign loan been settled?” “They have. The terms are indeed better than before. Hu Xueyan has been trying to make amends for his earlier faults. A memorial was intended long ago, but the Eastern Empress Dowager’s death forced a delay.” “There is no need for further delay. Tell Grand Secretary Zuo that if he seeks formal permission to borrow, the matter must go to the Ministry of Revenue for deliberation, and the old accounts will have to be examined.” Bao Yun abruptly asked, “Have you heard the story of Ding Zhihuang’s pawning?” Xu could not see why he asked but smiled deferentially. “It is a famous amusing story. Many people know it.” “It is not an amusing story,” Bao Yun said gravely. “If I were the appraiser and lent five thousand taels against a few old clothes, how could I answer to my employer? People would also talk about how much I had been paid to do it. He has to force my hand and refuse to show me his cards; then I can do nothing. Zuo’s loan is the same. Once the raw rice is cooked, the court will overlook the matter out of respect for his long service. Do you understand?” Xu understood perfectly, but was too tactful to answer directly. “I am sure Lord Zuo will appreciate Your Excellency’s good intentions.” “Good. But”—Bao Yun’s face grew stern—“Ding does this almost every month. Zuo’s foreign borrowing must be for this occasion only, never a precedent. Be sure to make that clear.” “Yes.”

答应归答应，说不说又另是一回事。徐用仪退值以后，先去访胡雪岩，将宝鋆的话，告诉了他，商量最后的那句话，要不要说。“当然不必说。”胡雪岩答道：“事情明摆在那里，西征军事成功了，以后也再不会借洋款了。至于海防要借，那也不是左大人跟我的事。既然如此，何必又说这话，惹左大人不高兴？”徐用仪听从他的主张，到了贤良寺，转达了宝鋆的意见。左宗棠本来就想这么办，但未想到宝鋆如此“大方”，欣慰之余，乘兴亲自妨笔起草奏槁。第一段当然是陈述边务之重要，以及各省协饷，不能及时而至，拖欠年复一年，越积越多的困难。接下来便叙此次筹借洋款的由来：说有“德国商伙福克，在兰州织呢局闻之，自称该国有巨款可借，息耗亦轻，并可由陕甘总督出票”，因子上年腊月初三日具奏，接到户部咨复，以借数虽经奏明为四百万，惟期限、利息，以及还款来源，应该补叙说明。但其时左宗棠已奉旨晋京，不在其位，似乎不应再谋其政，所以此处须作一番解释：“臣卸篆北上时，与刘锦棠、杨昌浚晤谈，均以甫经接任，筹饷艰难，属臣代为借筹。臣虽去任在即，亦不俗贻累替人，遂飞饬办理上海采运局道员胡光墉，速向洋商议借银四百万，以应急需。抵都后，连接杨昌浚、刘锡棠来函，言及饷源已涸，春夏之交，断难接续，恳即据情入告，情词迫切异常。”以下是根据“胡光塘偕同德国泰来行伙福克及英国汇丰行伙凯密伦”所称，开具办法。

Promising was one thing; delivering the message was another. After leaving duty, Xu first visited Hu and repeated Bao Yun’s words, asking whether he should convey the final sentence. “Of course not,” Hu replied. “The situation is plain. The western campaign has succeeded, and there will be no more foreign loans for it. Loans for coastal defence are another matter, and no concern of Lord Zuo’s or mine. Why say something that will only displease him?” Xu followed this advice. At Xianliang Temple he passed on Bao Yun’s suggestion. Zuo had already intended to act in that way, but had not expected such “generosity” from Bao Yun. Delighted and reassured, he personally took up his brush to draft the memorial. Its first paragraph naturally explained the importance of frontier affairs and the difficulties caused by provincial military contributions that arrived late, with arrears mounting year after year. It then described the origins of the proposed loan: “The German merchant Focke heard of the matter at the Lanzhou woollen mill and declared that substantial funds could be borrowed in his country at a low cost in interest and charges, against notes issued by the governor-general of Shaanxi and Gansu.” A memorial had therefore been submitted on the third day of the twelfth month of the preceding year. The Ministry of Revenue had replied that, although the amount had been stated as four million taels, the term, interest, and sources of repayment required further explanation. By then Zuo had been ordered to the capital and, no longer holding the provincial post, should seemingly no longer concern himself with its affairs. This too required explanation: “When I relinquished my seal and set out north, I spoke with Liu Jintang and Yang Changjun. Both said that, having only just assumed office, they faced difficulty in raising military funds and asked me to arrange borrowing on their behalf. Although about to leave my post, I did not wish to burden my successors. I therefore urgently directed Circuit Intendant Hu Guangyong, in charge of the Shanghai Purchasing and Transport Bureau, to negotiate a loan of four million taels from foreign merchants to meet the pressing need. Since reaching the capital, I have received successive letters from Yang Changjun and Liu Jintang reporting that their sources of funds are exhausted and that they cannot maintain payments at the turn of spring and summer. Their requests that I lay the facts before the throne are exceptionally urgent.” The terms that followed were based on the representations of “Hu Guangyong, together with Focke of the German Tailai Trading House and Cameron of the British HSBC.”

借款数目：库平足色宝银四百万两。期限：六年还清。利率：年息九分七厘五毫。付息办法：每六个月一付，六年共十二期。还本办法：第一、第二两年不还本，第三年起，每年还本一百万两。利息照减。保证办法：请户部催饬各省关，将应解新旧协饷，径交上海采运局，据付息还本。如协饷不至，上海采运局无款可拨，应准洋商凭陕甘总督所出印票，向户部如期兑取。这些条件与过去比较，好处有三：一是不需海关及有关各省督抚出票，可免周折，二是年息由一钱二分多减至不足一钱，合月息只八厘有零，三是头两年不还本，俾各省得以清理旧欠，“其力尚纾，并无窘迫之患。”因为如此，“已饬胡光墉、福克、凯密伦即依照定议，应仰恳天恩敕下总理衙门，札饬道员胡光塘及照会英国使臣转行汇丰银行，一体遵照，以便陕甘出票提银”。

The principal was four million treasury-standard taels of full-fineness silver sycee, repayable within six years, at an annual interest rate of 9.75 per cent. Interest was to be paid every six months, in twelve instalments over the six years. No principal would be repaid in the first two years. From the third year, one million taels would be repaid annually, with interest reduced accordingly. As security, the Ministry of Revenue was asked to urge the provinces and customs offices to remit their current and overdue military contributions directly to the Shanghai Purchasing and Transport Bureau for payment of interest and principal. If the contributions failed to arrive and the bureau had no funds to disburse, the foreign merchants were to be allowed to present the sealed notes issued by the governor-general of Shaanxi and Gansu and collect payment from the Ministry of Revenue when due. These terms offered three advantages over earlier loans. First, no notes were required from the customs or the governors and governors-general of the provinces concerned, avoiding much complication. Second, annual interest had fallen from more than twelve per cent to less than ten, equivalent to just over eight-tenths of one per cent a month. Third, postponing repayment of principal for two years would allow the provinces to clear their earlier arrears: “Their resources will thus have some respite, without the danger of being pressed beyond their means.” For these reasons, “Hu Guangyong, Focke, and Cameron have already been instructed to proceed under the agreed terms. We humbly implore Imperial grace to direct the Zongli Yamen to issue instructions to Circuit Intendant Hu Guangyong and to notify the British minister, for transmission to HSBC, that all parties shall comply, so that Shaanxi and Gansu may issue the notes and draw the silver.”
