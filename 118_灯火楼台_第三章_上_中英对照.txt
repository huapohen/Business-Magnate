第三章 元宝街｜CHAPTER THREE: YUANBAO STREET

八月初，在西湖上正是“一年好景君须记，最是橙黄橘绿时”，在上海已略感厌倦于酒绿灯红，脂香粉腻的宝森：为胡雪岩接到了杭州。他是由古应春陪着来的。船到望仙桥埠头上早有一乘绿呢、一乘蓝呢的大轿在等候，另外一匹顶马、两匹跟马，四名兵丁，都穿着布政司的号衣，四散排开，挡住了行人，留出一片空地，容宝森登岸。船家将船泊稳，搭好跳板，船家与岸上胡家的听差合作，伸出一条粗竹杆，捏稳两端，高及腰际，宝森以竹杆作扶手，自跳板登上埠头，立即便有一个穿是极体面的中年人，含笑迎上前来。宝森在上海也见过此人，名叫陶敦甫，字厚斋，捐了个候补知县，作胡雪岩的清客，专责是接待宾客。

Early in the eighth lunar month, when West Lake offered the prospect celebrated in the lines, “Remember the finest scenes of the year: the time when oranges turn yellow and tangerines are green,” Baosen was brought to Hangzhou by Hu Xueyan. He had begun to tire a little of Shanghai’s lights and wine, its perfumes and painted beauties. Gu Yingchun accompanied him. When their boat reached the landing at Wangxian Bridge, two large sedan chairs, one covered in green woolen cloth and the other in blue, were already waiting. There were also a horse to lead the procession and two to follow it. Four soldiers in the livery of the Provincial Treasury stood apart, holding back passersby and clearing a space for Baosen to land. The boatmen moored the vessel and laid the gangplank. Working with Hu’s servants on shore, they held a stout bamboo pole firmly at either end, waist-high, for Baosen to use as a handrail. As he stepped ashore, an impeccably dressed middle-aged man came smiling forward. Baosen had met him in Shanghai: Tao Dunfu, courtesy name Houzhai, who had purchased the rank of expectant county magistrate and served as a gentleman retainer in Hu’s household, chiefly responsible for receiving guests.

“森二爷到底到了，胡大先生盼望了好几天了。森二爷路上还舒服？”“舒服得很。”宝森舒了口气，游目四顾，看过往辐辏的行人，不由得赞叹：“都说杭州是洞夭福地，真是名不虚传。”“森二爷只看到今天的热闹，哪知道十六七年前满目凄凉，惨不忍睹。”杭州经两度战事，毁坏独重，善后复兴之功，推胡雪岩为首。做清客捧宾客以外，亦须不忌捧东主，但以不着痕迹为贵。听得这话，宝森连连点头，“雪岩之有今日、实在是积德之报。”他跟胡雪岩的交情已很厚了，所以径以雪岩相称。

“You have come at last, Second Master Sen. Mr Hu has been looking forward to your arrival for days. Was the journey comfortable?” “Very comfortable.” Baosen drew a contented breath and looked about at the throng of people. “They call Hangzhou a blessed retreat of the immortals. It certainly deserves the name.” “You see how lively it is today, Second Master Sen. You cannot imagine the desolation sixteen or seventeen years ago. It was unbearable to look at.” Hangzhou had suffered especially grievous destruction in two periods of warfare, and Hu deserved foremost credit for its relief and restoration. Besides flattering guests, a retainer must not shrink from praising his employer; the art lay in leaving no obvious trace. Baosen nodded repeatedly. “Xueyan’s good fortune is truly the reward of accumulated virtue.” His friendship with Hu had become close enough for him to use Xueyan without further formality.

陶敦甫觑空跟古应春招呼过了，请宝森坐上胡雪岩自用的绿呢大轿，古应春坐蓝呢轿，由顶马引导前行，陶敦甫乘一顶小轿自间道先赶往“元宝街”等候。“元宝街”满铺青石板，足容四马并行，街中突起，两头低下，形似元宝，因而得名。不过，胡雪岩当初铺这条街时，却并未想到这个能配合他的“财神”之号的俗气的街名，只是为了便于排水，当然，四周的阴沟经过细心修建，畅通无阻，每遇夏日暴雨，它处积水两三尺，元宝街却只要雨停，便即水消。由望仙桥到元宝街，只是一盏茶的工夫，坐在绿呢轿中的宝森，由左右玻璃窗中望出去，只见五，六丈高的一大圈围墙墙脚基石，竞有一人多高。大轿抬入可容两乘轿子进出的大门，穿过门楼，抬人二门歇轿，胡雪岩已站在大厅滴水檐前等候了。“森二爷，”胡雪岩拱拱手说：“一路好吧？”“很好，很好。”宝森扶着他的手臂，偏着脸细看了一下说：“雪岩，一个多月不见，你又发福了。”“托福，托福。请里面坐。”宝森点点头，已把脸仰了起来，倒不是他摆架子不理人，而是因为胡家的厅堂过于宏敞，必须仰着脸才能看清楚。

Finding a moment to greet Gu, Tao invited Baosen into Hu’s own green-covered chair and Gu into the blue one. The lead horse guided them forward, while Tao took a small sedan by a side route to reach Yuanbao Street ahead of them. The street was paved throughout with blue stone slabs and wide enough for four horses abreast. Raised in the middle and lower at either end, it resembled a silver ingot, hence its name, Yuanbao. When Hu had first laid it out, he had not intended this rather vulgar name, so appropriate to his own title of God of Wealth. His purpose had simply been to provide good drainage. The surrounding drains had also been carefully constructed and flowed freely. After a summer downpour, water might stand two or three chi deep elsewhere, but in Yuanbao Street it disappeared as soon as the rain stopped. The journey from Wangxian Bridge took no longer than a cup of tea. Through the glass windows on either side of his chair, Baosen saw a vast enclosing wall five or six zhang high, with stone foundations rising above a man’s height. His chair passed through a gateway wide enough for two sedans, beneath the gatehouse, and through the second gate before being set down. Hu was waiting beneath the eaves at the front of the main hall. “A good journey, Second Master Sen?” “Very good indeed.” Resting a hand on Hu’s arm, Baosen tilted his head to study him. “It has been over a month, Xueyan, and you have grown stouter again.” “Thanks to your good wishes. Please come inside.” Baosen nodded, his face already turned upward. He was not putting on airs or ignoring his host; the hall was so lofty and spacious that he had to look up to take it in.

未看大厅，先回顾天井，天井有七开间大，而且极深，为的是可以搭台唱戏。大厅当然也是七开间，估计可摆三十桌席。由于高敞之故，堂奥虽深，却很明亮，正中树一方蓝地金底，四周龙纹的大立匾，案巢大书“积菩衍庆”四个黑字，正中上端一颗大方印，一望即知是御玺，上下款却因相距得远，看不清楚，不知是慈禧皇太后，还是先帝的御笔。转眼看去，东西两面板壁上，各悬一方五尺高、丈余宽的紫檀挂屏，西面是一幅青绿山水，东面是贝子奕模写的《滕王阁序》，旁有两扇屏门，料想其中当是家词。旗人向来重礼节，当即表示，理当瞻拜。胡雪岩自然连称“不敢当”。只是宝森意思诚敬，当下唤人开了屏门，点燃香烛，宝森向神龛中“胡氏列祖神位”恭恭敬敬地磕了三个头，胡雪岩一旁陪礼，最后又向宝森磕头道谢。“还要见见老太太。”“改天吧！”胡雪岩说：“家母今天到天竺烧香去了。”“森二爷刚到，先请歇一歇。”陶敦甫插嘴说道：“我来引路，”

Before examining the hall, he looked back at the courtyard. It was seven bays wide and exceptionally deep, allowing room for a stage on theatrical occasions. The hall was likewise seven bays across and could, he estimated, hold thirty banquet tables. Its height made even the deep interior bright. In the center stood a great upright plaque, blue and gold with dragons around its edges, bearing four large black characters: “Accumulate Goodness, Extend Blessings.” A large square impression at the upper center was unmistakably an imperial seal. The inscriptions above and below were too far away to read, so Baosen could not tell whether the calligraphy was Empress Dowager Cixi’s or the late emperor’s. On the eastern and western paneled walls hung two red-sandalwood screens, each five chi high and more than a zhang wide. The western one showed a blue-and-green landscape; the eastern one bore the Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng, copied by Beizi Yimo. Beside it were two screen doors, beyond which Baosen supposed the family shrine must lie. Bannermen were always attentive to propriety, and he immediately said that he ought to pay his respects. Hu protested that he could not presume to accept such an honor. Baosen was sincerely insistent, however, so the doors were opened and incense and candles lit. Before the tablets of the Hu ancestors, Baosen respectfully kowtowed three times. Hu performed the rites at his side, then kowtowed to Baosen in thanks. “I must also call on your honored mother.” “Another day, perhaps. My mother has gone to offer incense at Tianzhu today.” Tao intervened. “Second Master Sen has only just arrived. Let him rest a little first. I shall lead the way.”

于是出了大厅，由西面走廓绕出去，往北一折，一带粉墙上开着个月洞门，上榜“芝径”二字，迎门一座玲珑剔透的假山，陶敦甫由东面绕了过去，豁然开朗，室森放眼一望，但见树木掩映，楼阁参差，窗子上的五色玻璃，为偏西的日光照耀得光怪陆离，真有目迷五色之感。“请过桥来！”宝森跟着陶敦甫经过一道三曲的石桥，踏上一座极大的白石露台，中间便是三开间大，正方的楠木“四面厅”，上悬一方黄杨木蓝字的匾额，榜书“迎紫”二字。进门可是一番光景，用紫檀隔板，隔出两间大小的一个长方形房间，里面是西式布置，四周红色丝绒的安乐椅，配着白色髹金漆的茶几，中间一张与茶几同一质料式样的大餐台，上面已摆好了八只纯银的高脚果盘。等主客坐定，随即有两个面目姣好的丫头来奉茶敬烟，至此才是开始寒暄的时候。“森二爷这一晌的酒兴怎么样？”“很好哇！”宝森笑道：“从天津上船那天起，酒兴就没有坏过。”“要这样才好。”胡雪岩问古应春，“森二爷怎么没有把花想容带来？”“多谢，多谢！”宝森抢着回答，“我到府上来作客，没有把她带来的道理。”原来花想容是“长三”上的“红倌人”，为宝森所眷。胡雪岩邀他来一赏西湖秋色，原曾在信上写明，不妨挟美以俱，而宝森却认为于礼不合，没有带花想容来。

They left the main hall by the western corridor, turned north, and came to a moon gate in a whitewashed wall, inscribed “Zhijing,” the Path of the Auspicious Herb. An intricate, openwork rockery faced the entrance. Tao went around its eastern side, and suddenly the view opened out. Baosen saw towers and pavilions at different heights among the trees, their windows of colored glass catching the declining sunlight in a bewildering play of hues. “This way across the bridge, please.” Following Tao over a stone bridge with three turns, Baosen reached a broad terrace of white stone. In its center stood a square nanmu building, three bays wide, known as the Four-Sided Hall. A boxwood plaque above it bore the blue characters “Welcoming Purple.” Inside was a different scene. Red-sandalwood partitions enclosed a rectangular room two bays in size, furnished in Western style. Red-velvet armchairs stood around it with white-and-gilt lacquer tea tables; in the middle was a large dining table of the same material and design, already set with eight solid-silver footed fruit dishes. Once host and guests were seated, two pretty maids brought tea and offered tobacco. Only now could leisurely conversation begin. “How has your appetite for wine been lately, Second Master Sen?” “Excellent!” Baosen laughed. “It has never failed me since the day I boarded the steamer at Tianjin.” “That is how it should be.” Hu turned to Gu. “Why did Second Master Sen not bring Hua Xiangrong?” “Thank you, thank you,” Baosen answered before Gu could speak. “I am your guest. It would hardly be proper to bring her to your house.” Hua Xiangrong was a sought-after courtesan of a changsan house, and Baosen was attached to her. In inviting him to enjoy the autumn scenery of West Lake, Hu had expressly written that he might bring his beauty along. Baosen, however, had considered it improper and left her behind.

按下来便纵谈上海声色与新奇之事，宝森兴味盎然他说他开了多少眼界，看了外国的马戏、东洋女子“天胜娘”的戏法。一面谈，一面不断有丫头送点心来，宝森喜欢甜食，最中意又香又软用冰糖煮的桂花栗子。“雪岩，”宝森是衷心向往，“我看当皇上都没有你舒服，简直是神仙嘛！”他指着窗外，耸起于假山上的那座“百狮楼”，忽然想起一句唐诗，便念了出来，“楼阁玲珑五云起。”“森二爷谈诗，我就接不上话了。”胡雪岩转脸说道：“厚齐，你看哪一天，把我们杭州城里那几位大诗翁请了来，陪森二爷谈谈。”“不，不！”宝森急忙摇手，“我哪里会做诗？千万不必，免得我受窘。”看他是真心话，胡雪岩一笑置之，不再多说。陶敦甫怕场面冷落，便即问说：“森二爷，上海消息灵通，不知道刘制台的参案怎么样了？”听得这话，宝森突然站了起来：“嘿！”他摹地里一拍双掌，声音极大，加以动作近乎粗鲁，倒让大家都吓一跳，再看他脸上有掩抑不住的笑容，便越发奇怪了。“森二爷，”胡雪岩说：“请坐下来，慢慢谈起。”“谈起刘岘庄的参案，可真是大快人心！”他摩腹说道：“我肚子里的积滞都消了……”刘岘庄便是两江总督刘坤一。自从出了盛宣怀的案子，李鸿章便觉得此人在两江，对他是一大妨碍，而盛宣怀更是耿耿在心，企图中伤。但刘坤一的官声不错，封疆大吏又不比京官，号称“都老爷”的监察御史，见闻不足，无法参他，就上折参劾，慈禧太后亦未必见听。几经筹划，认为只有一个人够资格参他，而且一定见效。

They went on to talk freely of Shanghai’s pleasures and novelties. Baosen spoke with lively interest of how much he had seen: a foreign circus and the tricks of the Japanese woman Tianshengniang. As they talked, maids kept bringing refreshments. Baosen had a sweet tooth, and his favorite was the soft, fragrant chestnuts cooked with rock sugar and sweet osmanthus. “Xueyan,” he said with heartfelt envy, “I doubt even the emperor lives as comfortably as you. You are simply an immortal!” Pointing through the window at the Hundred Lions Tower rising above the rockery, he suddenly recalled a line of Tang poetry and recited it: “Exquisite towers and pavilions rise amid five-colored clouds.” “Once you begin discussing poetry, Second Master Sen, I cannot keep up.” Hu turned to Tao. “Houzhai, find a day to invite our great Hangzhou poets here to talk with Second Master Sen.” “No, no!” Baosen waved his hands hastily. “I know nothing of writing poetry. Please do not embarrass me.” Seeing that he meant it, Hu smiled and let the matter drop. To keep the conversation from flagging, Tao asked, “News travels quickly in Shanghai, Second Master Sen. What has come of the impeachment of Governor-General Liu?” Baosen suddenly sprang to his feet. “Ha!” He clapped his hands so loudly, with such a rough gesture, that he startled everyone. The irrepressible smile on his face made them more puzzled still. “Sit down, Second Master Sen,” Hu said. “Tell us at your leisure.” “The impeachment of Liu Xianzhuang! Now that is something to gladden the heart.” He rubbed his stomach. “It has cleared all the stagnation out of my belly…” Liu Xianzhuang was Liu Kunyi, Governor-General of Liangjiang. Since the Sheng Xuanhuai affair, Li Hongzhang had regarded his presence there as a serious obstacle, while Sheng nursed resentment and sought to damage him. Liu’s reputation was good, however. A great provincial governor was less exposed than a capital official: the investigating censors lacked the information to impeach him, and even a memorial might not persuade Cixi. After much planning, they concluded that only one man had the standing to denounce him with certain effect.

此人就是“彭郎夺得小姑回”的彭玉鳞，湘军水师的领袖。洪杨失败，彭玉麟淡于名利，外不愿当督抚，内不愿当尚书，于是有人建议，长江水师龙蛇混杂，盐枭勾结，为害地方不浅。彭玉麟清刚正直，嫉恶如仇，在长江威望素著，不如仿照旗营“专操大臣”的制度，派他专门巡阅长江水师，得以专折奏事，并颁给“王命旗牌”，遇有不法官吏，得以便宜行事。彭玉麟接受了这个差使，一年一次巡阅长江水师，其余的日了，便住在西湖上，与他的孙儿女亲家俞曲园唱酬盘醒，消闲如鹤。不过到得彭玉麟出巡时，威名所播，确能使贪官墨吏，相顾敛迹，他所管的事，亦不限于整顿水师纪律，长江沿岸各地他看下顺眼的事都要管，职权仿佛明朝“代天巡守”的巡按御史。曾经在武昌请王命旗牌立斩不法的水师总兵谭祖纶，至于地方官经他参劾，革职查办的，亦颇不乏人。总之，只要彭玉麟参谁，准就非倒媚不可。

That man was Peng Yulin, the Peng associated with the verse “Young Peng carried Maiden Gu away,” and the leader of the Hunan Army’s naval forces. After the Taiping defeat, Peng showed little interest in fame or profit. He would accept neither a provincial governorship nor a ministry at court. Someone therefore proposed a different appointment. The Yangtze naval forces contained men of every description, some colluding with salt smugglers and doing great harm to the districts along the river. Peng was upright, stern, and fiercely opposed to wrongdoing, with an established reputation throughout the region. Why not follow the Banner armies’ system of special commissioners for drills and appoint him to inspect the Yangtze naval forces? He could submit memorials independently and receive the imperial command banner and tablets, authorizing him to act at his discretion against offending officials. Peng accepted. He made one inspection each year and spent the rest of his time at West Lake, exchanging poems and enjoying long visits with Yu Quyuan, whose grandchild had married his own, living as freely as a crane. When Peng set out on inspection, however, his reputation made corrupt officials look anxiously at one another and curb their conduct. He concerned himself with far more than naval discipline. Anything he found objectionable along the Yangtze came within his attention, much like a Ming censor inspecting on the sovereign’s behalf. At Wuchang he had invoked the imperial warrant to execute the offending naval brigade commander Tan Zulun on the spot. Many local officials had also been dismissed and investigated after his denunciations. Whoever Peng impeached was certain to suffer.

盛宣怀想到了这个人，李鸿章亦认为可加利用，于是摭拾浮言，激动了彭玉麟的脾气，真个以密折严劾刘坤一，大致是：第一，鸦片瘾大，又好逸乐，精神不济，无力整顿公事，第二，姨太太很多，稀见宾客，又纵容家丁，收受门包，第三点最厉害，亦是彭玉麟亲眼所见，最感不满而又是他应该管的事，“沿江炮台，多不可目，每一发炮，烟气迷目，甚或坍毁。”密折到京，慈禧太后召见军机，决定派彭玉麟进一步密查，同时内召来京觐见，打算不让他回任了。据说荣王曾经跟李鸿章商量过这件事，其时陕甘总督改派曾国荃，而曾国荃嫌地方太苦，又怕无法指挥左宗棠的嫡系部队，一直不愿就任，使得朝廷深感为难，不如乘此机会，改派刘坤一当陕甘总督。至于两江总督则以清望素著的四川总督丁宝祯调补，遗缺由李鸿章的胞兄李瀚章接任。

Sheng thought of him, and Li agreed that he could be used. They collected circulating allegations and inflamed Peng’s temper until he submitted a severe confidential impeachment of Liu. First, Liu was heavily addicted to opium and pleasure, leaving him too enervated to put public business in order. Second, he kept many concubines, seldom received visitors, and allowed his servants to demand payments at the door. The third charge was the strongest: something Peng had seen himself, deeply resented, and was responsible for correcting. “Many forts along the river are a disgrace to behold. Whenever a gun is fired, smoke blinds the eyes; some structures even collapse.” On receiving the memorial, Cixi summoned the Grand Council and ordered Peng to investigate further in secret. She also summoned Liu to Beijing for an audience, intending not to send him back. Prince Gong was said to have discussed the matter with Li. Zeng Guoquan had lately been appointed Governor-General of Shaanxi and Gansu, but considered the region too harsh and feared he could not command Zuo’s own troops. His continuing refusal to take up the post embarrassed the court. Liu might now be transferred there instead. Ding Baozhen, the well-regarded Governor-General of Sichuan, could take Liangjiang, and Li’s elder brother Li Hanzhang could succeed Ding.

这是李鸿章的一把如意算盘，原来清朝的制度，封疆大吏、划疆而治，总督往往亦仅管得一省，不比明朝的总督、巡抚是有流动性的。这种制度之形成，当然有许多原因，其中之一是，皇帝认为各有专责，易于考查，也就是易于驾驭。因此，尽管常有“不分畛域”的上谕，实际上限制甚严，不准有越权的行为，及至洪杨兵起，这个相沿两百年而不替的传统被打破了。清朝在道光以前，凡有大征伐、调兵遣将，权皆操之于皇帝，军饷亦由国库拨发，统帅功成还朝，缴还兵权，受赏而回本职，并无私有的军队。但自曾国藩创立湘军，而军饷又需带兵将帅就地白筹以后，整个情况大变，变成官不符职、守非其地、财难己用、兵为私有。曾国荃进围金陵时，他的官衔是浙江按察使，一省司法长官，带兵打伏，岂非“官不符职”？而打仗又非为浙江划守土之责，这就是“守非其地”。“财难己用”就更微妙了，本秦人视越，肥瘠漠不相关，但在左宗棠西征时，却非希望浙江丰收不可，因为浙江按月要交西征协晌十四万银子，而本省修理海塘，反需另筹财源。至于“兵为私有”，则以湘、淮两军原为子弟兵，爷子兄弟叔侄，递相率领，成为规例，淮军的这个传统，更是牢不可破。

This was Li Hongzhang’s carefully calculated plan. Under the Qing system, the great provincial authorities governed defined territories. Even a governor-general often administered only one province, unlike the Ming governors and governors-general, whose jurisdictions could shift. Many circumstances had produced this arrangement. One was the emperor’s belief that separate responsibilities made officials easier to assess and therefore easier to control. Despite frequent edicts urging them not to confine themselves to territorial divisions, restrictions were severe, and encroachments on another’s authority were forbidden. The Taiping rising broke a tradition that had endured for two hundred years. Before the Daoguang reign, the emperor had controlled every major expedition and deployment, and military expenditure had come from the imperial treasury. A victorious commander returned to court, surrendered his military authority, accepted his reward, and resumed his substantive post. He possessed no private army. Once Zeng Guofan created the Hunan Army and commanders had to raise funds locally, everything changed. Posts no longer matched duties; service extended beyond one’s jurisdiction; locally raised revenue was unavailable for local needs; armies became private property. When Zeng Guoquan advanced to besiege Jinling, his title was Judicial Commissioner of Zhejiang. For a province’s chief judicial officer to lead troops into battle was surely a case of office and duty failing to match. Nor was he fighting to defend Zhejiang: he was serving outside his own territory. The revenue problem was subtler still. Normally, one province’s prosperity might seem as remote as Yue’s affairs to a man of Qin. Yet during Zuo’s western campaign, good harvests in Zhejiang became essential. Zhejiang had to contribute a hundred and forty thousand taels every month to the western army, while money to repair its own coastal embankments had to be found elsewhere. As for private armies, both the Hunan and Anhui forces had begun as local bands, in which fathers, sons, brothers, uncles, and nephews served under one another. In the Anhui Army especially, this tradition had become unbreakable.

因为打破了疆域与职守的限制，李鸿章才能运用手腕，伸张其势力于两江——南洋。直督兼北洋大臣，江督兼南洋大臣，李鸿章一直强调，无论筹办防务或者与外洋通商，南北洋必须联络一致，不分彼此。话是如此，却只有北冰侵南洋之权，南洋的势力达不到北洋，因为北洋近在畿辅，得地利之便，可直接与各国驻华公使联络交涉，这样，有关南洋的通商事务，自然而然地由北洋代办了。同时“总理各国国务衙门”，为了在交涉上留有缓冲的余地，往往先委托北洋从事初步谈判，保留着最后的裁决权，这一来使得李鸿章更易于扩张势力了。如此这般，李鸿章就不能不关心两江总督的人选了。最好是能听他指挥，其次也要能合作。象刘坤一这样，李鸿章就觉得有许多不便，因而希望丁室祯接任江督。丁宝祯是他会试的同年，李鸿章一直很拉拢他。丁宝祯每次奉召述职时，京中上自王邸军机，下至同乡京官都要打点，无不是由李鸿章预备了整箱的现银，这样的交情，他相信丁宝祯调任江督，一定能跟他合作无间。至于李瀚章，除了贪默之外，别无他能，而四川经丁宝祯整顿以后，是个可以卧治的省份，李鸿章是想为他老兄找个奉母养老的好地方。这把算盘打得极精，哪知真如俗话所说的“人有千算，天有一算”，彭玉麟的复奏到京，大出李鸿章的意外，竞是痛劾李鸿章的至亲赵继元。

Only after the restrictions of territory and office had been broken could Li Hongzhang maneuver to extend his power into Liangjiang, the Southern jurisdiction. The Governor-General of Zhili also served as Beiyang Minister, while the Governor-General of Liangjiang served as Nanyang Minister. Li repeatedly insisted that coastal defense and foreign trade alike required the Northern and Southern administrations to act together without distinction. In practice, however, the North could encroach on the South, while Southern influence could not reach the North. Situated close to the capital, the Northern administration could deal directly with foreign ministers in China. Southern trading affairs consequently passed quite naturally into its hands. The Zongli Yamen, wishing to retain room for maneuver in negotiations, also often entrusted preliminary talks to the Northern administration while reserving the final decision. All this made Li’s expansion easier. He therefore had to concern himself with the Liangjiang appointment. Ideally its holder would obey him; failing that, he must at least cooperate. Liu Kunyi presented many inconveniences, so Li wanted Ding Baozhen to replace him. Ding had passed the metropolitan examination in the same year as Li, who had consistently cultivated him. Whenever Ding was summoned to report at court, gifts were needed for everyone from princes and grand councillors to fellow provincials holding capital posts. Li invariably prepared whole chests of ready silver for the purpose. Given such a friendship, he felt certain Ding would cooperate perfectly if transferred to Liangjiang. Li Hanzhang, meanwhile, had no talent beyond lining his own pockets. After Ding’s reforms, Sichuan could be governed with little effort. Li wished to find his elder brother a comfortable place to support their mother and spend his declining years. The calculation was meticulous. But, as the saying went, “A man may make a thousand plans; Heaven has a plan of its own.” Peng’s further memorial arrived in Beijing and, to Li’s astonishment, fiercely denounced his own close relative, Zhao Jiyuan.

赵继元是安徽太湖人，他的祖父叫赵文楷，嘉庆元年的状元。赵继元本人也是个翰林，但肚子里一团茅草，“散馆”时考列三等，分到部里当司官。做官要凭本事、讲资格，赵继元倒有自知之明，自顾当司官既不能“掌印”，而两榜出身虽可派为考官，却又须先经考试，这一关又是过不去的，不如当外官为妙。于是他加捐了一个道员，走门路分发两江。江督正是李鸿章的老师曾国藩，爱屋及乌，所以赵继元一到江宁“禀到”，立即“挂牌”派了他军需总局总办的肥差。

Zhao came from Taihu in Anhui. His grandfather, Zhao Wenkai, had placed first in the palace examination in the first year of Jiaqing. Zhao himself had entered the Hanlin Academy, but his head was full of straw. At the examination concluding his probation he ranked in the third class and was assigned to a ministry as a departmental official. Advancement required ability and seniority, and Zhao knew his limitations. In a ministry he could not hope to take charge of a departmental seal. His examination qualifications might make him eligible to serve as an examiner, but that too required a further test he could not pass. A provincial career seemed wiser. He paid an additional contribution for circuit-intendant rank and used influence to secure assignment to Liangjiang. The governor-general was then Li Hongzhang’s teacher, Zeng Guofan. Out of affection for Li, Zeng favored his relative: no sooner had Zhao reported at Jiangning than an official notice appointed him director of the lucrative Liangjiang General Military Supply Bureau.

从此赵继元便把持着两江军需总局，历任总督都看李鸿章的面子，隐忍不言。这一次到底由彭玉麟无情地揭发了他的劣迹，复奏中说：“两江军需总局，原系总督札委局员，会同司道主持，自赵继元入局，恃以庶常散馆，捐升道员出身，又系李鸿章之妻兄，卖弄聪明，妄以知兵自许，由是局员营员派往修筑炮台者，皆惟赵继元之言是听。赵继元轻前两江总督李宗羲为不知兵，忠厚和平，事多蔑视，甚至督臣有要务札饬总局，赵继元竟敢违抗不遵，直行己意。李宗羲旋以病告去，赵继元更大权独揽，目空一切。炮台坍塌，守台官屡请查看修补，皆为赵继元蒙蔽不行。”李宗羲字雨亭，四川开县人，道光二十七年进士，是李鸿章的同年。同治十二年曾国藩殁于两江总督任上，由于李鸿章的推荐，李宗羲竟能继任此一要缺。其人才具平常，李鸿章可以遥制，两江诸般设施，每听北洋指挥。盛宣怀以直隶候补道得以派到招商局去当会办，便是李宗轰任内之事。这样的一个人，赵继元自然不会将他放在眼里。

Zhao had controlled it ever since. Successive governors-general tolerated him for Li’s sake. Now Peng finally exposed his misconduct without mercy. His further memorial stated: “The staff of the Liangjiang General Military Supply Bureau were originally appointed by the governor-general to work under the joint supervision of the provincial commissioners and circuit intendants. Since entering the Bureau, Zhao Jiyuan has relied on his background as a former Hanlin probationer promoted by contribution to circuit rank, and on being Li Hongzhang’s wife’s elder brother. He parades his cleverness and presumptuously claims military expertise. Consequently, Bureau and army officers assigned to construct forts obey him alone. He despised the former Governor-General Li Zongxi as ignorant of military affairs and too gentle and accommodating, treating his authority with contempt in many matters. Even when the governor-general sent the Bureau directives on important business, Zhao dared disobey and follow his own wishes. After Li retired through illness, Zhao monopolized authority still more completely and recognized no one above him. When forts collapsed, their commanders repeatedly requested inspections and repairs; Zhao concealed the facts and prevented action.” Li Zongxi, courtesy name Yuting, came from Kai County in Sichuan and had become a jinshi in the twenty-seventh year of Daoguang, the same examination year as Li Hongzhang. When Zeng died in the Liangjiang post in the twelfth year of Tongzhi, Li’s recommendation enabled Li Zongxi to succeed to this important appointment. A man of ordinary abilities, he could be controlled from a distance, and Liangjiang affairs often followed Northern instructions. It was during his term that Sheng Xuanhuai, an expectant intendant assigned to Zhili, obtained the assistant directorship of China Merchants. Zhao naturally thought little of such a governor-general.

至于对刘坤一，据彭玉麟在复奏中说：“臣恐刘坤一为其所误，力言其人不可用。刘坤一札调出局，改派总理营务，亦可谓优待之矣，而赵继元敢于公庭大众向该督力争，仍旧帮理局务。本不知兵，亦无远识，嗜好复深，徒恃势榄权，妄自尊大，始则炫其长，后则自护其短，专以节省军费为口实，惑众而阻群言。“彭玉麟说，在赵继元看，跟洋人如果发生了纠纷，到头来无非归归之于“和”之一字。既然如此，“江防”也好，“海防”也好，都是白费心血，不过朝廷这样交代，不能不敷衍而已。但是真的节省经费、粉饰表面，也还罢了，实际上浪费甚多，只是当用不用而已，彭玉麟认为赵继元持这种论调，是件极危险的事，防务废弛，尽属虚文，一旦有警，无可倚恃，必至贻误大计。最后又说：“黜陟之柄，操自朝廷，差委之权，归于总督，臣不敢擅便，惟既有见闻，不忍瞻徇缄默，恐终掣实心办事者之时，而无以儆局员肆妄之心。”这意思是很明白的，如果他有权，即时会将赵继元撤差革职。此奏一上，慈槽太后震怒，初揽大权，正想整饬纳纪立威之时，当即批了个“劣迹昭著，即行革职”，再一次为彭玉磷显一显威风。这一来，李鸿章自亦大伤面子，不便对两江总督的入选，再表示意见，那把如意算盘，竟完全落空了。

As for Liu Kunyi, Peng wrote: “Fearing that Liu would be misled by Zhao, I strongly urged that this man could not be employed. Liu ordered his transfer out of the Bureau and appointed him to oversee army-camp affairs, which was generous treatment indeed. Yet Zhao dared argue with the governor-general before the assembled officials and continued assisting in the Bureau’s management. He understands neither warfare nor the longer view, and his addiction is deep. Relying on powerful connections to grasp authority, he is conceited and overbearing. First he displays his supposed strengths, then conceals his weaknesses, always using economy in military expenditure as his pretext, misleading others and silencing objections.” According to Peng, Zhao believed that any dispute with foreigners would eventually end in peace. River defense and coastal defense were therefore wasted effort. Since the court demanded them, one merely had to keep up appearances. Genuine economy, even accompanied by a show of compliance, would have been one thing. In fact, expenditure was wasteful; only necessary spending was withheld. Peng considered this outlook extremely dangerous. Defenses had decayed into empty formalities. At the first alarm there would be nothing to rely on, and the greater plans of the state would suffer. He concluded: “The power to dismiss and promote belongs to the throne; the power to assign duties rests with the governor-general. I dare not act without authority. Yet, having witnessed and heard these things, I cannot keep silent out of personal consideration. I fear that those sincerely attending to their work will remain hampered, while the Bureau’s staff receive no warning against their reckless arrogance.” His meaning was unmistakable: had he possessed the authority, he would have removed Zhao from both his assignment and his rank at once. Cixi was furious. Having just assumed sole power, she was eager to enforce discipline and establish her authority. She immediately wrote, “His misconduct is notorious. Dismiss him forthwith.” Once again Peng had demonstrated his power. Li naturally lost a great deal of face and could no longer conveniently express an opinion on the Liangjiang succession. His entire plan had fallen through.

听宝森谈完这段刚出炉的新闻，胡雪岩便即问道：“这么说，刘岘帅还会回任。”“回任大概不会了。”“那么是准来呢？”“当然是曾九帅。”“曾九帅”便是曾国荃。江宁是他在同治三年攻下来的，加以湘军旧部，遍布两江——上江安徽、下江江苏，所以每逢江督出缺，总有人把他列入继任人选。这一回，看起来真的要轮到“曾九帅”了。“曾九不相宜。”宝鋆说道：“他嫌陕甘太苦不肯去，最后拿富庶的两江给他，且不说人心不服，而且开挟持之渐，朝延以后用人就难了。”宝鋆是恭王的智囊，听他说得不错，便即问道：“那么，你看是让谁去呢？”“现成有一个人在那里：左季高。”“啊，啊！好。”恭王深深点头。原来左宗棠在军机处，主意太多，而又往往言大而夸，不切实际，宝望一直在排挤他。左宗棠一气之下，上折告病，请开缺回籍养菏，朝廷赏了他两个月的假。恭王毕竟忠厚，虽也讨厌左宗棠喋喋不休，但挤得他不安于位，也不免内疚神明，如今有两江这个“善地”让他去养老，可以略补歉疚，因而深为赞成。

When Baosen had finished this fresh news, Hu asked, “Then Governor-General Liu will return to his post?” “Probably not.” “Who will go, then?” “Ninth Marshal Zeng, of course.” Ninth Marshal Zeng was Zeng Guoquan. He had captured Jiangning in the third year of Tongzhi, and former Hunan Army officers were scattered throughout Liangjiang—Anhui, the Upper River province, and Jiangsu, the Lower. Whenever the governorship-general fell vacant, someone proposed him. This time his turn seemed finally to have come. “Zeng the Ninth would be unsuitable,” Bao Yun said. “He refuses Shaanxi and Gansu because conditions are harsh, and we then give him prosperous Liangjiang? Quite apart from the resentment it would cause, that would set a precedent for holding the court to ransom. Future appointments would become difficult.” Bao was Prince Gong’s trusted adviser. Recognizing the force of his argument, the prince asked, “Whom do you suggest, then?” “There is a man ready to hand: Zuo Jigao.” “Ah, yes. Excellent.” Prince Gong nodded emphatically. Zuo had too many ideas in the Grand Council, often grandiose and impractical, and Bao had continually tried to push him aside. Angered, Zuo had memorialized that he was ill and requested release from office to recover at home. The court had granted two months’ leave. Prince Gong was fundamentally a decent man. Though he too disliked Zuo’s incessant talk, he felt guilty that the pressure had made Zuo unable to remain comfortably in office. Liangjiang would provide a pleasant place for his old age and ease the prince’s conscience. He therefore approved warmly.

于是九月初六那天，由恭王面奏，说海防之议方兴，势在必行，主其事者是北洋、南洋两大臣，北洋有李鸿章在，可以政心，南洋需要有威望素著的重臣主持，几经考虑，认为以左宗棠为最适宜。而且，江南政风疲软，亦需象左宗棠那样有魄力的人去当总督，才能大事整顿。慈禧太后亦很讨厌左宗棠的口没遮拦，什么事想到就说，毫无顾忌，不过她很念旧，总想到左宗棠是艰难百战、立过大功劳的人，既然不宜于在朝，应该给他一个好地方让他去养老，所以同意了军机的建议，外放左宗棠为两江总督。

On the sixth day of the ninth lunar month, Prince Gong reported orally to the throne. Coastal defense was now a developing policy that must be carried through, principally by the Beiyang and Nanyang Ministers. With Li Hongzhang in the North there was no cause for anxiety. The South needed a senior minister of established prestige, and careful consideration showed Zuo to be the most suitable. Jiangnan administration was also lax and required an energetic governor-general like Zuo to undertake thorough reform. Cixi too disliked his unguarded tongue: he said whatever came into his head without restraint. Yet she remembered old service and could not forget the arduous campaigns and great victories he had won. If he was unsuited to court life, he should have a good place in which to grow old. She accepted the Council’s recommendation and appointed him Governor-General of Liangjiang.

这个消息传到时，恰好胡雪岩陪着畅游了西湖上六桥三竺之胜的室森回到上海。对他来说，这自然是个喜讯，不由得又在心里激起了好些雄图壮志。照例的，胡雪岩每一趟到上海，起码有半个月的工夫，要应付为他接风而日夜排满了的饭局，第一是官场，第二是商场，最后才轮到至亲好友。古应春和七姑奶奶夫妇是“自己人”，挨到他们做主人请客，已经是十月初，将近慈德太后万寿的日子了。这天请了两桌客，陪客也都是“自己人”，其中有刘不才，他如今管着胡庆余堂药店，这一回到上海是要转道北方去采办明年要用的药材，有宓本常，他是阜康雪记银号上海总号的“大伙”。此外也都是胡雪岩私人资本开设的丝号，典当的档手。酒阑人散，为时尚早，胡雪岩想趁此机会跟古应春夫妇好好谈一谈自己这几天的见闻与想法，所以决定留宿在古家。古家原替他预备得有宿处，是二楼后房极大的一个套间，一切现成，便将他的轿夫与跟班都打发了回去，只留下一个贴身的小跟班，名叫阿成的，随他住在古家。

The news reached Hu just as he returned to Shanghai with Baosen after showing him the Six Bridges and Three Tianzhu Temples at West Lake. Naturally, it was welcome news, and it stirred fresh ambitions in him. Each visit to Shanghai normally meant at least half a month of welcome dinners filling his days and evenings. Official circles came first, business circles second, and only then could close friends and relatives have their turn. Gu and the Seventh Lady were family. By the time they could entertain him, it was early in the tenth lunar month, near Empress Dowager Cixi’s birthday. They invited two tables of guests, all intimates. Among them was Liu Bucai, now managing Hu Qingyu Hall, who had come to Shanghai on his way north to purchase medicinal materials for the coming year. There was also Mi Benchang, head manager of the Shanghai headquarters of Fukang Xueji Bank. The others managed silk firms and pawnshops established with Hu’s private capital. When the wine was finished and the guests had gone, the hour was still early. Hu wanted a proper conversation with Gu and his wife about his recent observations and ideas, and decided to stay the night. A spacious suite at the back of the second floor was always kept ready for him. His chair bearers and attendants were sent home; only a young personal attendant named A’cheng stayed with him.

“应春，这回湘阴放两江，等于合肥掼了一大跤，你看，我们有点啥事情好做？”“小爷叔，”古应春答说：“我看你现在先不必打什么主意，不妨看看再说。”“为啥？”“事情明摆在那里，合肥、湘阴一向是对头，湘阴这趟放两江，第一，他不会象以前的几位制台那样，让北洋来管南洋的事，其次，湘阴跟刘岘帅是湖南同乡，刘岘帅吃了合肥的亏，湘阴只要有机会，自然要替他报复，这是湘阴这方面。再说合肥那方面，当然也要防备。论手段是合肥厉害，说不定先发制人，我们要防到‘吃夹档’。”“ ‘吃夹档’？”胡雪岩愕然，他想不通左李相争，何以他会受池鱼之殃？“两方面钩心斗角，不外乎两条计策，一种是有靠山的，擒威擒王，一种是有帮手的，翦除羽翼。湘阴是后面一种。小爷叔，合肥要动湘阴，先要翦除羽翼，只怕你是首当其冲。”胡雪岩惊然动容，但亦不免困惑，“莫非你要叫我朝合肥递降表？”他问，“我要这样做，怎么对得起湘阴？”“递降表当然说怎么样也不行的。我看，小爷叔要联络联络邵小村。”邵小村名友赚，浙江余姚人，也算是洋务人材，一向跟李鸿章接近，新近放的上海道。上海道本来是李鸿章的亲信刘瑞芬，另为刘坤一参盛宣怀一案，刘瑞芬秉公办理，因而得罪了李鸿章，设法将他调为江西藩司。刘去邵来，足以看出上海道这个管着江海关的肥缺，等于是由李鸿章在管辖。

“Yingchun, sending Xiangyin to Liangjiang amounts to a heavy fall for Hefei. Do you see any opportunity for us?” Xiangyin was Zuo’s home district, Hefei Li’s. “Little Uncle, I would not start devising anything yet. We might do better to wait and see.” “Why?” “It is plain enough. Hefei and Xiangyin have always been opponents. Now that Xiangyin is going to Liangjiang, he will not let the Northern administration interfere in Southern affairs as the earlier governors-general did. Then he and Governor-General Liu are fellow Hunan men. Liu has suffered at Hefei’s hands, and Xiangyin will naturally avenge him if he gets the chance. That is one side. Hefei, of course, must guard against it. In maneuvering, he is the more formidable. He may strike first. We must take care not to be caught between them.” “Caught between them?” Hu was astonished. How could a struggle between Zuo and Li bring disaster on him? “When two sides are scheming against each other, there are only two basic methods. If your opponent depends on a patron, capture the leader to defeat his followers. If he depends on helpers, clip his wings. Xiangyin is the latter kind. If Hefei means to move against him, Little Uncle, he must first remove his helpers. I fear you will be the first to bear the attack.” Hu’s expression changed in alarm, though he was still perplexed. “Do you mean I should submit my surrender to Hefei? How could I do that and face Xiangyin?” “Surrender is out of the question, of course. I think you should cultivate Shao Xiaocun.” Xiaocun was Shao Youlian, a man from Yuyao in Zhejiang, capable in foreign affairs and long close to Li Hongzhang. He had recently been appointed Shanghai Circuit Intendant. The post had previously belonged to Li’s trusted associate Liu Ruifen. By investigating Sheng impartially during Liu Kunyi’s impeachment case, Liu Ruifen had offended Li, who arranged his transfer as Provincial Treasurer of Jiangxi. Liu’s departure and Shao’s arrival showed how thoroughly the lucrative Shanghai post, which controlled Jianghaiguan Customs, lay within Li’s grasp.

“联络邵小村，不就是要吊合肥的膀子？莫非真的要磕了头才算递降表？”“吊膀子”是市井俚语，语虽粗俗，但说得却很透彻。古应春默然半晌，突然提出一个惊人的建议。“小爷叔，一不做。二不休，你索性花上二三十万银子，把邵小村攻掉！”这一下，胡雪岩更觉错愕莫名，“你是说，要我去当上海道？”他问。“是啊！”胡雪岩无从置答，站起来编着方步盘算了好一会，突然喊道：“七姐，七姐！”七姑奶奶正在剥蟹粉预备消夜点心，听得招呼，匆匆忙忙出来问道：“小爷叔叫我？”“应春要我去做上海道。你看他这个主意，行得通，行不通？”七姑奶奶愣了一下，“怎么一桩事情，我还弄不清楚呢？”她看着她丈夫问：“上海道不是新换的人吗？”这一下倒提醒了古应春，自觉虑事不周，邵友赚到任未几，倘非有重大过失，决无开缺之理，因而点点头答说：“看起来不大行得通。”“而且，我也不是做官的人。”胡雪岩问：“你看我是起得来早去站班的人吗？”胡雪岩虽戴“红顶”，毕竟是“商人”。如今发了大财，起居豪奢，过于王侯，分内该当可摆的官派，也不过是他排场的一部分。倘说补了实缺，做此官，行此礼，且不说象候补道那样，巴结长官，遇到督抚公出，早早赶到地方去站班伺候，冀邀一盼，至少大员过境，上海道以地方官的身分，送往迎来，就是他视为畏途的差使。

“Cultivating Shao Xiaocun—isn’t that simply flirting with Hefei? Must I actually kowtow before you will call it surrender?” The vulgar expression “flirting” made the point perfectly clear. Gu was silent for some time, then made a startling proposal. “Little Uncle, once we begin, why stop halfway? Spend two or three hundred thousand taels and force Shao Xiaocun out!” Hu was more bewildered than ever. “You mean I should become Shanghai Circuit Intendant?” “Yes!” Unable to answer, Hu rose and paced thoughtfully in measured steps. After a long while he suddenly called, “Seventh Sister! Seventh Sister!” The Seventh Lady was picking crabmeat for the late-night refreshments. At his call she hurried out. “Did you want me, Little Uncle?” “Yingchun wants me to become Shanghai Circuit Intendant. Do you think the idea is practicable?” She paused in surprise. “I don’t yet understand what this is about.” Turning to her husband, she asked, “Hasn’t the Shanghai intendant only just changed?” This reminded Gu of something he had overlooked. Shao had only recently taken office; without a serious fault, there could be no reason to remove him. He nodded. “It does look difficult.” “Besides, I am not made for official life,” Hu said. “Do I look like a man who can get up early to stand in attendance?” Red button or no red button, Hu was still a merchant. With his fortune made, he lived more extravagantly than princes, and whatever official ceremony properly belonged to his rank was merely part of his display. A substantive appointment would require him to perform its actual duties and observe its formalities. He need not go as far as the expectant intendants, who fawned on superiors and hurried ahead to stand waiting whenever a governor or governor-general went out, hoping for a glance. But even receiving and seeing off senior officials passing through Shanghai, an unavoidable local duty, seemed an ordeal to him.

七姑奶奶有些弄明白了，她也是听古应春说过，邵友濂是李鸿章的人，跟胡雪岩是左宗棠的人，算是敌对的。现在古应春建议胡雪岩去当上海道，取邵而代之，不是上海道对胡雪岩有何好处，只是要攻掉邵友濂而已。“不管行得通，行不通，也不管小爷叔舒服惯了，吃不吃得来做官的苦头，根本上就不该动这个念头！”七姑奶奶说话向来爽直深刻，因此何以不该动这个念头，在古应春与胡雪岩都要求她提出解释。“我倒先请问你，” 七姑奶奶问她丈夫：“上海道是不是天下第一肥缺？”“这还用你问？”七姑奶奶不理他，仍旧管自己问，“小爷叔是不是天下第一首富？”这就更不用问了，“不然怎么叫‘财神’呢？”古应春答说：“你不要乱扯了。”“不是我乱扯。如果小爷叔当了上海道，就有人会乱扯。小爷叔是做生意发的财，偏偏有人说他是做官发的财，而偏偏上海道又是有名的肥缺，你说，对敲竹杠的‘都老爷’，如果应酬得不到，硬说小爷叔的钱是做贪官来的，那一下跳到黄河都洗不清了。”这一说，吓出古应春一身冷汗，如果胡雪岩当了上海道，真的说不定会替他惹来抄家之祸。“应春，你听听。”胡雪岩说：“这就是为啥我要请教七姐的道理。”“小爷叔，你不要替我戴高帽子！倒是有句话，我……”七姑奶奶突然顿住，停了一会才说：“慢慢再谈吧！”说完，转身走了。

The Seventh Lady began to understand. Gu had told her that Shao Youlian belonged to Li’s camp, just as Hu belonged to Zuo’s, and so they were opponents. Her husband wanted Hu to replace Shao, not because the post would benefit Hu, but simply to remove Shao. “Whether the plan could work or not, and whether Little Uncle is too accustomed to comfort to endure official life, the idea itself should never have entered your head!” Her remarks were always frank and penetrating. Both men asked why she thought the idea so wrong. “Let me ask you first,” she said to her husband. “Is Shanghai Circuit Intendant the most lucrative post in the empire?” “Do you need to ask?” Ignoring his tone, she continued, “And is Little Uncle the richest man in the empire?” That needed asking still less. “Otherwise why call him the God of Wealth? Stop talking nonsense.” “I am not. But if Little Uncle became Shanghai Intendant, others would. He made his fortune in business, yet people insist he made it in office. And Shanghai is notoriously lucrative. Suppose the extortionate censors were not properly attended to and insisted that all his money came from official corruption. He could jump into the Yellow River and still never wash himself clean!” Gu broke into a cold sweat. Taking the Shanghai post might indeed expose Hu to the confiscation of his entire property. “Listen to her, Yingchun. Now you know why I ask Seventh Sister’s advice.” “Do not flatter me, Little Uncle! There is something, though, that I…” She stopped abruptly, waited a moment, then said, “We can speak of it later.” With that she turned and left.

胡雪岩并不曾留意于她那欲言又止的态度，重拾话题说道：“对邵小村，敷衍我不肯，要攻掉他，大可不必，那儿应春，你说，如何是好？”“当然只有不即不离。”“也就是一切照常？”“是的。”“那好。我们回头再来谈湘阴来了以后的做法。”胡雪岩说，“我想湘阴来后，我可以对怡和下杀手了。”怡和是指英商怡和洋行。这家洋行的在华贸易，发展得很快，跟胡雪岩的关系是亦友亦敌。胡雪岩为左宗棠采办军需，特别是西洋新式的军火，颇得力于怡和的供应，但在从事丝的出口方面，怡和是胡雪岩的第一劲敌。本来胡雪岩做丝生意，“动洋庄”是以怡和为对象。但怡和认为通过胡雪岩来买丝，价格上太吃亏，不如自己派人下乡收购，出价比胡雪岩高，养蚕人家自然乐意卖出，而在怡和，仍旧比向胡雪岩买丝来得划算。换句话说，养蚕人家跟怡和直接交易，彼此分享了胡雪岩的中间利益。不过，这一点胡雪岩倒不大在乎，因为他讲究公平交易，而且口头上常挂一句话：“有饭大家吃。”养蚕人家的新丝能卖得好价钱，于他有益无损，青黄不接，或者急景调年辰光放出去的帐，能够顺利收回，岂非一件好事。

Hu paid no attention to her hesitation and returned to the subject. “As for Shao Xiaocun, I will not court him, and there is no need to force him out. What do you suggest, Yingchun?” “Neither draw close nor keep too far away.” “In other words, carry on as usual?” “Yes.” “Very well. Let us return to what we can do once Xiangyin arrives. I think I can then strike a decisive blow at Jardine Matheson.” The British trading house had expanded rapidly in China. Its relations with Hu were those of both friend and enemy. He had relied heavily on its supplies when procuring military equipment for Zuo, particularly modern Western arms. In silk exports, however, it was his greatest competitor. In selling silk to foreign firms, Hu had chiefly dealt with Jardine Matheson. The firm thought buying through him too expensive and began sending agents into the countryside itself. It offered more than Hu did, so the silkworm-raising households were naturally pleased to sell; yet it still paid less than it would have paid him. In effect, producer and foreign buyer divided Hu’s middleman’s profit between them. This did not greatly trouble him. He believed in fair dealing and often said, “Let everyone share the rice.” A good price for the households’ new silk helped rather than harmed him: loans he had advanced during the lean season or the pressing needs of year’s end could then be repaid smoothly. Surely that was a good thing.

只是眼前有一样情况，非速谋对策不可，光绪五年怡和洋行在苏州河边，设了一家缫丝厂，今年——光绪七年，有个湖州人黄佐卿也开了一家，字号名为公和永，还有一家公平缫丝厂，由英商公平洋行投资，亦在密锣紧鼓地筹备之中。怡和与公和永这两家缫丝厂，都还没有开工，主要的原因是，反对的人太多。一部机器抵得上三十个人，换句话说，机器开工一日的产量，用人工要一个月。这一来，浙西农村中，多少丝户的生计，有断绝之虞。因此丝业公所发起抵制，实际上是胡雪岩发起抵制，丝业公所的管事，都唯他马首是瞻的。但这三家新式缫丝厂，势成骑虎，尤其是怡和、公平两家，倘或不办新式缫丝厂，他们在欧洲的客户，都会转向日本去买高品质的丝。因为如此，三家新式缫丝厂，居然联成一起，共同聘请意大利人麦登斯为总工程师，指导三厂的技师，操作购自意大利或法国的机器，同时派人下乡，预付价款，买明年的新丝。这一下，可以说与胡雪岩发起的抵制，进入短兵相接的局面了。

One development, however, demanded a prompt response. In the fifth year of Guangxu, Jardine Matheson had established a silk-reeling mill beside Suzhou Creek. This year, the seventh of Guangxu, Huang Zuoqing of Huzhou had opened another, Gongheyong. A third, the Gongping Silk-Reeling Mill, financed by the British firm Gongping Trading House, was also being prepared at a vigorous pace. Neither Jardine’s mill nor Gongheyong had begun production. The chief reason was widespread opposition. One machine did the work of thirty people: what it produced in a day would take a month by hand. The livelihoods of many silk-producing households in western Zhejiang were therefore threatened. The Silk Guild had organized resistance; in reality, Hu had organized it, for the Guild’s managers followed his lead in everything. But the three modern mills could no longer readily withdraw. Unless they adopted machine reeling, Jardine and Gongping in particular would lose their European customers to Japan’s higher-quality silk. The three mills consequently joined forces and hired an Italian, Maidengsi, as chief engineer, to guide their technicians in operating machinery purchased from Italy or France. They also sent agents into the countryside to pay in advance for next year’s new silk. Their confrontation with Hu’s resistance had become a close struggle.

胡雪岩手下的谋士，对这件事分成两派，大多数赞成抵制，少部分主张顺应潮流，古应春就曾很剀切地劝过他。“小爷叔，如今不是天朝大国的日子了，天外有天，人外有人，再狠也不能不看看潮流。机器缫丝，不断不毛，雪自发亮，跟发黄的土丝摆在一起看，真象大小姐跟烧火丫头站在一起，不能比了。这是没法子的事，当年英国发明蒸汽机，还不是多少人反对，可是到后来呢？”“你说的道理不错，不过乡下那许多丝户，手里没有‘生活’做，叫他们吃什么？”胡雪岩说：“我尽我的心，能保护住他一天，我尽一天的心。真的潮流冲得他们立脚不住，我良心上也过得去了。“这不是讲良心的事！古应春心里在想，如果真的能将三厂打倒，关门拍卖机器，那时不妨找几个人合伙接手，捡个现成的大便宜，当然，胡雪岩如果愿意，让他占大股，不过此时还不宜说破。

His advisers were divided. Most favored opposition; a minority wanted to move with the times. Gu had earnestly urged the latter course. “Little Uncle, these are no longer the days when the Celestial Empire stood above all. There is always a greater world beyond our own, and someone more capable than ourselves. However fierce you are, you must look at the direction things are moving. Machine-reeled silk has no breaks or fuzz; it is brilliant white. Beside yellowish native silk, it looks like a young lady standing next to a kitchen maid. There is no comparison. We cannot change that. How many people opposed the steam engine when it was invented in Britain? What happened in the end?” “Your argument is sound. But what will all those country households eat if there is no work for their hands? I shall do what I can. For every day I can protect them, I shall make the effort. If the tide finally sweeps their footing away, at least my conscience will be clear.” This was not a matter of conscience, Gu thought. If the three mills could actually be forced to close and auction their machinery, he might find partners to take them over and acquire a ready-made bargain. Naturally, Hu could hold the largest share if he wished. For now, however, this thought must remain unspoken.

于是古应春一变而为很热心地策划抵制的步骤，最紧要的一着是，控制原料，胡雪岩以同样的价钱买丝，凭过去的关系，当然比工厂有利。无奈怡和，公平两厂，财力雄厚，后又提高收购价格，胡雪岩一看情势不妙，灵机一动，大量出货，及至怡和、公平两行高价购入，行情转平，胡雪岩抢先补进，一出一进很赚了一笔。这第一回合，怡和、公平吃了亏，手中虽有存货，初期开工，不愁没有原料。但以后势必难乎为继。而就在这时候，胡雪岩又有机会了。机会就是左宗棠来当两江总督，“应春，”他说：“我们现在讲公平交易。怡和、公平用机器，我们用手，你说公平不公平？”“这不公平是没法子的事。”“怎么会没有法子？当然有，只看当道肯不肯做，如果是合肥只想跟洋人拉交情，不肯做，湘阴就肯做了。等我来说动他。”“小爷叔，”古应春笑了，“说了半天，到底什么事肯做不做做？”“加茧捐。要叫他们成本上涨，无利可图，那就一定要关门大吉了。”这茧捐当然是有差别的，否则成本同样增加，还是竞争不过人家。古应春觉得用这一着对付洋商，确是很厉害，但须防洋商策动总税务局英国人赫德，经由李鸿章的关系，向总理衙门提出交涉。“不会的。”胡雪岩另有一套看法：“合肥碰了两个钉子，不会再象从前那样多管闲事了。再说，我们江浙的丝业，跟他北洋风马牛不相及，他就想要管闲事，你想，湘阴会买他的帐吗？”

Gu accordingly became eager to plan the resistance. The essential move was to control the raw material. At equal prices, Hu’s established ties gave him an advantage over the mills in buying silk. Unfortunately, Jardine and Gongping had ample funds and raised their purchase prices. Seeing the unfavorable turn, Hu suddenly began selling heavily. Once the two firms had bought at high prices and the market settled, he replenished his stocks ahead of them, making a handsome profit on the outward and inward transactions. Jardine and Gongping lost this first round. Although their stocks would supply enough material to begin production, future supplies were bound to become difficult. At this moment another opportunity came to Hu: Zuo’s appointment to Liangjiang. “Yingchun, we are talking about fair dealing. Jardine and Gongping use machines; we use our hands. Is that fair?” “That is an inequality we cannot remedy.” “Why not? Of course it can be remedied. It depends on whether those in authority are willing. Hefei only wants to befriend the foreigners; he would refuse. Xiangyin will do it. I shall persuade him.” Gu laughed. “Little Uncle, after all this talk, what exactly is he to do?” “Raise the cocoon levy. Make their costs so high that no profit remains, and they will certainly have to close.” Naturally, the levy would have to be applied unequally; if everyone’s costs rose equally, they would still be unable to compete. Gu thought this a powerful way of dealing with the foreign firms. But they might enlist Hart, the British Inspector-General of Customs, and use Li’s connections to bring the dispute before the Zongli Yamen. “They will not,” Hu said, taking a different view. “After two rebuffs, Hefei will not interfere as freely as before. Besides, the silk industry in Jiangsu and Zhejiang has nothing to do with his Northern jurisdiction. Even if he does try to meddle, do you suppose Xiangyin will listen to him?”

完形：关注however、therefore、unless、once、in effect、even if的转折、因果与条件；同意行为不等于认同其公开理由。
排序：待客景物—官场新闻—制度背景—人事转折—私人议策—丝业冲突—税捐设想；追踪刘、赵、左之间的主语和话题转换。
