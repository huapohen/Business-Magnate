胡雪岩全传──烟消云散

Hu Xueyan: Vanishing into Thin Air

第一章 甲申之变｜CHAPTER ONE: THE CRISIS OF THE JIASHEN YEAR

上海的市面更坏了，是受了法国在越南的战事的影响。法国凯觎越南，由来已久。同治元年，法皇拿破仑第二，以海军大举侵入越南。其时清廷正因洪杨之变自顾不暇，所以越南虽是清王朝的属国，却无力出兵保护，越南被迫订了城下之盟，割让庆和、嘉定、定祥三省。嘉定省便是西贡，法国人在那里竭力经营，作为进一步侵略越南、进窥中国云南的根据地。

Business in Shanghai had deteriorated further under the impact of France's war in Vietnam. France had long coveted Vietnam. In the first year of Tongzhi, the French emperor Napoleon II launched a major naval invasion. The Qing court, fully occupied with the rebellion of Hong and Yang, had no strength to protect Vietnam, though it was a tributary state. Vietnam was forced to sign a humiliating treaty and cede the three provinces of Qinghe, Jiading, and Dingxiang. Jiading was Saigon, where the French worked energetically to establish a base for further aggression against Vietnam and eventual advances toward China's Yunnan Province.

同治十一年，越南内乱，头目叫做黄崇英，拥众数万，用黄旗，号称“黄旗军”。法国人勾通了黄崇英，规取“东京”，渡汉江。攻取广西镇南关外的谅山，广西巡抚是湘军宿将刘长佑，派兵助越平乱，同时邀请刘永福助剿——刘永福是广西上思州人，本是个私枭，咸丰年间，洪杨起事，刘永福却另有心胸，率领部下健儿三百人，出镇南关进入越南保胜。此地本为一个广东人何均昌所占领，为刘永福起而代之，所部用黑旗，号称“黑旗军”。既受刘长佑的邀请，复又受越南王的招抚，与广西官兵夹击法军，威震一时，但越南内部意见分歧，最后决定议和，所派遣的大臣三名，为法军拘禁，被迫订了二十二条的《西贡条约》，割地通商以外，承认受法国的保护。为了安抚刘永福，授职为三宣副提督。刘永福便在边境深山中，屯垦练兵，部下聚集至二十万之多，其中劲旅两万人，年龄在十七以上，二十四以下，一个个面黑身高，孔武有力，越林超涧，轻捷如猿，士气极其高昂，因而为法军视如眼中钉，曾经悬重金买他的首级。

In the eleventh year of Tongzhi, internal disorder broke out in Vietnam. Its leader, Huang Chongying, commanded tens of thousands of men under yellow banners, hence the name Yellow Flag Army. The French colluded with him to seize Tonkin, crossed the Han River, and captured Lang Son beyond Zhennan Pass on the Guangxi frontier. Liu Changyou, Governor of Guangxi and a veteran commander of the Hunan Army, sent troops to help Vietnam suppress the disorder and also invited Liu Yongfu to join the campaign. Liu Yongfu came from Shangsi in Guangxi and had originally been a smuggler. During the Xianfeng reign, when Hong and Yang rose in rebellion, he followed an ambition of his own, leading three hundred sturdy followers through Zhennan Pass into Baosheng in Vietnam. A Guangdong man named He Junchang had occupied the place, but Liu supplanted him. His men carried black banners and became known as the Black Flag Army. Having accepted Liu Changyou's invitation and the Vietnamese king's offer to bring him into the royal service, he joined the Guangxi troops in attacks on the French from two sides and won a formidable reputation. Opinion within Vietnam was divided, however, and peace negotiations were eventually chosen. The three envoys sent to negotiate were detained by the French and compelled to sign the twenty-two-article Treaty of Saigon. Besides ceding territory and opening trade, Vietnam accepted French protection. To placate Liu Yongfu, he was appointed deputy commander of Sanxuan. He settled in the remote mountain frontier, farming and training his troops. His followers grew to as many as two hundred thousand, including twenty thousand picked men between seventeen and twenty-four. Tall, dark-faced, and powerfully built, they crossed forests and ravines as nimbly as apes, their morale exceptionally high. The French regarded him as a thorn in their side and offered a large reward for his head.

自从《西贡条约》订立以后，越南举国上下，无不既悔且愤，越南王阮福时，决意重用黑旗兵。不道法国先下手为强，以重兵陷河内。于是在顺化的阮福时遂予黑旗军驱逐法军的任务。越南有失，广西、云南便受威胁，而且法国已正式向中国提出通商的要求。朝中议论，分为为战、主和两派，主战派以李鸿藻为首，除了支持云贵总督岑毓英支持刘永福以外，且特起曾国荃为两广总督，部署海防。此外左宗棠亦力主作战，清议更为激昂，但主和派的势力亦不小。 当然，李鸿章是主和的，驻法公使曾纪泽亦不主张决裂，但对其中的利害得失，看得最清楚的是曾经使法的郭嵩焘。这年光绪九年正月，李鸿章与法国公使宝海，本已达成“中国撤兵、法不侵越”的协议，不意法国发生政潮，内阁改组，新任外务部长拉克尔是个野心家，一面将宝海撤任、推翻成议，一面促使法国增兵越南。于是朝旨命丁忧守制之中的李鸿章迅往广东督办越南事宜，节制两广云南防军。就表面看，是派李鸿尊去主持战局，而实际上并非如此。此中消息为郭嵩焘所参透，特意从他的家乡、湖南湘阴派专差送了一封长信给李鸿章，以为“处置西洋，始终无战法”，他说，洋人意在通商，就跟他谈通商好了。只要一答应谈判通商，越南的局势自然就会缓和。如今派李鸿章出而督师，大张旗鼓，摆出一决雌雄的阵势，是逼迫法国作战。法国本无意于战，逼之应战，是兵法上的“不知彼”。

After the Treaty of Saigon, regret and resentment spread through Vietnam from the highest ranks to the lowest. King Ruan Fushi resolved to make full use of the Black Flags, but France struck first, capturing Hanoi with a powerful force. From Hue, the king ordered the Black Flags to drive the French out. If Vietnam were lost, Guangxi and Yunnan would be threatened; France had also formally demanded trading relations with China. At court, opinion divided between war and peace. The war party was led by Li Hongzao. Besides supporting Cen Yuying, Governor-General of Yunnan and Guizhou, in his support for Liu Yongfu, it secured the special appointment of Zeng Guoquan as Governor-General of Liangguang to organize coastal defence. Zuo Zongtang also urged war, and opinion among the scholar-officials grew more impassioned. Yet the peace party was far from weak. Li Hongzhang, naturally, favoured peace, and Zeng Jize, the minister to France, also opposed a rupture. The clearest understanding of the advantages and dangers, however, belonged to Guo Songtao, who had previously represented China in France. In the first lunar month of that year, the ninth of Guangxu, Li and the French minister Bourée had reached an agreement: China would withdraw its troops, and France would not invade Vietnam. But political upheaval in France brought a cabinet reshuffle. The new foreign minister, Challemel-Lacour, was ambitious: he recalled Bourée, repudiated the agreement, and urged the dispatch of more French troops to Vietnam. An imperial order then instructed Li, who was observing mourning, to proceed urgently to Guangdong, direct Vietnamese affairs, and command the defence forces of Liangguang and Yunnan. Outwardly, he appeared to be taking charge of a war, but Guo saw otherwise. From his home at Xiangyin in Hunan, he sent a special messenger with a long letter. “In dealing with the West, war has never offered a solution,” he argued. The foreigners wanted trade; China should discuss trade with them. Agreeing to negotiations would ease the situation in Vietnam. Sending Li to command armies with a great show of force, as if determined to fight to the finish, would compel France to fight. France had not originally wished for war. To force it into one was to fail, in military terms, to understand the enemy.

如果真的要战，又是“不知己”，他的话说得很沉痛：“用兵三十余年，聚而为兵，散而为盗，蔓延天下，隐患方深。重以水旱频仍，吏治调敝，盗贼满野，民不聊生，而于是时急开边畔，募兵以资防御，旷日逾时，而耗敝不可支矣。”这是就军费者言，说中国不能战。就算战胜了，又怎么办？战胜当然要裁兵，将刚招募的新兵遣散，结果是“游荡无所归”，聚集“饥困之民图逞”，是自己制造乱源。接下来，他转述京中的议论：“枢府以滇督援甲厉兵，而粤督处之泰然，数有訾议，是以属中堂以专征之任。”看起来是因为岑毓英想打，而曾国茎袖手旁观，前方将帅意见不一，需要一个位高权重的李鸿章去笼罩全面，主持一切。事实上呢“京师议论，所以属之中堂，仍以议和，非求战也”。

If China actually went to war, Guo continued, it would also be failing to understand itself. His words were bitter: “For more than thirty years we have waged war. Gathered together, men become soldiers; dispersed, they become bandits, spreading across the empire and leaving profound dangers in their wake. Add repeated floods and droughts, a decaying administration, robbers everywhere, and a population unable to sustain life. To provoke trouble on the frontier now and recruit still more troops for defence would, as the days and months passed, produce exhaustion beyond endurance.” In terms of military expenditure alone, China could not fight. Even if it won, what then? Victory would require demobilizing the newly recruited troops, leaving them “wandering with nowhere to return,” gathering “the hungry and destitute to pursue their designs.” China would be creating the sources of its own disorder. Guo next reported the discussion in Beijing: “The Grand Council has repeatedly criticized the contrast between the Governor-General of Yunnan's preparations for battle and the Governor-General of Guangdong's apparent unconcern. That is why Your Excellency has been entrusted with supreme command.” It seemed that Cen Yuying wanted to fight while Zeng Guoquan stood aside, and that a man of Li's seniority and authority was needed to bring the commanders into agreement and direct the whole undertaking. In reality, however, “the capital's purpose in entrusting this to Your Excellency is still to negotiate peace, not to seek war.”

李鸿章虽然在守制之中，但朝中情形，毫不隔膜，他在京师有好几个“坐探”，朝中一举一动，无不以最快的方法，报到合肥，知道恭王子和战之际，游移不决，而主战最力的是“北派”领袖李鸿藻及一班清流，尤其是左副都御史张佩纶。因此，李鸿章纵有议和之意，却不敢公然表示，因为清议的力量很大，而且刘永福的黑旗军打得很好，更助长了主战派的声势，此时主和是冒天下之大不韪，所以迟迟其行，到上海以后，与接替宝海的新任法国公使德理固，谈了几次，态度不软亦不硬，掌握了一个“拖”字诀。“拖”下去会有什么结果呢？这是李李鸿章自己都不知道的事，不过他在暗中大下功夫，想消除几个议和的障碍，第一个左副都御史张佩纶，他是清流的中坚，能把他疏通好，主战的高调不是唱得那么响，议和便较易措手。另一个是驻法公使曾纪泽，他不主张交涉决裂，但并不表示他主张对法让步，尤其是在从俄国回到巴黎以后，眼看法国的政策亦在摇摆之中，主战的只是少数。因此特地密电李鸿章及总理衙门，建议军事援越，对德理固的交涉不妨强硬。李鸿章对曾纪泽的意见，不置可否，但却致书郭嵩焘，暗示希望他能影响曾纪泽。郭嵩焘与曾纪泽的关系很深，而且驻法是前后任，他的言论一定能为曾纪泽所尊重。

Although in mourning, Li was fully informed about the court. He maintained several resident informants in Beijing, who reported every development to Hefei by the fastest available means. He knew that Prince Gong wavered between war and peace, while the strongest advocates of war were Li Hongzao, leader of the northern faction, and the men of the Pure Stream group, particularly Zhang Peilun, the Left Deputy Censor-in-Chief. Even if Li intended to negotiate peace, he dared not say so openly. Scholarly opinion carried great force, and the Black Flags' successes had further strengthened the war party. To advocate peace now would defy the sentiment of the whole country. He therefore delayed his departure. On reaching Shanghai, he held several talks with Tricou, the new French minister who had replaced Bourée, adopting a manner neither yielding nor aggressive and relying above all on delay. What would delay achieve? Li himself did not know. Behind the scenes, however, he worked hard to remove obstacles to negotiation. The first was Zhang Peilun, a pillar of the Pure Stream group. If Zhang could be won over and the clamour for war quieted, peace would be easier to arrange. Another obstacle was Zeng Jize. His opposition to a breakdown in negotiations did not mean that he favoured concessions to France. After returning from Russia to Paris, he had observed that French policy was still unsettled and that only a minority wanted war. He sent secret telegrams to Li and the Zongli Yamen recommending military aid to Vietnam and a firm line with Tricou. Li neither accepted nor rejected his advice, but wrote to Guo, hinting that he should influence Zeng. Guo's ties with Zeng were close; they had also served in succession as minister to France. Zeng would certainly respect his views.

就在这“拖”的一两个月中，法国与越南的情势，都起了变化，法国的政策已趋一致，内阁总理茹斐理向国会声称，决心加强在越南的军事行动，同时派出九千人援越，另遣军舰十二艘东来，水师提督古拔代陆军提督布意为法军统帅。越南则国王阮福时去世，由王弟阮福升继位，称号为“合和王”。由这称号，便知他是愿意屈服于法国的，即位只有一个月，便与法国订立了二十七条的《顺化条约》，正式承认越南为法国的保护国，而又仍旧尊重中国为宗主国，原来每年进贡，取道镇南关循陆路进京，今后改由海道入贡。这一法越《顺化条约》，促成了法国政策的一致，同时也赋予了法军名正言顺得以驱逐黑旗军的地位。因此，越南政府中的主战派大为不满。弑合和王而另立阮福吴，称号是“建福王”。尽管已到天津回任的李鸿章仍与法国公使在谈判越南的主权，而事实上中法双方剑拔弩张，开仗几不可免，尤其是特命彭玉鳞办理广东军务，消息一传，上海的人心越发恐慌。其时在九月中旬，正当螺蛳太太由上海回到杭州时。

During these one or two months of delay, developments in both France and Vietnam changed the situation. French policy grew more unified. Prime Minister Jules Ferry told parliament that he was determined to intensify military operations in Vietnam. Nine thousand reinforcements and another twelve warships were sent east, and Admiral Courbet replaced General Bouët as commander of the French forces. In Vietnam, King Ruan Fushi died and was succeeded by his younger brother Ruan Fusheng, under the title King Hehe, or “Harmonious Peace.” The title itself suggested a willingness to submit to France. Only a month after his accession, he signed the twenty-seven-article Treaty of Hue, formally acknowledging Vietnam as a French protectorate while continuing to recognize China as its suzerain. The annual tribute, previously carried overland through Zhennan Pass to Beijing, would henceforth travel by sea. This Franco-Vietnamese treaty helped unite French policy and gave the French troops a recognized basis for expelling the Black Flags. The war party within the Vietnamese government was furious. It killed King Hehe and enthroned Ruan Fuwu as King Jianfu. Though Li, now back at his post in Tianjin, continued negotiating with the French minister over Vietnamese sovereignty, the two countries were in fact poised for battle, and war seemed almost unavoidable. News of Peng Yulin's special appointment to direct military affairs in Guangdong deepened the panic in Shanghai. It was now the middle of the ninth lunar month, just when Madam Luosi returned from Shanghai to Hangzhou.

就在她回到杭州的第二夭，江宁派了个专差来，身穿红装，风尘满面，但头上一顶披满红丝穗的纬帽，高耸一粒红顶子，后面还拖一条花翎，身后跟着四名从人，亦都有顶戴。他们是由陆路来的，五匹高头大马，一路沙尘滚滚、辔铃当当、威风凛凛，路人侧目。一进了武林门，那专差将手一扬，都勒了马，其中一个戴暗蓝顶子的武官，走马趋前，听候吩咐。“问问路！”“喳！”那人滚鞍下马，一手执缰，一手抓住一个中年汉子问道：“来、来，老兄，打听一个地名，无宝街在哪里？““啊！你说啥？”原来那武官是曾国藩的小同乡，湖南话中湘乡话最难懂，加以武夫性急，说得很快，便越发不知他说些什么了。还好，那武官倒有自知之明，一字一句地答道：“元宝街。”说着还双手上捧，作手势示意元宝。“喔、喔、喔，你老人家是说元宝街！”那人姓卜，是钱塘县“礼房”的书办，不作回答，却反问：“请问，你们是从哪里来的？江宁？”“不错。”“这样说，到元宝街是去看胡大先生？”“胡大先生？”那人一愣，旋即想到：“不错，不错，胡大先生就是胡雪岩胡大人。”

The day after her return, a special envoy arrived from Jiangning. He wore red and was covered in the dust of travel, but on his hat, fringed all over with red silk, rose a red rank button, with a peacock feather trailing behind. Four attendants followed, each with an official button of his own. They had come by road on five tall horses, raising clouds of dust to the jingle of bridle bells, so imposing a procession that passers-by turned to stare. Just inside Wulin Gate, the envoy raised his hand and all five reined in. One officer, wearing a dark blue button, rode forward for orders. “Ask the way.” “Yes, sir!” He slipped from the saddle and, holding the reins in one hand, caught a middle-aged man with the other. “Here, my friend, a place I want to ask about: where's Wubao Street?” “What? What did you say?” The officer came from Zeng Guofan's own home district. Of all Hunan speech, the Xiangxiang dialect was the hardest to understand; delivered rapidly by an impatient soldier, it became even more incomprehensible. Fortunately, he recognized the problem. “Yuan-bao Street,” he repeated, syllable by syllable, cupping both hands to suggest a silver ingot. “Ah, Yuanbao Street! That's what you mean, sir.” The man, surnamed Bu, was a clerk in the rites section of the Qiantang county office. Instead of answering, he asked, “May I ask where you have come from? Jiangning?” “Yes.” “Then you are going to Yuanbao Street to see Mr Hu?” “Mr Hu?” The officer hesitated, then understood. “Yes, yes. Mr Hu is His Honour Hu Xueyan.”

卜书办点头，趋前一步，手指着低声问道：“马上那位红顶子的人，是什么人？”那武官有些不耐烦了，天下人走天下路，问路应是常事，知道而热心的，详细指点，知道而懒得回答的，说一声“不清楚”，真的不知道而又热心的，会表示歉意，请对方另行打听，不知道而又懒得回答的，只字不答，掉臂而去。象这样问路而反为别人所问，类似盘查，却还是第一次遇见。卜书办看那武官的脸色，急忙提出解释：“你老人家不要嫌我罗嗦，实在是马上那位大人一品武官，我不敢怠慢，晓得了身分，好禀报本县大老爷，有啥差遣，不会误事。”

Clerk Bu nodded, stepped closer, and pointed discreetly. “Who is the gentleman on horseback with the red button?” The officer was growing impatient. The roads were open to everyone, and asking directions was an ordinary matter. Someone who knew the way and felt helpful could explain it; someone who knew but could not be bothered could say he wasn't sure. A helpful person who did not know could apologize and suggest asking someone else, while an unhelpful one could simply turn and walk away. But to ask directions and be questioned in return, almost as though under investigation, was a new experience. Seeing his expression, Bu hastened to explain. “Please don't think me tiresome, sir. The gentleman on that horse is a first-rank military officer. I dare not neglect him. If I know who he is, I can report to our magistrate, and any service he requires can be attended to promptly.”

原来是这样一番好意！那武官倒觉得过意不去，但却不知如何回答——那专差本名高老三，投效湘军时，招募委员替他改名“乐山”来谐音，“仁者乐山”而又行三，因而又送他一个别号叫“仁叔”。这高乐山原隶刘松山帐下，左宗棠西征，曾国藩特拨刘松山一营隶属于左，时人称为“赠嫁”。刘松山在西征时，战功彪炳，左宗棠大为得力。左曾不和，在才气纵横的左宗棠眼中，曾国藩无一事可使他佩服，唯独对“赠嫁”刘松山，心悦诚服，感激不已。因为如此，左宗棠对刘松山，亦总是另眼看待。这高乐山原是刘松山的马弁，为人诚朴，有一次左宗棠去视察，宿于刘营，刘松山派高乐山去伺候，彻夜巡更，至晓不眠，为左宗棠所赏识，跟刘松山要了去，置诸左右，每有“保案”，在“密保”中总有高乐山的名字，现在的职衔是“记名总兵加提督衔”，在“绿营”中已是“官居极品”，但实际的职司，仍是所谓“材官”，仅奔走之役，在左宗棠的部属中，他的身份犹如宫中的“御前侍卫”。但一品武官不过是个“高等马弁”，这话说出去，贬损了高乐山的红顶子，所以那蓝顶子的武官含含糊糊地答说：“是左大人特为派来看胡大先生的。”

So that was his kindly intention! The officer felt embarrassed, but did not know how to answer. The envoy had originally been called Gao Laosan, Third Son Gao. When he joined the Hunan Army, the recruiting official had given him the more elegant name Leshan, playing on the sound of his old name and the saying that the benevolent delight in mountains. Because he was third-born, he also received the courtesy name Renshu. Gao had originally served under Liu Songshan. When Zuo began his western campaign, Zeng assigned Liu and his battalion to him, an arrangement people jokingly called a dowry. Liu's brilliant service proved invaluable. Zuo and Zeng did not get on; in the gifted Zuo's eyes, Zeng had otherwise done nothing worthy of admiration. For this gift of Liu, however, he was sincerely grateful, and he always treated Liu with special regard. Gao had been Liu's mounted orderly. Honest and sincere, he was once assigned to attend Zuo during an inspection visit. He patrolled through the night without sleeping, so impressing Zuo that he asked Liu to transfer him to his own staff. Whenever recommendations for promotion were submitted, Gao's name appeared among the confidential ones. He was now listed for appointment as a brigade commander, with the honorary rank of provincial military commander: the summit of advancement in the Green Standard Army. In practice, he remained a military attendant, carrying out errands, with a position among Zuo's staff rather like that of a bodyguard attending the emperor. To say that this first-rank officer was merely a superior sort of mounted orderly would diminish his red button. His blue-buttoned subordinate therefore answered vaguely, “Lord Zuo has sent him specially to see Mr Hu.”

“我就猜到，”卜书办又拍手、又翘拇指，“一定是左大人派来的。好、好、好，元宝街远得很，一南一北，等我来领路。你请等一等，等我去租匹马来。”武林门是杭州往北进出的要道，运河起点的拱宸桥就在武林门外，所以城门口有车有轿有骡马，雇用租赁，均无不可。卜书办租赁了一匹“菊花青”，洋洋得意地在前领路。那匹“菊花青”是旗营中淘汰下来的老马，驯顺倒很驯顺，但脚程极慢——马通灵性，为人雇乘太久，出发时知道负重任远，一步懒似一步，因为走得越快越吃亏，及至回程，纵不说如渴骥奔泉，但远非去路可比，昂首扬鬃，急于回槽。那匹菊花青，正是这样一个马中的“老油条”。当书办的，十之八九是“老油条”，这一下“老油条”遇着“老油条”，彼此得其所哉。卜书办款款徐行，后随五名武官，亦步亦趋，倒象是他的跟马。杭州的文武官员，品级最高的是“将军”，其次是巡抚，本身虽都是红顶子，但出行的随从，从无戴红顶子的。因此，卜书办满脸飞金，得意之状，难描难画，尤其是一路上遇着熟人，在马上一会儿抱拳扬臂，一会儿弯腰点头，同时一定要高声加一句，“我带他们去看胡大先生。”有几次得意忘形，几乎掉下马来，急急扳住马鞍上的“判官头”，才能转危为安。这样丑态百出，惹得路人笑逐颜开，而高乐山的脸色却越来越难看了。

“I guessed as much!” Bu clapped his hands and raised his thumb. “He must have been sent by Lord Zuo. Excellent! Yuanbao Street is a long way off, at the other end of the city. I'll lead you there. Just wait while I hire a horse.” Wulin Gate was Hangzhou's main northern entrance, and Gongchen Bridge, at the beginning of the canal, stood outside it. Carriages, sedan chairs, mules, and horses were all available for hire at the gate. Bu rented a dapple-grey and proudly led the way. It was an old horse discarded by the Banner garrison: docile enough, but painfully slow. Horses had understanding too. Hired out for so long, this one knew that setting out meant a burden and a distant destination; every step was lazier than the last, since the faster it walked, the worse its bargain. On the return journey, it might not quite rush like a thirsty steed toward a spring, but its pace was transformed, its head and mane high as it hurried back to its manger. It was an old hand among horses. Nine county clerks out of ten were old hands too. Here, one old hand had met another, and they suited each other perfectly. Bu proceeded at a leisurely pace, with five officers following step for step as though they were his mounted attendants. Hangzhou's highest-ranking official was the garrison general, followed by the governor. Though both wore red buttons, none of their attendants did. Bu glowed with pride, especially when he met acquaintances. He would raise clasped hands or bend forward in greeting, always adding loudly, “I'm taking them to see Mr Hu!” Several times he nearly fell off in his excitement, saving himself only by clutching the saddle pommel. His antics made the passers-by smile, while Gao's expression grew darker and darker.

快到元宝街时，卜书办在转角之时，向前扬一扬手，示意暂停，自己却双腿夹一夹马腹，催快往前，直到胡府大门前勒住了马。“老卜，”胡家门前的下人中，有一个认得他，“你来作啥？”“我来报信，两江总督左大人，派了红顶子的武官来看胡大先生，一进城门，是我领路来的。”“在哪里？”“在后面。”那人抬眼一看，果然有五匹马在后面，红蓝顶子在明亮的秋阳中看得很清楚。这一来，胡家门前的十几个人都紧张了。原来左宗棠派红顶子的戈什哈传令是常事，但当初是陕甘总督，公私事务派专差只到上海转运局。直接派到胡家却是头一回，少见自然多怪，顿时便有机灵的，不看热闹，抢先报到上房。

Near Yuanbao Street, Bu raised a hand at a corner to signal a halt, then squeezed his horse forward and rode ahead to the Hu residence. “Old Bu, what brings you here?” asked a servant who recognized him. “I've brought news. Lord Zuo, Governor-General of Liangjiang, has sent a red-buttoned officer to see Mr Hu. I met him at the city gate and guided him here.” “Where is he?” “Just behind.” The servant looked up. Sure enough, five horses were approaching, the red and blue buttons clear in the bright autumn sunlight. All dozen or so men at the entrance grew nervous. Zuo often sent red-buttoned guards with orders, but when he had been Governor-General of Shaanxi and Gansu, his messengers on public or private business had gone only to the transshipment bureau in Shanghai. This was the first to come directly to the Hu house. The unfamiliar caused alarm, and the quick-witted servants hurried to report instead of lingering to watch.

螺蛳太太一听吓一跳。原来胡家为了红顶子，花了好大的气力，胡雪岩本身是道员加按察使衔，三品顶戴蓝顶子，倘或胡雪岩肯做官，放一任实缺的道员，左宗棠保他加布政使的衔，是一定办得到的事，无奈胡雪岩只能做一个“官商”，如果真的“商而优则官，”必须“弃商从官”，不但“做此官，行此礼，胡，雪岩受不了那种拘束，而且也决不会是一个出色的官。这一点不但他本人有自知之明，凡是爱护他的，亦莫不认为胡雪岩要是真的去做官，便是舍长就短，最为不智。因为如此，要摆官派，只有拿钱来做官，本身捐官有限制，到三品便是“官居极品”，但父母的荣衔，却是花钱可以买体面的，十余年来每逢水旱灾荒，胡雪岩总是用胡老太大的名义，捐银、捐米、捐棉衣、捐药材，好不容易才得了个“一品夫人”的封典，胡雪岩“子以母贵”也能戴红顶子了。红顶子是如此珍贵，在螺蛳太太的记忆中，红顶子的文武大员登门拜访，没有几次，每一次都是事先得到信息，如何迎接、如何款待、如何打发从人，都要好几天筹划，临时郑重将事。象这样突然来了个红顶子的武官，自然要吓一跳，紧张得有些不知所措了。

Madam Luosi was startled. The Hu family had gone to great lengths for a red button. Hu himself held circuit-intendant rank with the honorary title of provincial judicial commissioner, entitling him to a third-rank blue button. Had he accepted an actual circuit post, Zuo could certainly have obtained the additional title of provincial treasurer for him. But Hu could only be an official merchant. To turn business success into an official career, he would have had to give up trade. Every office imposed its own rules of conduct; he could not bear such restrictions, nor would he have made an outstanding official. He knew this himself, and all who cared for him agreed that taking office would foolishly abandon his strengths for his weaknesses. If he wanted official splendour, therefore, he had to purchase it. Purchased rank for himself stopped at the third grade, but money could buy honorary distinctions for his parents. For more than ten years, whenever flood, drought, or famine struck, he had donated silver, rice, padded clothing, and medicines in his mother's name, finally obtaining for her the title of a first-rank lady. By sharing in his mother's distinction, he too could wear a red button. In Madam Luosi's memory, visits from red-buttoned civil or military dignitaries had been rare, always announced in advance and requiring days of careful planning for the reception, hospitality, and treatment of their attendants. Such an officer's sudden arrival naturally left her alarmed and at a loss.

但胡雪岩却是司空见惯的，高乐山又是熟人，不妨从容以礼款接。当下先交代了螺蛳太太一番，换了官服到花厅相见。一个称“雪翁”，一个称“高军门”，平礼相见，又到走廊上向高乐山的从人，请教了姓乐，寒暄了一阵，另外派人接待，然后说道：“请换便衣吧！”话刚说完，已有一名听差，捧着衣包，进屋伺候——官场酬酢，公服相见是礼，便衣欢叙是情，但总是客人忖度与主人的交情，预料有此需要，自己命跟班随带衣包，象这样由主人供应便衣的情形，高乐山不但是第一次经验，而且也是闻所未闻。不过，想到胡雪岩以豪阔出名，那么类此举动，自亦无足为奇。当下说道：“雪翁亦请进去换衣服吧！”“是，是，换了衣服细谈。”等胡雪岩换了衣服出来，只见高乐山已穿上簇新的一身铁灰的结夹抱、上套珊瑚扣的贡缎马褂，头上一顶红结子的青缎小帽，而且刚洗了脸，显得容光焕发，神采奕奕。“衣服倒还合身？”“多谢，多谢。比我自己叫裁缝来现制还要好，我也不客气了，雪翁，多谢，多谢！”说着高乐山又连连拱手。

Hu was accustomed to such visitors, however, and Gao was an old acquaintance. He could receive him calmly and courteously. After giving Madam Luosi a few instructions, he changed into official dress and met Gao in the garden drawing room. They greeted each other as equals, one addressing the other as Mr Xueyan, the other replying with General Gao. Hu then went into the corridor, asked Gao's attendants their names, exchanged a few courtesies, and assigned someone to look after them. “Please change into something informal,” he said. At once, a servant entered carrying a bundle of clothes to help Gao dress. In official society, meeting in ceremonial robes fulfilled the requirements of courtesy, while conversation in informal clothes expressed friendship. Normally, though, a guest judged the closeness of the relationship for himself and had his attendant bring a change of clothes. Gao had never experienced, or even heard of, a host supplying them. Still, Hu was famous for his lavishness, so perhaps it was not surprising. “Please go and change yourself too, Mr Xueyan.” “Yes, yes. Then we can talk at leisure.” When Hu returned, Gao was wearing a brand-new iron-grey lined silk robe, with a tribute-satin riding jacket fastened by coral buttons and a dark satin cap topped with a red knot. He had also washed his face and looked refreshed and vigorous. “Do the clothes fit reasonably well?” “Thank you, thank you! Better than anything I could have had a tailor make for me. I won't stand on ceremony. Many thanks!” Gao repeatedly clasped his hands in gratitude.

“左大人精神还好吧？”听这一说，高乐山的笑容慢慢收敛，“差得多了。”他说：“眼力大不如前，毛病不轻。”“请医生看了没有呢？”“请了。”高乐山答说：“看也白看！医生要他不看公事，不看书，闭上眼睛静养。雪翁，你想他老人家办得到吗？”“那么，到底是什么病呢？”“医生也说不上来。左眼上了翳，右面的一只迎风流泪。”“会不会失明？”“难说。”“我荐一个医生。”胡雪岩说：“跟了高军门一起去。”“是。”高乐山这时才将左宗棠的信拿了出来。信上很简单，只说越南军情紧急，奉旨南北洋的防务均须上紧筹划，并须派兵援越，因而请胡雪岩抽工夫到江宁一晤，至于其他细节，可以面问高乐山。

“Is Lord Zuo still in good spirits?” Gao's smile slowly faded. “He's much worse. His eyesight has deteriorated badly. It's no small trouble.” “Has a doctor seen him?” “Yes, but to no purpose. The doctor wants him to stop reading official papers and books, close his eyes, and rest. Can you imagine him doing that?” “What exactly is the disease?” “The doctor can't say. A film has formed over his left eye, and his right waters whenever the wind touches it.” “Could he go blind?” “It's hard to say.” “I'll recommend a doctor,” Hu said, “and have him go back with you.” “Yes.” Only now did Gao produce Zuo's letter. It was brief. The military situation in Vietnam was urgent; by imperial order, preparations for both the northern and southern maritime defences had to be hastened, and troops were to be sent to assist Vietnam. Zuo therefore asked Hu to find time for a meeting in Jiangning. Any further details could be obtained from Gao.

胡雪岩心想，这少不得又是筹械筹饷，不在其位，不谋其政，自己并未受两江总督衙门的任何委任，倘需效劳，纯粹是私人关系，这一层不妨先向高乐山说明白。“高军门晓得的，左大人说啥就是啥，我只有‘遵办’二字。不过，江宁不是陕甘，恐怕有吃力不讨好的地方。”“是的。”高乐山答道：“左大人亦说了，江宁有江宁的人，胡某替我办事，完全是交情，论到公事，转运局是西征的转运局，我只有跟他商量，不能下札子。这就是要请雪翁当面去谈的缘故。”“喔，不晓得要谈点啥？”胡雪岩问：“是钱，是械？”“是枪械。”“嗯，嗯。”胡雪岩稍稍放了些心，“不谈钱，事情总还好办。”“雪翁预备哪天动身？”“这还要跟内人商量起来看。”胡雪岩率直回答，他所说的“内人”，自然是指螺蛳太太，接下来又问：“左大人预备派哪位到广西？”“是王大人。”“王大人？”胡雪岩一时想不起来，左宗棠手下有哪个姓王的大将。“是，王阆帅。”“幄，是他。”

Hu supposed that, as usual, it would mean finding arms and military funds. A man should not concern himself with the duties of an office he did not hold. He had received no appointment from the Governor-General of Liangjiang's administration; any service he rendered would be purely a matter of personal friendship. He might as well make that clear to Gao first. “You know that whatever Lord Zuo says, I can only obey. But Jiangning is not Shaanxi and Gansu. I fear I may find myself working hard and getting no thanks.” “Yes. Lord Zuo said that Jiangning has its own people, and that anything Mr Hu does for him is done entirely out of friendship. As far as official business is concerned, your transshipment bureau serves the western expedition. He can only consult you; he cannot issue a written order. That is why he wants you to come and discuss matters in person.” “And what does he want to discuss? Money or weapons?” “Weapons.” “Ah.” Hu felt a little easier. “As long as it isn't money, things are more manageable.” “When do you intend to leave?” “I must discuss that with my wife,” Hu replied frankly, meaning, of course, Madam Luosi. “Who is Lord Zuo sending to Guangxi?” “Mr Wang.” “Mr Wang?” Hu could not immediately recall a senior commander of that surname on Zuo's staff. “Yes. Commander Wang Langqing.” “Oh, him.”

原来高乐山指的是王德榜，他踉高乐山一样，有个很雅致的别号叫阆青，是湖南永州府江华县人，这个偏僻小县，从古以来也没有出过什么出色的人物，但王德傍在湘军中却是别具一格，颇有可称的宿将。此人在咸丰初年，毁家练乡团，保卫家乡颇有劳绩，后来援江西有功，早在威咸七年，便叙文职“州同”，改隶左宗棠部下后，数建奇功，是有名的悍将，赐号“锐勇巴图鲁”，赏穿黄马褂，同治四年积功升至藩司，从左宗棠征新疆，功劳不在刘松山叔侄之下，但始终不得意，藩司虚衔领了六、八年，始终不能补实缺。原来王德榜是个老粗，当他升藩司奉召入觐时，语言粗鄙，加以满口乡音，两宫太后根本不知道他说些什么，因而名为藩司，当的却是总兵的职司。光绪元年丁忧回籍，六年再赴新疆，不久左宗棠晋京人军机，以大学士管兵部，受醇王之托，整顿旗营，特地保荐王德榜教练火器、健锐两营，他的部下兴修畿输水利，挑泥浚河，做的是苦工而毫无怨言，因而亦颇得醇王赏识。左宗棠当然深知他的长处，但他的短处实在也不少，只能为将，不能做官。这回彭玉麟向左宗棠求援，他想起王德榜，认为可以尽其所长，因而奏请赴援两广，归彭玉麟节制，并答应接济军械，找胡雪岩去，便是商量这件事。

Gao meant Wang Debang, who, like Gao himself, possessed an elegant courtesy name: Langqing. He came from Jianghua County in Yongzhou Prefecture, Hunan, a remote little county that had never produced any particularly outstanding figure. Within the Hunan Army, however, Wang was a distinctive and respected veteran. Early in the Xianfeng reign, he had devoted his family fortune to organizing a local militia and rendered valuable service in defending his home district. Later, he distinguished himself in Jiangxi and, as early as the seventh year of Xianfeng, received the civil rank of assistant department magistrate. After joining Zuo, he repeatedly won remarkable victories and became a famous fighting commander, receiving the title “Bold and Brave Baturu” and permission to wear the yellow riding jacket. In the fourth year of Tongzhi, his accumulated service brought him the rank of provincial treasurer. He followed Zuo into Xinjiang and performed services no less distinguished than those of Liu Songshan and his nephew, yet advancement never satisfied him. He held the honorary treasurer's rank for six or eight years without obtaining an actual post. Wang was an uncouth soldier. When summoned to court after his promotion, he spoke so crudely and with so strong a local accent that the two empress dowagers could not understand him. Thus, while nominally a provincial treasurer, he performed the duties of a brigade commander. In the first year of Guangxu, he returned home for mourning; in the sixth, he went back to Xinjiang. Soon afterwards, Zuo entered the Grand Council in Beijing and, as a grand secretary, took charge of the Ministry of War. At Prince Chun's request, he reorganized the Banner troops and specially recommended Wang to train the Firearms and Stalwart camps. Wang's men also worked on waterways near the capital, carrying earth and dredging rivers without complaint, which earned Prince Chun's approval. Zuo understood Wang's strengths well, but his weaknesses were considerable: he could command soldiers, not administer a civil office. When Peng Yulin asked for assistance, Zuo thought of him as the right man for the task. He requested imperial approval to send Wang to Liangguang under Peng's command and promised to supply weapons. This was what he wished to discuss with Hu.

了解了经过情形，胡雪岩心里有数了，“高军门，”他说，“你在这里玩两天，我跟内人商量好了，或许可以一起走。”“如果雪翁一起走，我当然要等，不然，我就先回去复命了。左大人的性子，你知道的。”“你想先回去复命亦好。哪天动身。”“明天。”当下以盛筵款待，当然不用胡雪岩亲自相陪，宴罢连从人送到客房歇宿，招呼得非常周到。第二天要动身了，自然先要请胡雪岩见一面，问问有什么话交代。

Now that Hu understood the circumstances, he knew where matters stood. “Stay here for a couple of days, General Gao. Once I've discussed it with my wife, perhaps we can leave together.” “If you are coming with me, I will certainly wait. Otherwise, I should return first to report. You know Lord Zuo's temperament.” “Then you may as well go back first. When would you leave?” “Tomorrow.” A sumptuous banquet was provided, though Hu did not need to attend personally. Afterwards, Gao and his men were shown to guest rooms and looked after with the greatest care. Before leaving the next day, Gao naturally asked to see Hu for any final instructions.

传话进去，所得到的答复是，胡雪岩中午请他吃饭，有带给左宗棠的书信面交。到了午间，请到花园里，又是一桌盛筵，连他的从人一起都请，厅上已摆好五份礼物，一身袍褂，两匹机纺，一大盒胡庆余堂所产的家用良药，另外是五十两银子一个的“官宝”两个。额外送高乐山的一个打簧金表，一支牙柄的转轮手枪。“本来想备船送你们回去，只怕脚程太慢，说不得只好辛苦各位老哥，仍旧骑马回去了。”“雪翁这样犒赏，实在太过意不去了。”高乐山连连搓手，真有却之不恭，受之有愧之概。“小意思、小意思！请宽饮一杯。”高乐山不肯多喝，他那四个部下，从未经过这种场面，更觉局促不安，每人闷倒头扒了三碗饭，站起身来向胡雪岩打千道谢兼辞行。由于红顶子的关系，胡雪岩自然开中门送客，大门照墙下一并排五匹马，仍是原来的坐骑，不过鞍辔全新，连马鞭子都是新的。胡雪岩自己有一副“导子”，两匹跟马将高乐山一行，送出武林门外，一路上惹得路人指指点点，都知道是“胡大先生家的客人”。

The answer was that Hu would invite him to lunch and give him a letter for Zuo in person. At noon, they were taken into the garden for another lavish meal, this time including Gao's attendants. Five sets of gifts had been laid out in the hall. Each contained a robe and riding jacket, two bolts of machine-woven cloth, a large box of household medicines made at Hu Qingyu Hall, and two official silver ingots of fifty taels each. Gao received, in addition, a gold repeater watch and an ivory-handled revolver. “I meant to send you home by boat, but I fear it would be too slow. I'm afraid I must ask you gentlemen to endure another journey on horseback.” Gao rubbed his hands repeatedly, feeling that refusal would be discourteous and acceptance undeserved. “Such generous rewards are really too much.” “A trifle, a trifle! Do have another cup.” Gao would not drink much. His four subordinates, unused to such hospitality, felt still more awkward. Each kept his head down and ate three bowls of rice, then rose, made the formal kneeling salutation to Hu, and thanked him as they took their leave. In honour of the red button, Hu naturally had the central gate opened to see them off. Five horses stood abreast by the screen wall outside, the same mounts as before but with new saddles, bridles, and even whips. Hu had his own ceremonial escort; two mounted attendants accompanied Gao's party out through Wulin Gate. People pointed along the way, knowing them to be Mr Hu's guests.

高乐山走后，胡雪岩与螺蛳太太商量行止。“第二批洋款也到期了，我想先到上海料理好了，再到江宁。”胡雪岩说，“好在王阆青也不过刚从京里动身，我晚一点到江宁也不至于误事。”“不好，既然左大人特为派差官来请，你就应该先到江宁，才是敬重的道理。至于上海这方面，有宓本常在那里，要付的洋款，叫他先到上海道那里去催一催，等你一到上海，款子齐了，当面交清，岂不是顺理成章的事？”“上海的市面，我也不大放心，想先去看看。”“那更用不着了，宓本常本事很大，一定调度得好好的。”螺蛳太太说：“你听我的话没有错，一定要先到江宁，后到上海，回来办喜事，日子算起来正好，如果先到上海，后到江宁，万一左大人有差使交派，误了喜期，就不好了。”

After Gao had left, Hu discussed his movements with Madam Luosi. “The second instalment of the foreign loan is also due. I thought I would go to Shanghai and settle that first, then proceed to Jiangning. Wang Langqing has only just left Beijing, so a slightly later arrival at Jiangning shouldn't delay anything.” “No. Since Lord Zuo has sent an officer specially to invite you, respect requires that you go to Jiangning first. Mi Benchang is in Shanghai. Let him press the circuit intendant for the foreign-loan payment in advance. By the time you arrive, the funds will be ready and you can make the payment in person. Isn't that the natural order?” “I'm uneasy about the Shanghai market too. I'd like to have a look first.” “There is even less need for that. Mi Benchang is extremely capable; he is certain to have everything properly arranged. Take my advice. Go to Jiangning first, then Shanghai, and come home for the wedding. The timing will be just right. If you go to Shanghai first and then Jiangning, Lord Zuo may give you some further task, and it would be a pity to miss the wedding date.”

在天津的李鸿章，经过深思熟虑，认为张佩纶才高志大，资格又好，决心要收他做个帮手。张佩纶的父亲在李鸿章的家乡安徽做过官，叙起来也算世交，便遣人专程将他接了来，在北洋衙门长谈了几次。原来李鸿章也有一番抱负，跟醇王秘密计议过，准备创办新式海军。他自己一手创立了淮军，深知陆军是无法整顿的了，外国的陆军，小兵亦读过书，看重懂书面的命令，中国的陆军，连营官都是目不识丁，怎么比得过人家？再说，陆军练好了，亦必须等到外敌踏上中华国上，才能发生保国卫民的作用，不如海军得以拒敌于境外。因此，李鸿章已悄悄着手修建旅顺港，在北洋办海军学堂。这番雄图壮志，非十年不足以见功，而且得在平定的局势之下，方能按部就班，寸寸积功。这就是李鸿章力主对法妥协的原因，忍一时之忿，图百年之计。张佩纶觉得谋国远谟，正应如此，因而也作了不少献议，彼此谈得非常投机。“老夫耄矣！足下才气纵横，前程远大，将来此席非老弟莫属。”这已隐然有传授衣钵之意。张佩纶想到曾国藩说过，“办大事以找替手为第一，”他当年遣散湘军，扶植淮军，便是找到了李鸿章作替手。想来，李鸿章以湘乡“门生长”自居，顾念遗训，找到他来作替手。这番盛意，关乎国家气运，当仁不让，倒不可辜负。

In Tianjin, after careful consideration, Li Hongzhang decided that Zhang Peilun, with his talent, ambition, and excellent qualifications, should be brought into his service. Zhang's father had held office in Li's home province of Anhui, giving them something of an inherited family connection. Li sent a special invitation and held several long conversations with him at the northern maritime administration. Li had an ambition of his own. In secret consultation with Prince Chun, he had been planning a modern navy. Having founded the Anhui Army himself, he knew that the army could no longer be put into proper order. In foreign armies, even ordinary soldiers had schooling and could read written orders; in China's army, even battalion commanders were illiterate. How could they compete? Besides, however well the army was trained, it could defend the country only after an enemy had set foot on Chinese soil. A navy could keep the enemy beyond the frontier. Li had therefore quietly begun developing the port of Lushun and establishing a naval school for the northern fleet. This great undertaking would require at least ten years and a peaceful situation in which to advance steadily, step by step. That was why he insisted on compromise with France: endure a moment's anger to secure the country's future for a century. Zhang agreed that this was the proper way to plan for the nation and offered many suggestions. They found themselves in complete accord. “I am an old man,” Li said. “You have extraordinary talent and a great future. In time, this seat must surely be yours.” There was already a hint of handing on his mantle. Zhang recalled Zeng Guofan's dictum that the first task in great undertakings was to find a successor. Zeng had disbanded the Hunan Army and fostered the Anhui Army because he had found Li. Li, who regarded himself as Zeng's foremost disciple, must now be following that teaching by choosing Zhang. This honour concerned the nation's destiny; Zhang should accept the responsibility and not disappoint him.

由于有了这样的默契，张佩纶在暗中亦已转为主和派。同时有人为李鸿章设计，用借刀杀人的手法，拆清流的台——将清流中响当当的人物，调出京去，赋以军务重任，书生都是纸上谈兵，一亲营伍，每每偾事，便可借此收拾清流，而平时好发议论的人，见此光景，必生戒心，亦是箝制舆论的妙计。李鸿章认为是借刀杀人，还是登坛拜将，视人而异，象张佩纶便属于后者，决定设法保他督办左宗棠所创办、沈葆祯所扩大的福建船政局，作为他将来帮办北洋海军的张本。此外就不妨借刀杀人了。

With this unspoken understanding, Zhang privately joined the peace party. Meanwhile, someone proposed to Li a way of using another's blade to strike at the Pure Stream group: send its most prominent men out of Beijing and give them important military responsibilities. Scholars who had only discussed war on paper would often fail when confronted with real troops. Their failures could then be used to break the group's power, while other habitual critics would learn caution. It was an excellent means of silencing opinion. Li thought that whether such an appointment amounted to destroying a man or raising him to high command depended on the man. Zhang belonged to the second category. Li decided to secure his appointment to direct the Fujian Navy Yard, founded by Zuo and expanded by Shen Baozhen, as preparation for his future service with the northern navy. The others could be dealt with by the borrowed blade.

但这是需要逐步布置，徐图实现的事，而眼前除了由张佩纶去压低主战的高调以外，最要紧的是，要让主战的实力派，知难而退，这实力派中，第一个便是左宗棠，得想法子多方掣肘，叫他支援彭玉麟的计划，步步荆棘，怎么样也走不通，这就是李鸿章特召邵友濂北上，要商量的事。“左湘阴无非靠胡雪岩替他出力。上次赈灾派各省协济，两江派二十万银子，江宁藩库，一空如洗，他到江海关来惜，我说要跟赫德商量。湘阴知难而退，结果是向胡雪岩借了二十万银子。湘阴如果没有胡雪岩，可说一筹莫展。”“胡雪岩这个人，确是很讨厌。”李鸿章说：“洋人还是很相信他，以致于我这里好些跟洋人的交涉，亦受他的影响。”“既然如此，有一个办法，叫洋人不再相信他。”邵友濂说：“至少不如过去那样相信他，”“不错，这个想法是对的，不过做起来不大容易，要好好筹划一下。”“眼前就有一个机会……”这个机会便是胡雪岩为左宗棠经手的最后一笔借款，到了第二期还本的时候了！

That plan would require gradual preparation and patient execution. For the moment, besides having Zhang quiet the clamour for war, the most urgent task was to make the advocates with real military power retreat before the difficulties. Foremost among them was Zuo. He must be obstructed from every direction until his plan to support Peng Yulin met barriers at every step and could not proceed. This was why Li specially summoned Shao Youlian north for consultation. “Zuo of Xiangyin relies entirely on Hu Xueyan's efforts,” Shao said. “When the provinces were last asked to contribute to disaster relief, Liangjiang was assigned two hundred thousand taels. The Jiangning treasury was empty. Zuo asked to borrow from the Jianghaiguan Customs, and I said I would have to consult Hart. He retreated before the difficulty and ended by borrowing the money from Hu. Without Hu, he would be helpless.” “Hu is a thoroughly troublesome man,” Li said. “The foreigners still trust him, and that affects many of my dealings with them.” “Then there is a way to make them stop trusting him,” Shao replied. “Or at least trust him less than before.” “Yes, that is the right idea. But it will not be easy. We must plan carefully.” “There is an opportunity at this very moment.” The second repayment of principal was now falling due on the final loan that Hu had arranged for Zuo.

英语一综合学习提示
阅读：首段战争背景与上海市面由因果连接；“表面奉命督师，实际希望议和”属于解释目的的题材。李迟行和拖延有多种动因，不能把每一段简单归结为胆怯。张支持海军建设与随后改变立场由中间的长谈联系起来。
完形：although、yet、besides、therefore、while 组织让步、补充、因果与同时发生的对照。区分主观判断 he supposed / he knew / she feared 与已经发生的 appointed / arrived / produced。

写作：可积累 abandon one's strengths for one's weaknesses、advance steadily, step by step、take the long view、win someone over、remove obstacles to negotiation 等表达。小说对战争与派系的判断属于人物和叙述，不直接当作论证现代议题的事实证据。
