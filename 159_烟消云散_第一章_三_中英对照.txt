第二天中午，古应春带来一个极好的消息，各省协助的“西饷”，已快收齐了，最早的一笔，在十月初便已汇到。“有这样的事！”胡雪岩大为困惑，“为啥邵筱村同我说一文钱都没有收到？你的消息哪里来的？”“我有个同乡晚辈，早年我照应过他，他现在是上海道衙门电报房的领班。“那就不错了！”胡雪岩既喜且怒，“邵筱村不晓得在打什么鬼主意？我要好好问他一问。““小爷叔不必如此。我想最好的办法是请左大人打个电报给邵筱村。”原来古应春从他同乡晚辈中，另获有很机密的消息，说是李鸿章正在设法打击左宗棠，因而想到，邵友濂对胡雪岩有意留难，是别有用心。但这个消息，未经证实，告诉了胡雪岩，反而会生出是非，只有用左宗棠出面，措词严厉些，带着警告的意味，让邵友濂心生顾忌，在期限之前拨出这笔代收的款子，了却胡雪岩的责任，最为上策。

At noon the next day, Gu Yingchun brought excellent news: almost all the provincial contributions to the western military funds had been collected. The earliest payment had arrived at the beginning of the tenth month. “Can that be true?” Hu Xueyan was thoroughly puzzled. “Why did Shao Xiaocun tell me he had not received a single cash? Where did you hear this?” “From a younger fellow townsman whom I helped years ago. He is now the head clerk in the telegraph room at the Shanghai circuit office.” “Then there can be no mistake!” Hu was delighted and angry at once. “What game is Shao Xiaocun playing? I shall have a word with him!” “There is no need for that, Little Uncle. I think the best course is to ask Lord Zuo to send him a telegram.” Gu had also obtained highly confidential information from his fellow townsman: Li Hongzhang was looking for ways to strike at Zuo Zongtang. This suggested that Shao Youlian had an ulterior motive for deliberately obstructing Hu. But the report had not been confirmed, and telling Hu might only cause trouble. The best course was to have Zuo intervene with a sternly worded warning, making Shao think twice and release the money he had collected before the deadline, thereby discharging Hu’s responsibility.

但胡雪岩又何从去了解他的用心，他仍旧是抱着在左宗棠面前要保持面子的用心。在江宁时，左宗棠原曾问过他，有什么事要他出面，意思就是指上海道代收“西饷”这件事，当时如说请他写封信催一催邵友濂，是很正常的回答，左宗棠不会想到别的地方去。已经回答没有什么事要他费心，而结果仍旧要他出面，这等于作了垫不出五十万银子的表示是一样的。因此，他这样答说：“不必劳动他老人家了，既然各省都快到齐了，我去催他。”

Hu, however, had no way of knowing what lay behind Gu’s advice. His concern was still to save face before Zuo. In Jiangning, Zuo had asked whether there was anything in which he should intervene, meaning the western military contributions collected through the Shanghai circuit office. Had Hu then asked him to write and hurry Shao along, it would have been a perfectly natural answer; Zuo would have thought nothing of it. But Hu had already said that there was nothing he needed to trouble him about. To ask for his intervention now would amount to admitting that he could not advance five hundred thousand taels. He therefore replied, “There is no need to trouble His Excellency. Since almost all the provincial payments have arrived, I shall go and press Shao myself.”

胡雪岩一向沉得住气，这一次因为事多心烦，竟失去了耐性，气冲冲地去看邵友濂，门上回答：“邵大人视察制造局去了。”吃了个闭门羹，心中越发不快，回到制造局命文案师爷写信给邵友濂，措词很不客气，有点打官腔的味道，而且暗示，邵友濂如果不能如期付款，只好请左宗棠自己来料理了。这封信送到江海关，立即转送邵友濂公馆，他看了自然有些紧张，因为“不怕官，只怕管”，自太平军被平息后，督抚权柄之重，为清朝开国以来所未有，左宗棠是现任的两江总督，如果指名严参，再有理也无法申诉，而况实际上确也收到了好几省的“西饷”，靳而不予，也是件说不过去的事。因此，他很不情愿地作了个决定，将已收到的“西饷”开单送交转运局，为数约四十万两，胡雪岩只需垫十万银子，便可保住他对洋人的信用。但就在写好复信，正待发出之际，来了一个人，使得他的决定整个儿被推翻。

Hu had always been able to keep his temper, but this time the strain of too many affairs made him lose patience. He went angrily to see Shao, only to be told at the door, “His Excellency has gone to inspect the Manufacturing Bureau.” This fruitless visit made him still more displeased. Returning to the Manufacturing Bureau, he ordered his correspondence secretary to write to Shao. The letter was decidedly discourteous, with something of an official’s peremptory tone; it also hinted that, if Shao could not pay on time, Hu would have to ask Zuo himself to deal with the matter. Delivered to Jianghaiguan Customs, it was immediately forwarded to Shao’s residence. Naturally, it made him uneasy. As the saying went, “It is not an official you need fear, but the man who has authority over you.” Since the suppression of the Taiping armies, provincial governors and governors-general had acquired powers unequalled since the founding of the Qing dynasty. Zuo was the incumbent Governor-General of Liangjiang. If he submitted a severe impeachment naming Shao, no argument, however sound, could win Shao a hearing. Besides, contributions from several provinces had indeed arrived; withholding them was indefensible. Very reluctantly, he decided to draw up a statement and send the money already received to the Transport Bureau. It came to about four hundred thousand taels. Hu would then need to advance only one hundred thousand to preserve his credit with the foreigners. But just as Shao finished his reply and was about to send it, a visitor arrived who overturned his decision completely.

这个人便是盛宣怀，由于筹办电报局大功告成，不但成了李鸿章面前有数的红人，而且亦马结上了醇亲王的关系。此番是衔李鸿章之命，到上海跟邵友濂来商量，如何“救人”？“救火”是盛宣怀形容挽救眼前局势的一个譬喻，这也是李鸿章的说法，他认为由越南危局引起的中法冲突，他有转危为安的办法，但主战派的行动，却如“纵火”，清流的高调，则是火上浇油。但如火势已灭，虽有助燃的油料，终无所用。意思就是打消了主战的行动，清流便不足畏。那么，谁是“纵火”者呢？在李鸿章看，第一个就是左宗棠，第二个是彭玉麟。至于西南方面如云贵总督岑毓英等，自有办法可以控制，即使是彭玉麟，倘无左宗棠的支持，亦可设法让他知难而退。换句话说，擒贼擒玉，只要将左宗棠压制住，李鸿章就能掌握到整个局势，与法国交涉化干戈为玉帛。

The visitor was Sheng Xuanhuai. Having successfully established the telegraph bureau, he had become one of Li Hongzhang’s leading favourites and had also formed a connection with Prince Chun. Li had sent him to Shanghai to discuss with Shao how to “put out the fire.” This was Sheng’s metaphor for rescuing the present situation, and Li used it too. Li believed he had a way to turn the danger of the Sino-French conflict over Vietnam into a peaceful settlement. The actions of the war party, however, were like setting fires, while the lofty rhetoric of the Pure Stream group poured oil on the flames. Once the fire was extinguished, even oil would have nothing to feed. In other words, if the moves towards war could be stopped, the Pure Stream group would cease to be a threat. Who, then, were the arsonists? In Li’s eyes, Zuo came first and Peng Yulin second. As for men in the southwest, such as Cen Yuying, Governor-General of Yunnan and Guizhou, there were ways to keep them under control. Even Peng, deprived of Zuo’s support, could be made to recognise the difficulties and withdraw. Capture the ringleader first: once Zuo had been restrained, Li could control the whole situation and negotiate peace with France.

“筱村兄，你不要看什么‘主战自强’、‘大奋天威’、‘同仇敌忾’，这些慷慨激昂的论调，高唱人云，这不过是听得见的声音，其实，听不见的声音，才是真正有力量的声音，中堂如果不是有这些听不见的声音撑腰，他也犯不着跟湘阴作对一一湘阴老境颓唐，至多还有三、五年的富贵而已，何必容不得他？反过来说，如果容不得他，就一定有非去他不可的缘故在内。筱村兄，中堂的心事，你先要明白。“中堂是指李鸿章。盛宣怀的词令最妙，他将李鸿章对左宗棠的态度，说得忠厚平和，一片恕词。但在邵友濂听来，是非常明白的，李、左之间已成势不两立，非拼个你死我活不可了。

“Brother Xiaocun, do not be misled by all the cries of ‘War and self-strengthening,’ ‘Let the empire display its might,’ and ‘Unite against the common enemy.’ Those impassioned declarations ring to the skies, but they are merely the voices you can hear. The voices you cannot hear are the ones with real power. Without those silent voices behind him, His Excellency would have no reason to oppose Zuo of Xiangyin. Zuo is declining in his old age; at most, he has another three or five years of wealth and honours ahead of him. Why should His Excellency be unable to tolerate him? Conversely, if he cannot tolerate him, there must be a reason why Zuo has to go. Brother Xiaocun, you must first understand what is in His Excellency’s mind.” By His Excellency, he meant Li Hongzhang. Sheng was a consummate speaker. He portrayed Li’s attitude towards Zuo as generous and temperate, all forbearance and charity. What Shao heard, however, was perfectly clear: Li and Zuo had become irreconcilable enemies, and their struggle could end only with one or the other destroyed.

“是的。”邵友濂矍然警觉，“我明白。不过，我倒要请问，是哪些听不见的声音？”“第一是当今大权独揽的慈禧皇太后，她辛苦了大半辈子，前两年又生了一场死去活来的大病，你想，五十岁的老太太，有几个不盼望过几年清闲日子的，她哪里要打什么仗？”“既然大权独揽，她说个‘和’字，哪个敢不奉懿旨？”“苦就苦在她什么话都好说，就是这个字说不出口。为啥呢？洪杨勘定大乱，从古以来，垂帘的太后，没有她这样的武功，哪里好向廷臣示弱。再说，清流的论调，又是如此嚣张，只好表面上也唱唱高调，实际上全不是这么回事。”“我懂了，这是说不出的苦。”邵友濂又问：“第二个呢？”

“Yes,” Shao said, suddenly alert. “I understand. But may I ask whose voices these are that cannot be heard?” “First, Empress Dowager Cixi, who holds all power in her hands. She has laboured for more than half her life, and two years ago she suffered an illness that nearly killed her. Think of it: how many fifty-year-old ladies do not long for a few peaceful years? Why should she want a war?” “If all power is hers, she need only say ‘Peace.’ Who would dare disobey her command?” “That is precisely her difficulty. She can say anything but that one word. Why? She put down the great rebellion of Hong and Yang. No empress dowager who ruled from behind the curtain has ever had military achievements to equal hers. How can she show weakness before her ministers? Besides, the Pure Stream group is so clamorous that she has to echo its lofty sentiments in public. In reality, her wishes are quite different.” “I understand. It is a difficulty she cannot voice.” Shao asked, “And the second?”

“第二个是当政的恭王，他一向主张跟洋人打交道，以和为贵，如今上了年纪，更谈不上什么雄心壮志了。”“英法联军内犯，恭王主和，让亲贵骂他是‘汉奸’、难怪他不敢开口。可是，醇王一向主战，怎么也不作声呢？““这就是关键所在。如今的醇王，不是当年的醇王了，这几年洋人的坚甲利兵，”盛宣怀停下来笑一笑说：“说起来倒是受了湘阴的教，西征军事顺手，全靠枪炮厉害，这一点湘阴在京的时候，跟醇王谈得很详细。醇王现在完全赞成中堂的主张，‘师夷之长以制夷’，正在筹划一个辟旅顺为军港，大办海军的办法。醇王对这件事，热中得不得了，自然不愿‘小不忍而乱大谋’。”“嗯！嗯！有这三位，中堂足足可以择善固执。”

“The second is Prince Gong, who is in charge of affairs. He has always believed that, in dealings with foreigners, peace comes first. Now that he is older, he has even less appetite for grand ambitions.” “When the Anglo-French forces invaded, Prince Gong advocated peace and the imperial princes and grandees called him a traitor. No wonder he is afraid to speak. But Prince Chun has always favoured war. Why is he silent too?” “That is the crucial point. The Prince Chun of today is not the man he was. In recent years, the foreigners’ strong armour and powerful weapons…” Sheng paused and smiled. “Indeed, it was Zuo who taught him that lesson. The western campaign went so smoothly entirely because of the superiority of its firearms. When Zuo was in the capital, he explained this to Prince Chun in detail. The prince now fully endorses His Excellency’s policy of learning the foreigners’ strengths in order to counter them. A plan is being prepared to make Lushun a naval port and develop a substantial navy. Prince Chun is intensely enthusiastic about it. Naturally, he does not want a moment’s impatience to ruin a great undertaking.” “Quite so! With those three behind him, His Excellency has every reason to hold firmly to the course he believes right.”

“提到择善固执，还有个人不能忽略。筱村，你是出过洋的，你倒说说看，当今之世，论洋务人才，哪个是此中翘楚？”“那当然是玉池老人。连曾侯办洋务都得向他请教。”“玉池老人”是郭嵩焘自署的别号，“曾侯”指驻法钦差大臣曾纪泽。事实上不仅曾纪泽，连李鸿章办洋务亦得向他请教，因为李鸿章虽看得多，却不如郭嵩焘来得透彻，同时亦因为李鸿章虽然亦是翰林，而学问毕竟不如郭嵩焘，发一议，立一论，能够贯通古今中外而无扞格，以李鸿章的口才，来解说郭嵩焘的理论，便越觉得动听了。

“Speaking of holding to the right course, there is another man we must not overlook. Xiaocun, you have been abroad. Tell me: among all the men dealing with foreign affairs today, who is the foremost?” “The Old Man of Yuchi, of course. Even Marquis Zeng has to seek his advice on foreign affairs.” The Old Man of Yuchi was a name Guo Songtao had chosen for himself; Marquis Zeng was Zeng Jize, the imperial envoy to France. In fact, not only Zeng but Li Hongzhang himself had to consult Guo. Li had seen a great deal, but his insight was less penetrating than Guo’s. Moreover, although Li too had belonged to the Hanlin Academy, his scholarship could not match Guo’s. Guo could advance an opinion or develop an argument that embraced ancient and modern times, China and foreign lands, without contradiction. Expounded with Li’s eloquence, Guo’s ideas sounded more persuasive still.

“现在彭雪琴要请款招兵，王阆青已经在湖南招足了四千人，这就是湘阴派出去‘纵火’的人，一旦祸发，立刻就成燎原之势。中堂为此，着急得很，不说别的，只说法国军舰就在吴淞口外好了，人家已经亲口告诉中堂了，随时可以攻制造局，这是北洋的命脉之一，你想，中堂着急不着急。”听得这话，邵友濂大吃一惊，他总以为中法如有冲突，不在广西，便在云南，如果进攻高昌庙的制造局，便是在上海作战，他是上海道，守土有责，岂不是要亲自上阵跟法国军队对垒。转念到此心胆俱裂，结结巴巴地说：“上海也有这样的话，我总以为是谣言，哪知道人家亲口告诉了中堂，是真有这回事！”“你也不要着急。”盛宣怀安慰他说：“人家也不是乱来的，只要你不动手，就不会乱挑衅，你要动手了，人家就会先发制人。”

“Now Peng Xueqin is asking for money to recruit troops, and Wang Langqing has already enlisted his full four thousand men in Hunan. These are the men Zuo is sending out to set fires. Once trouble starts, it will spread like a blaze across the plains. His Excellency is deeply anxious. To take just one example, French warships are lying outside the mouth of the Wusong. The French have told him to his face that they can attack the Manufacturing Bureau at any time. That is one of the lifelines of the Beiyang establishment. How can he help being anxious?” Shao was appalled. He had always assumed that any clash with France would occur in Guangxi or Yunnan. An attack on the Manufacturing Bureau at Gaochangmiao would mean fighting in Shanghai. As circuit intendant, he was responsible for defending his territory. Would he have to go into battle himself against the French? The thought terrified him. “People have been saying that in Shanghai too,” he stammered. “I always took it for a rumour. But if they told His Excellency themselves, then it is true!” “There is no need to be alarmed,” Sheng reassured him. “They do not act indiscriminately. If you do not strike, they will not provoke a quarrel. But if you are going to strike, they will forestall you.”

这话说得再明白不过，邵友濂立即答说：“无论如何不可让湘阴把这把火烧起来。放火要有放火的材料，没有美孚牌煤油、没有一划就来的火柴，火就放不起来。杏荪兄，你说是不是？”“一点不错，这就叫釜底抽薪。”“要釜底抽薪，只有一个办法。”邵友濂说：“煤油、火柴都在胡雪岩手里，没有胡雪岩，湘阴想放火也放下成。江宁官场都不大买湘阴的帐，他说出话去，多多少少要打折扣，只有一个人，他说一是一，说二是二，就是胡雪岩，譬如……”譬如山东火灾助赈，江宁藩座无法支应，左宗棠向胡雪岩借银二十万，如响斯应，这一回王德榜募兵援越不但四千杆洋枪由胡雪岩筹划供给，补助路费亦雪岩负责等等，邵友濂举了好些实例。结论是要使得左宗棠“纵火”不成，非除去胡雪岩不可。

The meaning could not have been clearer. Shao immediately replied, “Whatever happens, we cannot let Zuo start this fire. To set a fire, you need materials. Without Mobil kerosene or matches that light at a stroke, there can be no blaze. Am I right, Brother Xingsun?” “Exactly. That is what they call removing the firewood from under the pot.” “And there is only one way to do it,” Shao said. “Hu Xueyan has all the kerosene and matches. Without him, Zuo could not start a fire even if he wanted to. The officials in Jiangning pay little heed to Zuo; whatever he tells them to do, they comply only in part. There is just one man who does exactly what he says, and that is Hu. For instance…” He offered a number of examples. When relief was needed for the fire in Shandong and the Jiangning provincial treasurer could not provide the money, Zuo had asked Hu for a loan of two hundred thousand taels and received an immediate response. Now Wang Debang was recruiting men to assist Vietnam; Hu was arranging not only the supply of four thousand foreign rifles but also a contribution towards the troops’ travelling expenses. The conclusion was that, to prevent Zuo from setting his fire, Hu Xueyan had to be put out of action.

“本常，”胡雪岩指着邵友濂复他的信说：“你看了这封信就晓得了，人家说得很明白，各省的款子收齐了，马上送过来，限期以前，一定办妥当，误了期限，一切责任由他来负。他到底是上海道，说话算话，不要紧的。”宓本常看完了信问：“洋人的限期是哪一天？”“放宽十天，只要十一月初十以前付款，就不算违限。”“呃，”宓本常说，“大先生预备啥辰光回杭州？”这句话问得胡雪岩大为不悦，“十一月初五的好日子。”他说：“今天是十月二十九，你说我应该啥辰光动身回杭州？”由水路回杭州，用小火轮拖带，至少也要三天，喜期以前，有许多繁文缛节，即便不必由他来料理主持，但必须由他出面来摆个样子，所以无论如何，第二天——十月底一定要动身。宓本常碰了个钉子，不敢再多说一句，心里却七上八下，意乱如麻。但胡雪岩不知道他的心事，只看重在洋债的限期上。“这件事我当然要预备好。”他说：“限期是十一月初十，我们现在亦不必催邵筱村，到了初五、六，你去一趟，看有多少银子先领了回来，照我估计，没有九成，也有八成，自己最多垫个十万两银子，事情就可以摆平了。”“是的。”

“Benchang,” Hu said, pointing to Shao’s reply, “read this and you will understand. He makes it perfectly clear: as soon as all the provincial payments have been collected, he will send them over. Everything will be settled before the deadline, and if it is missed, he will take full responsibility. He is the Shanghai circuit intendant, after all. His word means something. There is nothing to worry about.” After reading it, Mi asked, “What deadline have the foreigners set?” “They have allowed another ten days. So long as the money is paid before the tenth of the eleventh month, it will not count as late.” “Ah. When do you intend to return to Hangzhou, sir?” The question greatly displeased Hu. “The wedding is on the fifth,” he said. “Today is the twenty-ninth of the tenth month. When do you think I ought to leave?” Even with a small steamer towing his boat, the journey to Hangzhou would take at least three days. Before the wedding there were many elaborate formalities; even if he did not have to arrange or preside over them, he had to put in an appearance. Whatever happened, he must therefore set out the next day, the last day of the tenth month. Rebuked, Mi dared not say another word, though inwardly he was in turmoil. Hu knew nothing of his anxiety; his thoughts were fixed on the foreign loan’s deadline. “Of course I must make provision for this,” he said. “Since payment is due on the tenth, there is no need to press Shao now. Go to see him on the fifth or sixth and collect whatever is available. By my estimate, it will be at least eighty per cent, if not ninety. We shall have to advance at most a hundred thousand taels, and that will settle the matter.” “Yes, sir.”

“现在现款还有多少？”问到这话，宓本常心里又是一跳。胡雪岩已经查过帐了，现款还有多少，他心里应该有数，如今提出来，不是明知故问？这样想着，便忘了回答，胡雪岩便再催问一句：“多少？”“呃！”宓本常说：“大先生不是看过帐了，总在四十万上下。”全上海的存银不过一百万两，阜康独家就有四十万，岂能算少？不过胡雪岩也知道他挪用了一部分，心想，四十万虽不足，三十万应该是有的，垫上十万两银子还不足为忧。话虽如此，也不妨再问一句：“如果调度不过来，你有什么打算？”这话就问得怪了！宓本常心想，现银不足，自然是向“联号”调动，无所谓“打算”。他问这话，是否有言外之意？一时不暇细想，只有先大包大揽敷衍了眼前再说，“不会调度不过来的。上海、汉口、杭州三十三处的收支情形，我都很清楚，垫十万银子，不算回事。“他又加了一句，”宁波两个号子，经常有十几万银子在那里。“这是为了掩饰他利用客户的名义，挪用存款。“光棍一点就透”，胡雪岩认为他是在暗示，承认他挪用了十几万银子，必要时他会想法子补足。这样就更放心了。

“How much cash have we now?” Mi’s heart gave another jump. Hu had examined the accounts and ought to know the answer. Why ask a question whose answer he already knew? So absorbed was Mi in this thought that he forgot to reply. Hu prompted him again. “How much?” “Ah! You have seen the accounts, sir. Somewhere around four hundred thousand.” The silver held throughout Shanghai amounted to only a million taels. If Fukang alone held four hundred thousand, how could that be considered little? Hu knew, however, that Mi had diverted some of it. Even if four hundred thousand was no longer there, he supposed that at least three hundred thousand must remain. An advance of one hundred thousand was therefore nothing to worry about. Still, it would do no harm to ask, “If you cannot arrange the funds, what do you intend to do?” An odd question! Mi thought. If ready silver was short, naturally he would draw on the other branches. What else was there to “intend”? Was Hu implying something? There was no time to consider it. For the moment, he could only offer sweeping assurances. “There will be no difficulty arranging the funds. I know the receipts and payments at all thirty-three establishments in Shanghai, Hankou and Hangzhou. Advancing a hundred thousand is nothing.” He added, “The two Ningbo branches regularly have over a hundred thousand taels on hand.” He was trying to conceal the fact that he had used customers’ names to divert deposits. “A shrewd fellow needs only a hint”: Hu took this as an indirect admission that Mi had diverted a hundred thousand taels or more and would find a way to replace it when necessary. This reassured him further.

但他不知道，市面上的谣言已很盛了；说胡雪岩摇摇欲坠，一说他跟洋人在丝茧上斗法，已经落了下风，上海虽无动静，但存在天津堆栈里的丝，贱价出售，尚无买主。又一说便是应付洋债，到期无法清偿。这个传说，又分两种，一种是说，胡雪岩虽好面子，但周转不灵，无法如期交付，已请求洋人展限，尚在交涉之中；又一种说法是，上海道衙门已陆陆续续将各省协饷交付阜康，却为阜康的档手宓本常私下弥补了自己的亏空。谣言必须有佐证才能取信于人，这佐证是个疑问：胡雪岩十一月初五嫁女儿，而他本人却一直逗留在上海，为什么？为的是他的“头寸”摆不平。否则以胡雪岩的作风，老早就该回杭州去办喜事了。这个说法，非常有力，因为人人都能看出这是件大出情理之外的事。但胡雪岩是“财神”，远近皆知，所以大家疑忧虽深，总还有一种想法，既名“财神”，自有他莫测的高深，且等着看一看再说。看到什么时候呢？十月底，看胡雪岩过得了关，过不了关。这些消息——一半假、一半真，似谣言非谣言的传言，大半是盛宣怀与邵友濂通过汇丰银行传出来的。因此众所瞩目的十月三十那天，有许多人到汇丰银行去打听消息，但更多的人是到阜康钱庄去察看动静。

What Hu did not know was how widely the rumours had already spread. People said that he was on the verge of collapse. According to one account, he had lost his contest with the foreigners over silk and cocoons. Although nothing had happened in Shanghai, the silk in his Tianjin warehouses could find no buyer even at a low price. Another concerned the foreign loan: he could not repay it when due. This report had two versions. One was that, proud though Hu was, he could not raise the money in time and had asked the foreigners for an extension, which was still being negotiated. The other was that the Shanghai circuit office had already forwarded the provincial military contributions to Fukang in successive payments, only for its manager, Mi Benchang, secretly to use them to fill the hole in his own accounts. Rumours needed supporting evidence if people were to believe them, and here the evidence took the form of a question: Hu’s daughter was to be married on the fifth of the eleventh month, so why was he still lingering in Shanghai? Because he could not balance his funds. Otherwise, a man of his habits would long since have returned to Hangzhou to prepare for the wedding. It was a powerful argument. Everyone could see how extraordinary his delay was. Yet Hu was known far and wide as the God of Wealth. Deeply troubled though people were, they still felt that someone with that name must possess resources beyond their understanding. They would wait and see. Until when? Until the end of the tenth month, to see whether he could get through the crisis. These reports, half true and half false, neither wholly rumour nor wholly fact, had mostly been circulated by Sheng and Shao through HSBC. Thus, on the anxiously awaited thirtieth of the tenth month, many people went to HSBC for news, and still more went to Fukang to watch what happened.

“胡大先生在不在？”有个衣冠楚楚的中年人踉阜康的伙计说，“我来看胡大先生。”“胡大先生回杭州了。”“回杭州了？”“是啊！胡府上十一月初办喜事，胡大先生当然要赶回去。”“幄，既然如此，应该早就动身了啊！为啥……”为啥？这一问谁也无法回答。那衣冠楚楚的中年人，便是盛宣怀所遣派的散播谣言的使者，他问别人说：胡雪岩看看事情不了，遁回杭州了。于是当天下午就有人持着阜康的银票来兑现，第一个来的“凭票付银”五百两，说是要行聘礼，不但要现银，而且最好是刚出炉的“官宝”。阜康的伙计，一向对顾客很巴结，特为到库房里去要了十个簇新的大元宝，其中有几个还贴着红纸剪成的双喜，正就是喜事人家的存款。第二个来兑现八百两，没有说理由，伙计也不能问理由，这也是常有的事，无足为奇，但第三个就不对了。

“Is Mr Hu here?” a well-dressed middle-aged man asked a Fukang employee. “I have come to see him.” “Mr Hu has returned to Hangzhou.” “Returned to Hangzhou?” “Yes. There is a wedding in his family early next month. Naturally, he has to hurry home.” “Oh? In that case, he ought to have left long ago. Why…” Why? Nobody could answer that question. The well-dressed visitor was an agent sent by Sheng to spread rumours. He went about telling people that Hu, seeing the situation was hopeless, had fled to Hangzhou. That afternoon, people began bringing Fukang notes to be redeemed for silver. The first presented a bearer note for five hundred taels. He explained that the money was for betrothal gifts: he needed actual silver, preferably official ingots fresh from the mould. Fukang’s employees had always been eager to oblige their customers. They made a special trip to the vault for ten brand-new large ingots. Several still bore the double-happiness characters cut from red paper: they had themselves been deposited by families celebrating weddings. The second customer redeemed eight hundred taels without giving a reason. The employees had no right to ask for one. Such a transaction was perfectly ordinary and gave no cause for surprise. The third was another matter.

这个人是带了一辆板车、两个脚夫来的，交到柜上一共七张银票，总数两万一千四百两。象这样大笔兑现银，除非军营发恼，但都是事先有关照的。伙计看苗头不对，赔着笑脸说：“请里面坐，吃杯茶，歇一歇。”“好，好，费你的心。”说完，那人徐步走到客座，接受款待。这时宓本常已接到报告，觉得事有蹊跷，便赶出来亲自接待，很客气地请教：“贵姓？”“敝姓朱。请教！”“我姓亦，宝盖下面一个必字。”宓本常说：“听说朱先生要兑现银？”“是的。”“两万多现银，就是一千两百多斤，大元宝四百多个，搬起来很不方便。”宓本常又说：“阜康做生意，一向要为主顾打算妥当，不晓得朱先生要这笔现银啥用场，看看能不能汇到那里，或者照朱先生指定的数目，分开来换票，岂不是省事得多。”“多谢关照。”姓朱的说：“这笔款子，有个无可奈何的用场，我不便奉告。总而言之，人家指定要现银，我就不能不照办。我也知道搬起来很笨重，所以带了车子带了人来的。”话说到这样，至矣尽矣，宓本常如果再饶一句舌，就等于自己在金字招牌砍了一刀，所以喏喏连声，马上关照开库付银。

He arrived with a handcart and two porters, and laid seven notes on the counter, totalling twenty-one thousand four hundred taels. A cash withdrawal on that scale generally meant a military camp was paying its men, and advance notice was always given. Sensing trouble, the employee put on a courteous smile. “Please come inside, have some tea and rest a little.” “Certainly, certainly. You are very kind.” The visitor strolled over to the guests’ seats and accepted the invitation. Mi had already been informed. Feeling that something was amiss, he hurried out to receive the man himself and politely asked his name. “Zhu. And yours?” “Mi—the character for ‘must’ under a roof,” Mi replied. “I understand that you wish to redeem your notes for silver?” “Yes.” “Over twenty thousand taels of silver weighs more than twelve hundred jin. That is upwards of four hundred large ingots, very awkward to move.” He continued, “At Fukang, we always try to make the best arrangements for our customers. What do you need this silver for, Mr Zhu? Perhaps we could remit it to the destination, or exchange these notes for smaller ones in whatever amounts you require. Would that not be much easier?” “Thank you for your consideration. I have an unavoidable use for this money that I am not at liberty to explain. In any case, the recipient insists on actual silver, and I have no choice but to comply. I know it is cumbersome to move. That is why I brought the cart and the men.” That left nothing more to be said. One further objection from Mi would be a blow struck at Fukang’s own golden reputation. He murmured his assent and at once ordered the vault opened and the silver paid out.

银子的式样很多，而两万多不是个小数目，也无法全付五十两一个的大元宝，大小拼凑，还要算成色，颇为费事。银子是装了木箱的，开一箱，验一箱，算一箱，搬一箱，于是聚集了许多看热闹的人，议论纷纷，到最后自然而然地形成一个疑问：莫非阜康的票子都靠不住，所以人家才要提现？等姓朱的一走，阜康则到了打烊的时候，上了排门吃夜饭。宓本常神情诅丧，食不下咽，勉强吃了半碗饭，站起身来，向几个重要的伙计招招手，到后面楼上他卧室中去密谈。“我看要出鬼！”他问：“现银还有多少？”“一万八千多，”管库的说。“只有一万八千多？”宓本常又问，“应收应解的一共多少？”于是拿总帐跟流水帐来看，应收的是外国银行的存款及各钱庄的票据，总共十五万六千多两，应付的只能算各联号通知的汇款，一共六万两左右，开出的银票，就无法计算了。

Silver came in many forms, and twenty thousand taels or more was no small sum. They could not pay it all in large fifty-tael ingots. They had to combine ingots of different sizes and allow for differences in purity, a troublesome business. The silver was stored in wooden boxes. Each box had to be opened, examined, reckoned and carried away in turn. A crowd gathered to watch, talking excitedly, until one question naturally emerged: were Fukang’s notes no longer trustworthy? Was that why people were demanding silver? By the time Zhu left, it was closing time. They put up the shutters and sat down to supper. Mi looked dejected and could scarcely swallow. He forced down half a bowl of rice, rose and beckoned several senior employees to follow him upstairs to his bedroom at the back for a private conference. “I think we are in for trouble. How much silver is left?” “A little over eighteen thousand taels,” the vault keeper replied. “Only eighteen thousand? What is the total of our receivables and payments due?” They consulted the general ledger and the daybook. Receivables consisted of deposits at foreign banks and notes drawn on other Chinese banks, totalling over one hundred and fifty-six thousand taels. For payments due, they could count only the remittances ordered by the other branches, about sixty thousand taels in all. There was no way to calculate how much would be claimed on the banknotes they had issued.

“这样子，今天要连夜去接头。都是大先生的事业，急难相扶。他们有多少现银，开个数目给我，要紧要慢的时候，请他们撑一撑腰。”所谓“他们”，是指胡雪岩在上海所设的典当、丝行、茧行。阜康四个重要的伙计，奔走半夜情况大致都清楚了，能够集中的现银，不过十二万两。宓本常将应收应付的帐目，重新仔细核算了一下，能够动用的现银，总数是二十三万两左右。“应该是够了。”宓本常说：“只要不出鬼，就不要紧。”他突然想起大声喊道：“阿章，阿章！”阿章是学徒中的头脑，快要出师了，一向经管阜康的杂务，已经上床了，复又被喊了起来说话。“你‘大仙’供了没有？”“供大仙是初二、十六，今天是月底。”“提前供，提前供！现在就供。”所谓“大仙”就是狐仙，初二、十六上供，一碗烧酒，十个白的蛋，酒是现成，蛋要上街去买。时已午夜，敲排门去买了蛋来，煮好上供，阿章上床已经两点钟了。

“In that case, you must make contact with them tonight. They are all Mr Hu’s businesses; we should support one another in an emergency. Get me figures for whatever silver they have. Ask them to stand behind us when things are urgent.” By “them,” he meant Hu’s pawnshops, silk firms and cocoon firms in Shanghai. Four senior employees spent half the night making their rounds. When they had a reasonably clear picture, the silver that could be brought together amounted to only one hundred and twenty thousand taels. Mi carefully recalculated what was receivable and payable. Altogether, about two hundred and thirty thousand taels could be made available. “That ought to be enough,” he said. “So long as no devilry breaks out, we shall be all right.” Suddenly remembering something, he shouted, “A’zhang! A’zhang!” A’zhang was the leading apprentice, almost ready to complete his training, and had always managed Fukang’s miscellaneous chores. He was already in bed, but was called up again. “Have you made the offering to the Great Immortal?” “The offerings are made on the second and the sixteenth. Today is the last day of the month.” “Make it early! Early! Do it now.” The Great Immortal was the fox spirit. On the second and sixteenth of each month, it received a bowl of spirits and ten white eggs. There was liquor to hand, but the eggs had to be bought. It was already midnight. A’zhang went out and knocked on shop shutters until he could buy some, then boiled them and set out the offering. It was two in the morning before he went back to bed.

第二天在床上被人叫醒，来叫他的是他的师兄弟小毛，“阿章，阿章！”他气急败坏地说：“真的出鬼了！”“你说啥？”“你听！”阿章侧耳静听了一下，除了市声以外，别无他异，不由得诧异地问：“你叫我听啥？”“你听人声！”说破了，果然，人声似乎比往日要嘈杂，但“人声”与“鬼”又何干？“你们去看看，排门还没有卸，主顾已经在排长龙了。”阿章一听，残余的睡意都吓得无影无踪了，急忙起来，匆匆洗把脸赶到店堂里，只见宓本常仰脸看着高悬在壁的自鸣钟。钟上指着八点五十分，再有十分钟就要卸排门了，就这时只听宓本常顿一顿足说：“迟开不如早开，开！”

The next morning, his fellow apprentice Xiaomao woke him. “A’zhang! A’zhang!” he cried in a panic. “The devilry really has begun!” “What are you talking about?” “Listen!” A’zhang listened intently. Apart from the usual sounds of the street, he heard nothing unusual. “What am I supposed to hear?” he asked in bewilderment. “The voices!” Now that it had been pointed out, the voices did seem louder than usual. But what did voices have to do with devilry? “Go and look! The shutters are still up, and the customers are already queuing down the street!” That frightened every last trace of sleep out of A’zhang. He scrambled up, hurriedly washed his face and ran into the banking hall. Mi was gazing up at the clock mounted high on the wall. It showed ten minutes to nine. There were still ten minutes before the shutters were due to come down. At that moment, Mi stamped his foot. “Better to open early than late. Open up!”

于是刚刚起床的阿章，即时参加工作，排门刚卸下一扇，人群如潮水般涌来，将他挤倒在地，阿章在叫：“要出人命了！要出人命了！”幸而巡捕已经赶到，头裹红布的“印度阿三”，上海人虽说司空见惯，但警棍一场，还是有相当的弹压作用，数百顾客，总算仍旧排好长龙。巡捕中的小头目，上海人称之为“三道头”，进入阜康，操着山东腔的中国话问道：“谁是掌柜？”“是我！”宓本常挺身而出。“你开钱庄？”“钱庄不是阿拉开的，不过归阿拉管。”“只要是你管就好。快把银子搬出来，打发人家走路，免得把市面弄坏。”“银子有的是。三道头，拜托你维持维持秩序，一个一个来。”三道头点点头，朝柜台外面大声说道：“银子有的是，统通有，一个一个来！”这一声喊，顾客又安静了些。伙计们都是预先受过叮嘱的，动作尽量放慢，有的拿存折来提存，需要结算利息，那一来就更慢了，站柜台的六个人，一个钟头只料理了四五十个客户，被提走的银子，不到一万，看样子局面可以稳住了。

A’zhang, barely out of bed, joined in the work at once. As soon as the first shutter was removed, the crowd surged in like a tide and knocked him to the ground. “Someone will be killed!” he cried. “Someone will be killed!” Fortunately, the police had arrived. Shanghai people were accustomed to the red-turbaned Indian policemen they called “A-san,” but raised batons still had a considerable restraining effect. The several hundred customers were finally put back into a long queue. A junior police officer, known in Shanghai as “Three Stripes,” entered Fukang and asked in Chinese with a Shandong accent, “Who is the manager?” “I am!” Mi stepped forward. “You own this bank?” “I do not own it. I manage it.” “As long as you manage it, that will do. Get the silver out and send these people on their way. We cannot have the whole street disrupted.” “There is plenty of silver. Officer, please keep order and let them come one at a time.” Three Stripes nodded, then shouted towards the customers beyond the counter, “There is plenty of silver! Enough for everyone! One at a time!” This quietened them further. All the employees had been instructed beforehand to work as slowly as possible. Some customers brought passbooks and required interest to be calculated on their withdrawals, which slowed matters still more. In an hour, the six men at the counter served only forty or fifty customers, who took away less than ten thousand taels. It looked as though the situation could be kept under control.

到了近午时分，来了一个瘦小老者，打开手巾包，将一扣存折递进柜台，口中说道：“提十万。”声音虽不高，但宓本常听来，恰如焦雷轰顶，急心亲自赶上来应付，先看折子户名，上写“馥记”二字，暗暗叫一声“不妙！”“请问贵姓？”“敝姓毛。”“毛先生跟兆馥先生怎么称呼？”“朋友。”“幄。毛先生请里面坐。”“也好。”姓毛的徐步踏入客座，小徒弟茶烟伺候，等坐定了，宓本常问道：“毛先生是代兆馥先生来提十万银子？”“是的。”“不晓得在什么地方用，请毛先生吩咐下来，好打票子。”“在本地用。”“票子打几张？姓毛的抬眼看了一下，慢吞吞地问道：“你是打哪里的票子？”宓本常一慢，心想自然是打阜康的银票，他这样明知故问，必有缘故在内，因而便探问他说：“毛先生要打哪里的票子？”“汇丰。”

Towards noon, a thin, small elderly man arrived. Opening a bundle wrapped in a handkerchief, he passed a bankbook over the counter and said, “A withdrawal of one hundred thousand.” His voice was quiet, but to Mi it was a thunderclap overhead. He hurried forward to deal with the customer himself and first examined the account name in the passbook: Fuji. Trouble, he thought. “May I ask your name?” “Mao.” “How are you connected with Mr Zhaofu?” “We are friends.” “I see. Please come inside and sit down, Mr Mao.” “Very well.” Mao strolled into the seating area, where an apprentice brought tea and tobacco. Once he was settled, Mi asked, “You have come to withdraw one hundred thousand taels on Mr Zhaofu’s behalf?” “Yes.” “Where is the money to be used? If you will let me know, I can have notes drawn up.” “Locally.” “How many notes would you like?” Mao raised his eyes and asked slowly, “Which bank’s notes are you going to give me?” Mi was taken aback. Fukang notes, naturally. There had to be a reason for asking something so obvious. He therefore sounded the man out. “Which bank’s would you prefer?” “HSBC.”

宓本常心里又是一跳，汇丰的存款只有六万多，开十万的支票，要用别家的庄票去补足，按规定当天不能抵用，虽可情商通融，但苦于无法抽空，而且当此要紧关头，去向汇丰讨情面，风声一传，有损信用。转念到此，心想与其向汇丰情商，何不舍远就近向姓毛的情商，“毛先生，”他说：“可不可以分开来开？”“怎样分法？”“一半汇丰、一半开本号的票子？”姓毛的微微一笑，“不必了。”他说：“请你把存折还给我。”宓本常心想，果不其然，是张兆馥耍花样，原来“馥记”便是张兆馥，此人做纱花生意，跟胡雪岩是朋友，宓本常也认识，有一回吃花酒，彼此都有了酒意，为一个姑娘转局，席面上闹得不大愉快。第二天宓本常酒醒以后，想起来大为不安，特意登门去赔不是，哪知张兆馥淡淡地答了一句：“我是你们东家的朋友，不必如此。”意思是不认他作朋友。如今派人上门来提存，自是不怀好意，不过何以要提又不提了，其中是何蹊跷，费人猜疑。

Mi’s heart jumped again. Fukang had only a little over sixty thousand taels on deposit at HSBC. To write a cheque for one hundred thousand, he would have to make up the difference with notes from other banks, and under the rules these could not be drawn against on the day they were deposited. He might persuade HSBC to make an exception, but he could not get away. Besides, if word spread that he had gone begging HSBC for a favour at such a critical moment, Fukang’s credit would suffer. Rather than appeal to HSBC, why not appeal to the man sitting before him? “Mr Mao,” he said, “could we divide the payment?” “How?” “Half in an HSBC cheque, and half in our own notes?” Mao smiled faintly. “There is no need,” he said. “Please give me back the passbook.” So he had been right, Mi thought: Zhang Zhaofu was playing a trick. The Fuji account belonged to Zhang, a cotton and yarn merchant and a friend of Hu’s whom Mi also knew. Once, while drinking at a courtesan banquet, they had quarrelled over a courtesan’s transfer from one party to another. Both had had too much wine. Sober the next morning, Mi felt deeply uneasy and made a special visit to apologise. Zhang had merely said coolly, “I am a friend of your employer’s. There is no need for this.” His meaning was that he did not regard Mi as a friend. Now he had sent someone to withdraw his deposit, clearly with unfriendly intentions. Yet why demand the money and then decide not to take it? That was a puzzle.

等将存折接到手，姓毛的说道：“你害我输了东道！”“输了东道？”宓本常问道：“毛先生你同哪位赌东道？赌点啥？”“自然是同张兆馥……”姓毛的说，这天上午他与张兆馥在城隍庙西园吃茶，听说阜康挤兑，张兆馥说情势可危，姓毛的认为阜康是金字招牌，可保无虞。张兆馥便说阜康在汇丰银行的存款，只怕不足十万，不信的话，可以去试一试，如果阜康能开出汇丰银行十万两的支票，他在长三堂子输一桌花酒，否则便是姓毛的作东。糟糕到极点了！宓本常心想，晚上这一桌花酒吃下来，明天十里夷场上就不知道有多少人会传说：阜康在汇丰银行的存款，只得五万银子。果然出现这样的情况，后果不堪设想，非力挽狂澜不可。宓本常左思右想，反复盘算，终于想到了一条路子，将上海道衙门应缴的协饷先去提了来，存在汇丰，作为阜康的头寸，明天有人来兑现提存，一律开汇丰的支票。

When Mao had his passbook back, he said, “You have cost me a dinner!” “A dinner?” Mi asked. “Who have you made a wager with, Mr Mao? And what was the bet?” “Zhang Zhaofu, of course…” Mao explained that he and Zhang had been taking tea that morning in the West Garden of the City God Temple when they heard of the run on Fukang. Zhang said its position was dangerous. Mao maintained that a bank with Fukang’s golden reputation was safe beyond question. Zhang then said that Fukang probably had less than one hundred thousand taels at HSBC. If Mao doubted it, he could put it to the test. If Fukang could write an HSBC cheque for one hundred thousand taels, Zhang would pay for a banquet at a changsan courtesan house; otherwise, Mao would be the host. This was disastrous! After that evening’s banquet, Mi thought, who could say how many people throughout the foreign settlements would be repeating the next day that Fukang had only fifty thousand taels at HSBC? The consequences were unthinkable. He had to turn the tide. After considering and reconsidering every possibility, he finally saw a way: collect the military contributions due from the Shanghai circuit office and deposit them at HSBC as Fukang’s available funds. Then, the next day, anyone demanding redemption or withdrawing a deposit could be paid with an HSBC cheque.

宓本常每回到上海道衙门去催款或打听消息，都找他的一个姓朱的同乡。这次一见面，姓朱的便问：“你怎么有工夫到这里来？”宓本常愕然：“为什么我没有工夫？”他反问一句。“听说阜康挤兑。”姓朱的说：“你不应该在店里照料吗？”宓本常一惊，挤兑的消息已传到上海道衙门，催款的话就难说，但他的机变很快，心想正好用这件事来作借口，“挤兑是说得过分了，不过提存的人比平常多，是真的，这都是十月二十一的一道上谕，沿江戒严，大家要逃难的缘故。阜康的头寸充足，尽管来提，不要紧。”他紧接着又说：“不过，胡大先生临走交代，要预备一笔款子，垫还洋款，如今这笔款子没有办法如数预备了，要请你老兄同邵大人说一说，收到多少先拨过来，看差多少，我好筹划。”“好！”姓朱的毫不迟疑地说：“你来得巧，我们东家刚到，我先替你去说。”宓本常满心欢喜，而且不免得意，自觉想出来的这一招很高明，哪知姓朱的很快地就回来了，脸上却有狐疑的神气。

Whenever Mi visited the Shanghai circuit office to press for payment or ask for news, he called on a fellow townsman named Zhu. This time, as soon as they met, Zhu asked, “How have you found time to come here?” Mi was astonished. “Why should I not have time?” he countered. “I hear there is a run on Fukang. Should you not be at the bank looking after things?” Mi was alarmed. News of the run had reached the circuit office, which would make it harder to ask for money. But he was quick-witted enough to see that he could use the situation as his excuse. “A run is an exaggeration. It is true that more people than usual are withdrawing their deposits, but that is because of the edict of the twenty-first of the tenth month, declaring martial law along the river. People are preparing to flee. Fukang has ample funds; they can withdraw as much as they please.” He hurried on. “But before he left, Mr Hu told me to set aside money to advance towards the foreign loan repayment. Now I cannot have the full amount ready. Please ask His Excellency to send over whatever he has already collected, so I can see how much is still lacking and arrange for it.” “Certainly!” Zhu replied without hesitation. “You have come at just the right moment. His Excellency has only just arrived. I shall speak to him for you.” Mi was delighted and could not help congratulating himself on his clever move. But Zhu returned very quickly, with suspicion on his face.

“你请放心回去好了，这笔洋款初十到期，由这里直接拨付，阜康一文钱都不必垫。”宓本常一听变色，虽只是一瞬间的事，姓朱的已看在眼里，越加重了他的疑心，“老宓，我倒问你句话，我们东家怪我，怎么不想一想，阜康现在挤兑，官款拨了过去，替你们填馅子，将来怎么交公帐。”他问，“你是不是有这样的打算？”宓本常哪里肯承认！连连摇手：“没有这话，没有这话！”“真的？”“当然真的，‘我怎么会骗你。”“我想想你也不会骗我，不然，你等于叫我来‘掮木梢’，就不象朋友了。“这话在宓本常是刺心的，惟有赔着笑道谢，告辞出来，脚步都软了，仿佛阜康是油锅火山等着他去跳似的。

“You can go back with your mind at rest. The foreign payment is due on the tenth, and this office will pay it directly. Fukang will not have to advance a single cash.” Mi changed colour. It lasted only an instant, but Zhu noticed, and his suspicions deepened. “Mi, let me ask you something. His Excellency blamed me for failing to think it through. There is a run on Fukang. If official funds are sent over to fill the hole in your finances, how are the public accounts to be settled afterwards? Was that what you had in mind?” How could Mi admit it? He waved his hands in repeated denial. “Nothing of the kind! Nothing of the kind!” “Really?” “Of course. How could I deceive you?” “I did not think you would deceive me. Otherwise, you would be making me shoulder the consequences. That would hardly be the act of a friend.” The words cut Mi to the heart. He could only offer a strained smile, thank Zhu and take his leave. His legs felt weak beneath him, as though Fukang were a cauldron of boiling oil or a blazing volcano waiting for him to leap into it.

回到阜康，他是从“灶披间”的后面进去的，大门外人声鼎沸，闻之心惊，进门未几，有个姓杜的伙计拦住他说：“宓先生，你不要到前面去！”“为啥？”“刚才来了两个大户，一个要提二十五万、一个要提十八万，我说上海的头寸，这年把没有松过，我们档手调头寸去了，他说明天再来，你一露面，我这话就不灵了。”山穷水尽的窗本常真有柳暗花明之乐，心想说老实话也是个搪塞法子，这姓社的人很能干，站柜台的伙计，以他为首，千斤重担他挑得动，不如就让他来挑一挑。于是他想了一下说：“不错！你就用这话来应付，你说请他们放心，我们光是丝就值几百万银子，大家犯不着来挤兑。”“我懂。”杜伙计说：“不过今天过去了，明天要有交代。”“那两个大户明天再来，你说我亲自到宁波去提现款，要五天工夫。”宓本常又说：“我真的要到宁波去一趟，现在就动身。”“要吃中饭了，吃了饭再走。”“哪里还吃得下饭。”宓本常拍拍他的肩，“这里重重托你。等这个风潮过去了，我要在大先生面前好好保荐你。”

Back at Fukang, Mi entered by the rear of the kitchen. The clamour outside the front door filled him with dread. He had scarcely come in when an employee named Du stopped him. “Mr Mi, do not go out to the front!” “Why?” “Two big depositors were here just now. One wanted two hundred and fifty thousand, the other one hundred and eighty thousand. I told them funds had been tight in Shanghai for the past year or so, and our manager had gone out to arrange the money. They said they would come back tomorrow. If you show yourself, that explanation will no longer hold.” At the end of his resources, Mi suddenly felt that a way had opened before him. The truth could serve as an excuse too. Du was a capable man, the senior clerk at the counter. He could shoulder a heavy burden; why not let him try? After a moment’s thought, Mi said, “Quite right! That is how you should answer them. Tell them there is nothing to worry about. Our silk alone is worth several million taels. There is no need for everyone to rush in and demand their money.” “I understand,” Du replied. “But even if we get through today, we must have an answer tomorrow.” “When those two big depositors come back, tell them I have gone to Ningbo myself to collect cash. It will take five days.” Mi added, “I really am going to Ningbo. I am leaving now.” “It is nearly lunchtime. Eat before you go.” “How could I swallow anything?” Mi patted his shoulder. “I am placing a great trust in you here. Once this storm has passed, I shall put in a strong recommendation for you with Mr Hu.”

哪知道午后上门的客户更多了，大户也不比上午的两个好说话，人潮汹涌，群情愤慨，眼看要出事故，巡捕房派来的那个“三道头”追问宓本常何在？姓杜的只好说实话：“到宁波去了。”“这里怎么办？”谁也不知道怎么办？只有阿章说了句：“只好上排门。”

But that afternoon still more customers arrived, and the large depositors were less accommodating than the two who had come that morning. The crowd surged angrily, and trouble looked imminent. The officer called Three Stripes demanded to know where Mi was. Du had no choice but to tell the truth. “He has gone to Ningbo.” “What is to be done here?” Nobody knew. Only A’zhang said, “We can only put up the shutters.”
